Universe | How many stars can you see on a clear, dark night? | Only about 2,500 to 5,000 at once with the naked eye, even far from city lights. The Milky Way alone has a few hundred billion.
Universe | Is the Sun a big star? | No, it is an ordinary, middle-sized yellow dwarf. Some stars, like Betelgeuse, are wide enough to swallow the orbits of Mars or beyond.
Universe | Why do stars twinkle but planets mostly do not? | Stars are so far away they look like tiny points, and moving air bends that thin beam. Planets show small discs, so the wobbles average out.
Universe | Is there a dark side of the Moon? | Not a permanently dark one. There is a far side we never see from Earth, but it gets as much sunlight as the near side.
Universe | How long does moonlight take to reach us? | About 1.3 seconds. The Moon is roughly 384,000 kilometres away.
Universe | What is a light-year? | A distance, not a time: how far light travels in one year, about 9.46 trillion kilometres.
Universe | Which is the nearest star after the Sun? | Proxima Centauri, about 4.2 light-years away. Its light left it more than four years ago.
Universe | Why is Mars red? | Its dust is rich in iron oxide, which is rust. Fine red dust blown into the air also tints its sky.
Universe | Which planet has the longest day? | Venus. It spins so slowly that one turn takes about 243 Earth days, longer than its year of about 225 days.
Universe | Is Pluto still a planet? | Since 2006 it is called a dwarf planet, because it has not cleared its path of other icy bodies.
Universe | What is a black hole? | A region where matter is packed so tightly that nothing, not even light, can climb out once it crosses the edge, the event horizon.
Universe | Are comets and asteroids the same? | No. Asteroids are mostly rock and metal; comets carry ice and dust and grow glowing tails when they come near the Sun.
Universe | Why does the Moon look bigger near the horizon? | It is an illusion of the brain. Photos show the Moon is the same size high in the sky and near the horizon.
Universe | What is a shooting star? | Not a star at all. It is a speck of space dust, often smaller than a grain of sand, burning up as it hits our air at high speed.
Universe | How old is the Sun? | About 4.6 billion years, and it is roughly halfway through its life as a steady star.
Universe | Can you hear sounds in space? | No. Sound needs air or another material to travel through, and space is almost empty.
Universe | Why does Saturn have rings? | They are billions of pieces of ice and rock in orbit, probably the remains of moons or comets broken up by Saturn's gravity.
Universe | What is the largest planet? | Jupiter. More than 1,300 Earths could fit inside it, and it has more mass than all the other planets put together.
Universe | How hot is the surface of the Sun? | About 5,500 degrees Celsius. Its thin outer layer, the corona, is strangely much hotter, over a million degrees.
Universe | What is the Milky Way? | Our home galaxy: a spiral of a few hundred billion stars. The milky band in a dark sky is its disc seen from inside.
Universe | Is the Moon moving away from Earth? | Yes, by about 3.8 centimetres a year. Lasers bounced off mirrors left by Apollo astronauts measure it.
Universe | What makes the northern and southern lights? | Charged particles from the Sun hit our upper air near the poles and make its gases glow green, red and purple.
Universe | Which planet is the hottest? | Venus, not Mercury. Its thick carbon dioxide air traps heat, so it stays around 465 degrees Celsius.
Universe | Why is the sky dark at night if there are so many stars? | The universe has an age, so light from very distant stars has not reached us yet, and expansion stretches old light out of sight.
Space | Who was the first person in space? | Yuri Gagarin of the Soviet Union, on 12 April 1961. His flight went once around Earth in about 108 minutes.
Space | When did humans first walk on the Moon? | On 20 July 1969, during Apollo 11. Neil Armstrong stepped out first, then Buzz Aldrin.
Space | Who was the first Indian in space? | Rakesh Sharma, in 1984, aboard a Soviet Soyuz spacecraft. He spent about eight days in orbit.
Space | What did Chandrayaan-3 achieve? | In August 2023 India became the first country to land a spacecraft near the Moon's south pole.
Space | What was Mangalyaan? | India's Mars Orbiter Mission. In 2014 it reached Mars orbit on the first try, a first for any country.
Space | How fast does the International Space Station move? | About 28,000 kilometres per hour. Its crew sees around 16 sunrises every day.
Space | Why do astronauts float in the space station? | Not because gravity is gone. The station and its crew are falling around Earth together, so they feel weightless.
Space | What was the first satellite? | Sputnik 1, launched by the Soviet Union on 4 October 1957. It was a metal ball about 58 centimetres wide that beeped radio signals.
Space | How far has a human-made object travelled? | Voyager 1, launched in 1977, is more than 24 billion kilometres away and has left the Sun's bubble of solar wind.
Space | Why are rockets launched toward the east? | Earth spins eastward, so launching that way borrows free speed from the spin. Launch sites near the equator get the most.
Space | What does ISRO stand for? | The Indian Space Research Organisation, founded in 1969. Vikram Sarabhai is called the father of India's space programme.
Space | What is the James Webb Space Telescope? | A huge infrared telescope launched in 2021. It sees heat light from the earliest galaxies and the air of distant planets.
Earth | How old is the Earth? | About 4.54 billion years, worked out from the oldest meteorites and rocks using radioactive clocks.
Earth | What is the Earth's core made of? | Mostly iron and nickel. The inner core is solid and the outer core is liquid metal, whose flow makes our magnetic field.
Earth | Why do earthquakes happen? | Earth's crust is broken into plates that slowly move. Stress builds where they lock together and is released in a sudden slip.
Earth | How much of Earth is covered by water? | About 71 percent. But about 97 percent of all water is salty sea; only a small share is fresh and easy to use.
Earth | What is the deepest point in the ocean? | The Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench, about 11 kilometres down. Mount Everest would fit with room to spare.
Earth | Why is the sea salty? | Rain slowly washes salts from rocks into rivers and then to the sea. Water evaporates, but the salt stays behind.
Earth | What makes a volcano erupt? | Molten rock rises because it is lighter than the rock around it. Gas dissolved in it expands near the surface and can blast it out.
Earth | Why do we have seasons? | Earth's axis is tilted about 23.4 degrees. Each half of the planet leans toward the Sun for part of the year and gets stronger sunlight.
Earth | Is Earth a perfect ball? | No. Its spin makes it bulge at the equator, about 43 kilometres wider across the equator than from pole to pole.
Earth | What is the highest mountain on Earth? | Mount Everest, about 8,849 metres above sea level. Measured from its base on the sea floor, Mauna Kea in Hawaii is taller.
Earth | What is a tsunami? | A series of long, fast waves pushed by a sudden shift of the sea floor, usually an undersea earthquake, which can grow huge near shore.
Earth | What are fossils? | Remains or traces of ancient life, often turned to stone as minerals slowly replace bones, shells or wood buried in sediment.
Earth | Where does sand come from? | Mostly from rocks broken down by water, wind and ice over long ages. Some beaches are made of crushed shells and coral.
Earth | Why does the Earth have a magnetic field? | Swirling liquid iron in the outer core acts like a giant dynamo. The field shields us from much of the Sun's harmful particles.
Earth | What is the largest hot desert? | The Sahara in Africa. The largest desert of all is Antarctica, because a desert is defined by how little rain or snow it gets.
Earth | Can a river flow uphill? | Not on its own over land, but some rivers flow north, like the Nile, because north is not the same as uphill.
Earth | What is the longest river in the world? | The Nile and the Amazon are both about 6,400 to 7,000 kilometres; experts still argue which is longer, depending on where each source is placed.
Weather | What causes thunder? | Lightning heats the air to around 30,000 degrees Celsius in an instant. The air expands so fast it makes a shock wave we hear as thunder.
Weather | How can you tell how far away lightning is? | Count the seconds between the flash and the thunder and divide by three. That gives the distance in kilometres.
Weather | What brings the Indian monsoon? | In summer the land heats faster than the sea, and the seasonal shift of winds draws moist air in from the Indian Ocean.
Weather | Why do clouds float if water is heavy? | Cloud droplets are tiny, about a hundredth of a millimetre, and rising air easily holds them up. They fall as rain when they merge and grow.
Weather | Why is it colder on mountain tops? | Rising air expands as pressure drops, and expanding air cools. Air thins and cools by about 6.5 degrees for every kilometre up.
Weather | What makes a rainbow? | Sunlight enters raindrops, bends, bounces off the back and bends again, splitting into colours. You see it with the Sun behind you.
Weather | What is the difference between weather and climate? | Weather is what happens today; climate is the average pattern over about thirty years or more.
Weather | What is a cyclone? | A huge spinning storm over warm seas. The same storm is called a hurricane in the Atlantic and a typhoon in the northwest Pacific.
Climate | What is the greenhouse effect? | Gases like carbon dioxide let sunlight in but slow heat leaving. Without them Earth would average about minus 18 degrees Celsius.
Climate | Why does melting sea ice not raise sea level much, but land ice does? | Floating ice already displaces its weight in water. Ice on land, like Greenland's, adds new water to the sea when it melts.
Climate | What is the ozone layer? | A thin layer of ozone gas about 15 to 35 kilometres up that absorbs most harmful ultraviolet light from the Sun.
Climate | Is the ozone hole getting better? | Yes. After the 1987 Montreal Protocol banned most ozone-harming chemicals, the layer has begun slowly healing.
Geography | Which is the largest country by area? | Russia, covering about 17 million square kilometres across 11 time zones.
Geography | Which country has the most people? | India, since 2023, with more than 1.4 billion people, just ahead of China.
Geography | What is the smallest country in the world? | Vatican City, inside Rome, at about 0.44 square kilometres.
Geography | Why are maps usually drawn with north at the top? | It is a habit, not a rule of nature. Many old maps put east or south at the top; European printing made north-up common.
Geography | Why does Greenland look as big as Africa on many maps? | The common Mercator map stretches areas near the poles. Africa is really about 14 times bigger than Greenland.
Geography | What is the Tropic of Cancer? | The line about 23.4 degrees north where the Sun is directly overhead at noon on the June solstice. It passes through eight Indian states.
Geography | Which is the largest ocean? | The Pacific. It is bigger than all the land on Earth put together.
Geography | What is a delta? | Land built where a river slows at the sea and drops its mud. The Ganga-Brahmaputra delta is the world's largest.
Geography | Why is the Thar Desert dry? | The monsoon winds pass over it with little to stop them, and the Aravalli hills run parallel to the winds instead of blocking them.
Geography | What is GIS? | Geographic Information System: software that stores data with locations, so you can map and analyse it, like flood risk or school access.
Geography | What is latitude and longitude? | A grid for finding any place: latitude measures north or south of the equator, longitude east or west of Greenwich in London.
Geography | Why does India have one time zone? | By choice. Indian Standard Time is set at 82.5 degrees east, near Mirzapur, even though the country spans about 30 degrees of longitude.
India | Why is the Aravalli range special? | It is one of the oldest fold mountain ranges on Earth, worn down over more than a billion years to low hills.
India | Which is the longest river in India? | The Ganga is the longest river flowing mostly within India, about 2,500 kilometres from Gangotri to the Bay of Bengal.
India | Where is the wettest place in India? | Mawsynram and Cherrapunji in Meghalaya, which get around 11,000 millimetres of rain in a year.
India | How many languages are listed in India's Constitution? | 22 languages in the Eighth Schedule. Hundreds more are spoken across the country.
India | What is the national animal of India? | The Bengal tiger. India holds about three quarters of the world's wild tigers.
India | Why is Rajasthan called the land of kings? | Its name means land of rulers: it was made of many princely states ruled by Rajput kings before 1947.
India | Which is the largest state of India by area? | Rajasthan, at about 342,000 square kilometres.
India | What is the Indian Constitution famous for? | It is one of the longest written constitutions of any country. It came into force on 26 January 1950.
India | Who designed India's national flag? | Pingali Venkayya's design was the basis. The final flag with the Ashoka Chakra was adopted on 22 July 1947.
India | Why is 26 January Republic Day? | On 26 January 1950 the Constitution came into force. The date was chosen because the call for complete independence was made on 26 January 1930.
India | What does the Ashoka Chakra have? | 24 spokes. It comes from the Lion Capital of Ashoka at Sarnath.
India | Why is Kota known across India? | It became a famous hub of coaching for engineering and medical entrance exams. It also sits on the Chambal river.
India | What is the Rawatbhata power station? | The Rajasthan Atomic Power Station, near Kota on the Chambal, one of India's nuclear power sites.
History | Who built the Great Pyramid of Giza? | Paid Egyptian workers, not slaves, for the pharaoh Khufu around 2560 BCE. It was the tallest human-made structure for nearly 4,000 years.
History | What was the Indus Valley Civilisation? | One of the first urban civilisations, around 2600 to 1900 BCE, with planned cities like Harappa and Mohenjo-daro and covered drains.
History | Has anyone read the Indus script? | Not yet. Its short seal inscriptions remain undeciphered, partly because no long texts or bilingual inscriptions have been found.
History | Who was Ashoka? | A Maurya emperor of the 3rd century BCE who, after the bloody Kalinga war, turned to Buddhism and carved messages of dhamma on rocks and pillars.
History | What was Nalanda? | A great centre of learning in Bihar from about the 5th century CE, with students from across Asia. It was destroyed around 1200.
History | When did the Second World War end? | In 1945: in Europe in May, and in Asia in September after Japan surrendered.
History | What was the Dandi March? | Gandhi's 1930 walk of about 385 kilometres to the sea at Dandi to make salt, breaking the British salt law.
History | Why was the printing press so important? | Gutenberg's movable-type press, around 1440, made books cheap and fast to copy, spreading ideas and reading across Europe.
History | Did Vikings wear horned helmets? | No. That image comes from 19th-century costumes and opera. Real Viking helmets were plain.
History | Who was the first woman to win a Nobel Prize? | Marie Curie, in 1903 for physics. She won again in 1911 for chemistry, the first person to win twice.
History | Was the Great Wall of China built at one time? | No. It is many walls built and rebuilt over about 2,000 years; most of what tourists see today dates from the Ming dynasty.
History | Why did the Mughal emperor build the Taj Mahal? | Shah Jahan built it in Agra as a tomb for his wife Mumtaz Mahal, who died in 1631. It took about 20 years.
History | What was the Silk Road? | Not one road but a web of trade routes linking China, India, Persia and the Mediterranean, carrying silk, spices, ideas and religions.
History | When did India become independent? | On 15 August 1947, after a long freedom struggle. It became a republic on 26 January 1950.
History | Who was Aryabhata? | An Indian mathematician and astronomer born in 476 CE. He said Earth spins on its axis and gave a very good value for pi.
History | Who wrote the Arthashastra? | It is credited to Kautilya, also called Chanakya, adviser to Chandragupta Maurya. It is an ancient guide to statecraft and economics.
History | What were the Olympic Games originally? | Religious festivals for Zeus at Olympia in Greece, held every four years from 776 BCE according to tradition.
History | What was the Renaissance? | A rebirth of art and learning in Europe from about the 14th to 17th centuries, inspired by ancient Greek and Roman ideas.
History | When was the first photograph taken? | Around 1826 or 1827, by Nicephore Niepce in France. The exposure took hours, maybe days.
Human body | How many bones are in the adult human body? | 206. Babies are born with nearly 300, and many fuse together as they grow.
Human body | What is the largest organ of the body? | The skin. An adult's skin weighs a few kilograms and covers nearly two square metres.
Human body | How long are your blood vessels? | Laid end to end, an adult's blood vessels would stretch roughly 100,000 kilometres, mostly tiny capillaries.
Human body | How often does the heart beat? | About 60 to 100 times a minute at rest, or around 100,000 times a day.
Human body | Why do we yawn? | Scientists are still not sure. One leading idea is that yawning helps cool the brain; it is also contagious between people.
Human body | Why do we have fingerprints? | They may help grip and sense texture. Their patterns form in the womb and are unique, even between identical twins.
Human body | What is the smallest bone? | The stapes, or stirrup, in the middle ear, about 3 millimetres long. It passes sound to the inner ear.
Human body | How much of your body is water? | About 60 percent for an adult man and a little less for a woman. A newborn is about 75 percent water.
Human body | Why do we get goosebumps? | Tiny muscles pull our hairs upright when we are cold or scared. In furry ancestors this fluffed up fur for warmth or looked bigger.
Human body | Do we use only 10 percent of our brain? | No, that is a myth. Brain scans show we use nearly all of it, though not every part at once.
Human body | Why do we sleep? | Sleep helps the brain store memories and clear out waste, and lets the body repair. Long without sleep, thinking falls apart.
Human body | Why do onions make us cry? | Cutting them releases a gas that turns into a mild irritant when it meets the water in our eyes, so tears flush it out.
Human body | Why does your voice sound strange in a recording? | You normally hear yourself partly through your skull bones, which adds deeper tones. A recording gives only the air-carried sound.
Human body | What do red blood cells do? | They carry oxygen from the lungs to every part of the body, using a protein called haemoglobin. They live about 120 days.
Human body | Why do we hiccup? | The diaphragm muscle suddenly twitches, pulling air in, and the vocal cords snap shut, making the hic sound.
Human body | Can your stomach digest itself? | It would, but a thick layer of mucus protects its wall from the strong acid, and the lining renews itself every few days.
Human body | What is the strongest muscle? | It depends how you measure. For force for its size, the jaw muscle, the masseter, is often named.
Human body | Why do we have two eyes? | Two slightly different views let the brain judge depth, and a second eye is a spare.
Human body | How fast do nerve signals travel? | The fastest travel at about 100 metres per second, over 350 kilometres per hour, along insulated nerve fibres.
Human body | Why does hair turn grey? | Hair cells slowly stop making the pigment melanin as we age. Genes mostly decide when it starts.
Health | Why should you finish washing your hands for 20 seconds? | Scrubbing with soap that long breaks up the oily coats of many germs and lifts them off so water can rinse them away.
Health | What do vaccines do? | They teach your immune system to recognise a germ safely, so it can fight the real one quickly. They have saved many millions of lives.
Health | Who discovered penicillin? | Alexander Fleming, in 1928, when mould killed bacteria on a plate he had left out. Others later turned it into a medicine.
Health | Why do antibiotics not work on colds? | Colds are caused by viruses, and antibiotics only kill bacteria. Using them needlessly helps bacteria become resistant.
Health | Why is sugar bad for teeth? | Bacteria in the mouth eat sugar and make acid, which slowly dissolves tooth enamel and causes cavities.
Health | What does yoga mean? | The Sanskrit word means to join or unite. The International Day of Yoga is on 21 June.
Health | Why do we need vitamin D? | It helps the body take in calcium for strong bones. Skin makes it in sunlight.
Health | How much water should you drink? | It depends on heat, size and activity. Food and other drinks count too; thirst and pale urine are good guides.
Biology | How many species live on Earth? | Scientists have named about two million, but estimates of the real total range from about eight million to far more.
Biology | What is DNA? | The molecule that carries the instructions for building and running living things, written in four chemical letters, A, T, G and C.
Biology | How much DNA do humans share with chimpanzees? | About 98 to 99 percent of the DNA sequence that can be lined up, though small differences have big effects.
Biology | How do plants make food? | By photosynthesis: using sunlight to turn carbon dioxide and water into sugar, releasing oxygen as a by-product.
Biology | Why are leaves green? | Chlorophyll absorbs red and blue light for photosynthesis and reflects green light back to our eyes.
Biology | Why do leaves change colour in autumn? | Trees stop making chlorophyll, so the yellow and orange pigments hidden underneath show through. Reds are made fresh.
Biology | Which is the largest animal ever? | The blue whale, up to about 30 metres long. It is larger than any known dinosaur.
Biology | How do bees make honey? | They sip flower nectar, add enzymes, and fan it with their wings in the hive until much of the water evaporates.
Biology | Are bats blind? | No. All bats can see, and many see well. Many also use echolocation, calling out and listening to echoes, to hunt in the dark.
Biology | Why do cats purr? | Usually when content, but also when hurt or stressed. Fast twitches of the voice box muscles make the sound.
Biology | Can an octopus change colour? | Yes, in a fraction of a second, using skin cells full of pigment. It also has three hearts and blue blood.
Biology | What is the oldest living tree? | A Great Basin bristlecone pine in California is over 4,800 years old. Some clonal plant colonies are far older.
Biology | Why do birds fly in a V shape? | Each bird rides the rising air from the wingtip of the one ahead, saving energy on long flights.
Biology | How do fireflies glow? | A chemical reaction in their bodies makes cold light, called bioluminescence, used to find mates.
Biology | Are tomatoes fruits or vegetables? | By botany, a fruit, because it grows from the flower and holds seeds. In the kitchen it is used as a vegetable.
Biology | Why are house sparrows declining in cities? | Likely causes include fewer nesting holes in modern buildings, fewer insects for chicks, and less green cover. World Sparrow Day is on 20 March.
Biology | Do goldfish have three-second memories? | No. Experiments show goldfish can remember things for months and can be trained.
Biology | How long can a camel go without water? | Often a week or more in hot weather, and much longer in cool weather. Its hump stores fat, not water.
Biology | What is the fastest land animal? | The cheetah, which can sprint at about 100 to 110 kilometres per hour for short bursts.
Biology | Do plants talk to each other? | In a way. Many release chemicals when attacked, and neighbours can sense them and prepare their defences.
Biology | Why do some animals hibernate? | To survive winters with little food. Their heartbeat, breathing and temperature drop so they burn far less energy.
Biology | What is a mushroom? | The fruiting part of a fungus. Most of the fungus lives as a hidden web of threads in soil or wood.
Biology | Are spiders insects? | No. Spiders are arachnids with eight legs and two body parts; insects have six legs and three body parts.
Biology | Why do zebras have stripes? | Studies suggest the stripes help keep biting flies away, which tend not to land on striped surfaces.
Biology | How do salmon find their home river? | They seem to use Earth's magnetic field to reach the right coast, then smell to find the stream where they hatched.
Biology | Can a starfish regrow an arm? | Yes. Many sea stars can regrow lost arms, and some can grow a whole body from one arm.
Biology | What is the national bird of India? | The Indian peafowl, the peacock, chosen in 1963.
Biology | Why are flamingos pink? | From pigments in the algae and shrimp they eat. Chicks are grey and turn pink as they feed.
Biology | Which animal has the longest pregnancy? | The African elephant, at about 22 months.
Physics | Why do things fall? | Gravity pulls masses toward each other. On Earth, all objects fall at the same rate in a vacuum, as Galileo argued.
Physics | Would a feather and a hammer fall at the same speed? | Yes, without air. Astronaut David Scott showed it on the Moon in 1971; both hit the ground together.
Physics | How fast does light travel? | About 300,000 kilometres per second in empty space. It could circle Earth over seven times in one second.
Physics | Why is ice slippery? | A very thin wet layer forms on the surface of ice, even below freezing, and it acts like a lubricant.
Physics | Why does a spoon in a glass of water look bent? | Light bends as it passes from water into air, because it travels at different speeds in each. This is refraction.
Physics | What is E = mc squared? | Einstein's equation saying mass and energy are two forms of the same thing; a tiny mass holds a huge amount of energy.
Physics | Why does metal feel colder than wood at the same temperature? | Metal pulls heat out of your hand much faster, so it feels colder, though both are the same temperature.
Physics | What is the speed of sound? | About 343 metres per second in air at 20 degrees Celsius. It is much faster in water and faster still in steel.
Physics | Why do magnets attract iron? | Iron's atoms act like tiny magnets. A magnet lines them up so the iron itself becomes magnetic for a while.
Physics | What is a sonic boom? | The shock wave from something moving faster than sound, heard as a loud crack, like a jet or the tip of a cracked whip.
Physics | Why do ships float though steel sinks? | A ship's hull pushes aside a lot of water; if that water weighs more than the ship, the water pushes it up.
Physics | What is absolute zero? | The coldest possible temperature, minus 273.15 degrees Celsius, where particles have the least possible motion.
Physics | Why do we see lightning before we hear thunder? | Light travels about a million times faster than sound, so the flash reaches you almost instantly.
Physics | What is an atom mostly made of? | Mostly empty space. A tiny nucleus holds almost all the mass, with electrons spread in a cloud around it.
Physics | Who was C. V. Raman? | An Indian physicist who discovered how light changes colour when it scatters from molecules. He won the Nobel Prize in 1930.
Physics | Why does a rubbed balloon stick to a wall? | Rubbing moves electrons, giving the balloon a charge, which attracts opposite charges in the wall.
Physics | Why do your ears pop in a plane? | Air pressure changes faster than the air inside your middle ear can adjust. Swallowing opens a tube to balance it.
Physics | How does a rainbow have seven colours? | It is really a smooth spread of colours. Isaac Newton chose seven names, partly to match the seven notes of a musical scale.
Physics | What is friction? | A force that resists sliding, caused by surfaces catching on each other. Without it you could not walk or hold a pen.
Physics | Why does hot air rise? | Heated air expands and becomes less dense than the cooler air around it, so it is pushed upward.
Physics | Who proposed that Earth goes around the Sun? | Nicolaus Copernicus argued it in 1543. Aristarchus of Samos had suggested it nearly 1,800 years before.
Physics | What are X-rays? | A kind of light with very short waves and high energy. They pass through soft tissue but are blocked more by bone.
Physics | Why does a microwave heat food? | Its waves make water molecules in the food spin back and forth very fast, and that motion becomes heat.
Chemistry | What is the most common element in the universe? | Hydrogen, about three quarters of all normal matter by mass. Helium comes second.
Chemistry | What is air made of? | About 78 percent nitrogen, 21 percent oxygen and 1 percent argon, plus small amounts of carbon dioxide and water vapour.
Chemistry | Why does iron rust? | Iron reacts with oxygen and water to form iron oxide. Salt water speeds it up, which is why coastal cars rust faster.
Chemistry | What is water made of? | Two hydrogen atoms joined to one oxygen atom, written H2O.
Chemistry | Why does salt melt ice on roads? | Dissolved salt lowers the temperature at which water freezes, so the ice melts unless it is very cold.
Chemistry | What is a diamond made of? | Pure carbon, the same element as pencil graphite. The atoms are just arranged in a different, very strong pattern.
Chemistry | Who made the periodic table? | Dmitri Mendeleev in 1869 arranged elements by weight and properties and left gaps for elements not yet found.
Chemistry | How many elements are there? | 118 confirmed elements. About 90 occur naturally; the rest were made in laboratories.
Chemistry | What is the only metal that is liquid at room temperature? | Mercury. Gallium melts just above room temperature, even in your hand.
Chemistry | Why does a cut apple turn brown? | An enzyme in the apple reacts with oxygen in the air. Lemon juice slows it down.
Chemistry | Why does popcorn pop? | Water inside each kernel turns to steam when heated; pressure builds until the hard shell bursts and the starch puffs up.
Chemistry | What is pH? | A scale from 0 to 14 for how acidic or basic something is. Pure water is 7; lemon juice is about 2; soap is basic.
Chemistry | Why is gold so valuable? | It is rare, beautiful, easy to shape and never rusts or tarnishes, so it keeps its shine for thousands of years.
Chemistry | Why does bread rise? | Yeast eats sugars and makes carbon dioxide gas; gluten in the dough traps the bubbles.
Chemistry | What gives fireworks their colours? | Metal salts. Strontium makes red, copper makes blue, sodium makes yellow and barium makes green.
Mathematics | What is pi? | The ratio of a circle's distance around to its width, about 3.14159. Its digits go on forever without repeating.
Mathematics | Why are there 60 minutes in an hour? | It comes from the ancient Babylonians, who counted in sixties. 60 divides neatly by 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20 and 30.
Mathematics | What is a prime number? | A whole number greater than 1 that divides evenly only by 1 and itself, like 2, 3, 5, 7 and 11.
Mathematics | Are there infinitely many prime numbers? | Yes. Euclid proved it over 2,000 years ago: multiply all known primes and add one, and you always get a new prime factor.
Mathematics | Where do our numerals 0 to 9 come from? | From India, carried west by Arab scholars, which is why they are called Hindu-Arabic numerals.
Mathematics | Who was Srinivasa Ramanujan? | A self-taught Indian genius who produced thousands of results in number theory before his death at 32 in 1920.
Mathematics | Why is 1729 famous? | It is the smallest number that is the sum of two cubes in two different ways, as Ramanujan pointed out: 1 plus 1,728, and 729 plus 1,000.
Mathematics | What is a googol? | The number 1 followed by 100 zeros. The company Google took its name from it, with a different spelling.
Mathematics | Can you fold a paper in half more than seven times? | It is very hard, because thickness doubles each fold. In 2002 a student folded a very long sheet 12 times.
