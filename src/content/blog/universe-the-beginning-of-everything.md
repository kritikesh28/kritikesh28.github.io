Every subject I study, whether rocks, rivers, maps or people, sits inside something bigger. Follow that chain far enough and you reach the Universe. So my first post starts there, at the beginning of everything.

## What is the Universe?

The Universe is the totality of everything that exists: space, time, matter, energy and every structure built from them. The word comes from the Latin *universum*, "everything taken together as one whole".

Earth is not outside it. You, me, the Aravallis and the most distant galaxy are all part of the same Universe.

It helps to see the levels, from small to large:

**planet → planetary system → galaxy → galaxy group or cluster → supercluster → observable Universe**

A **galaxy** is a huge system of stars, gas, dust and dark matter held together by gravity. The **observable Universe** is only the part whose light has had time to reach us. The whole Universe may be far larger.

## How old is it?

About **13.8 billion years**. Nobody watched a clock run that long. Scientists estimate the age from two kinds of evidence: how fast the Universe is expanding today, and the faint "afterglow" radiation left over from its hot early days, called the cosmic microwave background.

## How did it begin?

The **Big Bang** is the leading scientific model of the Universe's early history. It is easy to misread, so two points first:

1. It was **not an explosion** into empty space. It was space itself expanding from a far hotter, denser state.
2. It does **not** explain the very first instant. Before about 10⁻⁴³ seconds (the Planck time), our physics is incomplete.

As space expanded, the same energy spread over a larger volume, so the Universe cooled. Each drop in temperature let a new, more stable kind of structure survive. That is the whole story in one line: **expand, cool, build**.

## The timeline, step by step

| When (after the Big Bang) | What happened |
|---|---|
| ~10⁻⁴³ seconds | Planck time: the limit of today's physics |
| ~10⁻⁶ seconds | Quarks combine into protons and neutrons |
| ~1 second to a few minutes | The first nuclei form: mostly hydrogen and helium, a little lithium |
| ~380,000 years | Electrons join nuclei to make neutral atoms; light travels freely (this light is today's microwave background) |
| a few hundred million years | Gravity pulls gas clouds together and the first stars ignite |
| the first billion years onward | Galaxies assemble and keep growing and merging |
| ~9 billion years (4.6 billion years ago) | Our Sun and Solar System form from a cloud of gas and dust |
| today, 13.8 billion years | The Universe is still expanding, and the expansion is speeding up |

Two details I love from this list. **Atoms came long before stars**: the Universe was full of plain hydrogen and helium gas for millions of years before anything shone. And the iron in your blood and the silicon in the rocks under your feet were made inside earlier stars. Our Sun took over 99% of its birth cloud, and planets formed from the leftovers, which already carried those star-made elements.

## What is the Universe made of?

This is the most surprising part. Everything we can see, including stars, planets, gas and us, is only about **5%** of the Universe's total energy budget. The rest is:

- **Dark matter, about 27%.** It gives off no light we can detect, but its gravity shows up in how stars orbit inside galaxies and how light bends. It helped pull matter into galaxies. We still don't know what it is made of.
- **Dark energy, about 68%.** It is the name for whatever is making the expansion of the Universe speed up. We know its effect, not its nature.

Ordinary matter is also incredibly spread out. On average there is roughly one hydrogen atom in every few cubic metres of space.

## Is the Universe still growing?

Yes. Light from distant galaxies is stretched toward red (a "redshift"), which tells us they are moving away. In 1929 Edwin Hubble showed that the farther a galaxy is, the faster it recedes, building on Georges Lemaître's work. Today this is called the **Hubble–Lemaître law**: v = H₀ × d.

That does **not** make Earth the centre. Picture dots on a balloon being blown up. Every dot sees all the others moving away, and no dot is the middle. Nearby galaxies held by gravity, such as our Local Group, don't fly apart.

Even the expansion rate (H₀) is not settled. Different methods give slightly different values, a puzzle called the **Hubble tension**.

## How might it end?

Scientists discuss three main futures, and none is proven:

- **Heat death (Big Freeze):** expansion continues forever, stars burn out and the Universe grows cold and dilute over unimaginable time. Current evidence leans this way.
- **Big Crunch:** gravity wins, expansion reverses and everything collapses. Accelerating expansion makes this look unlikely.
- **Big Rip:** dark energy grows stronger and eventually tears apart galaxies, stars and even atoms. This needs a special kind of dark energy that has not been shown to exist.

Which one happens depends mostly on the thing we understand least: dark energy.

## Don't mix these up

- **Space vs Universe:** space is the expanse; the Universe is space plus time, matter, energy and everything else.
- **Astronomy vs cosmology:** astronomy studies objects like stars and planets; cosmology studies the Universe as a whole.
- **Dark matter vs dark energy:** dark matter pulls things together; dark energy pushes the expansion faster.
- **Expansion vs explosion:** an explosion throws material into space; expansion stretches space itself.

## The whole story in one breath

A hot, dense early Universe expanded and cooled. Particles formed, then nuclei, then atoms. Gravity gathered gas into the first stars and galaxies. Stars forged heavier elements, and from that recycled dust our Sun, Earth and we ourselves were made. The Universe is still expanding today, faster and faster, and we still don't know exactly why.
