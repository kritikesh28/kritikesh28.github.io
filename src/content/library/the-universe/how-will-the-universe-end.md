## A surprise in 1998

Gravity pulls things together, so astronomers expected the expansion to be slowing down. Two teams set out to measure how much, using a type of exploding star that shines with a known brightness.

They found the opposite. Distant explosions were fainter than expected, which meant **the expansion is speeding up**. The discovery won the 2011 Nobel Prize in Physics.

## Dark energy, a name for a mystery

Whatever drives the speed-up is called **dark energy**. It makes up about **68%** of the Universe's energy budget, more than everything else combined. The simplest idea is that it is a property of space itself: as space grows, there is more of it, so the push grows.

Recent results from a large galaxy survey called DESI hint that dark energy might be weakening over time. This is not confirmed yet, but if it holds up, it would change the story below.

## Three possible endings

None of these is proven. Each depends on how dark energy behaves:

- **Big Freeze (heat death).** Expansion continues forever. Galaxies drift out of sight, stars burn out over trillions of years, and the Universe becomes cold, dark and thin. Current evidence leans this way.
- **Big Crunch.** Expansion stops and reverses, and everything collapses back together. Unlikely if dark energy stays steady, but possible again if it fades.
- **Big Rip.** Dark energy grows stronger until it tears apart galaxies, stars and eventually atoms. This needs a kind of dark energy that has not been observed.

## Do not worry

These timescales are almost impossible to imagine: stars will keep forming for hundreds of billions of years at least. The Sun itself has about 5 billion years of life left.

## Remember this

Dark matter pulls things together; dark energy pushes the expansion faster. The fate of the Universe depends on the part we understand least, which is exactly why it is worth staying curious.
