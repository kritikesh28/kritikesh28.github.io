## Galaxies spin too fast

In the 1970s the astronomer **Vera Rubin** and her colleague Kent Ford measured how fast stars orbit in spiral galaxies. Stars near the edge should move slowly, the way outer planets in our Solar System do. They did not. They moved about as fast as stars much nearer the centre.

With only the visible stars and gas, those galaxies should fly apart. Something unseen must be adding gravity. Decades earlier, in 1933, Fritz Zwicky had noticed the same kind of problem with whole clusters of galaxies.

## More clues, same answer

- **Bent light.** Mass bends light passing near it. Galaxy clusters bend background light far more than their visible matter can explain.
- **The Bullet Cluster.** When two clusters collided, their hot gas slowed down, but most of their mass, mapped by bent light, kept going. The mass and the visible matter separated.
- **The baby picture.** The pattern in the cosmic microwave background (Leaf 7) only fits if dark matter was there from the start.

## How much is there?

Of the Universe's total energy budget, ordinary matter is about **5%** and dark matter about **27%**. Put another way, for every kilogram of ordinary matter there are roughly five kilograms of dark matter.

## What is it?

We do not know. It is not dark gas or dust, which we can detect. Most scientists think it is a kind of particle not yet discovered, and experiments deep underground are hunting for it. A smaller group of scientists explores whether gravity itself behaves differently on large scales instead.

## Remember this

Dark matter pulls things together. We can measure its gravity very well, but its identity is one of the biggest open questions in science.
