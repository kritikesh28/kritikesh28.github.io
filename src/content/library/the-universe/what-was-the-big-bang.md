## Not a bomb in the dark

Pictures often show the Big Bang as a fireball exploding into black space. That image is wrong in an important way. An explosion throws material **into** space that already exists. The Big Bang was **space itself stretching**, everywhere at once.

There was no centre and no outside. Every point in the Universe was part of the hot, dense beginning, including the place you are sitting now.

## Expand, cool, build

As space stretched, the same energy spread over more volume, so it cooled. Each drop in temperature let new, more stable things survive: first particles, then nuclei, then atoms, then stars. That one pattern runs through the rest of this course.

## Who had the idea

In 1927 the Belgian priest and physicist **Georges Lemaître** proposed that the Universe is expanding and later suggested it began from a "primeval atom". The name "Big Bang" came from the astronomer Fred Hoyle in a 1949 radio talk. He preferred a different theory, and some say he meant the name as a joke.

## Why scientists accept it

The theory is not believed on faith. It predicted things that were later found:

1. Galaxies moving apart (Leaf 11)
2. A glow of leftover light filling the sky (Leaf 7)
3. The exact mix of hydrogen and helium in the oldest gas (Leaf 6)

## What it does not tell us

The model works back to a tiny fraction of a second. Before about 10⁻⁴³ seconds, called the **Planck time**, our physics breaks down. What happened then, and whether "before" even has a meaning, is unknown. Anyone who tells you otherwise is guessing.

## Remember this

The Big Bang is the story of the Universe growing and cooling, backed by evidence. It is not an explosion, and it is not the answer to everything.
