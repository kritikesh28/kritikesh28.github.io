## Stretched light

When a source of light moves away from you, its light waves get stretched to longer, redder wavelengths. This is called **redshift**. (You hear a similar effect when an ambulance siren drops in pitch as it drives away.)

In the 1910s Vesto Slipher found that most galaxies show a redshift. Most of them are moving away from us.

## Farther means faster

In 1927 Georges Lemaître showed that this pattern fits an expanding Universe. In 1929 Edwin Hubble published measurements showing that **the farther a galaxy is, the faster it moves away**. Today this is called the **Hubble–Lemaître law**.

Very distant galaxies even recede faster than light. That does not break any rule, because nothing is moving through space faster than light; space itself is stretching.

## Are we at the centre?

No. Picture dots drawn on a balloon. As you blow it up, every dot sees all the other dots moving away, and the farthest ones moving fastest. No dot is the middle. The Universe works the same way, without needing a balloon or an outside.

Things held together by gravity do not expand. Your body, the Earth, the Solar System and even our group of galaxies stay together. The Andromeda Galaxy is actually moving towards us.

## An open puzzle

The rate of expansion today is called the **Hubble constant**. Measurements from the young Universe's light give about 67 km per second per megaparsec. Measurements using nearby stars give about 73. The gap is called the **Hubble tension**, and nobody knows yet whether it is a measurement problem or a clue to new physics.

## Remember this

Galaxies are not flying through space away from us. Space between them is growing, and everyone, everywhere, sees the same thing.
