## Stars are element factories

The Big Bang made almost only hydrogen and helium. Everything heavier was cooked later, inside stars:

- Ordinary stars fuse hydrogen into helium, and later helium into **carbon** and **oxygen**.
- Massive stars go further, up to **iron** at their cores.
- When massive stars explode as **supernovae**, they blast those elements into space and make some new ones.
- Many of the heaviest elements, such as **gold** and platinum, are thought to come mostly from collisions of neutron stars. One such collision was observed in 2017.

## Recycling the dust

Each generation of stars enriched the gas around it. New stars formed from that richer gas, made more elements, and died in turn.

About **4.6 billion years ago**, a cloud of this recycled gas and dust began to collapse. The centre became the **Sun**, which kept about 99.8% of the mass. The leftover disc of dust and gas clumped into planets, moons and asteroids. Earth was one of them.

## You are part of this story

By count of atoms, roughly six in every ten atoms in your body are hydrogen, made in the first minutes of the Universe (Leaf 6). The carbon in your cells, the oxygen you breathe and the iron in your blood were made inside stars that lived and died before the Sun existed.

The astronomer Carl Sagan put it simply: "We are made of star-stuff."

## Remember this

Your atoms are older than the Earth. The story of the Universe is, quite literally, your family history.
