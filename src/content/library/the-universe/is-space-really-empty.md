## Emptier than any vacuum we can make

The air in your room has about 25 billion billion molecules in every cubic centimetre. The best vacuums made in laboratories still hold thousands of molecules in that same small space.

Space between galaxies beats both. On average there is roughly **one atom in every few cubic metres**, mostly hydrogen. Inside our own galaxy, the gas between stars is thicker, around one atom per cubic centimetre, which is still far emptier than any lab vacuum.

## So what is in it?

Plenty, just spread very thin:

- **Gas and dust.** Mostly hydrogen and helium, with dust grains made by old stars. In some places it gathers into clouds where new stars are born.
- **Ancient light.** Every cubic centimetre of space holds about 400 tiny packets of light left over from the young Universe. You will meet this light again in Leaf 7.
- **Neutrinos.** Nearly massless particles that pass through almost everything. Trillions of them pass through your body every second without harming you.
- **Dark matter.** Something invisible whose gravity we can measure. More on it in Leaf 9.
- **Fields.** Magnetic fields and gravity reach across all of it.

## Why this matters

"Empty" space is where the whole story happens. Clouds of thin gas collapsed into stars, stars made new elements, and those elements became planets and people. Without the stuff in space, there would be nothing else.

## Remember this

Space is not nothing. It is the thinnest thing there is, and it is full of clues about where everything came from.
