# Vision and foundation: why this site exists and the rules it never breaks

[Back to the handbook](README.md)

Read this before planning, writing or building anything. Every other guide in this handbook sits on top of it. If any idea, feature or page conflicts with this page, this page wins.

## In Kritikesh's words

> "My main aim of this complete project is welfare. I want to do welfare by using human psychology, to transfer effortless learning, so that the reader doesn't even notice they are studying under pressure or stress. Honesty, no cheating at all, user benefit first, customer satisfaction, trust and all such ethical norms should be the foundation of this business; everything comes after it. I can afford living under loss, but I can't afford breaking and playing with the trust and emotions of my audience."
> (25 October 2026, translated lightly from Hinglish)

## The mission

Make it possible for people to enjoy understanding the world without feeling forced into a lesson. Help children, students and adults rediscover the pleasure of asking a question and following it until it makes sense. (Shared with the Curious Sparrows YouTube channel's show bible, section C02.)

## The foundation, in order of priority

1. **Trust before growth.** No trick, dark pattern or exaggeration is acceptable, even if it would bring more visitors or money. A loss is acceptable; broken trust is not.
2. **Reader benefit first.** Every page must leave the reader better off than before: clearer, more curious, more confident.
3. **Honesty and accuracy.** Entertainment chooses the route into an explanation; it never chooses what is true. Numbers are checked against current sources. Models, simplifications and uncertainty are labelled.
4. **Effortless, not shallow.** "Effortless" means no needless pressure, fear or confusion, not that deep ideas need no attention. Short pages lower the door; they don't lower the truth.
5. **Respect for time, attention and emotions.** Say up front what the reader will get. Never withhold an answer to force a click. Never use guilt, fear, false urgency or manipulated emotion.
6. **Openness about AI help.** Pages written with AI help say so (`aiAssisted: true`) and carry the author's own voice.
7. **Visible corrections.** When a mistake is found, fix it, add an `updated` date, and thank the person who reported it.

## What we use human psychology for, and what we never use it for

| We use psychology to... | We never use it to... |
| --- | --- |
| Spark curiosity with a real, honest question | Promise something the page doesn't deliver (clickbait) |
| Break big subjects into small, calm steps | Hide the "read all at once" option to inflate page views |
| Show progress so learners feel their growth | Use streaks or badges that guilt people for missing a day |
| End on a new question, after fully answering the first | Leave the main answer unfinished to force the next click |
| Make search always offer something useful | Pretend weak results are strong ones |
| Make the reader feel welcome even when they don't know | Make anyone feel stupid for not knowing |

## Hook integrity (from the show bible, C12.5)

The opening question must be answered honestly on the same page. A connected next question is welcome only after the main promise is fulfilled. If a topic has no settled answer, the honest payoff is to explain what is known, what is uncertain and why.

## How depth works: the Curiosity Ladder

Every subject climbs from the simplest view to expert depth, so a beginner and an expert both find their level (show bible, C12.2):

1. Surface observation: what do we see?
2. Mechanism: how does it happen?
3. Contradiction: where does the simple idea fail?
4. Deeper mechanism: what really matters?
5. Consequence: what follows from it?
6. Connection: how does it link to other systems and subjects?
7. Wonder: seeing the world with new eyes.

## How we measure success

Not by views alone. By readers who understand, ask a deeper question, return, share with someone, gain confidence that a subject is "for people like me", and trust us enough to report a mistake.

## The final test for any idea

> Would this make a curious person feel welcomed into a difficult idea, without any trick, pressure or false promise?

If yes, build it. If no, change it or drop it.
