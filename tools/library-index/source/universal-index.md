# READ THIS FIRST

What this book is. This is an extensive hierarchical index of academic and organised knowledge. It is designed to help a reader discover what fields exist, how they relate to one another, and how broad disciplines divide into branches, specialised fields, topics and smaller study units. It follows the specification in the supplied “Universal Index of Human Knowledge — Master Prompt.”

What this book is not. No finite catalogue can prove that it contains every named field, local knowledge tradition, institutional specialism, research topic and emerging area in the world. This edition is therefore described as an expanded, systematically structured first edition—not a claim of literal final completeness. The taxonomy is broad and detailed, but individual terminal entries are not all separately verified against a curriculum or scholarly source.

How to navigate. The main catalogue is alphabetical by principal field. Sibling entries are alphabetised within their parent. Each entry receives a hierarchical code and a unique edition identifier. The global alphabetical index at the end lets you start from a term and find its canonical path. Cross-disciplinary overlaps are recorded separately so that one topic need not be duplicated as if it were two unrelated disciplines.

The hierarchy. The hierarchy is subject-matter based, not a universal ranking of importance. A branch may be a discipline at one university, a concentration at another, and a research programme elsewhere. Levels are practical rather than absolute. When a meaningful subtopic can be identified, the catalogue descends further; it does not atomise every term, equation or individual research result into a field.

Coverage status. The formal reference frameworks listed in the methodology informed the broad organisation of this work. The detailed entries below are an editorial synthesis intended as a discovery map and should be extended, corrected and checked against local curricula and discipline-specific references before being used as an official scholarly classification.

# HOW TO USE THE TAXONOMY

## Primary placement and cross-reference

Each distinct subject has a canonical place in the main hierarchy. Fields that legitimately operate across disciplinary boundaries appear in their primary location and are also listed in the cross-disciplinary map. This prevents a topic from being duplicated without context while preserving its real connections.

## Discipline versus topic versus tool

A discipline is a sustained area of organised inquiry; a subdiscipline is a recognised branch within it; a topic is a narrower area of study; a method is a way of investigating; a tool is an instrument or software used in practice. These categories interact, but they are not interchangeable.

## Reading codes

Each principal field has a short root code, such as BIO for Biological Sciences. Its child codes extend hierarchically (for example BIO.03.02). The codes identify the structure within this edition; names and full paths remain the most useful way to identify an item across future revisions. Each entry also receives a unique identifier (UKH-\#\#\#\#\#\#) for the global index.

## Educational depth

The catalogue aims to include core foundations, common undergraduate subjects, advanced subject areas, postgraduate branches and selected research extensions. A topic’s presence does not mean it appears in every university programme. National qualification frameworks, professional licensing and curricula differ.

## Alphabetical convention

Primary fields and siblings are sorted alphabetically by their preferred English names, ignoring initial case. Synonyms are not counted as separate canonical entries. Where similarly named entities have different meanings, they should be disambiguated in a future expanded edition.

## Culturally situated knowledge

Traditional and Indigenous knowledge is included as living, contextual knowledge—not merely as historical material. Some knowledge is restricted, sacred, community-owned or not appropriate for public disclosure. Further expansion must respect consent, provenance, community authority, benefit-sharing and cultural protocols.

## How to extend the book

Add a new node only when it represents a distinct field, branch, topic, method or meaningful study unit. Record its parent, preferred name, status, scope, synonyms, sources and cross-disciplinary relations. Then update the global index and coverage audit. Flag uncertainty rather than inventing a definitive classification.

# CLASSIFICATION METHODOLOGY AND REFERENCE FRAMEWORKS

The taxonomy is a synthesis, not a verbatim reproduction of one institutional scheme. Three kinds of source were selected because they serve different purposes: education-field classification, research-field classification and library knowledge organisation.

## UNESCO Institute for Statistics: ISCED-F 2013

The International Standard Classification of Education—Fields of Education and Training (ISCED-F 2013) supplies an internationally comparable structure for classifying education and training fields. It contains broad, narrow and detailed field categories and is useful for checking whether major educational areas have been represented. It is an education classification, not a complete inventory of every research topic. \[S1\]

Source: [Open UNESCO ISCED-F 2013 PDF](#https://uis.unesco.org/en/files/isced-fields-education-and-training-2013-en-pdf)

## OECD: Frascati Manual 2015 and Fields of Research and Development

The Frascati Manual provides guidance for collecting and reporting research and development statistics and describes the Fields of Research and Development (FORD) classification. It is helpful for comparing research areas, while its primary purpose remains research-statistics classification. \[S2\]

Source: [Open OECD Frascati Manual 2015](#https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2015/10/frascati-manual-2015_g1g57dcb.html)

## Library of Congress Classification (LCC)

The Library of Congress Classification provides a broad library organisation of knowledge through major classes and subclasses, including philosophy, history, geography, social sciences, law, education, arts, languages, sciences, medicine, agriculture, technology, military and information resources. Its library purpose means it should not be confused with a universal academic ontology. \[S3\]

Source: [Open Library of Congress classification outline](#https://www.loc.gov/aba/cataloging/classification/lcco/)

## Additional discipline-level references

In later verification passes, each domain should be checked against scholarly societies, academic encyclopedias, national research classifications, subject headings, university programmes, postgraduate syllabi, technical standards and peer-reviewed review literature. A framework-level source does not independently verify every terminal topic in this book.

# HIERARCHY LEVELS AND ENTRY CONVENTIONS

| Level | Typical entity | Use in this book |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| L0 | Human knowledge | Entire scope of organised inquiry |
| L1 | Principal field / major domain | Broad entry point, alphabetised globally |
| L2 | Discipline or branch | Major internal area of study |
| L3 | Subdiscipline / specialised field | Recognised area within a broader discipline |
| L4 | Topic domain / subject | Coherent study area or curriculum unit |
| L5 | Topic / subtopic | Narrower conceptual or practical grouping |
| L6+ | Further subdivision | Meaningful technical, theoretical or methodological detail |
| Terminal unit | Smallest useful study unit | A defensible stopping point; not every fact or individual research result |

Levels are flexible. For instance, “attention” can be a topic in cognitive psychology, a construct in cognitive neuroscience, or a computational mechanism in artificial intelligence. The complete path and cross-references carry more meaning than the numerical depth alone.

# COVERAGE AUDIT AND EXPANSION ROADMAP

A taxonomy of world knowledge is an ongoing editorial and research project. This audit records what the present edition does and does not establish.

## Breadth of domains

Broad coverage of the principal formal, natural, human, social, applied, professional, artistic and traditional-knowledge domains is represented in the root catalogue.

## Recursive depth

Many branches descend through subdisciplines to topics and fundamental study units. Depth is uneven by design because fields vary in size and internal structure; all roots remain candidates for additional expansion.

## Alphabetical navigation

Principal fields and siblings are alphabetised. A global alphabetical index is appended for all node names recorded in this edition.

## Framework-level source review

UNESCO ISCED-F, the OECD Frascati/FORD framework and the Library of Congress Classification outline inform the design. These are complementary classification tools, not interchangeable or exhaustive taxonomies.

## Entry-level verification

Not all entries have been individually checked against current national curricula, professional standards or peer-reviewed discipline references. This remains a defined next-stage task.

## Regional and Indigenous knowledge

Relevant traditions are represented at a high level. Specific local taxonomies should be developed with reliable regional sources and appropriate community authority; public inclusion must not override restricted knowledge protocols.

## Emerging disciplines

Emerging and integrative fields are represented selectively. Their status changes; new editions should date-stamp and source-check all status labels.

## Terminal granularity

The stopping point is a meaningful study unit, not every fact, equation, organism, molecule, algorithm implementation or research paper. Highly specialised subjects can be expanded in domain-specific supplements.
