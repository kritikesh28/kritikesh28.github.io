# PART 3 — PHYSICAL SPORTS: INDOOR

> **About this Part.** Sports usually played under a roof: court and racket games, gymnastics and strength, combat and martial arts, cue sports, bowling and darts. Several (volleyball, kabaddi, futsal) are played both indoors and out.

## SPT-10 Indoor court and racket games

> **Before you start — SPT-10.** Several of these were invented on purpose by teachers or officers who needed a game for a hall or a rainy season.

- ☐ **SPT-10.1 Badminton**: shuttlecock over a high net; played by British officers in **Poona (Pune)** in the 1870s, named after the Duke of Beaufort's Badminton House; **BWF** (founded 1934 as the IBF); the All England Championships (1899); Olympic since 1992. India: Prakash Padukone (All England 1980), Pullela Gopichand (All England 2001), Saina Nehwal (Olympic bronze 2012), **P. V. Sindhu** (Olympic silver 2016, bronze 2020; world champion 2019), Satwiksairaj Rankireddy and Chirag Shetty, Lakshya Sen.
- ☐ **SPT-10.2 Table tennis**: "ping-pong" from Victorian England (1880s); **ITTF** (1926); Olympic since 1988; China's dominance; the "Ping-Pong Diplomacy" between the USA and China (1971); India's Sharath Kamal, Manika Batra.
- ☐ **SPT-10.3 Squash**: from Harrow School, England (about 1830); World Squash, PSA tour; joins the Olympics at Los Angeles 2028; India's Saurav Ghosal, Joshna Chinappa, Dipika Pallikal.
- ☐ **SPT-10.4 Basketball**: invented by **James Naismith** in Springfield, Massachusetts (December 1891) with peach baskets; **FIBA** (1932); the NBA (1946); 3×3 basketball is Olympic since 2020.
- ☐ **SPT-10.5 Volleyball**: invented by **William G. Morgan** in Holyoke, Massachusetts (1895) as "Mintonette"; FIVB (1947); Olympic since 1964; huge in Brazil, Italy, Poland, Japan; Kerala's village volleyball culture.
- ☐ **SPT-10.6 Handball**: Denmark and Germany, around 1900; IHF; Olympic; very popular in Europe.
- ☐ **SPT-10.7 Netball**: from early women's basketball (1890s); strong in England, Australia, New Zealand, Jamaica; Commonwealth Games sport.
- ☐ **SPT-10.8 Futsal, floorball, korfball** (Dutch mixed-gender game), **sepak takraw** (kick volleyball of South-East Asia; also outdoors), **throwball** (India), **tenniquoit (ring tennis)**.

## SPT-11 Gymnastics, strength and fitness sports

> **Before you start — SPT-11.** Sports of the body's control and power. Gymnastics comes from the German *Turnen* movement of Friedrich Ludwig Jahn (1810s); **FIG** (1881) is the oldest international sports federation still in existence.

- ☐ **SPT-11.1 Artistic gymnastics**: floor, vault, bars, beam, rings, pommel horse; Nadia Comăneci's first perfect 10 (1976); Simone Biles; India's Dipa Karmakar (fourth in vault, Rio 2016).
- ☐ **SPT-11.2 Rhythmic gymnastics, trampoline, acrobatic and aerobic gymnastics, parkour (FIG discipline)**.
- ☐ **SPT-11.3 Mallakhamb**: Indian pole and rope gymnastics from Maharashtra; revived by Balambhatdada Deodhar in the 19th century; now in the National Games and Khelo India; also SPT-17.
- ☐ **SPT-11.4 Weightlifting**: snatch, clean and jerk; Olympic; **Karnam Malleswari** (bronze 2000, the first Indian woman to win an Olympic medal) and **Mirabai Chanu** (silver, Tokyo 2020).
- ☐ **SPT-11.5 Powerlifting, strongman, bodybuilding, CrossFit, arm wrestling**: strength competitions outside the Olympics.
- ☐ **SPT-11.6 Yoga as sport (yogasana)**: competitive yogasana is in Khelo India and the National Games; International Yoga Day (21 June, since 2015).
- ☐ **SPT-11.7 Dance sport**: ballroom and **breaking** (breakdance, Olympic only at Paris 2024).

## SPT-12 Combat sports and martial arts

> **Before you start — SPT-12.** Combat sports have the oldest roots of all (wrestling is in the earliest art). Many Asian martial arts are also philosophies of self-discipline.

- ☐ **SPT-12.1 Wrestling**: freestyle and Greco-Roman; **United World Wrestling**; Indian **kushti** in the *akhara* (mud pit), Great Gama (undefeated in the early 20th century); **Khashaba Dadasaheb Jadhav** won independent India's first individual Olympic medal (bronze, Helsinki 1952); Sushil Kumar (2008, 2012), Sakshi Malik (2016), Bajrang Punia, Ravi Dahiya, Aman Sehrawat (bronze, 2024).
- ☐ **SPT-12.2 Boxing**: modern rules from the Marquess of Queensberry (1867); amateur and professional; Muhammad Ali; India's **M. C. Mary Kom** (six-time world champion, Olympic bronze 2012), Vijender Singh (2008), Lovlina Borgohain (2020), Nikhat Zareen.
- ☐ **SPT-12.3 Judo**: founded by **Kanō Jigorō** at the Kodokan, Tokyo (1882); Olympic since 1964.
- ☐ **SPT-12.4 Karate** (Okinawa), **taekwondo** (Korea; Olympic since 2000), **kung fu / wushu** (China; Asian Games), **aikido**, **kendo**, **sumo** (Japan's national sport), **jiu-jitsu** (Brazilian jiu-jitsu).
- ☐ **SPT-12.5 Fencing**: foil, épée, sabre; from duelling schools; Olympic since 1896; **Bhavani Devi** was India's first Olympic fencer (2021).
- ☐ **SPT-12.6 Kickboxing, Muay Thai, savate, sambo, MMA** (mixed martial arts; the UFC since 1993).
- ☐ **SPT-12.7 Indian martial arts**: **kalaripayattu** (Kerala; with weapons and healing), **silambam** (Tamil Nadu, staff), **thang-ta** (Manipur, sword and spear), **gatka** (Punjab, Sikh), **mardani khel** (Maharashtra), **lathi khela** (Bengal), **pari-khanda** (Bihar); several are in the Khelo India Games.

## SPT-13 Cue sports, bowling, darts and indoor athletics

> **Before you start — SPT-13.** Precision games of the club, the pub and the hall. Snooker was invented in India.

- ☐ **SPT-13.1 Billiards**: English billiards, carom (French), pool (American, eight-ball and nine-ball); India's **Pankaj Advani** has won more than 25 world titles in billiards and snooker; Wilson Jones (world amateur billiards champion, 1958) and Geet Sethi.
- ☐ **SPT-13.2 Snooker**: devised by British army officers at **Jabalpur** in 1875 (credited to the young officer Neville Chamberlain, later Colonel Sir Neville Chamberlain, not the prime minister) and codified at Ootacamund (Ooty) in 1882; the World Championship at the Crucible, Sheffield (since 1977).
- ☐ **SPT-13.3 Ten-pin bowling, nine-pin, duckpin, candlepin**; World Bowling.
- ☐ **SPT-13.4 Darts**: England; the PDC and WDF world championships; huge TV following.
- ☐ **SPT-13.5 Indoor athletics, indoor rowing, indoor cycling, climbing walls, table football (foosball), air hockey**.

# PART 4 — MIND GAMES

> **About this Part.** Games won by thinking: board games, cards, tiles, puzzles, words and quizzes. Some are pure skill (chess, Go), some mix skill and luck (backgammon, bridge, poker), and some are pure luck (Snakes and Ladders).

## SPT-14 Board games

> **Before you start — SPT-14.** Board games are among the oldest human inventions (SPT-00.01). India gave the world chess, pachisi (Ludo) and Snakes and Ladders.

### ☐ SPT-14.1 Chess and its family

- ☐ **Chess**: from Indian *chaturanga* (SPT-00.03); **FIDE** (founded Paris, 1924); the first official world champion was **Wilhelm Steinitz** (1886). **Viswanathan Anand** became India's first grandmaster (1988) and world champion (2000; undisputed 2007–2013). **D. Gukesh** became the youngest undisputed world champion (Singapore, December 2024, aged 18). In April 2026 **Javokhir Sindarov** (Uzbekistan) won the Candidates Tournament to challenge Gukesh, and **R. Vaishali** won the Women's Candidates. India won both gold medals at the 2024 Chess Olympiad (Budapest); **Divya Deshmukh** won the 2025 Women's World Cup.
- ☐ **Xiangqi** (Chinese chess, with a river and cannons), **shogi** (Japanese chess, where captured pieces return), **makruk** (Thai chess), **janggi** (Korean chess).
- ☐ **Chess variants**: Chess960 (Fischer Random), blitz and bullet, correspondence chess, chess problems and studies.

### ☐ SPT-14.2 Strategy games of the world

- ☐ **Go (weiqi, baduk)**: China, more than 2,500 years old; black and white stones on a 19×19 grid; **AlphaGo** (DeepMind) beat Lee Sedol in 2016, a landmark for AI.
- ☐ **Draughts (checkers)**, **Reversi/Othello**, **Nine Men's Morris**, **Hex**, **Connect Four**.
- ☐ **Aadu puli attam (goats and tigers)**: a South Indian hunt game; similar "hunt games" are played across Asia (the Nepali *bagh-chal*).
- ☐ **Mancala family**: *pallanguzhi* (Tamil Nadu), *oware* (West Africa), *bao* (East Africa).

### ☐ SPT-14.3 Race and chance board games

- ☐ **Senet** and the **Royal Game of Ur** (see SPT-00.01).
- ☐ **Backgammon**: from the Persian *nard*; skill and dice.
- ☐ **Pachisi and chaupar**: the cross-shaped Indian race game played with cowrie shells; Akbar's giant courtyard board at Fatehpur Sikri. It became **Ludo** (patented in England, 1896) and the American *Parcheesi*.
- ☐ **Snakes and Ladders**: from the Indian *Moksha Patam* / *Gyan Chaupar*, a moral game where virtues are ladders and vices are snakes; taken to England in the 1890s.
- ☐ **Correction from the brief: Chinese checkers is not Chinese.** It was invented in Germany (1892) as *Stern-Halma* and given the name "Chinese checkers" by American marketers in 1928.

### ☐ SPT-14.4 Modern board games

- ☐ **Monopoly**: from **Lizzie Magie**'s *The Landlord's Game* (1904), a critique of landlords; sold by Parker Brothers from 1935. India's *Business* (Trade) game is a local version.
- ☐ **Scrabble** (Alfred Butts, 1938), **Cluedo/Clue** (1949), **Risk** (1957), **Catan** (Klaus Teuber, 1995), **Ticket to Ride**, **Carcassonne**, **Pandemic**: the "Eurogame" boom.
- ☐ **Carrom**: South Asian finger-flick game; the International Carrom Federation (1988); India and Sri Lanka lead; often played at home and in clubs.
- ☐ **Tabletop and role-playing games**: *Dungeons & Dragons* (1974), miniature war games.

## SPT-15 Cards, tiles, puzzles, words and quizzes

- ☐ **SPT-15.1 Playing cards**: invented in Tang-dynasty China; spread through the Islamic world (Mamluk cards) to Europe in the 1370s; the French suits (hearts, diamonds, clubs, spades). India's **ganjifa**, round painted cards of the Mughal court and the Dashavatara sets of Odisha and Sawantwadi.
- ☐ **SPT-15.2 Card games**: bridge (the World Bridge Federation, 1958; a medal sport at the 2018 Asian Games, where India won gold in men's pairs), rummy, teen patti, poker (skill and chance; real-money versions are now banned online in India), solitaire, uno; trading-card games such as *Magic: The Gathering* and *Pokémon*.
- ☐ **SPT-15.3 Tile games**: **mahjong** (China, 19th century), **dominoes**, **rummikub**.
- ☐ **SPT-15.4 Puzzles**: the jigsaw (John Spilsbury, London, 1760s, as a geography lesson); the **crossword** (Arthur Wynne, New York World, 1913); **sudoku** (Howard Garns's "Number Place", 1979; named by Nikoli, Japan, 1984); the **Rubik's Cube** (Ernő Rubik, 1974) and speedcubing; tangram (China).
- ☐ **SPT-15.5 Word games**: Scrabble, Boggle, Wordle (2021), antakshari (the Indian song-chain game), shabd-khoj (word search), riddles (*paheli*).
- ☐ **SPT-15.6 Quizzes**: *Bournvita Quiz Contest* (Derek O'Brien), *Mastermind India*, *Kaun Banega Crorepati* (2000, from *Who Wants to Be a Millionaire?*), university quiz clubs, the Tata Crucible; quiz master Siddhartha Basu.
- ☐ **SPT-15.7 Memory and mental sports**: mental calculation, memory championships, the Mind Sports Olympiad.

# PART 5 — DIGITAL GAMES AND ESPORTS

> **About this Part.** Video games are the largest entertainment industry in the world by revenue. This Part maps their kinds, platforms and competitive scene. **See also:** TEC (computing), MOV (games and film).

## SPT-16 Video games and esports

### ☐ SPT-16.1 Genres

- ☐ **Action and platform** (*Super Mario Bros.*, 1985), **adventure**, **role-playing (RPG)** (*The Legend of Zelda*, *Final Fantasy*), **shooter** (first-person: *Doom*, *Counter-Strike*; battle royale: *PUBG*, *Fortnite*, *Free Fire*), **strategy** (real-time: *StarCraft*; turn-based: *Civilization*), **MOBA** (*League of Legends*, *Dota 2*), **sports and racing** (*FIFA/EA Sports FC*, *Gran Turismo*), **fighting** (*Street Fighter*), **puzzle** (*Tetris*, 1984), **simulation and sandbox** (*The Sims*, *Minecraft*), **rhythm**, **horror**, **casual and mobile** (*Candy Crush*, *Ludo King*).

### ☐ SPT-16.2 Platforms and milestones

- ☐ **Early**: *Tennis for Two* (1958), *Spacewar!* (1962), *Pong* (Atari, 1972).
- ☐ **Arcade** (1970s–80s: *Space Invaders*, *Pac-Man*); **home consoles** (Atari 2600, Nintendo Famicom/NES 1983, Sega, Sony PlayStation 1994, Xbox 2001, Nintendo Switch 2017); **PC**; **handhelds** (Game Boy 1989); **mobile** (*Snake* on Nokia phones, 1997; smartphones from 2007); **VR and AR** (*Pokémon Go*, 2016); **cloud gaming**.
- ☐ **Indian games and studios**: *Ludo King* (Gametion, 2016), *Raji: An Ancient Epic*, Indian mobile studios; *BGMI* (India's version of *PUBG Mobile*).

### ☐ SPT-16.3 Esports

- ☐ **Titles and tournaments**: *League of Legends* World Championship, *Dota 2*'s *The International*, *Counter-Strike* Majors, *Valorant* Champions, the FIFAe/EA FC competitions, *BGMI* and *Free Fire* leagues in India.
- ☐ **Multi-sport events**: demonstration at the 2018 Asian Games; medal events at Hangzhou 2022; the IOC's Olympic Esports plans (check olympics.com for status).
- ☐ **Careers**: players, coaches, analysts, casters, streamers, game designers; most earn modestly; a few earn millions.
- ☐ **India's law**: the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 bans real-money online games and sets up support for esports and social games.

### ☐ SPT-16.4 Healthy gaming

- ☐ WHO lists **gaming disorder** in ICD-11 (2019) for severe cases; most players are not affected. Healthy habits: time limits, breaks, sleep first, play with people you know, no gambling-like spending (SPT-29).
