## SPT-00.01 What was the first game?

**Short answer:** Running, wrestling and chasing games are surely older than history, because even young animals play-fight. The oldest game we can still play from its own board is the Royal Game of Ur, from Mesopotamia, about 4,600 years old; the Egyptian game senet is older still.

**Keywords:** first game ever, oldest board game, Royal Game of Ur, senet, history of games

### Play is older than humans

Puppies wrestle, crows slide down snowy roofs, and young chimpanzees play chase. Biologists think play trains bodies and brains for real life while keeping everyone safe. So human games began long before anyone wrote them down: races, wrestling, throwing at targets, hide-and-seek.

### The oldest boards

- **Senet** (Egypt): boards appear in tombs from about 3100 BCE; Tutankhamun was buried with several.
- **The Royal Game of Ur** (Mesopotamia): beautiful boards found by Leonard Woolley in the Royal Cemetery of Ur, about 2600 BCE. In the 1980s the British Museum curator **Irving Finkel** worked out its rules from a cuneiform tablet written in 177 BCE.
- **Mancala** (Africa and Asia): "count-and-capture" games played with seeds in pits; India's *pallanguzhi* is one.

### Games in ancient India

Dice made of bone and the *vibhitaka* nut appear in the Rig Veda's "Gambler's Hymn". The *Mahabharata* turns on a game of dice. Game boards were scratched into the steps of temples and forts.

**Remember this:** Play is older than humans; the oldest games we can still play were invented in the first cities.

## SPT-00.02 Why does every culture have a ball game?

**Short answer:** A ball is the simplest object that moves in surprising ways: it rolls, bounces and flies. That makes it perfect for skill, teamwork and chance, so peoples who never met each other invented ball games again and again.

**Keywords:** history of ball games, Mesoamerican ballgame, cuju, origin of football, why ball games are popular

### Many ball games, many places

- **Mesoamerica**: rubber balls from about 1650 BCE; the ballgame (*ōllamaliztli*) was played on stone courts and sometimes linked to ritual sacrifice. Rubber, unknown in Europe then, made balls that bounced high.
- **China**: *cuju* ("kick ball"), played from the Han dynasty; FIFA recognises it as the earliest documented form of football.
- **Japan**: *kemari*, a courtly game of keeping a ball in the air.
- **India**: *gend-balla* and many village ball games; Manipur's *sagol kangjei* (polo on horseback) and *yubi lakpi* (rugby with a greased coconut).
- **North America**: the Haudenosaunee game that the French called *lacrosse*.

### Why a ball works

A ball turns physics into drama. It can be passed, so it creates teamwork; it can be stolen, so it creates contest; it can bounce wrongly, so it creates luck. And it is cheap: a bundle of rags, a coconut or a pig's bladder will do.

### From folk games to world sports

In 19th-century Britain, schools and clubs wrote down rules: football (1863), rugby (1871), lawn tennis (1870s). Railways, newspapers and the British Empire then carried these rules around the world.

**Remember this:** The ball is the world's favourite toy because it mixes skill, teamwork and luck.

## SPT-00.03 How did chess travel from India to the world?

**Short answer:** Chess began in India as *chaturanga* around the 6th century CE, during or just after the Gupta period. It went to Persia as *shatranj*, spread with the Arab world to Europe, and took its modern rules in Spain around 1475, when the queen became the strongest piece.

**Keywords:** origin of chess, chaturanga, shatranj, history of chess, chess in India

### Chaturanga: a model army

*Chaturanga* means "four limbs", the four divisions of an Indian army: infantry, cavalry, elephants and chariots. These became the pawn, knight, bishop and rook. Its king was the *raja*, with a counsellor beside him.

### Persia and the Arab world

By the 7th century the game was played in Sassanid Persia as *chatrang*, later *shatranj*. The Persian *Shahnameh* tells how an Indian king sent it as a riddle to the Persian court. Words came with it: "check" from the Persian *shah* (king), "checkmate" from *shah mat* (the king is helpless).

### Europe and the modern queen

Shatranj entered Europe through Muslim Spain and Sicily around 1000 CE. Around 1475, Spanish and Italian players made the queen and the bishop long-range pieces; the faster game was called "the queen's chess".

### Back home

Chess also travelled east to become Chinese *xiangqi*, Japanese *shogi* and Thai *makruk*. In 2024 India won both gold medals at the Chess Olympiad and **D. Gukesh** became the youngest undisputed world champion at 18. The game came home.

**Remember this:** Chaturanga's army of four parts became the world's most played thinking game.

## SPT-00.04 Why did the Olympics stop for 1,500 years?

**Short answer:** The ancient Olympic Games at Olympia (by tradition from 776 BCE) were a religious festival for Zeus. When the Roman Empire became Christian, pagan festivals were banned, and the Games ended around 393 CE. Pierre de Coubertin revived them in Athens in 1896.

**Keywords:** ancient Olympics, why Olympics ended, Pierre de Coubertin, first modern Olympics 1896

### The ancient Games

Every four years Greek city-states held a truce so athletes could travel safely to Olympia. Events included running, wrestling, boxing, the pentathlon, chariot racing and the brutal *pankration*. Winners got an olive wreath, and fame. Married women were not allowed to watch.

### The end

Emperor **Theodosius I** banned pagan cults in the 390s; the traditional date for the last ancient Games is 393 CE, though some scholars think they lingered into the 5th century. Earthquakes and floods later buried the site.

### The revival

The Frenchman **Pierre de Coubertin** believed sport could build character and peace between nations. He founded the **International Olympic Committee** in 1894; the first modern Games were held in **Athens in 1896**, with 241 athletes, all men. Women competed from 1900 (Paris); the British tennis player Charlotte Cooper was the first woman Olympic champion.

### India and the Olympics

Norman Pritchard won two silver medals in athletics for India in 1900. India sent its first official team in 1920 and won its first hockey gold in 1928.

**Remember this:** The Olympics were sacred, then forbidden, then reborn as a world festival.

## SPT-00.05 What makes a sport a sport?

**Short answer:** Most definitions agree that a sport is a game of skill, with physical effort, rules and fair competition, organised by a governing body. The edges are fuzzy: chess, darts and esports call themselves sports too, and the IOC recognises chess and bridge as "sports of the mind".

**Keywords:** definition of sport, what is a sport, is chess a sport, game vs sport

### Game, play and sport

- **Play** is free activity done for its own sake.
- A **game** adds rules and a goal. The philosopher **Bernard Suits** defined it as "the voluntary attempt to overcome unnecessary obstacles": golf would be easier if you could carry the ball to the hole.
- A **sport** is a game where skill is tested, usually with the body, in organised competition.

### The tests people use

1. Is there physical skill or effort?
2. Are there fixed rules and a fair way to decide the winner?
3. Is there a governing body and regular competition?
4. Does skill matter more than luck?

Football passes every test. Snakes and Ladders fails test 4. Chess fails test 1 for some people and passes it for others, because elite chess is physically exhausting. The IOC has recognised chess (FIDE, since 1999) and bridge (the World Bridge Federation) as sports.

### Why it matters

Recognition brings money, school places, Olympic dreams and government support. That is why yoga, kabaddi, kho-kho, esports and mallakhamb have all argued to be counted.

**Remember this:** A sport is skill plus rules plus fair competition; the borders are argued, not fixed.

## SPT-00.06 Why do fans feel a win in their own body?

**Short answer:** Fans identify with their team, so the brain treats its wins and losses partly as their own. Heart rate and stress hormones rise during big matches, and a win can feel like personal success. Psychologists call it "basking in reflected glory".

**Keywords:** sports fan psychology, why fans care, basking in reflected glory, fandom science

### "We won", "they lost"

In a famous study (Robert Cialdini and colleagues, 1976), American students wore more university clothing after their football team won, and said "we won", but "they lost" after a defeat. This is **BIRGing** (Basking In Reflected Glory) and its opposite, **CORFing** (Cutting Off Reflected Failure).

### The body joins in

Studies of football fans have found raised heart rates and stress hormones during penalty shoot-outs; hospital visits for heart trouble rose in Germany on the days of tense 2006 World Cup matches (Wilbert-Lampen and colleagues, 2008). Fans' brains respond to their team's success in reward areas.

### Belonging

Fandom gives people a tribe, a calendar of rituals and shared memories across generations. A grandfather and grandchild may have little in common, except the 1983 or 2011 World Cup.

### The dark side

The same feelings can turn into hatred of rivals, violence and online abuse of players. Healthy fandom keeps the joy and lets go of the hate.

**Remember this:** Fans borrow the team's glory and pain, because belonging is part of being human.

## SPT-00.07 Are video games a sport?

**Short answer:** Competitive video gaming, called **esports**, has leagues, teams, coaches, prize money and fans in the hundreds of millions, and it was a medal event at the 2022 Asian Games. Whether it is a "sport" depends on the definition; it is certainly a skill competition.

**Keywords:** are esports a sport, esports Asian Games, gaming as a career, esports in India

### What esports is

Professional players compete in games such as *League of Legends*, *Dota 2*, *Counter-Strike*, *Valorant*, *FIFA/EA Sports FC* and, in India, *BGMI* and *Free Fire*. The finals fill stadiums. The first known video-game tournament was held at Stanford University in 1972 (*Spacewar!*).

### For

Elite players need fast reactions, hand-eye skill, teamwork, strategy and training of eight hours or more a day. Esports were a demonstration event at the 2018 Asian Games and medal events in 2022 (Hangzhou). The IOC has announced plans for Olympic esports events.

### Against

The physical effort is small compared with running or wrestling; the games are owned by companies, which can change or end them; and there are worries about screen time, addiction and gambling.

### In India

India's Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 bans real-money online games while promoting esports and social games. Esports is a growing career, but a narrow one: only a few make a living.

**Remember this:** Esports is skill competition at a high level; whether it is "sport" is a definition fight.

## SPT-00.08 Recap: the story of sports and games in one page

**Short answer:** Humans play because play trains us, bonds us and delights us. Games began with races and wrestling, grew into board games in the first cities, ball games on every continent, codified sports in 19th-century Britain, the modern Olympics in 1896, and digital games today.

**Keywords:** history of sports summary, sports explained, types of games

1. **Play** is older than humans.
2. **First boards**: senet and the Royal Game of Ur (about 3000–2600 BCE).
3. **Ancient festivals**: Olympia (776 BCE), Mesoamerican ballgames, Indian dice and chaturanga.
4. **Codification** (19th century): football, rugby, tennis, badminton and many others get written rules and governing bodies.
5. **Modern Olympics** (1896), then world championships, leagues, TV and money.
6. **Women's sport and parasports** grow through the 20th and 21st centuries.
7. **Digital games and esports** (from 1972).

**Remember this:** Games have rules that don't "matter", which is exactly why they can teach us what does.
