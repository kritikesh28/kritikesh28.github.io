# PART 6 — TRADITIONAL AND FOLK GAMES

> **About this Part.** The games of villages, streets and festivals, many of them centuries old. Some became world sports (kabaddi, polo, badminton); many are fading and need recording. **See also:** CRO/KNT (traditional crafts), the Curriculum Index (Art and Culture).

## SPT-17 Traditional games of India

> **Before you start — SPT-17.** India's traditional games need almost no equipment: a stick, a few stones, a line on the ground. The government's **Khelo India** programme (2018) and the Fit India Movement (2019) have brought several into schools. Names change from region to region; the common name comes first.

### ☐ SPT-17.1 Team chase and tag games

- ☐ **Kabaddi**: two teams of seven; a raider crosses the line, tags opponents and must return in one breath while chanting "kabaddi"; ancient roots in Tamil Nadu and Punjab (forms: Sanjeevani, Gaminee, Amar, Punjabi circle style); Asian Games since 1990 (Iran won the men's gold in 2018; India regained it in 2022); **Pro Kabaddi League** (2014).
- ☐ **Kho-kho**: a chase game of nine where chasers sit in a line facing opposite ways and "kho" (tag) a teammate to run; rules first written at Pune's Deccan Gymkhana (1914); India won both the men's and women's titles at the first **Kho Kho World Cup** (New Delhi, January 2025); Ultimate Kho Kho league (2022).
- ☐ **Atya-patya** (Maharashtra), **langdi** (hopping tag), **chor-police**, **chhupan-chhupai** (hide and seek), **oonch-neech** ("high and low").

### ☐ SPT-17.2 Stick, stone and marble games

- ☐ **Gilli-danda**: a small wooden peg (*gilli*) struck with a stick (*danda*), a cousin of cricket and the "tip-cat" of Europe; called *kitti-pullu* in Tamil Nadu and *chinni-dandu* in Andhra Pradesh.
- ☐ **Pitthu / lagori / sat-tolia** (seven stones): one team knocks down a stack of stones with a ball and rebuilds it while the other team tries to hit them.
- ☐ **Kanche (marbles, goti)**, **lattoo (spinning top)**, **gutte / pallanguzhi** (five stones and seed-pit games), **stapu** (hopscotch, *kith-kith*), **kite flying** (*patangbazi*; Uttarayan in Gujarat, 14 January).

### ☐ SPT-17.3 Strength, wrestling and martial games

- ☐ **Kushti / pehlwani** in the akhara (SPT-12.1); **mallakhamb** (SPT-11.3); **kalaripayattu**, **silambam**, **thang-ta**, **gatka** (SPT-12.7); **inbuan** (Mizo wrestling); **mukna** (Manipuri wrestling); **tug of war** (*rassa-kashi*; Olympic 1900–1920).

### ☐ SPT-17.4 Animal and water festival sports

- ☐ **Vallam kali** (Kerala snake-boat races, SPT-06.7); **kambala** (Karnataka buffalo race in paddy fields); **jallikattu** (Tamil Nadu bull-taming, upheld by the Supreme Court in 2023 under the state's amended law); **bullock-cart races** (Maharashtra, Punjab); **camel races** (Pushkar and Bikaner fairs).
- ☐ **Ethics note**: animal sports raise welfare questions; courts and laws have changed several times; the website presents both the cultural case and the welfare case.

### ☐ SPT-17.5 Regional games and festivals

- ☐ **Manipur**: *sagol kangjei* (polo), *yubi lakpi* (coconut rugby), *khong kangjei* (hockey on foot).
- ☐ **Punjab**: the **Kila Raipur Rural Olympics** (near Ludhiana, since 1933), with bullock-cart races and strength events; *kabaddi* circle style.
- ☐ **Himachal Pradesh**: *thoda* (a folk archery battle from the Mahabharata).
- ☐ **Rajasthan and Gujarat**: *kabaddi*, kite flying, *chaupar* (SPT-14.3).
- ☐ **North-east**: Naga wrestling, Mizo *inbuan*, Assam's *dhopkhel* (a ball game of the Bihu season).
- ☐ **Indian board games**: chaturanga, pachisi/chaupar, Moksha Patam, aadu puli attam, pallanguzhi (Part 4).

## SPT-18 Traditional games of the world

> **Before you start — SPT-18.** Every country has its folk games; UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage lists include several. **See also:** INS (instruments of the same festivals).

- ☐ **SPT-18.1 Ball and stick games**: the **Mesoamerican ballgame** (*ulama* survives in Mexico); **cuju** (China); **kemari** (Japan); **calcio storico** (Florence, a rough 16th-century football); **hurling** and **Gaelic football** (Ireland); **pelota** (Basque), **chinlone** (Myanmar, cane ball).
- ☐ **SPT-18.2 Wrestling traditions**: **sumo** (Japan), **Kırkpınar oil wrestling** (Turkey, by tradition since 1346), **schwingen** (Switzerland), **glíma** (Iceland), **ssireum** (Korea), Senegalese *laamb*, Mongolian *bökh*.
- ☐ **SPT-18.3 Festivals of games**: **Naadam** (Mongolia: wrestling, horse racing and archery), the **Highland Games** (Scotland: caber toss, hammer), the **Nomad Games** (Central Asia), **Kila Raipur** (India).
- ☐ **SPT-18.4 Horse and animal games**: **buzkashi** (Afghanistan, Central Asia), **chovgan** (Azerbaijani polo), camel racing (Gulf), **pato** (Argentina's national game).
- ☐ **SPT-18.5 Throwing and target games**: **pétanque** and **boules** (France), **bocce** (Italy), **kubb** (Sweden), **jianzi** (Chinese shuttlecock kicking), **sepak takraw** (South-East Asia).
- ☐ **SPT-18.6 Children's games everywhere**: hopscotch, marbles, tops, cat's cradle, skipping rope, tag, hide-and-seek: the same games turn up in every continent, a sign of shared human play.

# PART 7 — PARASPORTS AND INCLUSIVE GAMES

## SPT-19 Parasports and inclusive games

> **Before you start — SPT-19.** Parasports began in rehabilitation hospitals after the Second World War. Today the **Paralympics** are the second-largest multi-sport event. **Classification** groups athletes by how their impairment affects their sport, so competition is fair.

- ☐ **SPT-19.1 Origins**: **Ludwig Guttmann**'s Stoke Mandeville Games for wounded veterans (England, 1948); the first **Paralympic Games** (Rome, 1960); the **International Paralympic Committee** (1989).
- ☐ **SPT-19.2 Paralympic sports**: para athletics, para swimming, wheelchair basketball, wheelchair rugby, wheelchair tennis, para badminton, para archery, para shooting, sitting volleyball, boccia, **goalball** (for blind athletes, with a ball that has bells), blind football, para powerlifting, para canoe, para cycling, wheelchair fencing.
- ☐ **SPT-19.3 Other movements**: the **Deaflympics** (Paris, 1924), the **Special Olympics** (for people with intellectual disabilities; founded by Eunice Kennedy Shriver, 1968), the **Invictus Games** (for wounded service members, 2014), the World Dwarf Games.
- ☐ **SPT-19.4 Blind cricket and other adapted games**: blind cricket (a ball with ball-bearings that rattle; World Cups since 1998; India won the T20 World Cups of 2012, 2017 and 2022), wheelchair cricket, deaf cricket, adaptive chess (tactile boards).
- ☐ **SPT-19.5 India's Paralympians**: **Murlikant Petkar** (India's first Paralympic gold, swimming, 1972), **Devendra Jhajharia** (javelin gold 2004 and 2016), **Avani Lekhara** (shooting gold 2020 and 2024), **Sumit Antil** (javelin gold 2020 and 2024), **Sheetal Devi** (archer without arms, Paralympic bronze 2024), Pramod Bhagat, Mariyappan Thangavelu; India won 29 medals (7 gold) at Paris 2024, its best ever.
- ☐ **SPT-19.6 Inclusive play**: unified sports (players with and without disabilities together), accessible playgrounds, adapted PE in schools, the language of respect ("athlete with a disability", not "victim").

## SPT-21 The big stories of sport

- ☐ **SPT-21.1 Codification**: why 19th-century Britain wrote rules for so many games (railways, public schools, clubs, newspapers, the Empire); India's part in badminton, snooker and polo.
- ☐ **SPT-21.2 The amateur ideal and its end**: the Olympics banned professionals for decades (Jim Thorpe was stripped of his medals in 1913, restored in 1982); professionals were fully admitted from the 1980s–90s.
- ☐ **SPT-21.3 Sport and empire**: cricket in India and the Caribbean; C. L. R. James and Ashis Nandy on cricket as both colonial and anti-colonial (SPT-28); Mohun Bagan's barefoot win over the East Yorkshire Regiment in the 1911 IFA Shield.
- ☐ **SPT-21.4 Broadcasting and money**: radio (AIR cricket commentary from the 1930s–40s), TV (Doordarshan's 1982 Asian Games in colour), satellite TV and Kerry Packer's World Series Cricket (1977), streaming rights worth billions.
- ☐ **SPT-21.5 The rise of women's sport**: from the first women at the 1900 Olympics to equal numbers of men and women at Paris 2024 (the first "gender-equal" Games by athlete quota); Billie Jean King's "Battle of the Sexes" (1973); equal prize money at the Grand Slams; women's leagues (WPL, WSL); the Indian women's cricket World Cup of 2025.
- ☐ **SPT-21.6 Boycotts and politics**: the apartheid sports boycott of South Africa (1960s–1991), the Moscow 1980 and Los Angeles 1984 boycotts, the Munich massacre (1972), the Black Power salute (Mexico City, 1968).
- ☐ **SPT-21.7 Esports and the digital age**: SPT-16.

## SPT-25 India's sports awards

> **Before you start — SPT-25.** India's National Sports Awards are given by the Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports, traditionally around 29 August, National Sports Day (Major Dhyan Chand's birthday).

- ☐ **SPT-25.1 Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna** (since 1991–92; renamed in 2021 from the Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna): India's highest sports honour. The first winner was **Viswanathan Anand**. The awards for 2024 (presented January 2025) went to D. Gukesh, Manu Bhaker, Harmanpreet Singh and Praveen Kumar. For **2025**, announced in August 2026, **no Khel Ratna was given**.
- ☐ **SPT-25.2 Arjuna Award** (since 1961): for outstanding performance; the 2025 list (announced August 2026) includes, as reported, Tejaswin Shankar, and the badminton pair Treesa Jolly and Gayatri Gopichand; the full list is on yas.gov.in.
- ☐ **SPT-25.3 Dronacharya Award** (since 1985): for coaches; the **Dhyan Chand Award for Lifetime Achievement** (2002), the **Rashtriya Khel Protsahan Puraskar** (for organisations), the **Maulana Abul Kalam Azad (MAKA) Trophy** (for universities).
- ☐ **SPT-25.4 Padma awards and the Bharat Ratna**: **Sachin Tendulkar** became the first sportsperson to receive the Bharat Ratna (2014); many athletes have received Padma awards (Milkha Singh, Mary Kom, P. V. Sindhu, Neeraj Chopra and others).
- ☐ **SPT-25.5 State awards**: Shiv Chhatrapati (Maharashtra), Eklavya (Karnataka and others), Maharana Pratap (Rajasthan) and more.
