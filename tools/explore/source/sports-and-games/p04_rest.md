# PART 12 — THE SCIENCE OF SPORT

## SPT-27 Sports science, medicine, doping and technology

> **Before you start — SPT-27.** Modern sport is a laboratory: of the body, of fairness and of technology. **See also:** TEC (sensors, cameras, AI), COK (nutrition).

- ☐ **SPT-27.1 Exercise physiology**: aerobic and anaerobic energy; VO₂ max; altitude training (Kenyan and Ethiopian runners, Iten and the Rift Valley); heat and hydration.
- ☐ **SPT-27.2 Biomechanics**: the physics of the javelin throw, the fast-bowling action, the high-jump flop, the golf swing; motion-capture and force plates.
- ☐ **SPT-27.3 Sports nutrition**: carbohydrates for endurance, protein for repair, iron and vitamin D; Indian vegetarian athletes; supplements can be contaminated, so athletes must check (SPT-27.6).
- ☐ **SPT-27.4 Sports medicine and injuries**: ACL tears (more common in women footballers), concussion protocols (cricket's concussion substitute since 2019), overuse in young athletes, return-to-play rules; physiotherapists and sports doctors.
- ☐ **SPT-27.5 Sports psychology**: goal setting, visualisation, pre-performance routines, pressure and "choking", flow (SPT-28.2), mental health (Naomi Osaka, Simone Biles, Ben Stokes spoke openly).
- ☐ **SPT-27.6 Doping and anti-doping**: the **World Anti-Doping Agency** (WADA, 1999) and the World Anti-Doping Code; India's **NADA** (2005); the Ben Johnson (1988) and Lance Armstrong (stripped of seven Tour titles, 2012) cases; state-sponsored doping in Russia (the McLaren Report, 2016); "strict liability": athletes are responsible for what is in their bodies.
- ☐ **SPT-27.7 Fairness debates**: sex testing and eligibility rules; the Dutee Chand case at the Court of Arbitration for Sport (2015); transgender participation policies set by each federation; classification in parasport (SPT-19).
- ☐ **SPT-27.8 Officiating technology**: **Hawk-Eye** ball-tracking (tennis from 2006, cricket DRS from 2008–09), **goal-line technology** (FIFA World Cup 2014), **VAR** (World Cup 2018), semi-automated offside (2022), UltraEdge and snickometer, TMO in rugby, photo finish and electronic timing.
- ☐ **SPT-27.9 Equipment technology**: carbon-plated running shoes, the full-body swimsuits banned in 2010, aluminium bats (banned in cricket after Dennis Lillee, 1979), smart balls, wearables and GPS vests; data analytics ("Moneyball").

# PART 13 — IDEAS: PLAY, PHILOSOPHY, PSYCHOLOGY AND SOCIETY

## SPT-28 Why we play, and what sport does to us

> **Before you start — SPT-28.** Philosophers, psychologists and historians have asked what play is, why competition grips us and what sport does to nations. **See also:** BOK-20 (ideas), MUS-22 (music and the mind).

### ☐ SPT-28.1 Theories of play

- ☐ **Johan Huizinga**, *Homo Ludens* (1938): play is older than culture and lies at its root; play happens in a "magic circle" with its own rules.
- ☐ **Roger Caillois**, *Man, Play and Games* (1958): four kinds of play: *agôn* (competition), *alea* (chance), *mimicry* (role-play) and *ilinx* (vertigo, such as swings and roller-coasters).
- ☐ **Bernard Suits**, *The Grasshopper* (1978): a game is "the voluntary attempt to overcome unnecessary obstacles" (SPT-00.05).
- ☐ **Indian ideas**: *lila* (divine play, the world as Krishna's play); *krida* in Sanskrit texts; the *Mahabharata*'s dice game as a lesson on chance and dharma.

### ☐ SPT-28.2 The psychology of competition

- ☐ **Flow** (Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi, 1990): total absorption when challenge matches skill.
- ☐ **Motivation**: intrinsic (joy) and extrinsic (medals, money); why young athletes quit when sport stops being fun.
- ☐ **Home advantage**, **the "hot hand"** (once called a myth, now debated with new data), **choking under pressure**.

### ☐ SPT-28.3 Sport and society

- ☐ **Fandom** (SPT-00.06): belonging, identity and rivalry.
- ☐ **Nationalism**: George Orwell called international sport "war minus the shooting" ("The Sporting Spirit", 1945); sport can also build peace (Ping-Pong Diplomacy, the 2018 joint Korean team).
- ☐ **Cricket and India**: **C. L. R. James**, *Beyond a Boundary* (1963): "What do they know of cricket who only cricket know?"; **Ashis Nandy**, *The Tao of Cricket* (1989): "Cricket is an Indian game accidentally discovered by the English"; **Ramachandra Guha**, *A Corner of a Foreign Field* (2002) on caste and the Palwankar brothers.
- ☐ **Gender**: unequal pay and coverage; the women who changed sport; the safety of women athletes; India's women in cricket, wrestling, boxing, badminton and shooting.
- ☐ **Caste, class and access**: who gets to play; Palwankar Baloo (a Dalit spin bowler of the early 1900s); grassroots academies and scholarship schemes.
- ☐ **Money and ethics**: match-fixing (the 2000 cricket scandal, the 2013 IPL spot-fixing case), betting, player power, sportswashing by states.
- ☐ **Gaming and the brain**: action video games can improve some visual attention skills (Daphne Bavelier and colleagues); heavy use can harm sleep and study; WHO's gaming disorder (SPT-16.4).

# PART 14 — THE PRACTICAL GUIDE

## SPT-29 Playing, watching and gaming well

> **Before you start — SPT-29.** For readers of every age who want to start a sport, understand the rules, watch well, run a games night or game healthily. Dates and websites carry a "checked on" date on the site.

- ☐ **SPT-29.1 How much activity**: the WHO recommends at least 150–300 minutes of moderate (or 75–150 of vigorous) activity a week for adults, plus muscle-strengthening twice a week; children and teenagers an average of 60 minutes a day.
- ☐ **SPT-29.2 Starting a sport at any age**: start with what you enjoy; join a club or group; learn basic skills from a coach; build up slowly (the "10 per cent a week" rule for runners); older adults can start walking, swimming, yoga, table tennis, badminton, pickleball.
- ☐ **SPT-29.3 Rules in plain words**: one-page explainers for cricket (lbw, DRS, the follow-on), football (offside), tennis (scoring "love", deuce), kabaddi (raid, bonus line), chess (castling, en passant).
- ☐ **SPT-29.4 Safety**: warm up and cool down; helmets and guards; heat and hydration in Indian summers; concussion: "if in doubt, sit them out"; safeguarding of children in sport.
- ☐ **SPT-29.5 Where to watch and follow**: official broadcasters and apps (dated), free-to-air rules for major events in India (Prasar Bharati), official federation sites, radio commentary; avoid pirated streams.
- ☐ **SPT-29.6 Board-game and quiz nights**: pick games by group size and time; teach in five minutes; include older and younger players; Indian classics (carrom, Ludo, chaupar, antakshari).
- ☐ **SPT-29.7 Healthy gaming**: time limits, breaks every hour, no screens before sleep, parental controls, beware loot boxes and real-money games (illegal online in India since 2025), play with friends you know.
- ☐ **SPT-29.8 Careers in sport**: athlete, coach, physio, analyst, referee, sports journalist, sports management, esports; the Sports Authority of India (SAI) schemes and Khelo India scholarships.
