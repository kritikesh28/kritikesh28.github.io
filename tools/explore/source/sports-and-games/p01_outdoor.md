# PART 2 — PHYSICAL SPORTS: OUTDOOR

> **About this Part.** Every main outdoor sport, with what it is, where it began, who governs it and where it is popular, in one or two lines. "Olympic" means it is on the programme of the Summer or Winter Games; check olympics.com for each edition. The Sport Register (SPT-13.5) gives full record fields for 30 sports.

## SPT-02 Team ball games

> **Before you start — SPT-02.** Many team ball games share one family tree: the folk football of medieval Europe split in 19th-century England into association football (soccer) and rugby, which in turn gave rise to American, Canadian and Australian codes. Hockey and its cousins are stick-and-ball games.

### ☐ SPT-02.1 Football codes

- ☐ **Association football (soccer)**: two teams of 11; rules set by the Football Association, London, 1863; governed by **FIFA** (founded Paris, 1904) and the IFAB (law-makers, 1886); the world's most popular sport. Olympic.
- ☐ **Futsal and beach soccer**: five-a-side indoor football (FIFA) and sand football.
- ☐ **Rugby union**: 15 players; the "William Webb Ellis ran with the ball at Rugby School, 1823" story is a legend; Rugby Football Union 1871; **World Rugby**. Rugby sevens is Olympic (since 2016).
- ☐ **Rugby league**: 13 players; split from union in 1895 in northern England over payments to players.
- ☐ **American football**: from rugby and soccer at US colleges (1869 onward); Walter Camp's rules; the NFL (1920) and the Super Bowl (1967).
- ☐ **Canadian football**, **Australian rules football** (Melbourne, 1859; the AFL), **Gaelic football** (Ireland; the GAA, 1884).

### ☐ SPT-02.2 Stick and ball

- ☐ **Field hockey**: 11 a side, stick and hard ball; modern rules in England (1886); **FIH** (1924); India won eight Olympic golds (1928–1956, 1964, 1980) and bronze in 2020 and 2024. Olympic.
- ☐ **Ice hockey**: see SPT-07. **Bandy**: hockey on ice with a ball (Russia, Sweden).
- ☐ **Hurling and camogie** (Ireland, ancient): fast game with a *hurley* and *sliotar*.
- ☐ **Lacrosse**: from the Haudenosaunee and other First Nations; returns to the Olympics at Los Angeles 2028 (sixes).
- ☐ **Polo**: modern polo was learned by British officers from Manipur (*sagol kangjei*) in the 1850s–60s; the **Calcutta Polo Club** (1862) is among the oldest in the world. See SPT-09.
- ☐ **Shinty** (Scotland) and **golf** (see SPT-09).

### ☐ SPT-02.3 Hand and net team games played outdoors

- ☐ **Beach volleyball** (Olympic since 1996), **Gaelic handball**, **ultimate frisbee** (flying disc, self-refereed "spirit of the game"), **flag football** (non-contact American football, Olympic from 2028), **tchoukball**.
- ☐ **Kabaddi and kho-kho**: Indian team chase games played outdoors and indoors; see SPT-17.

## SPT-03 Bat-and-ball games

> **Before you start — SPT-03.** Bat-and-ball games share a pattern: one side bats and scores runs, the other bowls or pitches and fields. Cricket, baseball and rounders may have common roots in English folk games such as stoolball.

- ☐ **SPT-03.1 Cricket**: 11 a side; England, 16th century; the Laws are kept by the **MCC** (from 1788); governed by the **ICC** (founded 1909 as the Imperial Cricket Conference; HQ Dubai). Formats: Test (first Test, Melbourne, 1877), One-Day International (1971; first World Cup 1975, won by West Indies), Twenty20 (2003; first T20 World Cup 2007, won by India). Returns to the Olympics at Los Angeles 2028 (T20). India's men won the T20 World Cup in 2007, 2024 and **2026** (final against New Zealand, 8 March 2026), the ODI World Cup in 1983 and 2011, and the Champions Trophy in 2002 (shared), 2013 and 2025; India's women won their first ODI World Cup in **2025**.
- ☐ **SPT-03.2 Leagues**: the **Indian Premier League** (2008; Royal Challengers Bengaluru won in 2025 and **2026**), Big Bash (Australia), The Hundred (England), Women's Premier League (India, 2023), Pakistan Super League, Caribbean Premier League.
- ☐ **SPT-03.3 Baseball**: 9 a side; from English bat-and-ball games, shaped in New York (the Knickerbocker rules, 1845); Major League Baseball (1903 World Series); hugely popular in the USA, Japan, Korea, Cuba, the Dominican Republic; World Baseball Classic (2006). Olympic at some Games (returns 2028).
- ☐ **SPT-03.4 Softball**: larger ball, underarm pitching; Olympic at some Games.
- ☐ **SPT-03.5 Rounders** (Britain, Ireland), **stoolball** (England), **pesäpallo** (Finnish baseball), **lapta** (Russia).
- ☐ **SPT-03.6 Gilli-danda and street cricket**: India's stick-and-peg game and the *gully cricket* of lanes and rooftops (see SPT-17).

## SPT-04 Racket sports on open courts

> **Before you start — SPT-04.** Racket sports grew from the medieval French *jeu de paume* ("game of the palm") and real tennis. Lawn tennis was patented in 1874. Badminton, table tennis and squash are mostly indoor and appear in SPT-10.

- ☐ **SPT-04.1 Tennis**: singles or doubles on grass, clay or hard courts; Major Walter Clopton Wingfield's *sphairistike* (1873–74); the first **Wimbledon** (1877, won by Spencer Gore); the four Grand Slams (Australian Open, Roland-Garros, Wimbledon, US Open); **ITF** (1913), ATP and WTA tours; Davis Cup (1900), Billie Jean King Cup. Olympic. Indian stars: Ramanathan Krishnan, Vijay Amritraj, Leander Paes (Olympic bronze 1996), Mahesh Bhupathi, Sania Mirza, Rohan Bopanna.
- ☐ **SPT-04.2 Real tennis (court tennis)**: the indoor ancestor, still played in a few historic courts.
- ☐ **SPT-04.3 Padel**: doubles in a walled court (Mexico, 1969); fast-growing in Spain, Argentina and the Gulf.
- ☐ **SPT-04.4 Pickleball**: paddle game invented on Bainbridge Island, Washington (1965); the fastest-growing sport in the USA in the 2020s.
- ☐ **SPT-04.5 Beach tennis, platform tennis, soft tennis** (Japan, Korea).

## SPT-05 Athletics (track and field, road and cross-country)

> **Before you start — SPT-05.** "Athletics" is the oldest sport family: run, jump, throw. **World Athletics** (founded 1912 as the IAAF) governs it. It is the heart of the Olympic programme.

- ☐ **SPT-05.1 Sprints and hurdles**: 100 m to 400 m; Usain Bolt's 100 m world record of 9.58 s (Berlin, 2009); Florence Griffith-Joyner's 10.49 s (1988).
- ☐ **SPT-05.2 Middle and long distance**: 800 m to 10,000 m, steeplechase; the "four-minute mile" broken by **Roger Bannister** (1954).
- ☐ **SPT-05.3 Marathon and road running**: from the legend of Pheidippides; the distance was fixed at 42.195 km in 1921 (from the 1908 London Olympics course); Eliud Kipchoge's sub-two-hour run (an unofficial event, Vienna 2019); Mumbai Marathon (2004).
- ☐ **SPT-05.4 Relays**: 4 × 100 m, 4 × 400 m, mixed relays.
- ☐ **SPT-05.5 Jumps**: high jump (the Fosbury Flop, 1968), pole vault (Armand Duplantis's repeated world records), long jump, triple jump.
- ☐ **SPT-05.6 Throws**: shot put, discus, hammer, javelin. **Neeraj Chopra** won India's first Olympic gold in athletics (javelin, Tokyo 2020), silver at Paris 2024 and the 2023 world title.
- ☐ **SPT-05.7 Combined events**: decathlon (men), heptathlon (women).
- ☐ **SPT-05.8 Race walking, cross-country, trail and ultra running, mountain running**.
- ☐ **SPT-05.9 Indian athletics legends**: Milkha Singh ("The Flying Sikh"), P. T. Usha (fourth in the 400 m hurdles, 1984, by 1/100 of a second), Anju Bobby George (world championship bronze, 2003), Hima Das, Avinash Sable.

## SPT-06 Water sports

> **Before you start — SPT-06.** Water sports include racing in, on and under water. **World Aquatics** (founded 1908 as FINA) governs swimming, diving, water polo, artistic and open-water swimming.

- ☐ **SPT-06.1 Swimming**: freestyle, backstroke, breaststroke, butterfly, medley; Michael Phelps won 23 Olympic golds; Katie Ledecky; Indian swimmers Mihir Sen (Palk Strait, 1966), Sajan Prakash, Srihari Nataraj.
- ☐ **SPT-06.2 Diving, artistic swimming, water polo, open-water marathon swimming**.
- ☐ **SPT-06.3 Rowing**: the Oxford–Cambridge Boat Race (1829); World Rowing; Olympic.
- ☐ **SPT-06.4 Canoe and kayak**: sprint and slalom; dragon boat racing (China).
- ☐ **SPT-06.5 Sailing**: dinghies, keelboats, windsurfing and kitefoiling; the America's Cup (1851), the oldest international trophy in sport.
- ☐ **SPT-06.6 Surfing**: from ancient Hawaii (*heʻe nalu*); Duke Kahanamoku; Olympic from 2020; India's surfing coast at Mulki (Karnataka) and Kovalam.
- ☐ **SPT-06.7 Traditional boat races**: Kerala's **vallam kali** snake-boat races (the Nehru Trophy on Punnamada Lake since 1952); Indonesia's *pacu jalur*.
- ☐ **SPT-06.8 Other water sports**: water skiing, wakeboarding, jet ski, underwater hockey, free diving, fishing (sport angling).

## SPT-07 Winter sports

> **Before you start — SPT-07.** Snow and ice sports grew in Scandinavia, the Alps and North America. The first Winter Olympics were at Chamonix (1924). At **Milano Cortina 2026** (6–22 February 2026) Norway topped the medal table.

- ☐ **SPT-07.1 Skiing**: alpine (downhill, slalom), Nordic (cross-country), ski jumping, freestyle, biathlon (skiing plus shooting); governed by FIS.
- ☐ **SPT-07.2 Snowboarding**: from the 1960s–70s "snurfer"; Olympic since 1998.
- ☐ **SPT-07.3 Skating**: figure skating, speed skating, short track; ISU (1892).
- ☐ **SPT-07.4 Ice hockey**: Montreal, 1875 (first organised indoor game); NHL (1917); Canada, USA, Russia, Sweden, Finland, Czechia.
- ☐ **SPT-07.5 Sliding sports**: bobsleigh, luge, skeleton; India's Shiva Keshavan competed in luge at six Winter Olympics.
- ☐ **SPT-07.6 Curling**: Scotland, 16th century; "chess on ice".
- ☐ **SPT-07.7 Winter sports in India**: Gulmarg and Auli for skiing; the Khelo India Winter Games (since 2020) in Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh; ice hockey in Ladakh.

## SPT-08 Motor sports and air sports

> **Before you start — SPT-08.** Motor sports race cars, bikes, boats and more; the **FIA** (cars, 1904) and **FIM** (motorcycles, 1904) govern most. Air sports are governed by the **FAI** (1905).

- ☐ **SPT-08.1 Formula One**: the world championship began in 1950 (first race at Silverstone; first champion Giuseppe Farina); Michael Schumacher and Lewis Hamilton each won seven titles; Lando Norris won the 2025 title. India hosted the Indian Grand Prix at the Buddh International Circuit (2011–2013); Narain Karthikeyan (2005) and Karun Chandhok are India's F1 drivers.
- ☐ **SPT-08.2 Other car racing**: endurance (24 Hours of Le Mans, 1923), IndyCar (Indianapolis 500, 1911), NASCAR, rallying (WRC; the Dakar Rally), touring cars, karting, Formula E (electric, 2014).
- ☐ **SPT-08.3 Motorcycle racing**: MotoGP (world championship since 1949), Isle of Man TT (1907), motocross, speedway, superbike; India's Harith Noah and C. S. Santosh at the Dakar.
- ☐ **SPT-08.4 Powerboat racing and air racing**.
- ☐ **SPT-08.5 Air sports**: gliding, paragliding (Bir Billing, Himachal, hosted the Paragliding World Cup in 2015), hang gliding, skydiving, ballooning, aerobatics, drone racing.

## SPT-09 Adventure, equestrian and target sports

> **Before you start — SPT-09.** Three outdoor families: adventure and extreme (pushing against nature), equestrian (horse and rider together), and target (precision).

### ☐ SPT-09.1 Adventure and extreme

- ☐ **Mountaineering**: Everest first climbed by **Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay** (29 May 1953); Bachendri Pal the first Indian woman on top (1984); Arunima Sinha the first woman amputee (2013).
- ☐ **Sport climbing** (Olympic since 2020), bouldering, trekking, orienteering.
- ☐ **Triathlon** (1974 onward; Olympic since 2000) and the Ironman (Hawaii, 1978); duathlon, adventure racing.
- ☐ **Cycling**: road (Tour de France, 1903), track, mountain bike, BMX; UCI (1900).
- ☐ **Skateboarding** (Olympic since 2020), parkour, inline skating, roller sports.
- ☐ **Extreme sports**: base jumping, wingsuit flying, big-wave surfing, ice climbing; the X Games (1995).

### ☐ SPT-09.2 Equestrian

- ☐ **Olympic equestrian**: dressage, show jumping, eventing; men and women compete together; FEI (1921).
- ☐ **Polo** (see SPT-02.2) and **horse racing** (the Derby at Epsom, 1780; the Indian Derby, Mumbai; flat racing, steeplechase).
- ☐ **Endurance riding, vaulting, tent pegging** (cavalry sport popular in India and Pakistan), **buzkashi** (Central Asia).

### ☐ SPT-09.3 Target sports

- ☐ **Archery**: World Archery (1931); Olympic; Korea's dominance; India's Deepika Kumari; **Sheetal Devi** in para archery (SPT-19).
- ☐ **Shooting**: rifle, pistol, shotgun; ISSF; **Abhinav Bindra** won India's first individual Olympic gold (10 m air rifle, Beijing 2008); **Manu Bhaker** won two bronzes at Paris 2024, the first Indian to win two medals at one Games after independence.
- ☐ **Golf**: Scotland, 15th century; St Andrews; the R&A and USGA set rules; the four men's majors (The Masters, PGA Championship, US Open, The Open); Olympic again since 2016; Aditi Ashok fourth at Tokyo 2020.
- ☐ **Bowls (lawn bowls), pétanque, bocce, croquet, disc golf**: precision games of rolling and throwing.
