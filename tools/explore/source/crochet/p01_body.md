# PART 2 — TOOLS AND MATERIALS

## CRO-02 Hooks

> **Before you start — CRO-02.** Hook sizes are best given in **millimetres**, the international standard. The US uses letters and numbers (for example US H-8 = 5 mm); old UK sizes ran backwards (UK 6 = 5 mm). Steel hooks for thread use their own small numbers. Always check the millimetre size.

- ☐ **CRO-02.1 Standard hooks**: aluminium (smooth, cheap), steel (0.5 to 2.75 mm, for thread and lace), wood and bamboo (warm, less slippery), plastic (large sizes).
- ☐ **CRO-02.2 Ergonomic hooks**: thick, soft handles to reduce hand strain.
- ☐ **CRO-02.3 Inline and tapered heads**: inline heads (Bates style) suit some people, tapered (Boye style) suit others.
- ☐ **CRO-02.4 Tunisian (afghan) hooks**: long hooks, often with a cable, to hold many loops (CRO-04.6).
- ☐ **CRO-02.5 Double-ended hooks**: for two-colour reversible Tunisian work ("crochet on the double").
- ☐ **CRO-02.6 Jumbo hooks**: 15 to 25 mm and more, for chunky and arm-style work.
- ☐ **CRO-02.7 Light-up and specialty hooks**: hooks with lights for dark yarn; hairpin forks and broomstick pins (CRO-07).

## CRO-03 Yarns, fibres and notions

- ☐ **CRO-03.1 Yarn weights (Craft Yarn Council system)**: 0 lace, 1 super fine (fingering), 2 fine (sport), 3 light (DK), 4 medium (worsted, aran), 5 bulky, 6 super bulky, 7 jumbo; the UK uses "ply" names; the label lists suggested hook size and gauge.
- ☐ **CRO-03.2 Cotton**: firm, washable, good for amigurumi, dishcloths and summer tops; mercerised cotton thread for lace.
- ☐ **CRO-03.3 Wool**: warm, springy, can felt; merino is soft; superwash wool survives machines.
- ☐ **CRO-03.4 Acrylic**: cheap, washable, many colours; does not breathe and sheds microplastics (CRO-15.3).
- ☐ **CRO-03.5 Blends and other fibres**: bamboo viscose, linen, silk, alpaca, mohair; India's own jute, banana-fibre and khadi yarns.
- ☐ **CRO-03.6 Recycled yarns**: t-shirt yarn ("tarn"), plarn (plastic bag yarn), sari-silk yarn from Indian waste silk.
- ☐ **CRO-03.7 Notions**: stitch markers, tapestry needles, scissors, tape measure, row counter, safety eyes for toys, stuffing, blocking mats and pins, buttons.

# PART 3 — STITCHES

> **About this Part.** Every stitch with one or two lines. **US and UK names differ** for the same stitch: a US "single crochet" is a UK "double crochet". Patterns state which system they use; this booklet uses US names with UK names in brackets.

## CRO-04 Foundation and basic stitches

### ☐ CRO-04.1 US and UK stitch names

| US name | UK name | Symbol | Abbreviation (US) | Height | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chain | Chain | Oval | ch | Base | Foundation and spaces |
| Slip stitch | Slip stitch | Dot | sl st | Flattest | Joining, moving along |
| Single crochet | Double crochet | Plus or cross | sc | 1 chain | Dense fabric, amigurumi |
| Half double crochet | Half treble | T | hdc | 2 chains | Quick, warm fabric |
| Double crochet | Treble | T with one bar | dc | 3 chains | Open, fast fabric |
| Treble (triple) crochet | Double treble | T with two bars | tr | 4 chains | Tall, lacy fabric |

### ☐ CRO-04.2 The basics explained

- ☐ **CRO-04.2a Slip knot and chain**: the first loop and the chain row that starts most work.
- ☐ **CRO-04.2b Slip stitch**: joins rounds and moves the yarn without adding height.
- ☐ **CRO-04.2c Single crochet**: insert, yarn over, pull up a loop, yarn over, pull through both loops.
- ☐ **CRO-04.2d Half double, double and treble**: wrap the yarn once or more before inserting the hook, then work off loops two at a time (except the half double, three at once).
- ☐ **CRO-04.2e Foundation stitches**: chainless foundations (foundation single crochet) for a stretchier edge.
- ☐ **CRO-04.2f Front loop and back loop only**: working into one loop makes ridges and ribbing.

## CRO-05 Textured, post, colourwork and lace stitches

- ☐ **CRO-05.1 Bobble**: several unfinished double crochets in one stitch, closed together; makes a raised dot.
- ☐ **CRO-05.2 Puff**: several loops pulled up in one stitch; soft, rounded.
- ☐ **CRO-05.3 Popcorn**: several complete stitches folded and closed; pops to the front.
- ☐ **CRO-05.4 Cluster**: stitches worked into several stitches and joined at the top.
- ☐ **CRO-05.5 Shell and fan**: several stitches in one place, fanning out; the base of many lace edgings.
- ☐ **CRO-05.6 V-stitch**: a double, chain, double in one stitch.
- ☐ **CRO-05.7 Bullion**: yarn wrapped many times round the hook; a coil, used in Irish lace.
- ☐ **CRO-05.8 Crocodile stitch**: overlapping scales.
- ☐ **CRO-05.9 Post stitches**: worked around the stem of a stitch from the front or back; make cables and crochet ribbing.
- ☐ **CRO-05.10 Tapestry crochet**: carrying unused colours inside stitches; famous in Colombia's Wayuu *mochila* bags and Guatemala.
- ☐ **CRO-05.11 Mosaic crochet**: patterns from stitches dropped down over earlier rows; one colour per row.
- ☐ **CRO-05.12 Intarsia**: separate yarn for each colour block; large pictures with no carried strands.
- ☐ **CRO-05.13 Lace stitches**: mesh, picot, pineapple, spider, Solomon's knot (love knot).
- ☐ **CRO-05.14 Tunisian stitches**: simple stitch, knit stitch (looks like knitting), purl stitch, full stitch, honeycomb; worked forward and back on a long hook.
- ☐ **CRO-05.15 Surface crochet**: chain stitches on top of finished fabric, like embroidery.

# PART 4 — TECHNIQUES

## CRO-06 Making and finishing

- ☐ **CRO-06.1 Rows versus rounds**: rows turn at the end; rounds spiral or join; rounds suit hats, toys and squares.
- ☐ **CRO-06.2 Magic ring (adjustable ring)**: a loop pulled tight to close the centre of a round.
- ☐ **CRO-06.3 Increasing and decreasing**: two stitches in one to grow; two stitches worked together to shrink (invisible decrease for toys).
- ☐ **CRO-06.4 Turning chains**: the extra chains at a row's start that match the stitch height.
- ☐ **CRO-06.5 Changing colours**: finishing the last stitch in the new colour.
- ☐ **CRO-06.6 Joining**: slip-stitch, single crochet, whip stitch, join-as-you-go for squares.
- ☐ **CRO-06.7 Weaving in ends**: through stitches in several directions so they do not slip out.
- ☐ **CRO-06.8 Blocking**: wetting or steaming and pinning work to shape; essential for lace (not for acrylic with heat).
- ☐ **CRO-06.9 Gauge (tension)**: stitches and rows per 10 cm; make a swatch so garments fit.
- ☐ **CRO-06.10 Reading patterns**: abbreviations, brackets and repeats, asterisks, stitch counts.
- ☐ **CRO-06.11 Reading charts**: international symbols (as in Japanese pattern books), read from the centre out for rounds.
- ☐ **CRO-06.12 Left-handed crochet**: mirror the instructions; charts are reversed.
- ☐ **CRO-06.13 Edgings and borders**: crab stitch (reverse single crochet), picot edging, shell borders.

# PART 5 — STYLES AND TRADITIONS

## CRO-07 Styles and traditions of the world and India

- ☐ **CRO-07.1 Irish crochet lace**: separately made motifs joined with mesh (CRO-00.04).
- ☐ **CRO-07.2 Filet crochet**: a grid of filled and open squares that makes pictures; easy to chart.
- ☐ **CRO-07.3 Tunisian (afghan) crochet**: a dense, woven-looking fabric on a long hook.
- ☐ **CRO-07.4 Broomstick lace**: loops pulled up onto a thick pin (once a broom handle) and worked off in groups.
- ☐ **CRO-07.5 Hairpin lace**: strips worked on a U-shaped fork and joined; Victorian favourite.
- ☐ **CRO-07.6 Amigurumi**: Japanese crocheted (or knitted) stuffed toys, worked in tight spirals; *ami* means knitted or crocheted, *nuigurumi* means stuffed doll.
- ☐ **CRO-07.7 Freeform crochet**: improvised shapes and textures; Prudence Mapstone and James Walters and Sylvia Cosh pioneered it.
- ☐ **CRO-07.8 Overlay and Bavarian crochet**: overlay builds raised patterns on a base; Bavarian is a dense, two-sided round technique.
- ☐ **CRO-07.9 Tapestry and Wayuu mochila bags**: the Wayuu women of La Guajira, Colombia and Venezuela; each design carries meaning.
- ☐ **CRO-07.10 Granny squares**: motifs worked in rounds and joined; 1970s icons, back in fashion in the 2020s.
- ☐ **CRO-07.11 Crochet art and yarn bombing**: the *Crochet Coral Reef* (Margaret and Christine Wertheim, from 2005), which links crochet and hyperbolic geometry (Daina Taimiņa's crocheted hyperbolic planes, 1997); guerrilla "yarn bombing" of public objects.
- ☐ **CRO-07.12 India: Narsapur crochet lace**: Andhra Pradesh's West Godavari region; tablecloths, bedspreads and doilies in fine cotton thread; GI tag (registered 2003 to 2004); a women's home industry, mostly exported.
- ☐ **CRO-07.13 India: Goa and Kerala**: Portuguese-era lace and crochet traditions in Goan Catholic homes; convent schools in Kerala and Goa that taught crochet; church linens and christening robes.
- ☐ **CRO-07.14 India: everyday crochet**: *crosia* (Hindi word) work in North Indian homes: doilies, TV covers, toran door hangings, purses, baby sweaters; often taught by mothers and grandmothers.
- ☐ **CRO-07.15 Other traditions**: Turkish *oya* lace edgings (needle, hook and shuttle forms), Philippine and Latin American crochet, Russian and Ukrainian crochet lace.

# PART 7 — HISTORY, PEOPLE AND COMMUNITY

## CRO-09 The history of crochet

| Year | Event | Where | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ancient | Looping crafts such as nalbinding | Many cultures | Ancestors of crochet and knitting |
| 18th century | Tambour embroidery with a hook spreads to Europe | India, Persia, Turkey, then France | A likely source of the crochet hook |
| 1812 | Elizabeth Grant's memoir mentions "shepherd's knitting" (published 1898) | Scotland | Early slip-stitch crochet |
| 1824 | First printed crochet patterns in *Penélopé* | Netherlands | Crochet in print |
| 1840s | Mlle Riego de la Branchardière's pattern books | London | Crochet lace in fashion |
| 1844 | Crochet taught in Narsapur by missionaries (by local account) | Andhra Pradesh | Start of an Indian lace industry |
| 1845 to 1852 | Irish crochet lace as famine relief | Ireland | Craft as survival |
| 1851 | Irish crochet shown at the Great Exhibition | London | International fame |
| 1900s to 1930s | Filet crochet and thread crochet magazines | Europe, USA | Doilies and home linens |
| 1960s to 1970s | Granny squares and freeform crochet | Worldwide | Craft as fashion and art |
| 2000s | Amigurumi spreads from Japan; online patterns | Worldwide | Toys and the internet revival |
| 2003 to 2004 | Narsapur crochet lace gets a GI tag | India | Protected regional craft |
| 2007 | Ravelry, a social site for knitters and crocheters, opens | USA | Global crochet community |
| 2020s | Pandemic crafting boom; crochet on TikTok and Instagram; granny-square fashion | Worldwide | Young crocheters |

- ☐ **CRO-10.1 Historic figures**: Mlle Riego de la Branchardière (pattern author, 1840s to 1880s); the Ursuline nuns of Blackrock; Queen Victoria, who crocheted scarves for soldiers in the Boer War (by tradition).

# PART 9 — THE PRACTICAL GUIDE

## CRO-13 The first 10 projects

- ☐ **CRO-13.1 The order**: 1 chain bracelet, 2 dishcloth, 3 coaster set, 4 bookmark, 5 plain scarf, 6 granny square, 7 beanie in the round, 8 tote bag, 9 a small amigurumi ball, 10 a granny-square cushion or baby blanket.
- ☐ **CRO-13.2 What each teaches**: chains, rows, counting, rounds, increasing, colour change, shaping, joining, finishing.

## CRO-14 Troubleshooting

- ☐ **CRO-14.1 Edges not straight**: count stitches; check the turning chain and last stitch.
- ☐ **CRO-14.2 Work too tight or loose**: change hook size, relax the hand, check gauge.
- ☐ **CRO-14.3 Squares curling or rippling**: too few or too many stitches per round.
- ☐ **CRO-14.4 Holes in amigurumi**: smaller hook, invisible decrease, tighter tension.
- ☐ **CRO-14.5 Hand pain**: ergonomic hook, breaks, stretches; see a doctor for persistent pain.

## CRO-15 Care and selling

- ☐ **CRO-15.1 Care**: follow the yarn label; hand-wash wool in cool water; dry flat; never hang heavy pieces.
- ☐ **CRO-15.2 Storage**: clean, dry, with neem leaves or cedar against moths.
- ☐ **CRO-15.3 Selling handmade ethically**: price for time and materials; credit and respect pattern licences (many designers allow selling finished items, some do not); honest fibre labels; safety standards for children's toys (secure eyes, no small parts for under-3s).
