# PART 8 — HISTORY

> **About this Part.** The story of food in eight chapters, each a website page. The timeline (COK-18) gives the dates. **See also:** GRD (the history of farming).

## COK-20 From the first fire to global fusion

- ☐ **COK-20.1 Fire and the first cooks**: the cooking hypothesis (COK-00.01); earth ovens, roasting pits and stone boiling.
- ☐ **COK-20.2 Farming and the first cuisines**: grains, bread, beer and dairy; the Indus civilisation's crops, grinding stones and the Farmana spice traces.
- ☐ **COK-20.3 Food in ancient India**: Vedic offerings of barley cakes (*apupa*), ghee and soma; Buddhist and Jain rules on food; Ayurveda's *Charaka Samhita* and *Sushruta Samhita* on diet; Tamil Sangam poems describing feasts; K. T. Achaya's work as the guide.
- ☐ **COK-20.4 Medieval royal kitchens**: the *Manasollasa* (1130) with its recipes for rice, meats, sweets and drinks; the *Supa Shastra* of Mangarasa III (1508, Jain vegetarian cookery); the Mughal kitchens in the *Ain-i-Akbari* and the *Nuskha-e-Shahjahani*; the *Nimatnama* of the Malwa sultans (about 1500), with illustrated recipes.
- ☐ **COK-20.5 The spice routes**: Arab, Indian, Chinese and Roman traders; Muziris and Calicut; the Portuguese, Dutch and British East India Companies; nutmeg and the Banda Islands massacre (1621), a reminder of spice's cruel side.
- ☐ **COK-20.6 The Columbian Exchange and colonial kitchens**: new crops in India (COK-00.03); British tea plantations, Anglo-Indian cooking, the "curry house"; indentured Indian workers who took roti and curry to the Caribbean, Fiji, Mauritius and South Africa.
- ☐ **COK-20.7 Restaurants, industry and fast food**: Paris restaurants after 1765; Escoffier's brigade system; canning, refrigeration and supermarkets; McDonald's and global fast food; India's Udupi restaurants, dhabas, Irani cafés and the Delhi Punjabi restaurant after 1947.
- ☐ **COK-20.8 Global fusion and the digital kitchen**: Indian-Chinese, Nikkei, Tex-Mex; food delivery apps (Zomato 2008, Swiggy 2014); cooking shows and YouTube; the millet revival; climate-aware eating.

# PART 10 — IDEAS: PHILOSOPHY, PSYCHOLOGY AND SOCIETY

> **About this Part.** Food as identity, faith, memory, ethics and politics. Ideas are presented fairly, with their sources.

## COK-22 Food, identity and the sacred

- ☐ **COK-22.1 Food and identity**: "Tell me what you eat, and I will tell you what you are" (Jean Anthelme Brillat-Savarin, *The Physiology of Taste*, 1825); food marks region, caste, class and religion in India, sometimes painfully; Arjun Appadurai's essay "How to Make a National Cuisine" (1988).
- ☐ **COK-22.2 Satvik, rajasik and tamasik**: the *Bhagavad Gita* (chapter 17) classes food by its effect on the mind: pure and calming, stimulating, or dulling.
- ☐ **COK-22.3 Sacred food and fasting**: prasad and langar; Navratri, Ekadashi and Karva Chauth fasts with their own foods (sabudana, kuttu, singhara); Ramzan roza and iftar; Christian Lent; Jain *paryushan*; Yom Kippur.
- ☐ **COK-22.4 Food rules across faiths**: halal, kosher, Jain, Hindu vegetarian traditions, Buddhist monastic food, Rastafarian *ital*.
- ☐ **COK-22.5 Eating together**: the shared thali, the Sikh langar's equality, Mediterranean long tables; research links family meals with better diet in children.

## COK-23 Psychology, ethics and sustainability

- ☐ **COK-23.1 Comfort food and memory**: COK-00.06; why we crave childhood foods when stressed or homesick.
- ☐ **COK-23.2 Taste preferences**: babies prefer sweet and dislike bitter; liking for chilli, coffee and bitter greens is learned; "supertasters" (Linda Bartoshuk) find bitter tastes stronger.
- ☐ **COK-23.3 Food ethics**: vegetarianism and animal welfare (from Ashoka's edicts to Peter Singer), fair wages for farm and kitchen workers, cultural appropriation versus exchange.
- ☐ **COK-23.4 Sustainability**: food systems produce roughly a third of human greenhouse emissions (Crippa and colleagues, *Nature Food*, 2021); local and seasonal eating, plant-rich diets (the EAT-Lancet report, 2019), millets and water.
- ☐ **COK-23.5 Food waste**: the UNEP Food Waste Index (2024) estimates about 1.05 billion tonnes of food wasted in 2022; Indian traditions of using everything (peel chutneys, stale-roti dishes).
- ☐ **COK-23.6 Hunger and the right to food**: India's National Food Security Act (2013), mid-day meals (PM POSHAN), Annapurna traditions; the FAO's *State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World* reports.

# PART 11 — HEALTH AND SAFETY

> **About this Part.** Basic facts only. **Always consult a doctor or registered dietitian for medical diets, allergies, pregnancy, diabetes, kidney or heart disease.** Nutrition guidance follows ICMR–NIN's *Dietary Guidelines for Indians* (2024), the WHO and FSSAI.

## COK-24 Nutrition, safety and allergies

- ☐ **COK-24.1 Nutrition basics**: carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, fibre and water; the "My Plate for the Day" of ICMR–NIN suggests half the plate as vegetables and fruit.
- ☐ **COK-24.2 Salt, sugar and oil**: the WHO recommends less than 5 g of salt a day for adults and keeping free sugars under 10 per cent of energy; FSSAI's "Eat Right India" campaign targets less salt, sugar and trans fat.
- ☐ **COK-24.3 Food safety at home**: the WHO's five keys: keep clean, separate raw and cooked, cook thoroughly, keep food at safe temperatures (the danger zone is about 5 to 60 °C), use safe water and raw materials.
- ☐ **COK-24.4 Allergies and intolerances**: common allergens include milk, eggs, peanuts, tree nuts, soy, wheat, fish, shellfish and sesame; lactose intolerance and coeliac disease (gluten) are different conditions; read labels; FSSAI requires allergen labelling.
- ☐ **COK-24.5 Adulteration**: FSSAI's simple home tests (for example, water tests for chalk in sugar or starch in milk); report through the Food Safety Connect app.
- ☐ **COK-24.6 Special diets**: diabetic, low-salt, gluten-free, vegan, Jain and fasting diets; plan them with a professional.
