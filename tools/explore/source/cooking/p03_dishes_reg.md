# PART 6 — DISH FAMILIES

> **About this Part.** What food becomes. Each family lists dishes from India and the world, each with one line on what it is and where it began. Origins of popular dishes are often legends; we mark them as such.

## COK-13 Breads, rice and grain dishes

- ☐ **COK-13.1 Indian flatbreads**: roti and chapati (whole wheat, tawa), phulka (puffed on flame), paratha (layered, stuffed), puri and bhatura (fried), naan and kulcha (tandoor, leavened), bhakri and thalipeeth (Maharashtra), rotla (Gujarat), rumali roti, sheermal and baqarkhani (Mughlai).
- ☐ **COK-13.2 Batter breads and pancakes**: dosa, uttapam, appam, pesarattu, neer dosa, chilla (besan), malpua; crêpes, blini, Ethiopian injera.
- ☐ **COK-13.3 World breads**: baguette, sourdough, ciabatta, bagel, pita, lavash, tortilla, Chinese bao, pav (from Portuguese *pão*).
- ☐ **COK-13.4 Rice dishes**: biryani (many regional styles: Hyderabadi, Lucknowi, Kolkata with potato, Malabar, Ambur, Dindigul), pulao, khichdi (the ancestor of the Anglo-Indian kedgeree), pongal, curd rice, bisi bele bath, lemon rice; world rice dishes: paella, risotto, jollof, nasi goreng, Iranian polo, sushi.
- ☐ **COK-13.5 Porridges and grain dishes**: upma, poha, dalia, ragi mudde, sattu, haleem; oatmeal, congee, polenta, couscous, grits.
- ☐ **COK-13.6 Noodles and pasta**: Chinese noodles, ramen, udon, pho, pad thai, Italian pasta shapes; Indian sevaiyan, idiyappam (string hoppers), hakka noodles, Maggi (in India since 1983).

## COK-14 Curries, dals, soups and vegetables

> **Before you start — COK-14.** "Curry" is a European word, probably from Tamil *kari* (a spiced sauce or relish), used for any spiced dish with gravy. Indians name dishes by ingredient and method instead: *kadhai paneer*, *dum aloo*, *macher jhol*. Thai, Japanese, Malaysian and Caribbean curries are their own traditions.

- ☐ **COK-14.1 Dals**: dal tadka, dal makhani, sambar, rasam, varan, Gujarati sweet-sour dal, cholar dal (Bengal), dalma (Odisha), panchmel dal (Rajasthan).
- ☐ **COK-14.2 Gravies and curries, vegetarian**: shahi paneer, palak paneer, chole, rajma, kadhi, malai kofta, avial (Kerala), undhiyu, sarson ka saag, gatte ki sabzi.
- ☐ **COK-14.3 Gravies and curries, non-vegetarian**: rogan josh, laal maas, korma, butter chicken, chicken chettinad, Goan fish curry, macher jhol, vindaloo, nihari, Kerala stew, prawn malai curry.
- ☐ **COK-14.4 Dry vegetable dishes (sukhi sabzi)**: aloo gobi, bhindi masala, baingan bharta, thoran and poriyal (South), ker sangri.
- ☐ **COK-14.5 World curries and stews**: Thai green and red curry, Japanese kare (from British naval curry), Malaysian rendang, Caribbean curry goat, Ethiopian wat, Irish stew, French ratatouille and bouillabaisse, goulash.
- ☐ **COK-14.6 Soups**: rasam and shorba, mulligatawny (from Tamil *milagu tanni*, "pepper water"), thukpa (Himalaya), tom yum, miso soup, minestrone, French onion, borscht, gazpacho (served cold).

## COK-15 Snacks, street food and salads

- ☐ **COK-15.1 Chaat**: pani puri (golgappa, puchka), bhel puri, sev puri, aloo tikki, papdi chaat, dahi bhalla, raj kachori; a mix of sweet, sour, spicy, crunchy and soft.
- ☐ **COK-15.2 Fried snacks**: samosa (from Central Asian *sambusa*, recorded in India by the 13th century), kachori, pakora and bhajia, vada (medu, batata), mirchi vada, Bengal's telebhaja.
- ☐ **COK-15.3 Steamed and baked snacks**: dhokla, khandvi, idli, momo, patrode, Mumbai's vada pav and pav bhaji (invented for mill workers in the 1850s to 1860s, by popular account).
- ☐ **COK-15.4 Rolls and wraps**: Kolkata kathi roll (Nizam's, about 1932, by popular account), frankie (Mumbai), shawarma, burrito, spring roll.
- ☐ **COK-15.5 Namkeen and dry snacks**: Bikaneri bhujia (GI, 2010), Ratlami sev, chakli, murukku, mathri, chivda, khakhra.
- ☐ **COK-15.6 World street food**: tacos, falafel, banh mi, hot dogs, fish and chips, satay, dumplings; Singapore's hawker culture (UNESCO, 2020).
- ☐ **COK-15.7 Salads and raw dishes**: kachumber, koshimbir, Kerala's raw-mango salads; Greek salad, tabbouleh, som tam, ceviche, poke.
- ☐ **COK-15.8 Accompaniments**: chutneys, raita, pickles (achaar), papad, salan; world dips and sauces (salsa, hummus, guacamole, chimichurri, mayonnaise, ketchup, whose name may come from a Hokkien word for fish sauce).

## COK-16 Sweets, desserts and drinks

### ☐ COK-16.1 Indian sweets (mithai)

- ☐ **COK-16.1a Milk sweets**: rasgulla and sandesh (chhena, Bengal and Odisha), kalakand, peda (Mathura, Dharwad GI), barfi, rabri, rasmalai, kulfi, basundi.
- ☐ **COK-16.1b Flour and gram sweets**: laddu (besan, boondi, motichoor, Tirupati), Mysore pak, soan papdi, ghevar (Rajasthan), balushahi, gujiya, modak.
- ☐ **COK-16.1c Fried and syrup sweets**: jalebi (from West Asian *zalabiya*), imarti, gulab jamun (with a Persian name, "rose water drop"), malpua, chhena poda.
- ☐ **COK-16.1d Puddings and halwas**: kheer and payasam (the oldest recorded Indian dessert), gajar halwa, moong dal halwa, sheer khurma, phirni, shrikhand (Maharashtra and Gujarat), double ka meetha, bebinca (Goa).
- ☐ **COK-16.1e Jaggery and festival sweets**: til laddu and chikki (Makar Sankranti), pitha (Bihu and Poush Sankranti), ariselu, thekua (Chhath), nolen gur sandesh.

### ☐ COK-16.2 World desserts

- ☐ **COK-16.2a European**: cakes, tarts, croissant (Viennese-French), tiramisu, crème brûlée, gelato, macarons, Christmas pudding.
- ☐ **COK-16.2b West Asian**: baklava, kunafa, Turkish delight, halva.
- ☐ **COK-16.2c East Asian**: mochi, mooncake, bingsu, mango sticky rice.
- ☐ **COK-16.2d The Americas**: brigadeiro, tres leches, churros, brownies, pumpkin pie.
- ☐ **COK-16.2e Chocolate**: cacao drunk in Mesoamerica for some 3,000 years or more; solid eating chocolate is a 19th-century European invention.

### ☐ COK-16.3 Drinks

- ☐ **COK-16.3a Tea**: Camellia sinensis from China; Assam's native variety found by the British in the 1820s; Darjeeling (India's first GI, 2004), Nilgiri; masala chai spread widely after the Tea Board's promotions of the 20th century; Kashmiri noon chai and kahwa; world teas: matcha, oolong, British tea, Moroccan mint tea.
- ☐ **COK-16.3b Coffee**: from Ethiopia via Yemen; legend says Baba Budan smuggled seven beans to Chikmagalur, Karnataka, in the 17th century; South Indian filter coffee; espresso, Turkish coffee, the world café culture.
- ☐ **COK-16.3c Dairy and yoghurt drinks**: lassi, chaas (buttermilk), badam milk, thandai (Holi), haldi doodh; ayran, kefir.
- ☐ **COK-16.3d Sherbets and coolers**: aam panna, kokum sharbat, jaljeera, nimbu pani, rose and khus sherbets, sattu drink, sugarcane juice, nannari (sarsaparilla), Goli soda; the word "sherbet" comes from Arabic *sharbah* (drink).
- ☐ **COK-16.3e Fermented and traditional drinks**: kanji, toddy, rice beers of the North-East and tribal India, feni (Goa, GI); wine, beer, sake and spirits of the world. *Note:* alcohol laws and age limits vary by state and country.
