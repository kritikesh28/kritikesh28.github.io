## KNT-00 The Gateway: How do two sticks and a thread turn into a warm sweater?

In millions of Indian homes, winter used to begin with the click of needles: mothers and grandmothers knitting sweaters, caps and socks from balls of wool, often while talking, watching TV or waiting for the pressure cooker to whistle. Knitting turns a single strand into stretchy, warm fabric using only two stitches, knit and purl. From those two moves come Aran cables, Shetland lace, Norwegian star patterns and the T-shirt you are wearing, which was knitted by a machine.

## KNT-00.01 What is knitting?

**Short answer:** Knitting makes fabric by pulling new loops through a row of live loops held on needles. Each row hangs from the one below. Because the loops can stretch and slide, knitted fabric is elastic, which is why socks, sweaters, T-shirts and leggings are knitted rather than woven.

**Keywords:** what is knitting, knit and purl, knitting vs weaving, knitting vs crochet, how knitting works

### Two stitches, endless fabric

- **Knit stitch**: a smooth V on the front.
- **Purl stitch**: the back of a knit stitch, a little bump.
- **Stockinette (stocking stitch)**: knit one row, purl the next; smooth on one side.
- **Garter stitch**: knit every row; ridged on both sides.

### Its relatives

- **Weaving**: two sets of threads crossing at right angles; firm, not stretchy.
- **Crochet** (CRO): one live loop on a hook.
- **Nalbinding**: an older looping craft with a needle and short lengths; often mistaken for knitting in museums.
- **Machine, loom, arm and finger knitting**: the same loops, made with other tools.

**Remember this:** Knitting is rows of loops hanging from loops: two stitches, endless fabric.

**Next:** If knitting is so useful, who knitted first?

## KNT-00.02 Who knitted the first sock?

**Short answer:** The oldest true knitted objects known are fine cotton socks from Egypt, dated to roughly the 11th century CE, knitted in blue and white patterns, some with Arabic inscriptions. Older "Egyptian socks" (3rd to 5th centuries) with a separate big toe were made by nalbinding, not knitting.

**Keywords:** first knitted sock, history of knitting, Egyptian socks, nalbinding, oldest knitting

### From the Islamic world to Europe

Knitting likely spread from the Islamic world into Spain. Cushions knitted in silk were found in a royal tomb at the monastery of Las Huelgas, Burgos (13th century). Paintings of the 14th century, the "Knitting Madonnas", show the Virgin Mary knitting on four needles.

### Guilds and stockings

Knitting guilds formed in European cities from the 15th and 16th centuries. Knitted silk stockings became luxury items; England's Queen Elizabeth I loved them.

### Why sources are scarce

Wool and cotton rot; most early knitting has disappeared. Historians such as Richard Rutt (*A History of Hand Knitting*, 1987) rely on rare survivals and pictures.

**Remember this:** Knitting is about a thousand years old in the records, younger than weaving and nalbinding.

**Next:** Some of the most famous knitting was made for the sea. Why are fishermen's sweaters patterned?

## KNT-00.03 Why are fishermen's sweaters patterned?

**Short answer:** Fishermen's sweaters, the British *gansey* and the Irish *Aran*, were knitted tightly from oiled wool to keep out wind and spray. The patterns (cables, ladders, diamonds) add warmth and strength and show the knitter's skill and local style. The popular story that each family had its own pattern so drowned sailors could be identified is largely a romantic myth.

**Keywords:** Aran sweater history, gansey, fisherman sweater, Aran myth, cable knitting

### Ganseys

Knitted in the round in dark, tightly spun wool, often by fishermen's wives and by "herring girls" who followed the fishing fleets along Britain's coasts. Patterns were local and passed by example.

### Aran

The heavy, cream, richly cabled Aran sweater from Ireland's Aran Islands became famous in the 20th century, helped by exports, the Clancy Brothers (who wore them on stage in the 1960s) and marketing. Researchers such as Alice Starmore have traced how recent many "ancient" Aran patterns are.

### The myth

A version of the identification story appears in J. M. Synge's play *Riders to the Sea* (1904), where a drowned man is identified by his knitted stockings. It was later attached to Aran patterns, but there is little evidence for "family patterns".

**Remember this:** The patterns are about warmth and pride; the "drowned sailor" tale is mostly legend.

**Next:** Knitting went from hands to machines very early. How?

## KNT-00.04 How did knitting become a machine?

**Short answer:** In 1589 William Lee, a clergyman in Calverton, Nottinghamshire, invented the **stocking frame**, a machine with a row of hooked needles that knitted a whole row at once. Queen Elizabeth I refused him a patent, reportedly worried it would put hand-knitters out of work. The frame later spread and became a pillar of the textile industry.

**Keywords:** William Lee stocking frame, history of machine knitting, industrial revolution knitting, Luddites, knitting machine

### The stocking frame and after

- **1589**: Lee's frame; he moved to France to seek support.
- **1758**: Jedediah Strutt's attachment for ribbed stockings.
- **1811 to 1816**: the **Luddites**, many of them frame-work knitters around Nottingham, broke machines in protest against low wages and cheap work (TEC-21.1).
- **1847**: Matthew Townsend's latch needle, still used today.
- **20th century**: circular knitting machines produce T-shirts, socks and sportswear; home knitting machines (Brother, Passap) became popular.
- **Today**: whole-garment machines (Shima Seiki) knit a complete sweater without seams; India's Tiruppur in Tamil Nadu is a major centre of knitted garments.

**Remember this:** Knitting was one of the first crafts to become a machine, and its workers were among the first to protest automation.

**Next:** Knitting patterns look like instructions for a computer. Is knitting a code?

## KNT-00.05 Is knitting a kind of computer code?

**Short answer:** In a way, yes. A knitting pattern is a program: two basic operations (knit and purl, like 1 and 0), loops (repeats), conditions ("if the row is even") and variables (stitch counts). Knitters "execute" the code with their hands. During the World Wars, there were even stories of spies hiding messages in knitting.

**Keywords:** knitting and coding, knitting binary, knitting algorithm, knitting spies, mathematics of knitting

### Patterns as programs

- **Binary**: knit and purl can encode letters, like 0 and 1.
- **Loops**: "repeat from * to the end".
- **Charts**: grids like pixel images or matrices.
- **Errors**: a missed stitch is a bug; "frogging" (ripping out) is debugging.

### History and research

- **Jacquard's punched cards** (1804) for looms inspired Babbage and Lovelace's computing ideas (TEC-12.2).
- **Wartime stories**: knitters in occupied Belgium are said to have watched trains and recorded them in stitches; Britain's censors reportedly banned posting knitting patterns abroad in case they hid codes.
- **Mathematics**: knots, topology and hyperbolic surfaces are studied with knitting; MIT and other researchers have built software that writes knitting-machine programs.

**Remember this:** Every knitter is a programmer with yarn.
