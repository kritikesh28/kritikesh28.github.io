# PART 2 — FAMILIES, TOOLS AND MATERIALS

## EMB-02 Families of embroidery

- ☐ **EMB-02.1 Surface embroidery**: free stitches over a drawn design; crewel (wool), silk shading, kantha, chikankari.
- ☐ **EMB-02.2 Counted-thread embroidery**: cross stitch, kasuti, blackwork, Hardanger, needlepoint (canvas work), pattern darning (phulkari).
- ☐ **EMB-02.3 Whitework**: chikankari, cutwork, drawn-thread and pulled-thread work, broderie anglaise, Ayrshire and Dresden work.
- ☐ **EMB-02.4 Goldwork and metal thread**: zardozi, gota patti, mukaish (*badla*, tiny flattened wire dots), *tilla*, European church goldwork.
- ☐ **EMB-02.5 Appliqué and patchwork**: Pipli, khatwa, Rabari patchwork; reverse appliqué (the *mola* of Panama's Guna people).
- ☐ **EMB-02.6 Beadwork, sequins and mirror work**: shisha (*abhla*) mirrors, aari beadwork, Zulu and Native American beadwork.
- ☐ **EMB-02.7 Quilting stitches**: kantha, sujani, sashiko, ralli.
- ☐ **EMB-02.8 Machine and digital embroidery**: free-motion, Schiffli, multi-head computerised (EMB-00.05).

## EMB-03 Tools and materials

- ☐ **EMB-03.1 Needles**: crewel or embroidery (sharp, long eye), tapestry (blunt, for counted work), chenille, beading, milliner's (for bullion knots); the *aari* hook.
- ☐ **EMB-03.2 Hoops and frames**: round hoops, slate and scroll frames; the Indian *adda* or *karchob*, a large wooden frame at which several zardozi workers sit together.
- ☐ **EMB-03.3 Threads**: stranded cotton (six strands; Anchor and DMC are the common brands), pearl cotton, silk floss (*pat*), crewel wool, metallic *zari* (once real silver and gold; now mostly metallised polyester), rayon machine thread.
- ☐ **EMB-03.4 Fabrics**: even-weave linen and aida (for counting), muslin (*mulmul*), cambric, khaddar, silk, velvet, organza, matty cloth (used in Indian schools for cross stitch).
- ☐ **EMB-03.5 Transferring designs**: tracing with carbon or light, pricking and pouncing with powder, block printing (chikankari), water-soluble pens and stabilisers, iron-on transfers.
- ☐ **EMB-03.6 Extras**: thimble, embroidery scissors, mirrors, beads, sequins (*sitara*), *gota* ribbon, stabiliser.

# PART 3 — STITCHES

## EMB-04 The stitch families

> **Before you start — EMB-04.** Mary Thomas's *Dictionary of Embroidery Stitches* (1934) sorted hundreds of stitches into families. Almost every tradition on Earth uses the same few moves under different names; Indian names are given where they are common.

### ☐ EMB-04.1 Flat and line stitches

- ☐ **EMB-04.1a Running stitch**: in and out in a line; the base of kantha, sujani and sashiko; *tepchi* in chikankari; *kachcha tanka* in tailoring.
- ☐ **EMB-04.1b Back stitch**: each stitch goes back to meet the last; a solid line; *bakhiya* in Hindi (in chikankari, *bakhiya* means shadow work done with a double back stitch).
- ☐ **EMB-04.1c Stem stitch**: overlapping slanted stitches; a rope-like line for stems; *dandi tanka*.
- ☐ **EMB-04.1d Split stitch**: each stitch pierces the last; fine outlines in medieval English work (*opus anglicanum*).
- ☐ **EMB-04.1e Satin stitch**: straight stitches side by side to fill a shape; *bharwan* or *bharai*; Kashmiri and Chinese silk work.
- ☐ **EMB-04.1f Long-and-short stitch**: staggered satin stitches for shading ("needle painting").
- ☐ **EMB-04.1g Darning stitch**: rows of running stitch counted over threads; phulkari, Kasuti's *negi*.
- ☐ **EMB-04.1h Couching**: a thick or metal thread laid on the surface and tied down with small stitches; zardozi, Bayeux work.

### ☐ EMB-04.2 Looped, chained and knotted stitches

- ☐ **EMB-04.2a Chain stitch**: a line of loops; *zanjeer*; the main stitch of aari, Kashmiri crewel and Kutch Mochi work.
- ☐ **EMB-04.2b Lazy daisy (detached chain)**: single loops as petals.
- ☐ **EMB-04.2c Blanket and buttonhole stitch**: edge stitches; hold mirrors and appliqué; *kaj tanka*.
- ☐ **EMB-04.2d Feather stitch**: open loops to left and right; a branching line.
- ☐ **EMB-04.2e Fly stitch**: a V or Y shape.
- ☐ **EMB-04.2f French knot**: thread wrapped round the needle to make a dot; *phanda* is chikankari's cousin.
- ☐ **EMB-04.2g Bullion knot**: a long coil; roses; *murri* is related.
- ☐ **EMB-04.2h Shisha stitch**: a frame of stitches that holds a mirror without glue.

### ☐ EMB-04.3 Crossed and counted stitches

- ☐ **EMB-04.3a Cross stitch**: X-shaped stitches counted on even-weave cloth; *dosuti*; used worldwide.
- ☐ **EMB-04.3b Herringbone stitch**: crossed diagonal stitches; done from the back it gives shadow work; Kutch interlacing is built on it.
- ☐ **EMB-04.3c Holbein (double running) stitch**: looks the same on both sides; Kasuti's *gavanti* and European blackwork.
- ☐ **EMB-04.3d Tent stitch**: the small slanted stitch of needlepoint.
- ☐ **EMB-04.3e Interlacing stitch**: the *Sindhi taropa* or Kutchi *bavaliyo*, a woven lattice.
- ☐ **EMB-04.3f Pulled and drawn work**: tightening or removing threads to make holes; chikankari *jaali*, Hardanger.

# PART 4 — THE INDIAN EMBROIDERY MAP

> **About this Part.** India in depth, region by region. GI years are from the GI Registry and may be checked at ipindia.gov.in. Each style gets its place, stitches, materials, motifs and makers. Style cards are in EMB-09.

## EMB-05 North India

- ☐ **EMB-05.1 Chikankari (Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh)**: white-on-white shadow work, knots and jaali on muslin and now georgette (EMB-00.03); GI 2008.
- ☐ **EMB-05.2 Zardozi (Lucknow, Bareilly, Delhi, Agra, Hyderabad, Bhopal)**: metal-thread embroidery with *zari*, *dabka* (coiled wire), *salma*, *sitara* and stones on velvet and silk, worked on the *adda*; Persian *zar* (gold) and *dozi* (work); patronised by the Mughals; Lucknow Zardozi GI 2013.
- ☐ **EMB-05.3 Mukaish or badla (Lucknow)**: tiny dots and patterns of flattened metal wire pressed through the cloth; often added to chikan.
- ☐ **EMB-05.4 Phulkari and bagh (Punjab, Haryana)**: EMB-00.04; GI 2011.
- ☐ **EMB-05.5 Kashida, sozni and aari (Kashmir)**: *sozni* is fine needlework on pashmina shawls, often identical on both sides (*dorukha*); *aari* or crewel is hooked chain stitch on wool and cotton for furnishings; *tilla* is gold-thread work on pherans; motifs of chinar leaf, paisley (*buta*), iris and birds; Kashmir Sozani Craft GI 2008.
- ☐ **EMB-05.6 Chamba rumal (Himachal Pradesh)**: square cloths embroidered in double satin stitch (*dorukha*), showing scenes from the Krishna legends and the Ramayana in the style of Pahari miniature painting; GI 2007.
- ☐ **EMB-05.7 Gota patti (Rajasthan: Jaipur, Ajmer, Khandela)**: gold and silver ribbon (*gota*) cut into petals and leaves and stitched down; Rajasthani bridal wear.
- ☐ **EMB-05.8 Danka and karchobi (Udaipur and Rajasthan)**: small metal squares couched with zari; raised zardozi.
- ☐ **EMB-05.9 Rampur and Agra work**: fine appliqué (Rampur's *khatwa*-style patti ka kaam of Aligarh, known as *phool patti*) and zardozi of Agra.

## EMB-06 West India

- ☐ **EMB-06.1 Kutch embroidery (Gujarat)**: many community styles with mirrors and bright silk or cotton; Kutch Embroidery GI 2013.
- ☐ **EMB-06.2 Rabari**: bold mirrors, chain stitch outlines and back-stitch (*bakhiya*) details by the pastoral Rabari community; black wool and cotton.
- ☐ **EMB-06.3 Ahir**: flowing floral and bird motifs in chain stitch with many small mirrors.
- ☐ **EMB-06.4 Banni, Mutwa, Jat and Sodha (Suf, Khaarek, Pakko)**: the Maldhari and Sodha communities of northern Kutch; *suf* is counted triangles worked from the back; *khaarek* is geometric bars; *pakko* is dense and raised; Mutwa work uses the tiniest mirrors.
- ☐ **EMB-06.5 Mochi bharat (aari)**: silk chain stitch with a cobbler's hooked awl, once made for Kutch royalty.
- ☐ **EMB-06.6 Kathiawar and Saurashtra**: Kathi and Kanbi embroideries, *torans*, *chaklas* (wall squares) and bead work.
- ☐ **EMB-06.7 Parsi gara (Mumbai, Gujarat)**: Chinese-origin silk embroidery of birds and flowers on saris, brought through the China trade of the 19th century.
- ☐ **EMB-06.8 Banjara or Lambani (Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, Andhra)**: mirrors, cowrie shells, coins and bright patchwork with strong geometric stitches; Sandur Lambani embroidery (Karnataka) GI 2010.

## EMB-07 South, East and North-East India

- ☐ **EMB-07.1 Kasuti (Karnataka: Dharwad, Hubballi)**: counted-thread work on Ilkal saris with four stitches: *gavanti* (double running), *murgi* (zigzag), *negi* (darning) and *menthi* (cross stitch); motifs of temple towers, chariots, lamps and elephants; GI 2006.
- ☐ **EMB-07.2 Toda embroidery (Nilgiris, Tamil Nadu)**: *pukhoor*, red and black wool darning on white cotton shawls (*poothkuli*) by Toda women; looks woven; GI 2013.
- ☐ **EMB-07.3 Kantha (West Bengal and Bangladesh)**: layers of old saris and dhotis quilted with running stitch, with borders and figures; *nakshi kantha* carries stories; Nakshi Kantha GI 2008.
- ☐ **EMB-07.4 Sujani (Bihar)**: quilts of old cloth with running-stitch backgrounds and chain-stitch outlines; today's sujani shows social themes; Sujini Embroidery Work of Bihar GI 2006.
- ☐ **EMB-07.5 Khatwa (Bihar)**: appliqué for tents and canopies; Applique (Khatwa) Work of Bihar GI.
- ☐ **EMB-07.6 Pipli appliqué (Odisha)**: bright cut-cloth canopies, umbrellas and lampshades, made for the Jagannath temple's Rath Yatra; *chandua*; GI 2008.
- ☐ **EMB-07.7 Manipuri embroidery**: dark-red silk on the border of the *phanek* (women's wrap); *Shaphee Lanphee* shawls (GI 2014) woven and embroidered by Meitei women with motifs once awarded to Naga warriors.
- ☐ **EMB-07.8 Naga and North-East**: embroidery, cowries and beadwork on woven shawls marking tribe and status; Mizo and Arunachali textiles are mostly woven.
- ☐ **EMB-07.9 Aari work (pan-India)**: chain stitch with a hooked needle on a frame; Tamil Nadu and Andhra's bridal blouse "aari work", Kashmir, Kutch, Lucknow.
- ☐ **EMB-07.10 Shisha (mirror) work**: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Haryana, Sindh; once mica or blown-glass pieces, now cut mirror.
- ☐ **EMB-07.11 Convent and church embroidery (Goa, Kerala, Tamil Nadu)**: whitework, cross stitch and lace taught in mission schools.

# PART 5 — THE WORLD EMBROIDERY MAP

## EMB-08 Embroidery traditions of the world

- ☐ **EMB-08.1 China**: four famous schools: Su (Suzhou, fine silk, double-sided work), Xiang (Hunan), Shu (Sichuan) and Yue (Guangdong); silk embroidery goes back over 2,000 years.
- ☐ **EMB-08.2 Japan**: *sashiko* ("little stabs", white running stitch on indigo, for strengthening work clothes) and *nihon shishu* (*nui*), silk and gold embroidery for kimono.
- ☐ **EMB-08.3 Korea**: *jasu*, silk embroidery on screens, robes and wrapping cloths.
- ☐ **EMB-08.4 Central Asia**: Uzbek and Tajik *suzani*, large dowry hangings with suns, flowers and pomegranates in chain and couching (*basma*) stitch.
- ☐ **EMB-08.5 Persia and Turkey**: Rasht patchwork embroidery, Ottoman court embroidery, Turkish towels and *oya*.
- ☐ **EMB-08.6 Palestine**: *tatreez*, cross-stitch on dresses (*thobe*) whose patterns show a woman's village; inscribed by UNESCO in 2021.
- ☐ **EMB-08.7 Ukraine and Eastern Europe**: the *vyshyvanka* embroidered shirt; Russian, Romanian and Bulgarian shirts; Hungary's Matyó flowers (UNESCO, 2012).
- ☐ **EMB-08.8 Western Europe**: English crewel (Jacobean wool work), *opus anglicanum* (medieval English church embroidery, famed across Europe), the Bayeux embroidery, blackwork, samplers; Swedish and Norwegian folk embroidery, Hardanger; Portuguese Madeira and Castelo Branco work; Italian Assisi work.
- ☐ **EMB-08.9 The Americas**: Mexico's Otomí *Tenango* animals and flowers; Guatemalan *huipil* blouses; Panama's Guna *molas*; North American quilting and samplers.
- ☐ **EMB-08.10 South-East Asia**: Hmong *paj ntaub* ("flower cloth"): cross stitch, reverse appliqué and, after the wars of the 1970s, story cloths; Vietnamese and Philippine embroidery (piña cloth).
- ☐ **EMB-08.11 Africa**: Hausa embroidered robes (Nigeria), Kuba cloth appliqué (Congo), Ethiopian and Moroccan (Fez) embroidery.
- ☐ **EMB-08.12 South Asia beyond India**: Sindhi and Balochi embroidery and *ralli* quilts (Pakistan), Bangladeshi nakshi kantha, Afghan *khamak*.

# PART 7 — HISTORY, PEOPLE AND AWARDS

## EMB-11 The history of embroidery

- ☐ **EMB-11.1 Before history**: bone needles and beads; decorated skins.
- ☐ **EMB-11.2 The ancient world**: Chinese silk chain stitch; Egyptian appliqué and beadwork (Tutankhamun's tunic); references to embroidered cloth in Vedic and epic literature and in Megasthenes's account of flowered muslins.
- ☐ **EMB-11.3 Medieval courts and churches**: Byzantine gold, *opus anglicanum*, the Bayeux embroidery; Sultanate and Mughal India's royal workshops (*karkhanas*), described in the *Ain-i-Akbari*.
- ☐ **EMB-11.4 Trade and empire**: Indian chintz and embroideries for Europe; the Kashmir shawl craze; colonial exhibitions; mission-school needlework.
- ☐ **EMB-11.5 Machines**: Heilmann, Schiffli, the sewing machine's zigzag, multi-head and computerised machines.
- ☐ **EMB-11.6 Decline and revival**: cheap machine goods and Partition hurt many crafts; Kamaladevi Chattopadhyay, Pupul Jayakar, the Crafts Council of India (1964), SEWA, Dastkar, Shrujan (1969, Kutch), Kala Raksha, Kalaraksha Vidhyalaya and Rangsutra revived them.
- ☐ **EMB-11.7 Today**: designer collaborations, online sales by artisans, GI protection, copying by machines, and embroidery as contemporary art.

## EMB-12 Artisans, designers, museums and awards

- ☐ **EMB-12.1 Honours in India**: the **National Awards** for master craftspersons (since 1965), the **Shilp Guru** awards (since 2002) and the Sant Kabir awards (for weavers), all from the Ministry of Textiles, and Padma awards. Latest lists are published at handicrafts.nic.in and through PIB; they were not individually verified for this edition.
- ☐ **EMB-12.2 Master artisans (examples)**: Lalita Vakil (Chamba rumal; Padma Shri 2022); Pabiben Rabari (Rabari embroidery entrepreneur); Chandaben Shroff (1933 to 2016, founder of Shrujan; Rolex Award 2006); Rumana and Ayub Khan and other chikan Shilp Gurus of Lucknow; Kasuti and Toda artisans' cooperatives.
- ☐ **EMB-12.3 Revivalists and scholars**: Kamaladevi Chattopadhyay, Pupul Jayakar, Jasleen Dhamija (*Asian Embroidery*, 2004), Laila Tyabji (Dastkar), Judy Frater (Kala Raksha, Somaiya Kala Vidya), Ruth Clifford, Rta Kapur Chishti.
- ☐ **EMB-12.4 Designers who work with embroidery**: Ritu Kumar, Abu Jani and Sandeep Khosla (chikankari), Sabyasachi Mukherjee, Rahul Mishra, Anita Dongre (gota patti), Manish Malhotra; Mumbai's embroidery ateliers that supply Paris couture houses (Chanakya, founded 1984; the Chanakya School of Craft, 2016).
- ☐ **EMB-12.5 World figures**: Mary Thomas, Erica Wilson (crewel), Jessie Newbery and Ann Macbeth (Glasgow School), May Morris (daughter of William Morris), Constance Howard; contemporary artists Tracey Emin, Ghada Amer, Faith Ringgold (story quilts), the Gee's Bend quilters.
- ☐ **EMB-12.6 Museums**: Calico Museum of Textiles (Ahmedabad), National Crafts Museum (New Delhi), LLDC Living and Learning Design Centre (Kutch), V&A (London), Royal School of Needlework, Textile Museum (Washington), Musée de la Tapisserie de Bayeux, China National Silk Museum (Hangzhou).

# PART 8 — IDEAS: PHILOSOPHY, PSYCHOLOGY AND SOCIETY

## EMB-13 Embroidery as language, identity and care

- ☐ **EMB-13.1 Women's language and memory**: kantha, phulkari, samplers and story cloths as records made by women who were denied writing; Rozsika Parker, *The Subversive Stitch* (1984).
- ☐ **EMB-13.2 Identity**: motifs that tell village, tribe, marriage status and faith (tatreez, Rabari, Naga).
- ☐ **EMB-13.3 Protest and politics**: suffragette banners (1908 to 1914); Chile's *arpilleras* under Pinochet; the AIDS Memorial Quilt (1987); Hmong story cloths; Gandhi's khadi movement as a textile politics.
- ☐ **EMB-13.4 Therapy and well-being**: needlework was used to help shell-shocked soldiers after the First World War; today's studies of textile crafts link them with calm and connection (CRO-00.05, KNT-13).
- ☐ **EMB-13.5 Fair trade and livelihoods**: piece rates, middlemen, credit to artisans, design copying; the Craftmark (All India Artisans and Craftworkers Welfare Association) and Handloom Mark; buying direct.
- ☐ **EMB-13.6 Authenticity and ownership**: who may use a community's motifs? GI tags, cultural appropriation and respectful collaboration.

# PART 9 — THE PRACTICAL GUIDE

## EMB-14 First stitches, care and buying

- ☐ **EMB-14.1 A first sampler**: on cotton cloth in a 15 cm hoop, rows of running, back, stem, chain, lazy daisy, satin, blanket, French knot, cross and herringbone stitch; then a small motif using five of them.
- ☐ **EMB-14.2 Starting and ending threads**: waste knots and weaving in, not bulky knots; use about 45 cm of thread; separate strands.
- ☐ **EMB-14.3 Patterns**: draw your own, trace from nature, or use copyright-free historical designs; credit living designers.
- ☐ **EMB-14.4 Washing and care**: hand-wash gently in cool water with mild soap; never wring; dry in shade; iron face down on a towel so stitches stay raised; dry-clean zardozi and store it wrapped in muslin, away from damp (real metal thread tarnishes).
- ☐ **EMB-14.5 Buying authentic craft**: look at the back; ask who made it and where; look for GI, Craftmark or cooperative labels; buy from state emporia, craft bazaars (Dilli Haat, Dastkar) and artisan cooperatives; expect a fair price.
- ☐ **EMB-14.6 Learning more**: Royal School of Needlework stitch bank (free online), state craft institutes, NIFT and NID craft clusters, local teachers.
