## TEC-00.01 What was the first technology?

**Short answer:** The oldest known tools are sharp stone flakes from Lomekwi, Kenya, about 3.3 million years old, older than our genus *Homo*. Control of fire, at least a million years ago, was the second great technology. India's own stone tools at Attirampakkam (Tamil Nadu) go back about 1.5 million years.

**Keywords:** first tool ever, Lomekwi stone tools, Oldowan, Acheulean handaxe, prehistoric technology India

### Stone that cuts

Striking one stone with another breaks off a flake with an edge sharper than teeth. The **Oldowan** tools (about 2.6 million years ago, Olduvai Gorge) and the teardrop-shaped **Acheulean handaxes** (from about 1.76 million years ago) were used for a million years and more.

### Fire, clothing, containers

Fire gave warmth, protection and cooked food (see COK). Needles of bone (about 40,000 years ago or earlier) gave sewn clothing (SEW). Pottery (about 20,000 years ago in China) gave cooking pots and storage.

### Why it matters

Each tool made the next one possible. Archaeologists describe this as *cumulative culture*: humans learn from others and add improvements, so technology grows across generations.

**Remember this:** Technology is older than our species; it began with a sharp edge.

## TEC-00.02 Why did the wheel take so long to invent?

**Short answer:** The hard part was not the round wheel but the **axle**: a smooth pin that fits a hole in the wheel tightly enough to carry weight but loosely enough to turn. That needed metal tools and careful carpentry, so wheeled vehicles appear only about 3500 BCE, after farming, pottery and copper.

**Keywords:** invention of the wheel, history of the wheel, Ljubljana Marshes wheel, potter's wheel, wheel and axle

### The evidence

- **Potter's wheel**: Mesopotamia, about 4000 BCE or earlier.
- **Wheeled vehicles**: pictures and models appear around 3500 to 3300 BCE from Mesopotamia to Central Europe; the Ljubljana Marshes wheel and axle (Slovenia) is about 5,150 years old.
- **India**: toy carts of clay from Harappan sites and real solid-wheeled bullock carts; the spoked wheel came later and became the *chakra* of the Konark Sun Temple and the national flag.

### Why late?

- **Axle fit**: too tight and it jams; too loose and it wobbles.
- **Roads and animals**: wheels need flat ground and draught animals.
- **No need**: in the Americas, without horses or oxen, wheels appeared on toys but not carts.

**Remember this:** The wheel's genius is the axle, and that needed metal tools.

## TEC-00.03 How does a screw hold things together?

**Short answer:** A screw is an inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder. Turning it pushes it forward with great force, and the friction of its thread against the material holds it in place. That is why a small twist with a screwdriver can hold a heavy shelf.

**Keywords:** how screws work, screw simple machine, Archimedes screw, nut and bolt, fasteners

### From pump to fastener

The screw as a machine is credited to Archimedes and others in the 3rd century BCE (the water-lifting Archimedes screw). Screw presses squeezed olives and grapes, and later printed books (TEC-08). Metal screws as fasteners became common only after machine-made threads in the 18th century.

### Standard threads

Joseph Whitworth proposed a standard thread in 1841; later came metric (ISO) threads. Standards let a nut from one factory fit a bolt from another, a quiet revolution behind mass production.

### Screws and their cousins

- **Wood screw**: cuts its own thread.
- **Bolt and nut**: clamp parts together.
- **Rivet**: a permanent pin (aircraft, bridges).
- **Nail**: holds by friction alone.

**Remember this:** A screw turns a gentle twist into a strong, lasting grip.

## TEC-00.04 Why did electricity change everything?

**Short answer:** Electricity turns energy from one place (a dam, a coal plant, a solar panel) into a form that travels instantly through wires and can become light, heat, motion, sound or information anywhere. Every later technology, from the fridge to the internet, depends on it.

**Keywords:** history of electricity, Faraday, Edison and Tesla, how electricity works, electrification of India

### Key steps

- **1800**: Alessandro Volta's battery.
- **1831**: Michael Faraday discovers electromagnetic induction, the principle of every generator and transformer.
- **1879**: practical incandescent lamps by Thomas Edison and Joseph Swan.
- **1880s and 1890s**: the "current wars"; Nikola Tesla and George Westinghouse's alternating current (AC) wins for long-distance supply.
- **India**: Kolkata got electric street lighting in 1879 (demonstration) and public supply in 1899; Asia's early hydroelectric station at Sivasamudram, Karnataka (1902), powered the Kolar gold fields. India declared every village electrified in 2018.

### What it unlocked

Lighting, motors, radio, refrigeration, computers and the grid itself. The US National Academy of Engineering ranked electrification the greatest engineering achievement of the 20th century (2000).

**Remember this:** Electricity is energy that can travel and transform.

## TEC-00.05 How does a fridge make cold?

**Short answer:** A fridge does not make cold; it moves heat out. A liquid refrigerant evaporates in pipes inside the fridge, soaking up heat (just as sweat cools your skin). A compressor then squeezes the vapour, and the coils at the back release that heat into the room as the refrigerant turns back into liquid.

**Keywords:** how a refrigerator works, vapour compression cycle, refrigerant, compressor, history of refrigeration

### The cycle

1. **Evaporator** (inside): low-pressure liquid boils and absorbs heat.
2. **Compressor**: squeezes the gas, making it hot.
3. **Condenser** (coils at the back): hot gas gives heat to the room and condenses.
4. **Expansion valve**: pressure drops, and the cycle repeats.

### A short history

Jacob Perkins patented a vapour-compression machine (1834); household fridges spread in the 1920s and 1930s. CFC refrigerants harmed the ozone layer and were phased out under the Montreal Protocol (1987); the Kigali Amendment (2016) phases down HFCs, which warm the climate.

### The same idea, elsewhere

Air conditioners and heat pumps use the same cycle. India's traditional cooling, the clay *matka* and *khus* screens, works by evaporation too.

**Remember this:** A fridge is a heat pump: it carries heat from inside to outside.

## TEC-00.06 How does the internet reach your phone?

**Short answer:** Your message is split into small packets with addresses. They travel by radio to a mobile tower or Wi-Fi router, then through fibre-optic cables (including undersea cables) and routers to a data centre, and the reply comes back the same way, all in a fraction of a second. Common rules (TCP/IP) let different networks talk.

**Keywords:** how the internet works, packet switching, TCP/IP, undersea cables, 5G India

### The building blocks

- **Packet switching**: Paul Baran and Donald Davies (1960s); ARPANET (1969).
- **TCP/IP**: Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn (1974); the internet adopted it on 1 January 1983.
- **World Wide Web**: Tim Berners-Lee at CERN (1989 to 1991).
- **Fibre optics**: light in glass; Narinder Singh Kapany's pioneering work (1950s) and Charles Kao's Nobel-winning insight (2009 prize).
- **Undersea cables**: carry about 99 per cent of intercontinental data.

### India online

Public internet arrived with VSNL on 15 August 1995. Cheap data after 2016, Aadhaar, UPI (2016) and 5G (from October 2022) made India one of the world's largest online populations.

**Remember this:** The internet is a network of networks sharing one set of rules.

## TEC-00.07 What is the newest technology today?

**Short answer:** As of November 2026, the fastest-moving fields are generative artificial intelligence, AI chips and data centres, renewable energy and batteries, gene editing and mRNA medicine, quantum computing, reusable rockets and brain-computer interfaces. Each is real but also surrounded by hype: check claims against peer-reviewed or official sources.

**Keywords:** latest technology 2026, emerging technologies, generative AI, quantum computing, brain computer interface

### Frontier fields (dated, November 2026)

- **AI**: large language models that write, code and reason; chip makers such as NVIDIA (its CEO Jensen Huang received the IEEE Medal of Honor, 2026).
- **Neural interfaces**: the 2026 Queen Elizabeth Prize for Engineering honoured pioneers of cochlear implants and brain-computer interfaces.
- **Light-controlled neurons**: the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine honoured optogenetics.
- **Quantum information**: the 2025 Turing Award (announced 2026) went to Charles Bennett and Gilles Brassard for quantum cryptography.
- **Energy**: cheap solar and batteries; India's target of 500 GW of non-fossil capacity by 2030.
- **Space**: reusable rockets, India's Chandrayaan-3 landing near the Moon's south pole (2023) and its first space docking (SpaDeX, 2025).

### How to judge "new"

Ask: does it work outside the lab, at what cost, for whom, and with what risks?

**Remember this:** The frontier moves every year; this page is dated so you know when it was true.
