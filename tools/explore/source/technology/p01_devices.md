# PART 2 — TRUNK A: ELECTRONIC DEVICES AND HOME APPLIANCES

> **About this Part.** The devices of daily life. Each line says what it is, the idea behind it, who or when it began, and where it stands now. "Now" means November 2026; specifications change fast, so the website pages will carry dated details from manufacturers.

## TEC-02 Electronic devices

> **Before you start — TEC-02.** Almost every electronic device rests on one invention: the transistor (Bell Labs, 1947, John Bardeen, Walter Brattain and William Shockley), and on the integrated circuit (Jack Kilby, 1958; Robert Noyce, 1959) that packs billions of transistors on a chip. Gordon Moore observed in 1965 that the number doubled regularly ("Moore's law"); the pace has slowed but not stopped.

### ☐ TEC-02.1 Handheld and wearable

- ☐ **TEC-02.1a Mobile phone**: Martin Cooper's Motorola call (1973); India's first mobile call on 31 July 1995 (Jyoti Basu to Sukh Ram); smartphones from IBM Simon (1994) to the iPhone (2007) and Android (2008); India is the world's second-largest smartphone market and a major manufacturer.
- ☐ **TEC-02.1b Tablet and e-reader**: touchscreen slates; Kindle (2007) used E Ink (electronic paper that reflects light like print).
- ☐ **TEC-02.1c Smartwatch and fitness band**: sensors for steps, heart rate and sleep; accuracy varies and they are not medical devices unless certified.
- ☐ **TEC-02.1d Wearables, AR and VR headsets**: smart glasses, mixed-reality headsets; still searching for everyday uses.
- ☐ **TEC-02.1e Calculator and pocket electronics**: Texas Instruments' handheld (1967 prototype); a cheap calculator holds more computing than Apollo's guidance computer.

### ☐ TEC-02.2 Computers

- ☐ **TEC-02.2a Desktop PC**: from the Altair (1975), Apple II (1977) and IBM PC (1981) to today's machines.
- ☐ **TEC-02.2b Laptop**: portable computers from the 1980s; India's affordable laptop schemes for students.
- ☐ **TEC-02.2c Servers and data centres**: buildings of computers that run the cloud and AI; huge users of electricity and water.
- ☐ **TEC-02.2d Supercomputers**: India's PARAM (C-DAC, 1991) and the National Supercomputing Mission; the world's fastest are listed twice a year on top500.org.
- ☐ **TEC-02.2e Single-board computers**: Raspberry Pi (2012) and Arduino microcontrollers (2005) for learning and making.
- ☐ **TEC-02.2f Parts of a computer**: CPU, GPU, memory (RAM), storage (hard disk, SSD), motherboard, power supply, input and output.

### ☐ TEC-02.3 Audio and display

- ☐ **TEC-02.3a Speakers and headphones**: a coil moves a cone (the moving-coil loudspeaker, 1925, Rice and Kellogg); noise cancellation adds opposite sound waves.
- ☐ **TEC-02.3b Microphones**: dynamic, condenser and the MEMS microphones in phones.
- ☐ **TEC-02.3c Television**: John Logie Baird's mechanical TV (1926), electronic TV (Philo Farnsworth, Vladimir Zworykin); Doordarshan began in Delhi on 15 September 1959; colour in India from 1982 (Asian Games).
- ☐ **TEC-02.3d Display technologies**: CRT, LCD, LED-backlit, OLED, micro-LED; resolutions from HD to 8K.
- ☐ **TEC-02.3e Projectors and monitors**: DLP and LCD projectors; high-refresh monitors for gaming.

### ☐ TEC-02.4 Networking

- ☐ **TEC-02.4a Modem and router**: the modem translates the line signal; the router directs traffic in the home.
- ☐ **TEC-02.4b Wi-Fi**: based on IEEE 802.11 (1997); CSIRO of Australia's patent was key; versions up to Wi-Fi 7.
- ☐ **TEC-02.4c Mobile networks**: 1G analogue to 5G; India's indigenous 4G and 5G stack by C-DOT and partners (BSNL rollout from 2024 to 2025).
- ☐ **TEC-02.4d Fibre optics and switches**: light pulses in glass carry the internet's backbone (TEC-00.06); BharatNet aims to connect every gram panchayat.
- ☐ **TEC-02.4e Satellite internet**: low-Earth-orbit constellations; licensing in India under the Telecommunications Act (2023).

### ☐ TEC-02.5 Power

- ☐ **TEC-02.5a Batteries**: Volta (1800), lead-acid (Gaston Planté, 1859), lithium-ion (Nobel in Chemistry 2019 to John Goodenough, Stanley Whittingham and Akira Yoshino); sodium-ion and solid-state are next.
- ☐ **TEC-02.5b Chargers and power banks**: USB-C is now the common standard (India moved new phones to it from 2025, in line with the EU).
- ☐ **TEC-02.5c UPS and inverters**: backup power, common in Indian homes.
- ☐ **TEC-02.5d Solar inverters and rooftop solar**: PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana (2024) subsidises rooftop solar.

### ☐ TEC-02.6 Cameras, drones, consoles and the smart home

- ☐ **TEC-02.6a Cameras**: from the camera obscura (described by Ibn al-Haytham, about 1021) and photography (Niépce, Daguerre, 1820s to 1839) to the digital sensor (CCD, Boyle and Smith, 1969; Nobel 2009) and the CMOS sensors in phones.
- ☐ **TEC-02.6b Drones**: remote and autonomous aircraft for photography, mapping, farming (Namo Drone Didi scheme) and delivery; India's Drone Rules (2021).
- ☐ **TEC-02.6c Gaming consoles**: Magnavox Odyssey (1972) to today's consoles; see SPT for esports.
- ☐ **TEC-02.6d Smart home and IoT**: connected lights, plugs, cameras, speakers; the Matter standard (2022) for compatibility; privacy and security risks.

## TEC-03 Home appliances

> **Before you start — TEC-03.** Appliances changed daily life, especially women's unpaid work, more than many famous inventions; the economist Ha-Joon Chang argued that the washing machine changed the world more than the internet (*23 Things They Don't Tell You about Capitalism*, 2010). India's Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE) star labels (from 2006) help compare running costs.

- ☐ **TEC-03.1 Refrigerator**: vapour-compression cycle (TEC-00.05); frost-free and inverter models; BEE star ratings.
- ☐ **TEC-03.2 Microwave oven**: Percy Spencer noticed a melted chocolate bar near a radar magnetron (1945); microwaves shake water molecules to heat food.
- ☐ **TEC-03.3 Oven, OTG and air fryer**: hot air and radiant heat; the air fryer is a small, fast convection oven.
- ☐ **TEC-03.4 Induction cooktop**: a changing magnetic field heats an iron pan directly; efficient and safe, but needs magnetic cookware.
- ☐ **TEC-03.5 Mixer-grinder**: India's kitchen workhorse; Sumeet (1963, designed by Satya Prakash Mathur) made it a household name for Indian wet grinding.
- ☐ **TEC-03.6 Pressure cooker**: see COK-10.3c.
- ☐ **TEC-03.7 Washing machine**: drum machines from the early 1900s; semi-automatic and fully automatic types; front- and top-loading.
- ☐ **TEC-03.8 Air conditioner**: Willis Carrier (1902); inverter ACs and star ratings; refrigerants with lower warming potential.
- ☐ **TEC-03.9 Air cooler**: evaporative cooling; works best in dry heat (Rajasthan), poorly in humid coasts.
- ☐ **TEC-03.10 Ceiling fan**: Philip Diehl (1882); BLDC (brushless) fans use far less power.
- ☐ **TEC-03.11 Water purifier**: filters, UV and RO (reverse osmosis); RO wastes water and removes minerals, so use it only where the water needs it (check TDS).
- ☐ **TEC-03.12 Geyser and water heater**: storage, instant and solar water heaters.
- ☐ **TEC-03.13 Vacuum cleaner and robot vacuum**: Hubert Cecil Booth's powered vacuum (1901); robot vacuums map rooms with sensors.
- ☐ **TEC-03.14 Electric iron**: Henry Seeley's patent (1882); steam irons.
- ☐ **TEC-03.15 Lighting**: incandescent, CFL, LED; India's UJALA scheme (2015) distributed over 36 crore LED bulbs.
