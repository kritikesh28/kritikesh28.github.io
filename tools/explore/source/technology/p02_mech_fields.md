# PART 3 — TRUNK A: MECHANICAL DEVICES, MACHINES, VEHICLES AND TOOLS

> **About this Part.** From the smallest fastener to the largest machines ever built. Mechanical technology is older than electronics and still holds the world together.

## TEC-04 Fasteners and simple machines

> **Before you start — TEC-04.** Greek thinkers (Archimedes, Hero of Alexandria) listed the simple machines; Renaissance scientists settled on six. Each trades force for distance: you push less but farther. **See also:** TEC-00.03.

- ☐ **TEC-04.1 Lever**: a bar on a pivot (fulcrum); seesaw, crowbar, scissors, the *dhenkli* well-sweep of Indian villages. "Give me a place to stand and I will move the Earth" (attributed to Archimedes).
- ☐ **TEC-04.2 Wheel and axle**: TEC-00.02; door knobs, steering wheels, the Persian wheel (*rahat*) for lifting water.
- ☐ **TEC-04.3 Pulley**: a wheel with a rope; well pulleys, cranes, lifts.
- ☐ **TEC-04.4 Inclined plane**: a ramp; how pyramids and temple towers were likely raised.
- ☐ **TEC-04.5 Wedge**: two inclined planes back to back; axe, knife, chisel, plough.
- ☐ **TEC-04.6 Screw**: an inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder (TEC-00.03).
- ☐ **TEC-04.7 Fasteners**: screws, nuts and bolts, nails (hand-forged, then machine-cut and wire nails), rivets, pins, washers, clips, zippers (Whitcomb Judson, 1893; Gideon Sundback's modern zip, 1913), Velcro (George de Mestral, 1955).
- ☐ **TEC-04.8 Gears, cams, springs and bearings**: gears transmit turning force (the Antikythera mechanism, about 100 BCE, is the oldest known geared device); ball bearings reduce friction.
- ☐ **TEC-04.9 Locks and keys**: Egyptian wooden pin locks (about 4,000 years ago), Linus Yale Jr's pin-tumbler lock (1860s), Aligarh's lock industry, today's smart locks.
- ☐ **TEC-04.10 Clocks**: sundials and water clocks (*ghati* in India); the mechanical escapement (China's Su Song, 1090s; European clocks, 14th century); Christiaan Huygens's pendulum clock (1656); quartz (1927) and atomic clocks; India's time is kept by CSIR-NPL, New Delhi.

## TEC-05 Engines, vehicles and giant machines

- ☐ **TEC-05.1 Steam engine**: Thomas Newcomen (1712), James Watt's separate condenser (1769); the power of the Industrial Revolution.
- ☐ **TEC-05.2 Internal combustion engine**: Nikolaus Otto's four-stroke (1876), Rudolf Diesel (1890s); now being replaced in many uses by electric motors.
- ☐ **TEC-05.3 Electric motor**: Faraday's rotation (1821); motors drive fans, trains, EVs and robots.
- ☐ **TEC-05.4 Turbines and jet engines**: steam turbine (Charles Parsons, 1884), jet engines (Frank Whittle and Hans von Ohain, late 1930s).
- ☐ **TEC-05.5 Bicycle**: Karl Drais's running machine (1817), the chain-driven "safety bicycle" (John Kemp Starley, 1885); India's Atlas and Hero made it the people's vehicle.
- ☐ **TEC-05.6 Cars**: Karl Benz's Motorwagen (1885 to 1886); Ford's Model T assembly line (1913); Maruti 800 (1983) put India on wheels; electric vehicles and the FAME and PM E-DRIVE schemes.
- ☐ **TEC-05.7 Trains**: Stephenson's *Rocket* (1829); India's first passenger train, Bori Bunder to Thane (16 April 1853); Vande Bharat trains (2019) built at ICF Chennai; metros in many cities; the Mumbai–Ahmedabad high-speed rail under construction.
- ☐ **TEC-05.8 Ships and submarines**: dhows and the Indian Ocean trade, steamships, container ships (Malcolm McLean, 1956); India's indigenous aircraft carrier INS *Vikrant* (commissioned 2022).
- ☐ **TEC-05.9 Aircraft**: the Wright brothers (17 December 1903); India's first commercial flight, J. R. D. Tata from Karachi to Bombay (15 October 1932); HAL's Tejas light combat aircraft.
- ☐ **TEC-05.10 Rockets**: Tipu Sultan's iron-cased Mysorean rockets (1780s and 1790s) inspired Britain's Congreve rockets; Konstantin Tsiolkovsky, Robert Goddard (first liquid-fuel rocket, 1926); India's first rocket launch from Thumba (21 November 1963); reusable boosters today.
- ☐ **TEC-05.11 Cranes, excavators and tunnel-boring machines**: from Roman treadwheel cranes to TBMs that dug the Gotthard Base Tunnel (57 km, 2016) and India's Atal Tunnel (9 km, 2020, built by drill and blast).
- ☐ **TEC-05.12 The largest machines ever built**: the Large Hadron Collider (27 km ring, CERN, 2008); the Bagger 293 bucket-wheel excavator; container ships of about 400 m; the International Space Station (from 1998).

## TEC-06 Tools and instruments

- ☐ **TEC-06.1 Hand tools**: hammer, screwdriver, spanner (wrench), pliers, saw, chisel, file, drill; the Indian *basula* (adze) and *randa* (plane) of the carpenter.
- ☐ **TEC-06.2 Power tools**: electric drill (Wilhelm Fein, 1895; Black & Decker's pistol grip, 1917), angle grinder, circular saw, jigsaw, cordless tools.
- ☐ **TEC-06.3 Measuring instruments**: ruler, vernier caliper (Pierre Vernier, 1631), micrometer, spirit level, multimeter, thermometer (Galileo's thermoscope; Fahrenheit and Celsius scales), barometer (Torricelli, 1643), weighing scales; the SI units (redefined in 2019).
- ☐ **TEC-06.4 Scientific instruments**: microscope (Leeuwenhoek's single-lens microscopes, 1670s), telescope (Galileo, 1609), spectroscope; India's observatories (Jantar Mantar, Jaipur, UNESCO 2010).
- ☐ **TEC-06.5 Farm tools**: plough, sickle, hoe (*kudal*), *khurpi*, seed drill (the Babylonian seed plough; India's bamboo seed drill, *tifan*; Jethro Tull's drill, 1701), threshers, tractors (India is the world's largest tractor maker by volume).
- ☐ **TEC-06.6 Craft tools**: the loom, spinning wheel (*charkha*), potter's wheel, needles and hooks (CRO, KNT, EMB, SEW).

# PART 4 — TRUNK B: TECHNOLOGY BY FIELD (1)

> **About this Part.** Technology applied to a domain. Each field lists its main branches, its landmark inventions and India's contribution. The brief names many fields; they are grouped into two Parts.

## TEC-07 Textiles and clothing technology

- ☐ **TEC-07.1 Fibres**: natural (cotton, first spun in the Indus valley; silk, China; wool; jute; linen), regenerated (rayon, viscose) and synthetic (nylon, Wallace Carothers at DuPont, 1935; polyester, 1941).
- ☐ **TEC-07.2 Spinning**: hand spindle, *charkha*, the spinning jenny (Hargreaves, 1764), water frame (Arkwright, 1769), mule (Crompton, 1779), ring and rotor spinning.
- ☐ **TEC-07.3 Weaving**: handlooms, the flying shuttle (John Kay, 1733), power loom (Cartwright, 1785), the Jacquard loom (1804), whose punched cards inspired early computers.
- ☐ **TEC-07.4 Dyeing and printing**: indigo, madder, the Indian chintz and *kalamkari* traditions that astonished Europe; synthetic mauve (William Perkin, 1856).
- ☐ **TEC-07.5 Smart and technical textiles**: conductive and sensor fabrics, fire-resistant and medical textiles; India's National Technical Textiles Mission (2020).
- ☐ **TEC-07.6 The sewing machine and knitting machine**: Elias Howe (1846) and Isaac Singer (1851); William Lee's stocking frame (1589). See SEW and KNT.

## TEC-08 Printing, books and art technology

- ☐ **TEC-08.1 Writing materials**: clay, palm leaf (Indian manuscripts), birch bark, papyrus, parchment, paper (Cai Lun, China, 105 CE, by tradition).
- ☐ **TEC-08.2 Printing**: woodblock printing (China; the *Diamond Sutra*, 868 CE), movable type (Bi Sheng, 1040s; Korea's metal type), Gutenberg's press (about 1450); India's first press at Goa (1556); Serampore Mission Press (1800).
- ☐ **TEC-08.3 Modern printing**: lithography, offset, digital printing, 3D printing (Charles Hull's stereolithography, 1984).
- ☐ **TEC-08.4 Art technology**: pigments (ochre, Indian yellow, lapis ultramarine), oil paint, paint tubes (John Rand, 1841), canvas, photography, digital art and drawing tablets, generative AI images (with debates about artists' consent and copyright). See BOK and MOV.

## TEC-09 Construction, transport and energy

- ☐ **TEC-09.1 Construction materials**: mud brick, fired brick (Harappan standardised bricks), lime mortar, Roman concrete, iron and steel (Delhi's rust-resistant iron pillar, about 400 CE), Portland cement (1824), reinforced concrete.
- ☐ **TEC-09.2 Architecture and structures**: arches, domes and trusses; stepwells; skyscrapers (Home Insurance Building, Chicago, 1885); bridges (India's Chenab rail bridge, the world's highest railway arch bridge, opened 2025).
- ☐ **TEC-09.3 Transport systems**: roads and highways (Grand Trunk Road; the Golden Quadrilateral, 2001 to 2012), railways, ports, airports, traffic signals and FASTag.
- ☐ **TEC-09.4 Energy**: firewood to coal, oil, gas, nuclear (India's three-stage programme of Homi Bhabha; Tarapur, 1969), hydro, solar (Bhadla Solar Park, Rajasthan), wind, green hydrogen (National Green Hydrogen Mission, 2023), the grid (One Nation One Grid, 2013).

## TEC-10 Agriculture, food, water and sanitation

- ☐ **TEC-10.1 Agriculture technology**: irrigation, the plough, the Green Revolution's high-yield seeds, tractors, drip irrigation, soil health cards, drones, precision farming. See GRD.
- ☐ **TEC-10.2 Food technology**: preservation, canning, refrigeration, pasteurisation (Louis Pasteur, 1860s), the cold chain, milk processing (Amul and Operation Flood), packaging, food fortification. See COK.
- ☐ **TEC-10.3 Water**: stepwells and tanks, dams, canals, water treatment (chlorination from 1900s), desalination, Jal Jeevan Mission (2019) tap connections.
- ☐ **TEC-10.4 Sanitation**: Harappan drains (among the world's earliest), the flush toilet (John Harington, 1596; Alexander Cumming's S-bend, 1775), sewers, twin-pit toilets, Swachh Bharat Mission (2014); Bindeshwar Pathak's Sulabh toilets (1970).
