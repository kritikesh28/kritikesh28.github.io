# PART 2 — SEWING MACHINES

## SEW-02 Kinds of machine

- ☐ **SEW-02.1 Hand-crank machine**: turned by a handle; cheap, needs no power, still common in Indian homes and villages.
- ☐ **SEW-02.2 Treadle machine**: worked by the feet on an iron stand, leaving both hands free; the classic black Usha or Singer on a wooden table, used by many darzis.
- ☐ **SEW-02.3 Electric (mechanical) machine**: a motor and foot pedal; stitches chosen by dials. Most home learners start here.
- ☐ **SEW-02.4 Computerised machine**: stitches chosen on a screen, automatic buttonholes, needle threader, speed control; more features, more to repair.
- ☐ **SEW-02.5 Overlocker (serger)**: trims and wraps the raw edge with 3 to 5 threads in one pass; the neat inside seam of ready-made T-shirts.
- ☐ **SEW-02.6 Coverstitch machine**: the double-row hem on knit garments.
- ☐ **SEW-02.7 Industrial machines**: single-needle lockstitch, overlock, flatlock, buttonhole and bar-tack machines that run all day at high speed; each does one job very well.
- ☐ **SEW-02.8 Embroidery and quilting machines**: computerised embroidery units (EMB-02.8) and long-arm quilting machines on frames.
- ☐ **SEW-02.9 The Indian story: Singer and Usha**: Singer machines reached India in the late 19th century; Usha machines were made in Kolkata from 1935, and Indian brands (Usha, later Jack, Brother and others in industry) put a machine in millions of homes. In India the machine is also a dowry item, a welfare gift and a small business.

## SEW-03 Parts, care and troubleshooting

- ☐ **SEW-03.1 Main parts**: spool pin, thread guides, tension discs, take-up lever, needle bar and clamp, presser foot and lifter, feed dogs, throat (needle) plate, bobbin, bobbin case and hook, hand wheel, stitch length and width controls, reverse lever.
- ☐ **SEW-03.2 Presser feet**: all-purpose, zipper foot, buttonhole foot, blind-hem foot, walking (even-feed) foot, rolled-hem foot, piping foot.
- ☐ **SEW-03.3 Care**: brush out lint after each project; oil where the manual says (old machines need more); change the needle every 8 or so hours of sewing; cover the machine against dust.
- ☐ **SEW-03.4 Troubleshooting**: thread nests underneath → rethread the top with the foot **up**; skipped stitches → new or right-type needle; puckering → shorter stitch or less tension; needle breaking → bent needle or pulling the cloth; loops on top → bobbin tension or wrong bobbin.

# PART 3 — TOOLS AND MATERIALS

## SEW-04 Tools

- ☐ **SEW-04.1 Hand needles**: sharps (general), betweens (quilting), crewel (embroidery), ballpoint, darners; sizes get finer as the number rises.
- ☐ **SEW-04.2 Machine needles**: the home system is 130/705H; sizes 70/10 (fine) to 110/18 (heavy). Types: universal, ballpoint or jersey (knits), stretch, microtex or sharp (silk, tight weaves), denim, leather, twin, topstitch.
- ☐ **SEW-04.3 Cutting**: dressmaking shears (only for cloth), paper scissors, snips, pinking shears, rotary cutter and mat, seam ripper (*the* most used tool).
- ☐ **SEW-04.4 Measuring and marking**: inch tape, ruler, French and hip curves, tailor's chalk, washable pens, tracing wheel and carbon.
- ☐ **SEW-04.5 Pressing**: iron with steam, pressing cloth, tailor's ham, sleeve roll, point presser, clapper.
- ☐ **SEW-04.6 Others**: pins and pin cushion, thimble, bodkin, loop turner, dress form, pattern paper.

## SEW-05 Threads, fabrics and notions

- ☐ **SEW-05.1 Threads**: polyester all-purpose (strong, slight stretch), cotton (for cotton, does not melt under the iron), silk (fine work, tailoring), topstitch, elastic shirring, overlocker cones; thickness given as Tex or weight.
- ☐ **SEW-05.2 Woven fabrics**: threads cross at right angles; grain (lengthwise), crossgrain and bias (45°, stretchy and drapey); poplin, cambric, lawn, voile, denim, twill, canvas, linen.
- ☐ **SEW-05.3 Knit fabrics**: looped (KNT-01), stretchy; jersey, rib, interlock; need ballpoint needles and stretch stitches.
- ☐ **SEW-05.4 Indian fabrics**: **khadi** (hand-spun and hand-woven, Gandhi's cloth of self-reliance); **mul** or mulmul (soft fine cotton); **cambric** and **poplin** (kurtas and shirts); **rubia** (lining voile); **casement** and **2x2 rubia** for blouses; **silks** (Banarasi, Kanchipuram, tussar, mulberry, eri, muga); **chanderi** and **maheshwari** (silk-cotton); **ikat** and **ajrakh**; **georgette** and **chiffon** (often synthetic now).
- ☐ **SEW-05.5 Preparing cloth**: pre-wash to shrink, straighten the grain, iron, then cut. Most "my kurta shrank" stories start here.
- ☐ **SEW-05.6 Interfacings**: fusible (iron-on) or sew-in layers that stiffen collars, cuffs, plackets and waistbands; Indian tailors call buckram and canvas *kaccha* and *pakka*.
- ☐ **SEW-05.7 Notions**: buttons, hooks and eyes (the blouse *hook*), press studs, zips (invisible, metal, nylon; Gideon Sundback's "hookless fastener" was patented in 1917), elastic, drawstrings (*nada*), piping, bias tape, lace, latkan (tassels).

# PART 4 — TECHNIQUES

## SEW-06 Stitches and seams

- ☐ **SEW-06.1 Hand stitches**: running, tacking (basting, *kacchi silai*), back stitch, slip stitch, hemming stitch, whip stitch, blanket stitch, catch (herringbone) stitch, buttonhole stitch.
- ☐ **SEW-06.2 Machine stitches**: straight (length 2.5 mm is a good start), zigzag, stretch (lightning), blind hem, overlock-style, buttonhole.
- ☐ **SEW-06.3 Seam allowance**: commercial patterns use 1.5 cm (5/8 inch); Indian tailors often leave 2.5 cm or more in kurtas and blouses for later alteration.
- ☐ **SEW-06.4 Seam types**: plain seam, French seam (raw edge hidden inside, for sheer fabric), flat-felled seam (jeans, shirts), bound or Hong Kong seam, lapped seam.
- ☐ **SEW-06.5 Seam finishes**: pinking, zigzag, overlocking, turned-and-stitched, binding. The inside of a garment shows its quality.

## SEW-07 Shaping, openings and finishing

- ☐ **SEW-07.1 Darts**: folded wedges that turn flat cloth into 3D shape at bust, waist and back; the heart of the blouse.
- ☐ **SEW-07.2 Pleats, tucks and gathers**: knife, box and inverted pleats; pin tucks; gathers for skirts, lehengas and puffed sleeves; *kalis* (panels) in anarkalis and lehengas.
- ☐ **SEW-07.3 Hems**: double-fold, blind hem, rolled hem (dupattas, sarees), faced hem, *fall* stitching on a sari.
- ☐ **SEW-07.4 Facings and binding**: finishing necklines and armholes; Indian kurtas use shaped facings for "U", "V", boat and *patti* (placket) necks.
- ☐ **SEW-07.5 Openings and fastenings**: plackets, zips (centred, lapped, invisible), buttonholes and buttons, hooks, loops (*tukmas*).
- ☐ **SEW-07.6 Pockets**: patch, side-seam (in kurtas and pyjamas), welt, jeans pockets.
- ☐ **SEW-07.7 Collars and cuffs**: shirt collar and stand, Chinese or mandarin (*bandhgala*) collar, Peter Pan, shawl collar; cuffs and plackets on sleeves.
- ☐ **SEW-07.8 Sleeves**: set-in, raglan, kimono, cap, puff, bell; easing a sleeve cap is a classic skill.
- ☐ **SEW-07.9 Linings**: hide seams, add body and comfort; blouses, lehengas, jackets and coats.
- ☐ **SEW-07.10 Pressing**: press each seam as you go; pressing is lifting and setting the iron, ironing is sliding it. Tailors say a garment is half stitched and half pressed.

# PART 5 — PATTERN MAKING AND FIT

## SEW-08 Measure, draft, fit

- ☐ **SEW-08.1 Measurements**: bust or chest, waist, hip, shoulder, armhole, sleeve length and round, neck depth (front and back), garment length; take them over light clothing, tape snug not tight.
- ☐ **SEW-08.2 Indian tailoring systems**: the darzi drafts directly on the cloth with chalk from measurements, often in inches, with rules of thumb (such as armhole from chest); taught in ITIs, polytechnic courses and many Hindi and regional "cutting and tailoring" books.
- ☐ **SEW-08.3 Western flat pattern making**: a basic block (sloper) for bodice, skirt, trousers and sleeve, then altered into styles by moving darts, slashing and spreading. Ebenezer Butterick sold graded tissue-paper patterns from 1863.
- ☐ **SEW-08.4 Draping**: pinning cloth on a dress form to find the shape; the way couturiers such as Madeleine Vionnet (bias cut) worked.
- ☐ **SEW-08.5 Grading**: making a pattern in a range of sizes; the basis of ready-made sizing (and why "M" differs between brands).
- ☐ **SEW-08.6 Fitting**: a toile or muslin trial; common changes for full bust, sway back, sloping shoulders.
- ☐ **SEW-08.7 Alterations**: shorten hems and sleeves, take in or let out side seams, change buttons, re-set zips; the everyday work of most Indian tailors.
- ☐ **SEW-08.8 Digital tools**: pattern-making software (CAD), 3D garment simulation, made-to-measure from body scans; used in industry and taught at NIFT.

# PART 7 — BEYOND GARMENTS

## SEW-11 Other sewing

- ☐ **SEW-11.1 Home furnishing**: curtains, cushion covers, bedsheets, table linen, *gaddi* covers; the easiest straight-line sewing.
- ☐ **SEW-11.2 Bags**: tote, jhola, pouch, potli, laptop sleeve; boxed corners and straps.
- ☐ **SEW-11.3 Quilting and patchwork**: layers stitched together. **Godhadi** (Maharashtra and Karnataka quilts of old saris), **kantha** (EMB-07.3), **ralli** (appliqué and patchwork quilts of Sindh, western Rajasthan and Gujarat), **sujani** (EMB-07.4); world: American patchwork, Gee's Bend quilts (Alabama), Japanese **boro**.
- ☐ **SEW-11.4 Upcycling**: sari into quilt or dupatta, old kurta into cushion, jeans into bag; India's long habit of reuse before the word existed.
- ☐ **SEW-11.5 Mending and visible mending**: darning, patching, *rafoo* (invisible weaving repair for shawls and silks), sashiko (EMB-08) as decoration; mending shown proudly rather than hidden.
- ☐ **SEW-11.6 Costume and theatre**: period costume, film costume (MOV), dance costumes for Bharatanatyam and Kathak with pleated fans, cosplay.
- ☐ **SEW-11.7 Other materials**: leather (*mochi* work), canvas and tents, sails, upholstery, medical stitching (sutures are sewing too).

# PART 8 — HISTORY, PEOPLE AND AWARDS

## SEW-12 The history of sewing

- ☐ **SEW-12.1 Prehistory**: awls, then eyed needles at least about 40,000 years ago (SEW-00.01).
- ☐ **SEW-12.2 Ancient and medieval**: guild tailors in Europe; India's darzi communities serving courts and towns; draped and stitched dress side by side (SEW-00.02).
- ☐ **SEW-12.3 The machine age**: Thimonnier, Howe, Singer, the "sewing machine war" over patents and the first patent pool (SEW-10).
- ☐ **SEW-12.4 Ready-made clothing**: uniforms for armies, then shirts and dresses in standard sizes; the sweatshop and the trade union (the ILGWU in New York).
- ☐ **SEW-12.5 India's tailoring culture**: the neighbourhood darzi, made-to-measure for ordinary people, the blouse-stitching economy around the sari.
- ☐ **SEW-12.6 India's garment industry**: hubs at Tiruppur (knitwear), Ludhiana, Bengaluru, Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Jaipur and Kolkata; ready-made garment exports of roughly US$ 15 to 16 billion in 2024–25 (AEPC figures; check latest).
- ☐ **SEW-12.7 Fast and slow fashion**: cheap trend clothes from the 1990s onward; slow fashion, repair and local tailoring as a counter-movement (SEW-00.05).

## SEW-13 Couturiers, designers, institutions and awards

- ☐ **SEW-13.1 World couturiers**: Charles Frederick Worth (haute couture), Coco Chanel, Madeleine Vionnet, Cristóbal Balenciaga, Christian Dior, Yves Saint Laurent, Issey Miyake, Vivienne Westwood.
- ☐ **SEW-13.2 Indian designers**: Ritu Kumar (from 1969, reviving hand-block prints; Padma Shri 2013), Rohit Bal (1961–2024), Wendell Rodricks (Goa, Padma Shri 2014; 1960–2020), Tarun Tahiliani, Manish Malhotra (film costume), Sabyasachi Mukherjee, Anita Dongre, Rahul Mishra (Woolmark Prize 2014; shows at Paris Haute Couture Week), Gaurav Gupta.
- ☐ **SEW-13.3 The unnamed masters**: the karigars and darzis whose names rarely appear on labels; credit them where you can.
- ☐ **SEW-13.4 Institutions**: NIFT (1986), FDCI (1998), Pearl Academy, ITIs (Sewing Technology and Dress Making trades), Apparel Export Promotion Council (AEPC); abroad, Central Saint Martins, Parsons, Bunka Fashion College, Fashion Institute of Technology.
- ☐ **SEW-13.5 Fashion awards**: CFDA Fashion Awards (USA; 2026 American Womenswear Designer of the Year: Rachel Scott of Diotima); The Fashion Awards by the British Fashion Council (2025 Designer of the Year: Jonathan Anderson, his third win); LVMH Prize for young designers; International Woolmark Prize.
- ☐ **SEW-13.6 Skills competitions**: WorldSkills includes **Fashion Technology** (pattern making, cutting and sewing under time limits); India selects competitors through **IndiaSkills** (Skill India, NSDC). Latest medal lists are on worldskills.org and skillindia.gov.in.
- ☐ **SEW-13.7 Museums**: Victoria and Albert Museum (London), The Met Costume Institute (New York), Kyoto Costume Institute, Calico Museum of Textiles (Ahmedabad), National Museum (New Delhi) costume galleries.

# PART 9 — IDEAS: PHILOSOPHY, PSYCHOLOGY AND SOCIETY

## SEW-14 Identity, sustainability and livelihood

- ☐ **SEW-14.1 Identity**: clothes say who we are (region, religion, class, profession); tailoring lets the body, not the size chart, decide.
- ☐ **SEW-14.2 Sustainability and textile waste**: production doubled 2000–2014 and under 1% is recycled into new clothes (SEW-00.05); repair, alteration and buying less are the strongest steps.
- ☐ **SEW-14.3 The economics of tailoring**: made-to-measure is cheap in India because labour is cheap; that is good for buyers and hard for tailors. A fair price is part of slow fashion.
- ☐ **SEW-14.4 Livelihood and empowerment**: stitching is among the most common home-based work for women in India; schemes give machines and training, but steady orders and fair rates matter more than the machine.
- ☐ **SEW-14.5 Psychology**: sewing as focus and flow, patience, problem-solving, and the pride of "I made this".
- ☐ **SEW-14.6 Philosophy**: Gandhi's khadi and the spinning wheel as self-rule (*swaraj*); the debate between handmade and machine-made, and who controls the tool (SEW-00.04).

# PART 10 — THE PRACTICAL GUIDE

## SEW-15 Start sewing

- ☐ **SEW-15.1 First machine**: a sturdy mechanical electric machine with straight, zigzag and a buttonhole is enough; check local service centres before buying; a well-kept old treadle is also good.
- ☐ **SEW-15.2 First 10 projects**: (1) sew straight lines on paper; (2) hand-sew a button and a hem; (3) cushion cover; (4) tote bag; (5) drawstring pyjama; (6) petticoat; (7) A-line skirt; (8) straight kurta; (9) kids' frock or simple top; (10) one alteration on your own clothes.
- ☐ **SEW-15.3 Safety**: fingers away from the needle; switch off before changing needle or foot; never sew over pins; keep shears closed and away from children; unplug the iron; take breaks for back and eyes.
- ☐ **SEW-15.4 Home studio**: good light from the left, a table at elbow height, an ironing board nearby, labelled boxes for notions, a scrap bin for upcycling.
- ☐ **SEW-15.5 Small tailoring business**: start with alterations and blouses; keep a measurement register and order book; price for time, not just cloth; learn about MSME (Udyam) registration and the PM Vishwakarma scheme; photograph your work for social media.
- ☐ **SEW-15.6 Learning**: ITI and polytechnic courses, NIFT short courses, Skill India, YouTube teachers, local tailoring classes; practise more than you watch.
