# PART 9 — HISTORY

> **About this Part.** The story of gardening in seven chapters, each a website page. The timeline (GRD-18) gives the dates.

## GRD-19 From the first farmers to city rooftops

> **Before you start — GRD-19.** Gardening's history is also a history of plants moving: chilli, potato, tomato and maize reached India only after 1498 with Portuguese and other traders, and India's mango, rice, sugarcane, cotton and black pepper travelled the other way. **See also:** COK (the history of food).

- ☐ **GRD-19.1 The first farmers**: the Neolithic revolution in many cradles (GRD-00.01); Mehrgarh and the Indus cities' crops.
- ☐ **GRD-19.2 Ancient gardens**: Egypt, Mesopotamia and the Hanging Gardens (location unknown), Persia's paradise gardens, the parks of Ashoka, Greek and Roman gardens, China's imperial parks.
- ☐ **GRD-19.3 Medieval gardens**: monastery herb and physic gardens; Islamic Spain; Chola and Vijayanagara temple gardens; Japanese Zen gardens.
- ☐ **GRD-19.4 Botanical gardens**: Pisa (1544) and Padua (1545) as the first university gardens; Kew (1759); Kolkata (1787); Peradeniya (Sri Lanka, 1821); Singapore Botanic Gardens (1859, UNESCO 2015). Today Botanic Gardens Conservation International (BGCI) counts more than 3,500 botanic gardens and arboreta worldwide (see bgci.org).
- ☐ **GRD-19.5 The plant hunters and empire**: collectors such as Joseph Hooker in Sikkim (1848 to 1850), Robert Fortune, who smuggled tea plants out of China for the East India Company (1848 to 1851), and Frank Kingdon-Ward in the eastern Himalaya; rubber seeds taken from Brazil to Kew (1876) and on to Asia. Gardens were also tools of empire, and the knowledge of local people was rarely credited.
- ☐ **GRD-19.6 The Green Revolution and after**: high-yield wheat and rice, fertiliser and irrigation from the 1960s (Norman Borlaug, M. S. Swaminathan) ended famine fears in India; later came soil fatigue, groundwater loss and the organic and natural-farming movements.
- ☐ **GRD-19.7 Gardening today**: urban and terrace gardening, community gardens, social-media gardeners, seed libraries, climate-wise gardening and the "grow your own" boom of the 2020 pandemic lockdowns.

## GRD-20 Gardeners, botanists and designers

> **Before you start — GRD-20.** Gardening's heroes include scientists, designers, writers and farmers who never had a title. This list balances India and the world.

### ☐ GRD-20.1 India

- ☐ **GRD-20.1f Kamala Pujari (died 2024)**: Odisha farmer who conserved hundreds of paddy varieties; Padma Shri 2019.

- ☐ **GRD-20.2c Gregor Mendel (1822 to 1884)**: discovered the laws of heredity with garden peas.
- ☐ **GRD-20.2d André Le Nôtre (1613 to 1700)**: designer of Versailles.

# PART 11 — IDEAS: PHILOSOPHY, PSYCHOLOGY AND SOCIETY

> **About this Part.** The philosophy and psychology angles of the brief. These are ideas to think with, presented fairly, with the evidence for each.

## GRD-22 Why gardening heals

> **Before you start — GRD-22.** The evidence is promising but still growing (GRD-00.06). Good studies compare gardeners with similar non-gardeners and measure outcomes over time.

- ☐ **GRD-22.1 The evidence**: meta-analysis of 22 studies (Soga and colleagues, 2017); allotment gardeners' wellbeing (Van den Berg and colleagues, 2010); community-garden trials in the USA (Litt and colleagues, *Lancet Planetary Health*, 2023: more fibre, more activity, less stress).
- ☐ **GRD-22.2 Theories**: biophilia (E. O. Wilson, 1984: an inborn love of living things), attention restoration (Kaplan and Kaplan), stress reduction (Ulrich).
- ☐ **GRD-22.3 Horticultural therapy and social prescribing**: the NHS in England prescribes gardening and nature activities; India's horticultural therapy is growing in hospitals and special schools.
- ☐ **GRD-22.4 Soil microbes and mood**: early research on the soil bacterium *Mycobacterium vaccae* in mice (Lowry and colleagues, 2007); a fascinating lead, not proof for humans.

## GRD-23 Gardens in thought, faith and society

- ☐ **GRD-23.1 Gardens in religion**: Eden and the Qur'anic *jannah* (garden); the Bodhi tree; Krishna's Vrindavan groves; tulsi worship; Sikh *gurdwara* gardens; Japanese Zen gardens as meditation.
- ☐ **GRD-23.2 Gardens in poetry and art**: Kalidasa's gardens in *Abhijnanashakuntalam*; Persian poets Hafez and Saadi (*Gulistan*, "The Rose Garden", 1258); Tagore's *The Gardener* (1913); Andrew Marvell's "The Garden"; Monet's Giverny; Frances Hodgson Burnett's *The Secret Garden* (1911).
- ☐ **GRD-23.3 The philosophy of patience and seasons**: Voltaire's "we must cultivate our garden" (*Candide*, 1759); the *Bhagavad Gita*'s work without attachment to fruit; gardening as a lesson that growth takes time.
- ☐ **GRD-23.4 Food security**: FAO's work on home gardens; India's nutrition gardens; Cuba's urban farms after 1990.
- ☐ **GRD-23.5 Climate and biodiversity**: gardens as habitat corridors, carbon in soil, the pollinator crisis, peat-free composts, native planting and the fight against invasives.
- ☐ **GRD-23.6 Justice and access**: who gets land, water and green space; the role of women farmers; credit for indigenous knowledge (Nagoya Protocol, 2010; India's Biological Diversity Act, 2002).

## GRD-24 A first garden in 30 days

- ☐ **GRD-24.1 Days 1 to 3, look**: watch where the sun falls and for how many hours; choose 3 to 5 crops you love to eat.
- ☐ **GRD-24.2 Days 4 to 7, prepare**: get containers with drainage holes or dig a small bed; make potting mix (soil, compost, coco peat).
- ☐ **GRD-24.3 Days 8 to 10, sow**: methi, coriander, spinach and radish directly; buy a few healthy seedlings (chilli, tomato) from a nursery.
- ☐ **GRD-24.4 Days 11 to 20, care**: water when the top 2 cm is dry; mulch; check leaves every day; start a compost bucket.
- ☐ **GRD-24.5 Days 21 to 30, harvest and note**: pick microgreens and methi; write what worked in a garden diary; plan the next sowing.

## GRD-26 Tools, budgets and gardening with children
