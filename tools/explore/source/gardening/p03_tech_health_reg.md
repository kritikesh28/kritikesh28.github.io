# PART 6 — TECHNIQUES

> **About this Part.** The gardener's skills, from the first seed to the saved seed. Each technique has a one-line "how" and a link to a fuller website page.

## GRD-13 Sowing and propagation

> **Before you start — GRD-13.** Plants make new plants in two ways: sexually, by seed (children differ from parents), and vegetatively, from a piece of the parent (clones that keep the variety true). Mangoes, roses and most fruit trees are propagated vegetatively for this reason.

- ☐ **GRD-13.1 Direct sowing**: seeds sown where they will grow (radish, carrot, coriander, beans, gourds); the rule of thumb is to sow at a depth about two to three times the seed's width.
- ☐ **GRD-13.2 Nursery and seed trays**: sow small seeds in trays or a nursery bed (tomato, chilli, brinjal, cabbage, flowers), then transplant.
- ☐ **GRD-13.3 Transplanting and hardening off**: move seedlings at four to six true leaves, in the evening, with a ball of soil; give them a few days of gradual sun first.
- ☐ **GRD-13.4 Seed treatment**: soaking hard seeds, scarification (nicking the coat), stratification (cold treatment for temperate seeds), and *beejamrut* or fungicide coatings.
- ☐ **GRD-13.5 Cuttings**: stem cuttings (rose, hibiscus, money plant, bougainvillea), leaf cuttings (succulents, snake plant), root cuttings; rooting hormone helps woody cuttings.
- ☐ **GRD-13.6 Layering**: rooting a branch while still on the parent; simple layering (jasmine), air layering or *gootee* (litchi, pomegranate, guava).
- ☐ **GRD-13.7 Grafting**: joining a shoot (scion) to a rooted plant (rootstock); mango's veneer and soft-wood grafting, apple's whip-and-tongue, cleft grafting.
- ☐ **GRD-13.8 Budding**: grafting a single bud; T-budding in roses and citrus, patch budding in ber.
- ☐ **GRD-13.9 Division**: splitting clumps of perennials, grasses and bulbs (lemongrass, aloe, spider plant).
- ☐ **GRD-13.10 Runners, suckers and offsets**: strawberry runners, banana suckers, pineapple crowns.
- ☐ **GRD-13.11 Tissue culture**: growing plants from tiny pieces in sterile jars; India produces tissue-culture bananas and orchids at scale; certified under the government's National Certification System for Tissue Culture Raised Plants.

## GRD-14 Care, training and planning

> **Before you start — GRD-14.** Good gardens are planned before they are planted: what grows next to what, where it grew last year, and what you will eat when.

- ☐ **GRD-14.1 Pruning**: removing dead, crossing or crowded branches; heading back (bushier) versus thinning (airier); prune most trees in their dormant or post-harvest season.
- ☐ **GRD-14.2 Pinching and deadheading**: pinching tips for bushy basil and marigold; removing spent flowers for more blooms.
- ☐ **GRD-14.3 Training, staking and trellising**: stakes for tomatoes, *pandal* (bamboo trellis) for gourds, espalier for fruit trees on walls.
- ☐ **GRD-14.4 Thinning**: removing extra seedlings so the rest have room (carrots, radish, beet).
- ☐ **GRD-14.5 Weeding**: by hand, hoe or mulch; some "weeds" are edible (bathua, chaulai) or medicinal.
- ☐ **GRD-14.6 Companion planting**: plants that help each other; marigold with tomato (traditional, with some evidence against root-knot nematodes), the "three sisters" (maize, beans, squash), basil near tomato. Many companion claims are folklore; test them.
- ☐ **GRD-14.7 Intercropping and mixed cropping**: two or more crops in one plot; India's traditional *baranaja* ("twelve grains") of Uttarakhand.
- ☐ **GRD-14.8 Crop rotation**: change the family grown in a bed each season (legumes, then leafy brassicas, then fruiting nightshades, then roots) to break pest cycles and balance nutrients.
- ☐ **GRD-14.9 Succession sowing**: sow small batches every two to three weeks (coriander, radish) for a steady harvest.
- ☐ **GRD-14.10 Harvesting**: pick greens young, okra and beans every two days, tomatoes at the colour break; harvest in the cool of the morning.
- ☐ **GRD-14.11 Seed saving**: save seed from open-pollinated, healthy plants; hybrids (F1) do not breed true. Community seed banks such as those of the Deccan Development Society and Navdanya keep heirloom varieties alive. India's Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act (2001) protects farmers' right to save and share seed.

# PART 7 — PLANT HEALTH

> **About this Part.** What goes wrong and how to respond, using the ladder of integrated pest management (IPM): prevent first, watch closely, act gently, and use stronger measures only when needed.

## GRD-15 Pests, diseases and integrated pest management

> **Before you start — GRD-15.** IPM is the approach recommended by the FAO and by India's Directorate of Plant Protection. Most pests have natural enemies: ladybirds eat aphids, spiders and birds eat caterpillars. Sprays that kill everything also kill the helpers. **Caution:** even organic sprays should be used carefully, away from bees in flower and out of children's and pets' reach.

### ☐ GRD-15.1 Common pests

- ☐ **GRD-15.1a Aphids**: tiny soft insects on new shoots; spray water, neem oil soap, encourage ladybirds.
- ☐ **GRD-15.1b Whiteflies and mealybugs**: sap-suckers that leave sticky honeydew and sooty mould; yellow sticky traps, neem oil.
- ☐ **GRD-15.1c Mites**: tiny spiders causing speckled, dusty leaves in dry heat; mist and wash leaves.
- ☐ **GRD-15.1d Caterpillars and borers**: fruit and shoot borer in brinjal, fruit borer in tomato; hand-pick, pheromone traps, *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt) sprays.
- ☐ **GRD-15.1e Fruit flies**: maggots in gourds and mangoes; bag fruits, methyl eugenol or cue-lure traps.
- ☐ **GRD-15.1f Snails and slugs**: monsoon pests; hand-pick at night, barriers.
- ☐ **GRD-15.1g Root-knot nematodes**: microscopic worms making galls on roots; rotation, marigold, neem cake.
- ☐ **GRD-15.1h Birds, squirrels, monkeys and rats**: nets and cages; share a little.

### ☐ GRD-15.2 Common diseases

- ☐ **GRD-15.2a Damping-off**: seedlings collapse at the soil line; sterile mix, less water, good air.
- ☐ **GRD-15.2b Powdery mildew**: white powder on leaves of gourds and roses; airflow, diluted milk or baking-soda sprays (evidence mixed), sulphur.
- ☐ **GRD-15.2c Leaf spots and blights**: early and late blight of tomato and potato; late blight caused the Irish famine of 1845 to 1849.
- ☐ **GRD-15.2d Wilts and root rots**: *Fusarium*, bacterial wilt; resistant varieties, *Trichoderma* biocontrol, good drainage.
- ☐ **GRD-15.2e Viruses**: leaf curl in chilli and tomato, yellow vein mosaic in okra, spread by whiteflies; pull out infected plants, control the insect.

### ☐ GRD-15.3 The IPM ladder

- ☐ **GRD-15.3a Prevent**: healthy soil, resistant varieties, rotation, spacing, clean tools.
- ☐ **GRD-15.3b Monitor**: check leaves weekly, especially undersides; use sticky and pheromone traps.
- ☐ **GRD-15.3c Physical and cultural control**: hand-picking, pruning, nets, water jets.
- ☐ **GRD-15.3d Biological control**: ladybirds, lacewings, *Trichogramma* wasps, Bt, *Trichoderma*.
- ☐ **GRD-15.3e Botanical sprays**: neem (azadirachtin), garlic-chilli sprays; still use with care.
- ☐ **GRD-15.3f Chemical control as a last resort**: only registered products from the Central Insecticides Board list, at the label dose.

## GRD-16 Nutrient deficiencies (symptoms)

> **Before you start — GRD-16.** Where the yellowing appears tells you a lot. Nutrients that move within the plant (N, P, K, Mg) show first on **old, lower leaves**; nutrients that do not move (Fe, Ca, B, S) show first on **young, upper leaves**. Wrong pH can lock nutrients away even when they are present.

- ☐ **GRD-16.1 Nitrogen (N)**: old leaves turn evenly pale yellow; slow growth. Fix: compost, manure, legumes.
- ☐ **GRD-16.2 Phosphorus (P)**: dull, dark or purplish leaves, poor roots and flowering. Fix: bone meal, rock phosphate, compost.
- ☐ **GRD-16.3 Potassium (K)**: scorched, brown leaf edges on old leaves; weak stems. Fix: wood ash, banana-peel compost, potash.
- ☐ **GRD-16.4 Magnesium (Mg)**: yellow between veins on old leaves, veins stay green. Fix: Epsom salt (magnesium sulphate) in small doses.
- ☐ **GRD-16.5 Iron (Fe)**: yellow between veins on young leaves; common in alkaline soil. Fix: lower pH, chelated iron.
- ☐ **GRD-16.6 Calcium (Ca)**: blossom-end rot in tomatoes (black base of fruit), tip burn; usually caused by uneven watering. Fix: steady watering, lime or gypsum if soil is low.
- ☐ **GRD-16.7 Boron (B)**: hollow stems in cauliflower, cracked fruit, deformed tips. Fix: a pinch of borax, carefully; too much is toxic.
- ☐ **GRD-16.8 Zinc (Zn)**: small, narrow leaves (little leaf); India's most widespread micronutrient deficiency in soils. Fix: zinc sulphate as advised by a soil test.
