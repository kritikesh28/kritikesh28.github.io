# PART 2 — KINDS OF GARDENS

> **About this Part.** Every kind of garden in the brief, sorted by what it grows, where it grows and how. A garden often belongs to several kinds at once (an organic balcony herb garden), and the website tags each one.

## GRD-02 Food gardens

> **Before you start — GRD-02.** Food gardening is the oldest kind. In India it is the *kitchen garden* or *poshan vatika* (nutrition garden), promoted by the government's POSHAN Abhiyaan and by Krishi Vigyan Kendras. **See also:** COK (Cooking), GRD-09 (seasons).

- ☐ **GRD-02.1 Kitchen and vegetable garden**: greens, roots, gourds and fruiting vegetables grown near the home; the classic Indian backyard *bari* and the English allotment.
- ☐ **GRD-02.2 Nutrition garden (poshan vatika)**: a planned plot with greens, a pulse, a fruit tree such as moringa or papaya, and a root crop, so a family gets vitamins A and C and iron through the year.
- ☐ **GRD-02.3 Fruit garden and orchard**: trees such as mango, guava, citrus, pomegranate and amla; temperate apples, pears and stone fruit in the hills; dwarf and grafted trees for small spaces.
- ☐ **GRD-02.4 Herb garden**: culinary herbs (coriander, mint, basil, curry leaf, ajwain, lemongrass, rosemary, thyme).
- ☐ **GRD-02.5 Medicinal garden**: plants of Ayurveda, Unani, Siddha and folk medicine (tulsi, ashwagandha, giloy, aloe, brahmi, neem); the National Medicinal Plants Board promotes home herbal gardens. *Caution:* growing a plant is not the same as safe dosing; ask a qualified practitioner.
- ☐ **GRD-02.6 Spice garden**: chilli, turmeric, ginger, black pepper on a support tree, cardamom in shade (Kerala and Karnataka hills).
- ☐ **GRD-02.7 Microgreens and sprouts**: seedlings harvested at 7 to 14 days; the fastest food garden on a windowsill.
- ☐ **GRD-02.8 Mushroom growing**: oyster and button mushrooms on straw or compost in a dark room; strictly speaking fungi, not plants.

## GRD-03 Ornamental and special-purpose gardens

> **Before you start — GRD-03.** Ornamental gardens are grown for beauty, scent, shade or meaning. Special-purpose gardens serve wildlife, water, health or learning.

### ☐ GRD-03.1 Beauty and design

- ☐ **GRD-03.1a Flower garden**: annual beds (marigold, zinnia, petunia), perennial borders, cutting gardens; India's winter flower season in the plains runs October to March.
- ☐ **GRD-03.1b Rose garden (rosarium)**: the Zakir Hussain Rose Garden in Chandigarh (1967) is among Asia's largest.
- ☐ **GRD-03.1c Ornamental foliage and shrub garden**: crotons, coleus, hibiscus, bougainvillea, ixora.
- ☐ **GRD-03.1d Lawn and turf**: grass for play and calm; in India doob grass (*Cynodon dactylon*) and its improved forms; a thirsty choice in dry regions.
- ☐ **GRD-03.1e Bonsai**: miniature trees trained in shallow pots by pruning and wiring; Chinese *penjing* came first, Japan refined it into bonsai.
- ☐ **GRD-03.1f Succulents and cacti**: water-storing plants; easy in sunny, dry spots and the first plants of many indoor gardeners.
- ☐ **GRD-03.1g Topiary and hedges**: shrubs clipped into shapes; Roman in origin, revived in Renaissance and English gardens.
- ☐ **GRD-03.1h Rock garden**: alpine and dry plants among stones; also the name of Nek Chand's sculpture garden in Chandigarh (GRD-20).

### ☐ GRD-03.2 Water, glass and moss

- ☐ **GRD-03.2a Aquatic and water garden**: ponds and tubs with lotus, water lily, water hyacinth (invasive in India: never release into streams), and fish that eat mosquito larvae.
- ☐ **GRD-03.2b Terrarium**: a garden in a glass jar; the closed Wardian case (1830s) carried tea and rubber plants across oceans (GRD-19).
- ☐ **GRD-03.2c Kokedama**: a Japanese "moss ball" with a plant's roots wrapped in soil and moss, hung on a string.
- ☐ **GRD-03.2d Moss and fern garden**: shade gardens in humid places.

### ☐ GRD-03.3 Gardens with a purpose

- ☐ **GRD-03.3a Wildlife and pollinator garden**: native flowers, water and shelter for bees, butterflies and birds; host plants such as curry leaf and citrus for swallowtail caterpillars.
- ☐ **GRD-03.3b Rain garden**: a shallow planted dip that catches roof and road run-off so it soaks in instead of flooding drains.
- ☐ **GRD-03.3c Xeriscape and desert garden**: water-wise design with native and drought-tolerant plants; natural in Rajasthan (khejri, rohida, ber, kair, aak).
- ☐ **GRD-03.3d Therapeutic and sensory garden**: gardens in hospitals and care homes designed for healing; scent, touch and wheelchair-height beds (GRD-22).
- ☐ **GRD-03.3e School garden**: learning by growing; India's school nutrition gardens under PM POSHAN.
- ☐ **GRD-03.3f Memorial and sacred garden**: temple gardens, *nakshatra vanas*, memorial groves and cemetery gardens (GRD-05).
- ☐ **GRD-03.3g Botanical garden and arboretum**: living collections for science and conservation (GRD-19.4).

## GRD-04 Gardens by space and by method

> **Before you start — GRD-04.** The same tomato can be grown in a field, a pot, a pipe of water or a wall pocket. This section sorts gardens by where they are and by the system used.

### ☐ GRD-04.1 By space

- ☐ **GRD-04.1a Backyard and front garden**: the classic home garden.
- ☐ **GRD-04.1b Balcony and terrace garden**: containers, grow bags and raised beds on roofs; check load-bearing and waterproofing first.
- ☐ **GRD-04.1c Container and pot garden**: clay, plastic, fabric grow bags, recycled tins; drainage holes are non-negotiable.
- ☐ **GRD-04.1d Indoor plants**: money plant (pothos), snake plant, ZZ plant, peace lily, spider plant; needs bright indirect light. The famous NASA Clean Air Study (Wolverton, 1989) was done in sealed chambers; later reviews (Cummings and Waring, 2019) found that houseplants do little to clean air in real homes compared with ventilation. Grow them for joy, not as air purifiers.
- ☐ **GRD-04.1e Vertical garden and green wall**: pockets, panels or trellises on walls; Patrick Blanc's *mur végétal* made it famous.
- ☐ **GRD-04.1f Rooftop and urban farming**: city rooftops, Mumbai's and Bengaluru's terrace farming communities; Singapore and New York rooftop farms.
- ☐ **GRD-04.1g Community garden and allotment**: shared plots; Britain's allotments, Cuba's *organopónicos*, and city community gardens in Indian metros.

### ☐ GRD-04.2 By method

- ☐ **GRD-04.2a Organic gardening**: no synthetic pesticides or fertilisers; India's standard is the NPOP, and Sikkim was declared a fully organic state in 2016.
- ☐ **GRD-04.2b Natural farming**: low-input methods such as Masanobu Fukuoka's "do-nothing" farming (*The One-Straw Revolution*, 1975) and Subhash Palekar's zero-budget natural farming with *jeevamrut*; India's National Mission on Natural Farming (2024). Yield claims are debated; see GRD-E.
- ☐ **GRD-04.2c Permaculture**: "permanent agriculture" designed on natural ecosystems, coined by Bill Mollison and David Holmgren (1978); guilds, zones and food forests.
- ☐ **GRD-04.2d Hydroponics**: growing in nutrient-rich water instead of soil (NFT, deep water culture, wick systems); popular for lettuce and herbs.
- ☐ **GRD-04.2e Aquaponics**: fish and plants in one loop: fish waste feeds plants and plants clean water for the fish; the Aztec *chinampas* are an ancient cousin.
- ☐ **GRD-04.2f Square-foot and raised-bed gardening**: Mel Bartholomew's grid method (1981) for small, intensive beds.
- ☐ **GRD-04.2g No-dig gardening**: mulch and compost on top instead of digging; championed by Charles Dowding (UK) and Ruth Stout's deep-mulch method (US).
- ☐ **GRD-04.2h Biodynamic gardening**: Rudolf Steiner's 1924 lectures, with lunar calendars and preparations; popular with some growers but its special preparations lack scientific support.
- ☐ **GRD-04.2i Polyhouse and protected cultivation**: greenhouses, shade nets and polytunnels; widely subsidised by India's horticulture missions.

# PART 3 — GARDEN TRADITIONS OF INDIA AND THE WORLD

> **About this Part.** How cultures have shaped gardens, starting with India. Each tradition gets its idea, its look and its famous examples. Most examples can be visited; check timings locally.

## GRD-05 Garden traditions of India

> **Before you start — GRD-05.** India's gardens are older and more varied than the Mughal gardens alone: there are sacred groves, temple flower gardens, palace gardens, homegardens and colonial botanical gardens. Sanskrit texts such as the *Vrikshayurveda* ("science of plant life") of Surapala (about 10th century CE) give advice on sowing, watering and plant care. **See also:** GRD-00.04.

- ☐ **GRD-05.1 Vrikshayurveda**: Sanskrit treatises on tree care, including Surapala's text and chapters in Varahamihira's *Brihat Samhita* (6th century CE); they describe *kunapajala*, a fermented liquid manure still tested by organic farmers today.
- ☐ **GRD-05.2 Sacred groves**: patches of forest protected by local deities and custom: *devrai* in Maharashtra, *kavu* in Kerala, *orans* in Rajasthan, *sarna* in Jharkhand, *law kyntang* in Meghalaya (Mawphlang). India has many thousands; they are biodiversity refuges.
- ☐ **GRD-05.3 Nakshatra and Navagraha vanas**: gardens planting the tree linked with each of the 27 lunar mansions or the nine planets (for example amla, peepal, khair); a living tradition promoted by forest departments and temples.
- ☐ **GRD-05.4 Temple flower gardens (nandavanam)**: gardens that grow jasmine, lotus, hibiscus, tulsi and bel for daily worship, especially in South India.
- ☐ **GRD-05.5 Buddhist and Jain gardens**: the Bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya, monastery groves; Ashoka's edicts record planting banyans and mango groves along roads for shade (3rd century BCE).
- ☐ **GRD-05.6 Mughal gardens**: char bagh, terraces and water; Ram Bagh (Agra), Humayun's Tomb (Delhi), Shalimar and Nishat Bagh (Srinagar), Pinjore (Yadavindra) Gardens (Haryana), the Taj Mahal.
- ☐ **GRD-05.7 Rajput gardens**: palace and lake gardens in Rajasthan, such as Saheliyon-ki-Bari in Udaipur (18th century) and the Kesar Kyari saffron garden in Amber's Maota lake; they blend Mughal water and local courtyards.
- ☐ **GRD-05.8 Kerala homegardens**: multi-storey food forests around houses, with coconut and arecanut at the top, pepper and banana in the middle, and tubers and herbs below; studied by scientists as a model of sustainable agroforestry.
- ☐ **GRD-05.9 Courtyard and tulsi gardens**: the *aangan* with its tulsi platform (*tulsi chaura*), found across Indian homes.
- ☐ **GRD-05.10 Colonial and botanical gardens**: the Indian Botanic Garden at Howrah (1787), Lalbagh in Bengaluru (begun by Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan, glasshouse 1889), Ooty Botanical Garden (1848); hill-station gardens in Darjeeling and Shimla.
- ☐ **GRD-05.11 Modern Indian gardens**: Amrit Udyan at Rashtrapati Bhavan (renamed from Mughal Gardens in January 2023), Nek Chand's Rock Garden (Chandigarh), Lodhi Garden (Delhi), Brindavan Gardens (Mysuru), the Indira Gandhi Memorial Tulip Garden (Srinagar, 2007).

## GRD-06 Garden traditions of the world

> **Before you start — GRD-06.** Each tradition answers the same question differently: should a garden tame nature, copy it or improve on it? Formal gardens use straight lines; landscape gardens hide them.

- ☐ **GRD-06.1 Ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia**: walled gardens with pools; the Hanging Gardens of Babylon, one of the Seven Wonders, have never been found (Stephanie Dalley argues they were at Nineveh, built by Sennacherib).
- ☐ **GRD-06.2 Persian paradise garden**: the walled *bagh* with crossing channels; Pasargadae (6th century BCE) is the oldest known; nine Persian gardens are a UNESCO World Heritage Site (2011).
- ☐ **GRD-06.3 Islamic and Spanish Moorish gardens**: courtyard gardens with water at the centre; the Alhambra and Generalife in Granada.
- ☐ **GRD-06.4 Chinese scholar gardens**: rocks, water, pavilions and borrowed views designed for poetry and thought; the Classical Gardens of Suzhou (UNESCO).
- ☐ **GRD-06.5 Japanese gardens**: *karesansui* dry gardens of raked gravel and stone (Ryōan-ji, Kyoto); tea gardens (*roji*) with stepping stones; stroll gardens around ponds (Katsura). Ideas: *wabi-sabi*, borrowed scenery (*shakkei*).
- ☐ **GRD-06.6 Roman gardens**: peristyle courtyards, topiary and frescoes, preserved at Pompeii.
- ☐ **GRD-06.7 Medieval monastery gardens**: herb, physic and vegetable beds; the Plan of St Gall (about 820 CE).
- ☐ **GRD-06.8 Italian Renaissance gardens**: terraces, fountains and statues on hillsides; Villa d'Este at Tivoli.
- ☐ **GRD-06.9 French formal gardens**: grand geometry and parterres; André Le Nôtre's Versailles (from 1661).
- ☐ **GRD-06.10 Dutch gardens**: small, neat and full of bulbs; the tulip mania of 1636 to 1637 (whose scale historians now debate); Keukenhof today.
- ☐ **GRD-06.11 English landscape gardens**: "nature improved": rolling lawns, lakes and clumps of trees by William Kent and Lancelot "Capability" Brown (Stowe, Blenheim).
- ☐ **GRD-06.12 English cottage gardens**: crowded, informal beds of flowers, herbs and vegetables; refined by Gertrude Jekyll.
- ☐ **GRD-06.13 African gardens**: homestead gardens, Ethiopian *enset* gardens, Kirstenbosch in Cape Town (1913) for South Africa's unique fynbos flora.
- ☐ **GRD-06.14 Latin American gardens**: Aztec *chinampas* (floating gardens), Roberto Burle Marx's modernist tropical gardens in Brazil.
- ☐ **GRD-06.15 Modern and naturalistic gardens**: the New Perennial movement of Piet Oudolf (High Line, New York, 2009); Singapore's Gardens by the Bay (2012) and its "city in nature" plan.
