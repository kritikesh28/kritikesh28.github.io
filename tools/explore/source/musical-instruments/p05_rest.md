# PART 7 — THE CRAFT: ACOUSTICS, MAKING, CARE AND TUNING

> **About this Part.** How instruments work as physics, how they are made, and how to look after and tune them. **See also:** TEC (audio technology), the Curriculum Index (SNT: sound and waves).

## INS-26 The physics of sound in instruments

> **Before you start — INS-26.** Every instrument has three parts: an **exciter** (bow, breath, finger, stick), a **vibrator** (string, reed, lips, skin, solid body) and a **resonator** (body, tube, gourd, room). Change any of them and the sound changes.

- ☐ **INS-26.1 Frequency, pitch and the octave**: doubling the frequency gives the octave (Sa to the upper Sa); humans hear roughly 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz, less with age.
- ☐ **INS-26.2 Strings**: Mersenne's laws (INS-00.02); harmonics touched lightly at half, third or quarter length; the "stick-slip" motion of the bow (Hermann von Helmholtz, 1860s).
- ☐ **INS-26.3 Air columns**: open tubes (flute) give all harmonics; tubes closed at one end (clarinet) mostly odd ones; conical bores (oboe, saxophone, shehnai) behave like open tubes; finger holes and valves change the effective length.
- ☐ **INS-26.4 Membranes and plates**: drum skins and gongs vibrate in complex patterns (Chladni figures, shown with sand by Ernst Chladni, 1787); C. V. Raman's work on the harmonic Indian drums (1920) and on the veena and tanpura bridge.
- ☐ **INS-26.5 Resonance and the body**: soundboards, gourds and shells amplify; sympathetic strings resonate by themselves (INS-00.05).
- ☐ **INS-26.6 Timbre**: overtone spectrum plus envelope (INS-00.03); formants explain why a sarangi sounds vocal.
- ☐ **INS-26.7 Tuning systems**: just intonation (pure ratios), Pythagorean tuning, **equal temperament** (12 equal semitones, standard in Western music since the 19th century), and the Indian *shruti* (22 microtonal positions in the old texts); the tanpura and harmonium debate (INS-21.5).
- ☐ **INS-26.8 Concert pitch**: A = 440 Hz was agreed at a 1939 London conference and set as ISO 16 in 1975; Baroque groups often use 415 Hz; Indian music uses a movable Sa chosen to suit the singer or instrument.
- ☐ **INS-26.9 Room acoustics**: reverberation time; why the same instrument sounds different in a hall, a temple courtyard and a studio (MUS-20.7).

## INS-27 Making instruments: luthiery and materials

> **Before you start — INS-27.** Instrument making is called **luthiery** for strings (from "lute") and has its own crafts for drums, winds and brass. In India most makers learn in family workshops. **See also:** INS-23 (makers).

### ☐ INS-27.1 Materials

- ☐ **Woods**: spruce (soundboards: light and stiff), maple (violin backs), rosewood and ebony (fingerboards, backs), mahogany, cedar; Indian **tun** (red cedar, sitars and sarangis), **teak** (sarods), **jackfruit** (veena, mridangam), **sheesham** (tabla, khartal), **bamboo** (flutes); **pernambuco** (the best bows, now endangered).
- ☐ **Gourds and shells**: the *tumba* gourds of sitars and tanpuras grown around **Pandharpur** (Maharashtra), coconut shells (ravanhatha, pena), calabash (kora, balafon).
- ☐ **Skins and gut**: goat, sheep, buffalo and cow skins for drums; gut strings (sheep intestine) for old violins and sarangis; ethical and caste questions attach to skin work (INS-23.6).
- ☐ **Metals**: brass, bronze and bell metal (kansa, about 78 per cent copper and 22 per cent tin in many Indian traditions), steel strings, silver flutes, cymbal alloys (B20 bronze).
- ☐ **Synthetics**: nylon (guitar strings since the 1940s, Albert Augustine with Segovia), plastic drumheads (Remo, 1957), carbon fibre (cellos and bows), 3D-printed instruments.

### ☐ INS-27.2 Making by family

- ☐ **Bowed and plucked strings**: carving the top and back, bending the sides, setting the neck, varnish (the "Stradivari varnish" myth); the sitar's gourd joined to a tun neck, frets tied with gut, the jawari shaped by hand (INS-00.05).
- ☐ **Drums**: shell turning or carving, skin preparation, lacing (*baddhi*), the multiple-layer syahi of the tabla applied in many thin coats and polished with a stone.
- ☐ **Wind**: bamboo selection and heat-drilling of bansuri holes; reed making for oboe, shehnai (from palm leaf or *pala* grass) and nadaswaram (from *naanal* reed); brass bending and valve fitting.
- ☐ **Clay and metal idiophones**: the ghatam's clay mixed with metal filings and fired; gongs hammered and tuned; bells cast by the lost-wax or sand method.
- ☐ **Craft clusters and GI tags**: Cremona, Mirecourt, Mittenwald (violins); Paracho (guitars); Miraj (sitar, tanpura), Thanjavur and Bobbili (veena), Manamadurai (ghatam), Pilibhit (bansuri), Kolkata and Varanasi (sitar, tabla).

## INS-28 Care, repair and tuning

> **Before you start — INS-28.** Most damage to instruments comes from heat, damp and dryness. Wood and skin move with the weather.

- ☐ **INS-28.1 Climate**: keep wooden instruments at moderate humidity (roughly 40–60 per cent) and away from direct sun, heaters and car boots; monsoon care for tabla and sitar (silica gel, a cloth cover, never sealed plastic in damp).
- ☐ **INS-28.2 Strings and bows**: change strings regularly; wipe off rosin; have bows re-haired; loosen the bow after playing.
- ☐ **INS-28.3 Drums**: tune tabla with a hammer on the *gatta* (wooden blocks) and the rim; keep the syahi dry; mridangam left head paste removed after playing.
- ☐ **INS-28.4 Winds and brass**: dry and swab after playing; reeds stored flat; valve oil; bansuri oiled lightly to prevent cracks.
- ☐ **INS-28.5 Pianos and harmoniums**: tune a piano once or twice a year; harmonium reeds kept dust-free; old ivory keys are legal to keep but trade rules apply (INS-33.5).
- ☐ **INS-28.6 Tuning tools**: tuning fork, electronic tuners and apps, the tanpura or electronic shruti as reference, and the ear above all.
- ☐ **INS-28.7 Repair and the repairer's craft**: when to visit a luthier; the honest value of an old instrument.

# PART 8 — IDEAS: INSTRUMENTS, MIND, PHILOSOPHY AND SOCIETY

> **About this Part.** Why humans give instruments sacred, social and personal meaning, and what learning an instrument does to us. Claims come with their evidence; open questions are flagged.

## INS-29 Instruments in mind and society

> **Before you start — INS-29.** An instrument is never just a tool. It can be a god's emblem, a nation's symbol, a family's inheritance or a person's voice. **See also:** MUS-22, BOK-20.

### ☐ INS-29.1 Sacred instruments

- ☐ **Indian gods and their instruments**: Saraswati's veena, Krishna's flute, Shiva's damaru, Narada's mahati veena, Nandi's mridangam; the shankh and ghanta in every Hindu temple; the nadaswaram as *mangala vadya* (auspicious instrument).
- ☐ **World**: the church organ and bells; the shofar; Tibetan dungchen and conch; Sufi ney and daf; Shona mbira that calls ancestral spirits; Aboriginal yidaki; Japanese temple bells.
- ☐ **Nada brahma**: the idea that sound is the root of the universe (MUS-22.1), and of instruments as forms of prayer.

### ☐ INS-29.2 Identity, status and power

- ☐ **National instruments**: the harp of Ireland (on coins and passports), the kora, the dombra, the valiha, the steelpan; India has no single national instrument, and that fits its plurality.
- ☐ **Royal and court instruments**: the *naubat* and *naqqar khana* at palace and shrine gates; drums as signs of kingship in Africa and India.
- ☐ **Caste, community and inheritance**: hereditary musician communities (Manganiyar, Langa, Mirasi, Isai Vellalar, griots) and the low status once given to them despite their art; who makes the instrument and who gets the fame (INS-23.6).
- ☐ **Gender**: instruments once kept from women (tabla, nadaswaram, drums in many cultures) and the women who broke through: Annapurna Devi, N. Rajam, Anuradha Pal, Evelyn Glennie.
- ☐ **Colonialism and exchange**: the violin, harmonium, clarinet and brass band absorbed into Indian music; Indian instruments in the Beatles era (George Harrison's sitar on "Norwegian Wood", 1965).

### ☐ INS-29.3 Healing and well-being

- ☐ **Music therapy with instruments**: drumming circles, the didgeridoo for sleep apnoea (a small *BMJ* trial, 2006), singing bowls (evidence is limited); flag claims that go beyond the evidence.
- ☐ **Instruments and disability**: adapted instruments, Evelyn Glennie's "listening with the whole body", accessible digital instruments.

### ☐ INS-29.4 Learning an instrument and the brain

- ☐ **What studies show**: musicians' brains differ in areas for hearing, movement and the link between the hemispheres (for example, Schlaug and colleagues, 1995); training improves listening and fine motor skill.
- ☐ **What is debated**: big "far transfer" claims (that music lessons raise IQ or school marks) are weak in careful meta-analyses (Sala and Gobet, 2017 and 2020).
- ☐ **What is surely true**: playing brings joy, discipline, community and a lifelong skill.

### ☐ INS-29.5 Philosophy of the instrument

- ☐ **The instrument as body**: players speak of an instrument becoming part of the body; the Indian ideal of the instrument "singing" (*gayaki ang*).
- ☐ **Authenticity**: period instruments versus modern; the electric versus acoustic debate (Bob Dylan "goes electric", Newport, 1965).
- ☐ **Machines and humans**: is a drum machine or an AI model an instrument, or a performer? Who is the artist?

# PART 10 — PRACTICAL GUIDE: CHOOSING, LEARNING, VISITING, BUYING

> **About this Part.** Hands-on help for the reader who wants to play, learn, see or buy. Prices and websites change: every page on the site will carry a "checked on" date.

## INS-33 The player's practical guide

### ☐ INS-33.1 Choosing a first instrument

- ☐ **By love**: start from the music you want to play (INS-00.07).
- ☐ **By ease of first sound**: easiest (keyboard, ukulele, recorder, harmonium, djembe, shaker); medium (guitar, bansuri, tabla, cajón); slower at first (violin, sarangi, oboe, French horn, veena).
- ☐ **By age**: under six, rhythm and singing games; from about six, recorder, keyboard, small violins; brass after adult front teeth.
- ☐ **By budget and space**: rent before you buy; second-hand from teachers; a good cheap instrument beats a bad expensive one.

### ☐ INS-33.2 Learning paths

- ☐ **Guru–shishya parampara**: the traditional Indian way, years with one teacher; the *ganda bandhan* thread ceremony.
- ☐ **Graded exams**: Gandharva Mahavidyalaya Mandal (since 1931), Prayag Sangit Samiti (Allahabad, 1926), Bhatkhande Sanskriti Vishwavidyalaya; Trinity College London and ABRSM grades for Western instruments and Rockschool for rock.
- ☐ **Universities and conservatories**: Banaras Hindu University, Indira Kala Sangit Vishwavidyalaya (Khairagarh), Kalakshetra (Chennai), KM Music Conservatory (Chennai, founded by A. R. Rahman); Juilliard, Royal Academy of Music, Berklee.
- ☐ **Online and self-learning**: video lessons, apps, tuners, metronomes; the risk of bad habits without feedback; record yourself.
- ☐ **Practice (riyaz)**: short daily practice, slow first, with a drone or metronome; play with others early.

### ☐ INS-33.3 Museums and collections

- ☐ **India**: Sangeet Natak Akademi's Gallery of Musical Instruments (Rabindra Bhawan, New Delhi); the **Indian Music Experience** museum (Bengaluru, opened 2019); National Museum, New Delhi (musical instruments gallery); Indian Museum (Kolkata); state museums of Rajasthan and Kerala. (Check opening times before a visit.)
- ☐ **World**: **Musical Instrument Museum** (Phoenix, Arizona, opened 2010; instruments from about 200 countries and territories); the **Metropolitan Museum of Art**'s musical instruments department (the oldest Cristofori piano); Musical Instruments Museum (MIM) Brussels; Musée de la Musique (Paris); Horniman Museum (London); Museo del Violino (Cremona); National Music Museum (Vermillion, South Dakota).
- ☐ **Online**: MIMO (Musical Instrument Museums Online, mimo-international.com) and Europeana; the Met's open-access collection images.

### ☐ INS-33.4 Buying well

- ☐ **New or second-hand**: try before you buy; take a teacher; check for cracks, warped necks, buzzing frets, tuning stability, leaky pads.
- ☐ **Indian classical instruments**: buy from established makers or with a teacher's help; ask about the gourd, wood, jawari and the after-sales "setting".
- ☐ **Online shopping**: read return policies; beware fake brand names; set-up costs extra.

### ☐ INS-33.5 Buying ethically: wildlife and wood rules

- ☐ **CITES** (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species, 1975) controls trade in endangered materials. **Brazilian rosewood** (*Dalbergia nigra*) has been on Appendix I since 1992; other *Dalbergia* rosewoods were listed in 2016–17, and finished musical instruments were exempted in 2019. **Pernambuco** (bow wood) is on Appendix II.
- ☐ **Ivory and tortoiseshell**: old piano keys, bow tips and sitar fittings may contain ivory; international trade is restricted, and travel with such instruments may need papers.
- ☐ **India's Wildlife (Protection) Act**: skins of protected animals (for example monitor lizard on old kanjiras) may not be used; makers now use goatskin or synthetic heads.
- ☐ **Fair pay for makers**: buy from makers directly when possible; credit them by name.
