# PART 1 — HOW INSTRUMENTS ARE CLASSIFIED

> **About this Part.** Before the families, the two great maps: India's ancient four-fold system and the Hornbostel–Sachs system used by museums worldwide. A third, China's eight materials, is added for comparison. Every instrument in this booklet carries both labels.

## INS-01 The Indian system: the four vadyas of the Natyashastra

> **Before you start — INS-01.** The *Natyashastra* (chapters 28 to 34 deal with music) is attributed to the sage Bharata and was compiled roughly between 200 BCE and 200 CE. Later texts such as Sharngadeva's *Sangita Ratnakara* (13th century) kept the four classes and described many more instruments. **See also:** MUS-01, MUS-22.1, INS-00.04.

- ☐ **INS-01.1 Tata vadya (stringed)**: any instrument whose sound comes from a stretched string, plucked, bowed or struck: veena, sitar, sarod, sarangi, santoor, violin, guitar.
- ☐ **INS-01.2 Sushira vadya (hollow, wind)**: instruments sounded by a column of air: bansuri, shehnai, nadaswaram, shankh (conch), harmonium (by later writers), trumpet.
- ☐ **INS-01.3 Avanaddha vadya (covered, membrane)**: drums with a skin stretched over a frame or shell: mridangam, pakhawaj, tabla, dholak, nagara, damaru, chenda. Bharata names the *pushkara* drums (mridanga, panava, dardura) as the most important.
- ☐ **INS-01.4 Ghana vadya (solid)**: instruments that vibrate as one solid body: cymbals (manjira, tala), bells (ghanta), gongs, the clay ghatam, the jal tarang, the wooden khartal and the morchang (jaw harp).
- ☐ **INS-01.5 Sharira vina, the body as instrument**: Indian texts call the human voice the *gatra vina* or *sharira vina* (the body-veena) and treat it as the model for all instruments (see INS-00.07).
- ☐ **INS-01.6 Later Indian refinements**: the *Sangita Ratnakara* and later treatises sub-divided each class (for example, drums by shape and stroke; strings by plucking or bowing). Modern Indian scholars such as **B. C. Deva** (*Musical Instruments of India*, 1978) mapped the old classes onto the Hornbostel–Sachs numbers.

## INS-02 The Hornbostel–Sachs system (1914, revised 2011)

> **Before you start — INS-02.** Erich Moritz von Hornbostel and Curt Sachs published *Systematik der Musikinstrumente* in the *Zeitschrift für Ethnologie* in 1914, building on Victor-Charles Mahillon's Brussels catalogue (1880). It uses decimal numbers, like a library: the more digits, the more exact the class. Sachs added electrophones in 1940. The MIMO (Musical Instrument Museums Online) consortium published a revised version in 2011. **See also:** INS-03 (how the systems match).

### ☐ INS-02.1 Class 1: Idiophones (the body itself vibrates)

- ☐ **11 Struck idiophones**: hit directly or indirectly: cymbals, gongs, bells, xylophones, the ghatam, castanets, the steelpan, the cajón.
- ☐ **12 Plucked idiophones (lamellophones)**: a tongue of metal or wood is plucked: the jaw harp (morchang), the mbira and kalimba, the music box.
- ☐ **13 Friction idiophones**: rubbed: the glass harmonica, the musical saw, the singing bowl when rubbed.
- ☐ **14 Blown idiophones**: the air makes a solid vibrate (rare): the aeolsklavier.

### ☐ INS-02.2 Class 2: Membranophones (a stretched skin vibrates)

- ☐ **21 Struck drums**: by hand or stick: tabla, mridangam, djembe, timpani, the drum kit. Sub-divided by shape (tubular, barrel, goblet, hourglass, kettle, frame).
- ☐ **22 Plucked drums**: a string fixed to the skin is plucked, so the skin sounds: the Baul *gopichand* (ektara), the *khamak* and the Rajasthani *bhapang*.
- ☐ **23 Friction drums**: a stick or cord rubbed against the skin: the Brazilian *cuíca*, the Spanish *zambomba*.
- ☐ **24 Singing membranes (kazoos)**: the voice makes a membrane buzz: the kazoo, the comb-and-paper.

### ☐ INS-02.3 Class 3: Chordophones (a string vibrates)

- ☐ **31 Simple chordophones (zithers)**: the strings run along a body that is just a string-bearer: santoor, qanun, koto, guzheng, the piano and harpsichord (board zithers with keyboards), the rudra veena and vichitra veena (stick zithers), the Malagasy valiha (tube zither).
- ☐ **32 Composite chordophones**: the string-bearer and resonator are joined and the resonator cannot be removed without destroying the instrument. Includes **lutes** (strings parallel to the body: sitar, sarod, Saraswati veena, guitar, violin, oud), **harps** (strings at right angles to the body) and **harp-lutes** (the kora).
- ☐ **How they are played**: a suffix shows plucking, bowing, striking or a keyboard, so the violin and the guitar share a class (necked box lutes, 321.322) but differ in suffix.

### ☐ INS-02.4 Class 4: Aerophones (air itself vibrates)

- ☐ **41 Free aerophones**: the air vibrates but is not enclosed: the bullroarer, the whip; **free reeds** such as the harmonium, harmonica, accordion, sheng and khaen belong here (412.13).
- ☐ **421 Edge-blown (flutes)**: air is split on an edge: end-blown (shakuhachi, ney), side-blown (bansuri, concert flute), duct or fipple flutes (recorder, algoza, tin whistle), vessel flutes (ocarina, xun) and panpipes.
- ☐ **422 Reed aerophones**: a reed chops the air: double reeds (shehnai, nadaswaram, oboe, bassoon, duduk), single reeds (clarinet, saxophone, the pungi), capped reeds (crumhorn) and bagpipes (mashak).
- ☐ **423 Trumpets (lip-vibrated)**: the player's lips buzz: trumpet, trombone, tuba, horn, didgeridoo, shankh, karnal, ransingha.

### ☐ INS-02.5 Class 5: Electrophones (electricity makes or shapes the sound)

- ☐ **Electro-acoustic**: an acoustic vibration picked up and amplified: the electric guitar, the electric piano (Rhodes).
- ☐ **Electro-mechanical**: a moving part makes an electric signal: the Hammond organ's tonewheels, the Telharmonium.
- ☐ **Electronic (analogue and digital)**: sound made by circuits or computers: theremin, synthesizers, drum machines, samplers, software instruments. The MIMO revision (2011) re-organised this class.

### ☐ INS-02.6 Using an H–S number

- ☐ **Example**: the bansuri is 421.121.12 (a side-blown flute, open, with finger holes); the tabla's right-hand drum is a struck, single-headed, tubular membranophone with a tuning paste. You need not memorise numbers: the website shows them on each instrument page so museums and scholars can find a match.

## INS-03 How the systems match (and a third from China)

> **Before you start — INS-03.** The Indian and the European systems agree on four of five classes. The key difference: India sorts by the material in action, while Hornbostel–Sachs adds a second level by how the instrument is built. China's *bayin* (eight sounds), recorded in the Zhou dynasty texts, sorts by the material it is made of.

| Indian (Natyashastra) | Hornbostel–Sachs | Chinese bayin (material) | Indian examples | World examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tata (stretched) | 3 Chordophones | Silk (strings were silk) | Veena, sitar, sarangi, santoor | Violin, guitar, koto, guqin |
| Sushira (hollow) | 4 Aerophones | Bamboo, gourd | Bansuri, shehnai, shankh | Flute, oboe, trumpet, sheng |
| Avanaddha (covered) | 2 Membranophones | Skin (leather) | Tabla, mridangam, dhol | Djembe, timpani, taiko |
| Ghana (solid) | 1 Idiophones | Metal, stone, clay, wood | Manjira, ghatam, jal tarang | Gong, xylophone, mbira |
| (none) | 5 Electrophones (1940) | (none) | Electronic tanpura, Octapad | Synthesizer, theremin |

- ☐ **INS-03.1 Where they disagree**: the harmonium is a sushira instrument in India but a "free aerophone" in H–S; the gopichand looks like a string instrument (tata) but H–S calls it a plucked drum because the skin, not the string, gives the tone. The piano is a struck zither (H–S), but many Indian writers treat it as a keyboard tata vadya.
- ☐ **INS-03.2 Classing by role, not sound**: orchestras and bands sort by role: strings, woodwinds, brass, percussion, keyboards. This is why the flute (often metal) is a "woodwind" and the saxophone (brass) is also a woodwind (it uses a reed).
- ☐ **INS-03.3 Other systems**: the Javanese gamelan groups instruments by musical function (melody, elaboration, punctuation); West African griot traditions group by caste and ceremony; the Indian *Naubat* or *Naqqar khana* courts grouped drums and shehnai as royal ensembles.
- ☐ **INS-03.4 Our choice**: each instrument in this booklet carries both the Indian vadya and the H–S class, so Indian learners and world museums read the same map.
