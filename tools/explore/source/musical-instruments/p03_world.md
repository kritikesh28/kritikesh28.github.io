# PART 3 — THE WORLD MAP OF INSTRUMENTS

> **About this Part.** The same instruments, now arranged by place: India state by state in depth, then each continent. Families are in Part 2; this Part answers "what do people play here?" The Instrument Register at the end (INS-19.4) gives the full record fields for 30 landmark instruments.

## INS-15 India, state by state

> **Before you start — INS-15.** India may have more living instrument traditions than any other country. Many belong to hereditary musician communities (Manganiyar, Langa, Bhopa, Mirasi, Isai Vellalar and others). Each state below lists its signature instruments; names in **bold** have their own entries in Part 2. Sources: B. C. Deva (1978), the Sangeet Natak Akademi collections and state cultural departments. **See also:** MUS-02 (folk music of India).

### ☐ INS-15.1 North and north-west India

- ☐ **Rajasthan**: **kamaicha**, **ravanhatha**, **morchang**, **algoza** (and the *satara* double flute of Barmer), **khartal**, **sarangi** (sindhi, gujratan and jogiya), **bhapang** (Alwar's Mewati Jogis), **nagara**, **chang**, **mashak**, **pungi** (Kalbeliya), dholak; the Manganiyar and Langa musicians of Jaisalmer, Barmer and Jodhpur; the Bhopas of Pabuji ki phad.
- ☐ **Punjab**: **dhol**, **tumbi**, **algoza**, **chimta**, sarangi, *dhadd* (small hourglass drum of the *dhadi* ballad singers), **rabab** and **taus** (Sikh kirtan heritage), *bugchu* and *kato* (folk clappers).
- ☐ **Haryana**: *been*, *dhol*, *nagada*, *dhapli* (frame drum of the *ragini* singers), *khartal*.
- ☐ **Jammu, Kashmir and Ladakh**: **santoor**, **rabab**, *tumbaknari* (clay goblet drum of Kashmiri wedding songs), *noet* (clay pot), *sarangi*; Ladakh's *daman* (drum) and *surna* (shawm).
- ☐ **Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand**: **karnal**, **ransingha**, *dhol-damau* (the drum pair of hill rituals), **hudka**, **mashakbeen**, *turhi*; the *Dhol Sagar* drumming tradition of Garhwal.
- ☐ **Uttar Pradesh and Delhi**: **shehnai** (Banaras), **tabla** (Delhi, Lucknow, Farukhabad and Banaras gharanas), **pakhawaj**, **sitar** and **sarod** (Delhi and Lucknow courts), **nagara** (Braj), dholak; makers in Delhi and Lucknow.
- ☐ **Bihar and Jharkhand**: *mandar* (Jharkhand clay drum), *nagara*, *dhak*, *banam*, bamboo flutes of the Santal, Oraon and Munda; *jhanj* cymbals.

### ☐ INS-15.2 East and north-east India

- ☐ **West Bengal**: **ektara (gopichand)**, **dotara**, **khamak** (the Baul singers), **dhak** (Durga Puja), **khol** (kirtan), **esraj** (Rabindra Sangeet), **sarod** (the Maihar tradition took deep root in Calcutta); Kolkata's sitar and tanpura makers.
- ☐ **Odisha**: **mardala** (Odissi), **gini** cymbals, **khol**, *ghanta*, *changu* (frame drum), bell-metal from Kantilo; temple ensembles of Puri.
- ☐ **Assam**: **dhol** (Bihu), **pepa** (buffalo-horn pipe of Bihu), **gogona** (bamboo jaw harp), **toka**, *taal* (cymbals), **khol** of Sattriya music, *bahi* (flute).
- ☐ **Manipur**: **pung**, **pena**, the large cymbals of *cymbal cholom*, the Nata Sankirtana tradition (UNESCO 2013).
- ☐ **Meghalaya, Nagaland, Mizoram, Arunachal, Tripura, Sikkim**: the Khasi *duitara* (lute) and *ksing* (drum); Naga log drums; the Mizo *khuang* (drum) and gongs; the Tripuri *sumui* (flute); Buddhist ritual horns and drums in Sikkim and Arunachal. (Each needs a local-source check before its full page.)

### ☐ INS-15.3 West and central India

- ☐ **Gujarat**: **dhol** (garba), *jodiya pava* (Kutch double flute), *jantar* (stick zither of Kutch), **ravanhatha**, *bhungal* (long brass horn of the Bhavai theatre), *manjira*.
- ☐ **Maharashtra**: **dhol-tasha** (Pune), **halgi**, *dimdi*, *tuntune* (one-string folk instrument of the tamasha and gondhal), *sambal* (drum pair of the Gondhal ritual), **dholki** (Lavani), **harmonium** (natya sangeet); Miraj sitar and tanpura makers (GI tag, 2024).
- ☐ **Goa**: **ghumot** (heritage instrument, 2019), *madlem* and *tasso*; Goan musicians who shaped Bombay film orchestras (violin, trumpet, saxophone).
- ☐ **Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh**: the *bana* fiddle of the Gond Pardhan bards; *tambura* of the Pandavani singers (Teejan Bai); *mandar*, *nagada*, *timki*; bamboo flutes of the Bhil.

### ☐ INS-15.4 South India

- ☐ **Tamil Nadu**: **nadaswaram** and **thavil** (temple music), **mridangam**, **Saraswati veena** (Thanjavur, GI), **ghatam** (Manamadurai), **kanjira**, **morsing**, **parai**, *urumi*, *udukkai* (hourglass drum), *thappu*; the ancient **yazh**.
- ☐ **Kerala**: **chenda**, **maddalam**, **edakka**, **timila**, **kombu**, **kuzhal**, *elathalam*, **mizhavu** (Koodiyattam); the *panchavadyam* and *chenda melam* of Thrissur Pooram.
- ☐ **Karnataka**: **Saraswati veena** (Mysore court tradition), nadaswaram, *dollu*, *chande* and *maddale* (Yakshagana), *kinnari*, *tamate* (frame drum).
- ☐ **Andhra Pradesh and Telangana**: **Bobbili veena** (GI), *dappu*, *kinnari* (Telangana bards), the *tambura* and *gummeta* drums of **Burra Katha** storytelling, *Oggu katha* drums.

## INS-16 Asia and the Middle East

> **Before you start — INS-16.** The great classical traditions of Asia each built their own orchestras: gamelan, gagaku, Chinese silk-and-bamboo, maqam ensembles. **See also:** MUS-04.

- ☐ **INS-16.1 South Asia beyond India**: Pakistan (sitar, sarangi, tabla, alghoza, *benju*, *suroz* fiddle of Balochistan, the harmonium of qawwali); Bangladesh (dotara, ektara, khol); Nepal (*sarangi* of the Gandharva, *madal*, *panche baja* wedding band); Sri Lanka (*geta bera*, *yak bera*, *thammattama*); Bhutan (*dramnyen* lute); Afghanistan (**rubab**, *dutar*, *zerbaghali*, *tula* flute).
- ☐ **INS-16.2 China**: guqin, guzheng, pipa, erhu, dizi, sheng, suona, yangqin, xun, bianzhong; the Chinese orchestra (formed in the 20th century); the eight-material *bayin* (INS-03).
- ☐ **INS-16.3 Japan**: koto, shamisen, shakuhachi, taiko, biwa, shō, hichiriki, ryūteki (the last three in *gagaku* court music, among the oldest orchestral music still played).
- ☐ **INS-16.4 Korea**: gayageum, geomungo (six strings, struck with a stick), haegeum, piri, daegeum, janggu, buk; *samul nori* percussion quartet (1978).
- ☐ **INS-16.5 South-East Asia**: gamelan (Indonesia), angklung, kulintang (Philippines), Thai *piphat* ensemble (ranat, khong wong, pi), Lao khaen, Vietnamese đàn bầu and đàn tranh, Burmese saung-gauk and the *hsaing waing* drum-circle ensemble, Cambodian *pinpeat*.
- ☐ **INS-16.6 Central Asia and Mongolia**: dombra, komuz, dutar, tanbur, doira (frame drum), morin khuur, and Tuvan throat singing with the *igil* fiddle.
- ☐ **INS-16.7 Middle East and Turkey**: oud, qanun, ney, darbuka, riq, daf, santur, tar, setar, kamancheh (Iran; UNESCO 2017 with Azerbaijan), bağlama, kemençe, zurna and davul, duduk (Armenia), shofar, mijwiz, rababa (Bedouin).

## INS-17 Europe

> **Before you start — INS-17.** Europe built the orchestra, the piano and the guitar, but it is also a continent of folk instruments. **See also:** MUS-05.

- ☐ **INS-17.1 The orchestra**: strings (violin, viola, cello, double bass), woodwinds (flute, oboe, clarinet, bassoon), brass (horn, trumpet, trombone, tuba), percussion (timpani and others), harp, keyboard. The modern symphony orchestra took shape between Haydn (1760s) and Berlioz and Wagner (19th century).
- ☐ **INS-17.2 British Isles**: harp (Ireland, Wales), uilleann pipes, Highland bagpipe, bodhrán, tin whistle, fiddle, concertina, brass bands of northern England.
- ☐ **INS-17.3 Iberia**: classical and flamenco guitar, castanets, cajón (in flamenco since the 1970s, via Paco de Lucía), gaita, Portuguese guitar, cavaquinho.
- ☐ **INS-17.4 Central and western Europe**: accordion, alphorn, zither, hurdy-gurdy, musette, organ, harpsichord, recorder; violin making at **Cremona** (UNESCO 2012), **Mittenwald** and **Mirecourt**.
- ☐ **INS-17.5 Nordic and Baltic**: hardanger fiddle, nyckelharpa, kantele, kanklės, the Sámi drum.
- ☐ **INS-17.6 Eastern Europe and the Balkans**: balalaika, domra, gusli, cimbalom, kobza and bandura (Ukraine), tambura (Balkans), gusle, kaval, gaida, tapan; Romanian nai.
- ☐ **INS-17.7 Mediterranean**: mandolin, zampogna, launeddas, bouzouki, lyra (Crete), tamburello.

## INS-18 The Americas and the Caribbean

> **Before you start — INS-18.** The Americas mix Indigenous, African and European traditions, which produced the banjo, the steelpan and the drum kit. **See also:** MUS-06.

- ☐ **INS-18.1 Indigenous instruments**: the Plains flute, powwow drum, rattles and water drums of North America; Andean quena, siku, charango (post-contact), bombo; Mesoamerican clay whistles and the *teponaztli* slit drum; Amazonian flutes and panpipes.
- ☐ **INS-18.2 USA and Canada**: banjo, fiddle, harmonica, steel-string and electric guitar, pedal steel, Dobro, drum kit, saxophone and trumpet in jazz, the Hammond organ in gospel, the Appalachian dulcimer.
- ☐ **INS-18.3 Mexico and Central America**: mariachi (violins, trumpets, vihuela, guitarrón), marimba (Guatemala, Chiapas), jarana (son jarocho).
- ☐ **INS-18.4 The Caribbean**: steelpan (Trinidad and Tobago), conga, bongo, timbales, güiro, claves, tres (Cuba), cuatro (Puerto Rico), the *tambora* and accordion of Dominican merengue.
- ☐ **INS-18.5 South America**: bandoneón (tango), berimbau (a musical bow of capoeira, Brazil), pandeiro, cuíca, surdo, cavaquinho, the Venezuelan cuatro and harp, the Colombian *gaita* flute and *tiple*, the Peruvian cajón; Venezuela's **El Sistema** youth orchestras (founded 1975 by José Antonio Abreu).

## INS-19 Africa and Oceania

> **Before you start — INS-19.** Africa is the homeland of many instrument ideas: the harp-lute, the lamellophone, the talking drum and the xylophone with gourds. Oceania gave the world the didgeridoo and the ukulele's island home. **See also:** MUS-07.

- ☐ **INS-19.1 West Africa**: kora, balafon, djembe, dùndún (talking drum), ngoni, xalam, akonting (Gambia, a banjo ancestor), goje (fiddle), shekere, udu; griots (jeli) as hereditary keepers.
- ☐ **INS-19.2 North and East Africa**: oud, darbuka, gimbri (Gnawa), imzad (Tuareg), krar, begena and masenqo (Ethiopia), the *nyatiti* lyre (Kenya), *adungu* harp and amadinda xylophone (Uganda).
- ☐ **INS-19.3 Central and Southern Africa**: mbira, likembe, timbila, marimba, the *uhadi* and other musical bows (South Africa), the vuvuzela; the valiha (Madagascar).
- ☐ **INS-19.4 Oceania**: didgeridoo (yidaki) and clapsticks (Aboriginal Australia), bullroarer, Māori *taonga pūoro* (kōauau flute, pūtōrino, pūkāea horn), Hawaiian *pahu*, *ipu* gourd drum, ʻukulele and steel guitar, the slit drums of Fiji (*lali*), Vanuatu and Papua New Guinea (*garamut*), and the PNG hourglass *kundu* drum (on the national emblem).
