## INS-21 Great inventions and journeys

> **Before you start — INS-21.** Some instruments changed the world's music. Here are their stories in brief; each becomes a full page.

- ☐ **INS-21.1 The veena's journey**: from the Vedic *vana* and the arched-harp vina of early sculpture, to the stick-zither *rudra veena* of medieval North India, to the fretted lute of the South (the Saraswati veena took its present form in Thanjavur around the 17th century, credited to the court of Raghunatha Nayak). One name, three kinds of instrument.
- ☐ **INS-21.2 The Cremona miracle**: Andrea Amati, then the Guarneri and Stradivari families, set the violin's shape for 400 years. Blind tests (Claudia Fritz and colleagues, 2012 and 2014) found that top soloists often could not tell Stradivaris from good new violins, and some preferred the new ones.
- ☐ **INS-21.3 The piano's rise**: Cristofori's hammer action; the Viennese and English pianos of Mozart and Beethoven; the iron frame; the piano as the centre of 19th-century homes; the upright in Indian homes and churches.
- ☐ **INS-21.4 Adolphe Sax and the saxophone**: designed in the 1840s to bridge woodwind and brass in military bands; nearly forgotten, then reborn in jazz; Kadri Gopalnath's Carnatic sax.
- ☐ **INS-21.5 The harmonium in India**: from French parlour to Indian floor; banned by All India Radio (1940–71) after critics, Rabindranath Tagore among them, argued it could not play Indian microtones; today indispensable.
- ☐ **INS-21.6 The tabla's birth**: legends credit Amir Khusrau; historians date the tabla to the 18th century, perhaps developed from the pakhawaj and the dholak in Delhi (Sudhar Khan and Siddhar Khan Dhadi are traditional founders of the Delhi gharana).
- ☐ **INS-21.7 The electric guitar**: from loudness for big bands to the voice of rock; the solid body; distortion as art.
- ☐ **INS-21.8 Synthesizers and drum machines**: from laboratories to bedrooms; the TR-808 was a commercial flop before it shaped hip-hop.
- ☐ **INS-21.9 Digital and AI instruments**: software instruments, sample libraries of Indian instruments, AI-generated sound, and debates about the future of hand-made instruments.
- ☐ **INS-21.10 Instruments lost and revived**: the yazh (reconstruction efforts in Tamil Nadu), the surbahar's decline, the baroque revival of the harpsichord and viols, endangered folk instruments of India and their revival through fairs and festivals (Rajasthan International Folk Festival, Jodhpur).

# PART 6 — AWARDS, COMPETITIONS AND HONOURS

> **About this Part.** The great instrument competitions, Grammy instrumental categories and honours for instrumentalists in India. Latest results were checked on 3 November 2026.

## INS-24 International instrument competitions

> **Before you start — INS-24.** Big competitions launch careers but also draw criticism for rewarding safe, perfect playing over personality. Many belong to the World Federation of International Music Competitions (WFIMC).

- ☐ **INS-24.1 International Chopin Piano Competition** (Warsaw, since 1927, every five years): only Chopin's music. Winners include Maurizio Pollini (1960), Martha Argerich (1965), Krystian Zimerman (1975), Seong-Jin Cho (2015), Bruce Liu (2021) and **Eric Lu** (USA, 19th competition, October 2025).
- ☐ **INS-24.2 International Tchaikovsky Competition** (Moscow, since 1958): piano, violin, cello, voice and more; the American **Van Cliburn** won the first piano prize at the height of the Cold War. The WFIMC expelled it in 2022 after Russia's invasion of Ukraine.
- ☐ **INS-24.3 Van Cliburn International Piano Competition** (Fort Worth, Texas, since 1962, every four years): Yunchan Lim (2022), **Aristo Sham** (Hong Kong, 2025).
- ☐ **INS-24.4 Queen Elisabeth Competition** (Brussels; began 1937 as the Eugène Ysaÿe Competition): rotates piano, violin, cello and voice. **Nikola Meeuwsen** (Netherlands) won piano in 2025; **Ettore Pagano** (Italy) won cello in 2026.
- ☐ **INS-24.5 Premio Paganini** (Genoa, violin, since 1954): the winner may play Paganini's own Guarneri violin, "Il Cannone".
- ☐ **INS-24.6 Other leading competitions**: Leeds International Piano Competition (since 1963), ARD International Music Competition (Munich), Menuhin Competition (young violinists), Sibelius Violin Competition (Helsinki), Indianapolis Violin, Long-Thibaud (Paris), Hamamatsu (piano, Japan), Michele Pittaluga (guitar, Alessandria), Guitar Foundation of America, the Herbie Hancock Institute of Jazz competition (rotating instruments).
- ☐ **INS-24.7 Percussion and band**: Percussive Arts Society Hall of Fame; drum corps (DCI, USA); the World Pipe Band Championships (Glasgow); Trinidad's Panorama steelband competition (since 1963).

## INS-25 Grammys and national honours for instrumentalists

> **Before you start — INS-25.** Grammy categories change often; check grammy.com for the current list. Indian honours for instrumentalists include the Bharat Ratna, the Padma awards and the Sangeet Natak Akademi awards and fellowships.

- ☐ **INS-25.1 Grammy instrumental categories**: Best Contemporary Instrumental Album, Best Jazz Instrumental Album, Best Improvised Jazz Solo, Best Classical Instrumental Solo, Best Chamber Music/Small Ensemble Performance, Best Orchestral Performance, Best Instrumental Composition, Best Global Music Album and Performance.
- ☐ **INS-25.2 Indian instrumentalists at the Grammys**: Ravi Shankar (*The Concert for Bangladesh*, Album of the Year 1973, with others; and later wins); Vishwa Mohan Bhatt with Ry Cooder (1994); Vikku Vinayakram with Mickey Hart's *Planet Drum* (1992); Zakir Hussain (*Planet Drum* 1992, *Global Drum Project* 2009, and **three in one night in February 2024**: Best Global Music Album for Shakti's *This Moment*, Best Global Music Performance for "Pashto" and Best Contemporary Instrumental Album for *As We Speak*); Anoushka Shankar has many nominations.
- ☐ **INS-25.3 Bharat Ratna instrumentalists**: Ravi Shankar (1999), Bismillah Khan (2001).
- ☐ **INS-25.4 Padma Vibhushan instrumentalists (selection)**: Hariprasad Chaurasia (2000), Shivkumar Sharma (2001), Amjad Ali Khan (2001), Ram Narayan (2005), Umayalpuram Sivaraman (2010), Zakir Hussain (2023). The full list is at padmaawards.gov.in.
- ☐ **INS-25.5 Sangeet Natak Akademi**: annual awards for instrumental music (Hindustani and Carnatic separately), the Fellowship (Akademi Ratna), and the Ustad Bismillah Khan Yuva Puraskar for young artists.
- ☐ **INS-25.6 Other Indian honours**: Sangita Kalanidhi (Madras Music Academy; veena artist Jayanthi Kumaresh is the 2026 designate), Kalidas Samman (Madhya Pradesh), Tansen Samman (Gwalior), state academy awards.
- ☐ **INS-25.7 Honours for makers**: the Sangeet Natak Akademi has honoured instrument makers; GI tags for Thanjavur veena, Bobbili veena and Miraj sitar and tanpura recognise craft regions.
