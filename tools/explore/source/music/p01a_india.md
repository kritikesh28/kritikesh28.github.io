# PART 1 — MUSIC OF THE WORLD

> **About this Part.** The first great classification is where music comes from. It begins with India in depth (classical, semi-classical, devotional, folk by region, film and independent music), then travels continent by continent. Each tradition has an identity card (what it is, where, key forms and instruments, legends) and, where useful, a **Music Register** of landmark recordings, compositions or albums. **Registers are a curated landmark selection, not a complete list** (see MUS-07.3). Every instrument named links to the Musical Instruments index (INS); film songs link to Movies (MOV).

> **Register fields.** Year · Title · Artist / composer · Genre or form · Awards and reception · Why it matters. Sales and stream counts are given only with a source and date. A dash (—) means not recorded here, never a guess.

## MUS-01 Indian classical music: two great systems

> **Before you start — MUS-01.** Indian classical music is built on **raga** (melodic framework) and **tala** (rhythmic cycle), and is largely improvised within strict rules. From about the 13th century it developed into two systems: **Hindustani** (North) and **Carnatic** (South). Both trace their roots to the Samaveda, Bharata's *Natyashastra* and Sharngadeva's *Sangita Ratnakara* (13th century). **See also:** MUS-11 Raga and thaat, MUS-12 Tala, INS-01 Indian instruments.

### ☐ MUS-01.1 Hindustani classical music

The classical music of North India, Pakistan, Bangladesh and Nepal, shaped by court patronage under Sultanate and Mughal rulers and by Persian influence. **Tags:** #hindustani #classical #india

- ☐ **Dhrupad**: the oldest surviving form; austere, meditative, sung with the pakhawaj; four "banis" (Gauhar, Dagar, Khandar, Nauhar); the Dagar family and the Gundecha Brothers.
- ☐ **Khayal**: the main vocal form today, more flexible and ornamented, with slow (vilambit) and fast (drut) bandishes; developed by Sadarang and Adarang in the 18th century.
- ☐ **Tarana**: fast composition of meaningless syllables (ta, na, dere), credited by tradition to Amir Khusrau.
- ☐ **Instrumental music**: sitar, sarod, sarangi, bansuri, shehnai, santoor, violin, rudra veena; the alap–jor–jhala–gat structure.
- ☐ **Concert structure**: alap (unmetred exploration), then composition with tabla, improvisation (taans, bol-baant), climax.
- ☐ **Gharanas of khayal**: Gwalior (the oldest), Agra, Jaipur-Atrauli (Alladiya Khan), Kirana (Abdul Karim Khan, Bhimsen Joshi), Patiala (Bade Ghulam Ali Khan), Rampur-Sahaswan, Bhendibazaar, Mewati (Pandit Jasraj), Indore (Amir Khan).
- ☐ **Instrumental gharanas**: Maihar (Allauddin Khan; pupils Ravi Shankar, Ali Akbar Khan, Annapurna Devi, Nikhil Banerjee, Pannalal Ghosh), Senia, Imdadkhani (Vilayat Khan); tabla gharanas of Delhi, Ajrara, Lucknow, Farrukhabad, Benares, Punjab.
- ☐ **Legends**: Tansen (16th century, Akbar's court), Vishnu Narayan Bhatkhande and Vishnu Digambar Paluskar (20th-century reformers), Bade Ghulam Ali Khan, Kesarbai Kerkar, Gangubai Hangal, Kumar Gandharva, Kishori Amonkar, Bhimsen Joshi (Bharat Ratna 2009), Pandit Jasraj, Girija Devi, Parveen Sultana, Ravi Shankar (Bharat Ratna 1999), Ali Akbar Khan, Bismillah Khan (shehnai; Bharat Ratna 2001), Hariprasad Chaurasia, Shivkumar Sharma, Zakir Hussain (tabla, 1951–2024), Amjad Ali Khan, Rashid Khan, Kaushiki Chakraborty.

### ☐ MUS-01.2 Carnatic classical music

The classical music of South India (Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Kerala), centred on composed songs (kritis) and devotional lyrics. **Tags:** #carnatic #classical #south-india

- ☐ **Purandara Dasa** (1484–1564): "father of Carnatic music", who set the graded teaching lessons (sarali varisai, alankaras) still used today.
- ☐ **The Trinity** (18th–19th centuries, all born in Thiruvarur): **Tyagaraja** (Telugu kritis; the Pancharatna kritis sung every year at the Thiruvaiyaru Aradhana), **Muthuswami Dikshitar** (Sanskrit kritis) and **Syama Sastri**.
- ☐ **Other composers**: Swathi Thirunal, Annamacharya, Gopalakrishna Bharathi, Papanasam Sivan, Mysore Vasudevachar, Oothukkadu Venkata Kavi.
- ☐ **Forms**: varnam (concert opener), kriti (main song: pallavi, anupallavi, charanam), ragam–tanam–pallavi (the improvised centrepiece), javali, tillana, padam.
- ☐ **Concert (kutcheri)**: vocalist with violin, mridangam, ghatam, kanjira, morsing; the tani avartanam (percussion solo).
- ☐ **Melakarta system**: 72 parent scales (Venkatamakhin, 17th century; MUS-11.3).
- ☐ **Legends**: Ariyakudi Ramanuja Iyengar (concert format), G. N. Balasubramaniam, M. S. Subbulakshmi (first musician to receive the Bharat Ratna, 1998; sang at the United Nations, 1966), M. L. Vasanthakumari, D. K. Pattammal, Semmangudi Srinivasa Iyer, M. Balamuralikrishna, Lalgudi Jayaraman (violin), T. N. Krishnan, U. Srinivas (mandolin), K. J. Yesudas, Bombay Jayashri, T. M. Krishna, Sanjay Subrahmanyan.

### ☐ MUS-01.3 Comparing the two systems

- ☐ **Same roots, different growth**: both use raga and tala; Hindustani stresses long improvised alap, Carnatic stresses composed kritis and gamaka (oscillating ornaments).
- ☐ **Scales**: Hindustani uses Bhatkhande's ten thaats; Carnatic uses 72 melakartas.
- ☐ **Some shared ragas** with different names: Yaman (Hindustani) ≈ Kalyani (Carnatic); Bhairavi differs between the two.
- ☐ **Jugalbandi**: duets between the systems (for example, Hariprasad Chaurasia with Carnatic musicians).

## MUS-02 Semi-classical, devotional and folk music of India

> **Before you start — MUS-02.** Between classical concert music and everyday song lies a vast world: light classical forms, devotional music of every faith, and folk music of every region. This is where most Indians first meet music. **See also:** BOK-06.4 (ghazal as poetry), INS-01.

### ☐ MUS-02.1 Semi-classical and light classical

- ☐ **Thumri**: romantic, expressive songs of Lucknow and Benares, often about Krishna; Begum Akhtar, Siddheshwari Devi, Girija Devi ("Queen of Thumri"), Shobha Gurtu.
- ☐ **Dadra, kajri, chaiti, hori, jhoola**: seasonal and lighter forms of the Purab (eastern) tradition.
- ☐ **Tappa**: quick, zigzag phrases from Punjabi camel-drivers' songs; Shori Miyan.
- ☐ **Ghazal**: Urdu love poetry sung; Begum Akhtar, Mehdi Hassan, Ghulam Ali, Jagjit Singh, Pankaj Udhas, Talat Aziz, Iqbal Bano, Farida Khanum.
- ☐ **Natya sangeet**: Marathi stage songs (Bal Gandharva).
- ☐ **Rabindrasangeet and Nazrulgeeti**: Bengali song traditions of Tagore (over 2,000 songs) and Kazi Nazrul Islam.

### ☐ MUS-02.2 Devotional music

- ☐ **Vedic chanting**: Samaveda melodies; UNESCO Intangible Heritage (2008).
- ☐ **Bhajan and kirtan**: Hindu devotional songs; Meera, Surdas, Tulsidas, Kabir; Purushottam Das Jalota, Anup Jalota, Hari Om Sharan; Chaitanya's sankirtan in Bengal; Haridasa in Karnataka.
- ☐ **Abhang**: Marathi Varkari songs of Tukaram and Namdev; Pandit Bhimsen Joshi's "Santavani".
- ☐ **Gurbani kirtan**: singing of the Guru Granth Sahib in prescribed ragas; the ragis of the Golden Temple.
- ☐ **Qawwali**: Sufi devotional song with harmonium, tabla and chorus; founded by tradition by Amir Khusrau; Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, the Sabri Brothers, Aziz Mian, Abida Parveen (kafi), Warsi Brothers.
- ☐ **Sufi music of Rajasthan and Punjab**; **Baul** of Bengal (UNESCO Intangible Heritage 2008; Purna Das Baul, Parvathy Baul).
- ☐ **Christian hymns and carols** of Goa, Kerala and the North-east; **Buddhist chants** of Ladakh (UNESCO 2012); **Jain stavans**.
- ☐ **Temple music**: nadaswaram and thavil of South India, Sopana sangeetham of Kerala, Haveli sangeet of Pushtimarg.

### ☐ MUS-02.3 Folk music by region

- ☐ **Rajasthan**: Manganiyar and Langa hereditary musicians (kamaicha, sarangi, khartal); Kalbelia songs (UNESCO 2010); Maand (Allah Jilai Bai, "Kesariya Balam"); Pabuji ki phad; Bhopa songs.
- ☐ **Punjab**: bhangra and giddha songs, boliyan, Heer singing, dhol; the roots of bhangra pop.
- ☐ **Gujarat**: garba and dandiya raas songs (Garba of Gujarat, UNESCO 2023), dayro.
- ☐ **Uttar Pradesh and Bihar**: birha, kajri, chaiti, Alha, sohar, Bhojpuri folk (Sharda Sinha, "Bihar Kokila", 1952–2024).
- ☐ **Bengal and Odisha**: Baul, Bhatiyali (boatmen's songs), Bhawaiya, Gambhira; Odissi music.
- ☐ **Maharashtra**: lavani, powada (ballads), bharud, koli songs.
- ☐ **Karnataka**: Bhavageete (light poetry songs), Janapada geete, Yakshagana music.
- ☐ **Tamil Nadu and Kerala**: gaana (Chennai street music), villu paatu (bow song), oppari (laments), Mappila pattu, Sopana, vadakkan pattu.
- ☐ **Andhra Pradesh and Telangana**: Burrakatha, Oggu Katha, folk songs of Bathukamma.
- ☐ **North-east**: Bihu songs (Assam), Naga and Mizo choral and folk song, Manipuri Nat Sankirtana (UNESCO 2013), Khasi songs.
- ☐ **Himalaya**: Pahari songs of Himachal, Garhwal and Kumaon, Kashmiri chhakri and Sufiana kalam.
- ☐ **Central India and tribal music**: Gond, Baiga, Bhil, Santal and Munda songs and dances; Pandavani (Teejan Bai, Chhattisgarh).

## MUS-03 Film, popular and independent music of India

> **Before you start — MUS-03.** For most of the 20th century, film songs were India's popular music, sung by "playback singers" while actors mimed. Each film industry has its own music. Since the 1990s, Indipop, independent bands, fusion, hip-hop and regional pop have grown, and streaming has opened new paths. **See also:** MOV-01 Indian cinema, MOV-14.5 Composers and singers.

### ☐ MUS-03.1 Hindi film music

- ☐ **Early talkies** (1931–1940s): *Alam Ara*'s "De De Khuda Ke Naam Par" (W. M. Khan, 1931), the first film song; K. L. Saigal, the first superstar singer; New Theatres (R. C. Boral, Pankaj Mullick).
- ☐ **Playback begins**: *Dhoop Chhaon* (1935), music by R. C. Boral, is often credited with the first playback songs.
- ☐ **Golden age** (1940s–1960s): composers Naushad, S. D. Burman, Shankar–Jaikishan, C. Ramchandra, Madan Mohan, O. P. Nayyar, Roshan, Salil Chowdhury, Hemant Kumar; singers Lata Mangeshkar, Mohammed Rafi, Asha Bhosle, Kishore Kumar, Mukesh, Manna Dey, Geeta Dutt, Talat Mahmood; lyricists Sahir Ludhianvi, Shailendra, Majrooh Sultanpuri, Kaifi Azmi, Shakeel Badayuni.
- ☐ **R. D. Burman era** (1970s–80s): rock, jazz and Latin sounds; Laxmikant–Pyarelal, Kalyanji–Anandji, Bappi Lahiri (disco).
- ☐ **1990s revival**: Nadeem–Shravan, Jatin–Lalit, Anu Malik; A. R. Rahman's arrival in Hindi (*Roja* dubbed, 1993; *Rangeela*, 1995); Kumar Sanu, Udit Narayan, Alka Yagnik, Kavita Krishnamurthy.
- ☐ **2000s–today**: Shankar–Ehsaan–Loy, Vishal–Shekhar, Pritam, Amit Trivedi, Sachin–Jigar; Sonu Nigam, Shreya Ghoshal, Sunidhi Chauhan, Arijit Singh, Shilpa Rao; lyricists Gulzar, Javed Akhtar, Prasoon Joshi, Irshad Kamil, Amitabh Bhattacharya.
- ☐ **Global moments**: A. R. Rahman's two Oscars for *Slumdog Millionaire* (2009); M. M. Keeravani and Chandrabose's Oscar for "Naatu Naatu" from the Telugu film *RRR* (2023).

### ☐ MUS-03.2 Film music of other Indian industries

- ☐ **Tamil**: G. Ramanathan, M. S. Viswanathan–T. K. Ramamoorthy, **Ilaiyaraaja** (over 1,000 films, symphonic and folk fusion), A. R. Rahman, Harris Jayaraj, Yuvan Shankar Raja, Anirudh Ravichander; singers T. M. Soundararajan, P. Susheela, S. P. Balasubrahmanyam (Guinness-cited for a huge number of songs), K. S. Chithra, S. Janaki.
- ☐ **Telugu**: Ghantasala, K. V. Mahadevan, M. M. Keeravani, Devi Sri Prasad, Thaman S.
- ☐ **Malayalam**: G. Devarajan, Salil Chowdhury, Johnson, Raveendran, Vidyasagar; K. J. Yesudas, K. S. Chithra.
- ☐ **Kannada**: G. K. Venkatesh, Vijaya Bhaskar, Hamsalekha; P. B. Sreenivas, Rajkumar (actor-singer).
- ☐ **Bengali**: Hemanta Mukherjee, Salil Chowdhury, Satyajit Ray (scored his own films), Manna Dey.
- ☐ **Marathi, Punjabi, Bhojpuri, Gujarati, Assamese (Bhupen Hazarika)** and others.

### ☐ MUS-03.3 Indipop, independent and new Indian music

- ☐ **Indipop** (1990s): Alisha Chinai (*Made in India*, 1995), Lucky Ali, Daler Mehndi ("Bolo Ta Ra Ra", 1995), Shaan, Euphoria, Colonial Cousins; MTV and Channel V.
- ☐ **Fusion**: Shakti (John McLaughlin, Zakir Hussain, L. Shankar; *This Moment* won a Grammy in 2024), Indian Ocean, Karsh Kale, Raghu Dixit, Coke Studio (Pakistan from 2008; India 2011–2015).
- ☐ **Rock and metal**: Parikrama, Indus Creed, Thermal and a Quarter, Bloodywood.
- ☐ **Hip-hop and rap**: Baba Sehgal (early 1990s), Bohemia, Yo Yo Honey Singh, Badshah, Divine and Naezy (Mumbai street rap, inspiring *Gully Boy*, 2019), Raftaar, Seedhe Maut, Hanumankind ("Big Dawgs", 2024).
- ☐ **Punjabi pop**: Gurdas Maan, Sidhu Moose Wala (1993–2022), Diljit Dosanjh (first Punjabi act at Coachella, 2023), AP Dhillon, Karan Aujla.
- ☐ **Independent singer-songwriters and pop**: Prateek Kuhad, Anuv Jain, Ritviz (electronic), Nucleya (bass), Lifafa, When Chai Met Toast.
- ☐ **Devotional and regional pop on streaming**: Haryanvi, Bhojpuri and Punjabi hits often lead YouTube views in India.
