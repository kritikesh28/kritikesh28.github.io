## MUS-04 Music of Asia and the Middle East

> **Before you start — MUS-04.** Asia holds great classical systems built on melodic modes (maqam, dastgah, makam), ensemble traditions (gamelan, gagaku) and huge modern pop industries (K-pop, J-pop, C-pop). **See also:** INS-02 Asian instruments.

### ☐ MUS-04.1 Arabic, Persian and Turkish traditions

- ☐ **Arabic maqam**: a system of melodic modes with microtones (quarter tones), improvised taqsim; Umm Kulthum (Egypt, "the Star of the East"), Mohammed Abdel Wahab, Fairuz (Lebanon), Abdel Halim Hafez; Iraqi maqam (UNESCO 2008).
- ☐ **Persian dastgah**: seven main modal systems and the radif repertoire; the tar, setar, santur and kamancheh; Mohammad-Reza Shajarian.
- ☐ **Turkish makam**: Ottoman court music, Mevlevi Sufi music (the whirling ceremony, UNESCO 2008), ney flute.
- ☐ **Modern pop**: Arabic pop (Amr Diab), Turkish pop (Tarkan), Persian diaspora pop (Googoosh).

### ☐ MUS-04.2 South and Central Asia beyond India

- ☐ **Pakistan**: qawwali (Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, Abida Parveen), ghazal (Mehdi Hassan, Noor Jehan), Coke Studio Pakistan ("Pasoori", 2022), rock (Junoon).
- ☐ **Bangladesh and Nepal**: Baul and Lalon songs; Nepali folk (dohori) and modern songs (Narayan Gopal).
- ☐ **Sri Lanka**: baila, Kandyan drumming.
- ☐ **Afghanistan and Central Asia**: rubab music (Ustad Mohammad Omar); Uzbek and Tajik shashmaqam (UNESCO 2008); Mongolian throat singing (khöömei) and long song.

### ☐ MUS-04.3 East Asia

- ☐ **China**: ancient pitch-pipe theory and the pentatonic scale; the guqin (UNESCO 2008), pipa, erhu; Peking opera (UNESCO 2010); C-pop and Mandopop (Teresa Teng, Jay Chou).
- ☐ **Japan**: gagaku (imperial court music, among the oldest orchestral music still performed), shakuhachi, koto, shamisen, noh and kabuki music; enka (sentimental ballads); J-pop and city pop; anime and video-game music (Joe Hisaishi, Koji Kondo).
- ☐ **Korea**: pansori (epic storytelling song, UNESCO 2008), sanjo, samulnori percussion; **K-pop** (from Seo Taiji and Boys, 1992, to BTS, BLACKPINK, Stray Kids, the training-idol system).

### ☐ MUS-04.4 South-East Asia

- ☐ **Indonesia**: **gamelan** ensembles of Java and Bali (tuned bronze gongs and metallophones; UNESCO 2021), kecak, dangdut (Rhoma Irama).
- ☐ **Thailand, Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar**: piphat ensembles, khmer pinpeat, Lao khaen (mouth organ, UNESCO 2017), Myanmar saing waing.
- ☐ **Vietnam**: ca trù (UNESCO 2009), quan họ songs, đàn bầu.
- ☐ **Philippines**: kulintang gong music, OPM (Original Pilipino Music).

## MUS-05 Music of Europe

> **Before you start — MUS-05.** Europe gave the world the Western art-music tradition (MUS-08.1), staff notation and the symphony orchestra, plus rich folk traditions. **See also:** MUS-14 Timeline.

### ☐ MUS-05.1 European folk and regional traditions

- ☐ **Celtic** (Ireland, Scotland, Brittany, Galicia): fiddle, uilleann pipes, bagpipes, sean-nós singing.
- ☐ **Flamenco** (Andalusia, Spain; UNESCO 2010): song (cante), guitar (toque), dance (baile); Roma roots; Paco de Lucía, Camarón.
- ☐ **Fado** (Portugal; UNESCO 2011): songs of longing (saudade); Amália Rodrigues.
- ☐ **Balkan and Roma music**: brass bands, odd metres (7/8, 9/8), Bulgarian women's choirs.
- ☐ **Nordic**: joik of the Sámi people, Norwegian Hardanger fiddle.
- ☐ **Russian and Slavic**: folk choirs, balalaika, Orthodox chant.
- ☐ **Polyphonic singing** of Georgia (UNESCO 2008) and Corsica.
- ☐ **Chanson** (France): Édith Piaf, Jacques Brel; **canzone** (Italy); **Schlager** (Germany).

### ☐ MUS-05.2 European popular music

- ☐ **British Invasion**: The Beatles, The Rolling Stones, The Who; **Britpop**: Oasis, Blur; UK punk, new wave, grime (Stormzy), drum and bass.
- ☐ **Eurovision Song Contest** (1956): ABBA (Sweden, 1974), Céline Dion (for Switzerland, 1988), Måneskin (Italy, 2021).
- ☐ **European electronic music**: Kraftwerk (Germany), Daft Punk (France), Berlin techno, Ibiza house.

## MUS-06 Music of the Americas

> **Before you start — MUS-06.** The Americas mixed Indigenous, African and European music into many of the world's most influential popular genres: blues, jazz, country, rock and roll, soul, hip-hop, samba, salsa, reggae. **See also:** MUS-09 Popular genres.

### ☐ MUS-06.1 North America

- ☐ **Indigenous music**: pow-wow drums and songs, Inuit throat singing (katajjaq).
- ☐ **African American roots**: spirituals, work songs, ragtime (Scott Joplin), blues, gospel, jazz; the source of most American popular music.
- ☐ **Country and folk**: Appalachian ballads, bluegrass (Bill Monroe), the Grand Ole Opry (1925), Johnny Cash, Dolly Parton; folk revival (Woody Guthrie, Pete Seeger, Bob Dylan, Joan Baez).
- ☐ **Rock and roll and pop**: Elvis Presley, Chuck Berry, Little Richard; Motown (1959); Michael Jackson (*Thriller*, 1982); Madonna, Beyoncé, Taylor Swift.
- ☐ **Hip-hop**: born in the Bronx, New York, in 1973 (DJ Kool Herc's party); Grandmaster Flash, Run-DMC, Tupac, Notorious B.I.G., Jay-Z, Eminem, Kendrick Lamar (Pulitzer Prize for Music 2018, the first for a non-classical, non-jazz work).
- ☐ **Canada**: Joni Mitchell, Leonard Cohen, Céline Dion, Drake, The Weeknd.

### ☐ MUS-06.2 Latin America

- ☐ **Mexico**: mariachi (UNESCO 2011), ranchera, son jarocho, norteño and corridos.
- ☐ **Cuba and the Caribbean**: son cubano, rumba, mambo, cha-cha-chá, salsa (developed in New York by Cuban and Puerto Rican musicians), Buena Vista Social Club (1997).
- ☐ **Brazil**: samba (UNESCO 2005, samba de roda), bossa nova (João Gilberto, Antônio Carlos Jobim, "The Girl from Ipanema", 1962), MPB, Tropicália (Caetano Veloso, Gilberto Gil), funk carioca.
- ☐ **Argentina and Uruguay**: tango (UNESCO 2009; Carlos Gardel, Astor Piazzolla's nuevo tango).
- ☐ **Andes**: huayno, panpipes (siku) and charango; Colombian cumbia and vallenato; Dominican merengue and bachata.
- ☐ **Reggaeton and Latin pop**: Puerto Rico and Panama (Daddy Yankee, Bad Bunny), Shakira, Karol G.

### ☐ MUS-06.3 The Caribbean

- ☐ **Jamaica**: mento, ska, rocksteady, **reggae** (Bob Marley and the Wailers; UNESCO 2018), dub (sound-system culture), dancehall.
- ☐ **Trinidad and Tobago**: calypso, soca, the steelpan (invented in the 20th century from oil drums); chutney music of Indo-Trinidadians (Sundar Popo), blending Bhojpuri folk song with soca.
- ☐ **Haiti and the French Caribbean**: kompa, zouk.

## MUS-07 Music of Africa and Oceania

> **Before you start — MUS-07.** African music is the root of much of the world's popular music through the Atlantic diaspora. It is known for polyrhythm, call-and-response and the close link of music, dance and speech (talking drums). **See also:** INS-04 African instruments.

### ☐ MUS-07.1 Africa

- ☐ **West Africa**: griot (jeli) praise singers with kora and balafon (Toumani Diabaté, Salif Keita); highlife (Ghana), **Afrobeat** (Fela Kuti, Nigeria, 1970s), **Afrobeats** (Burna Boy, Wizkid, Tems; Grammy category Best African Music Performance from 2024), mbalax (Youssou N'Dour), desert blues (Tinariwen, Ali Farka Touré).
- ☐ **Central Africa**: Congolese rumba and soukous (UNESCO 2021; Franco, Papa Wemba).
- ☐ **East Africa**: taarab (Zanzibar), benga (Kenya), Ethiopian modes and Ethio-jazz (Mulatu Astatke).
- ☐ **Southern Africa**: isicathamiya (Ladysmith Black Mambazo), mbube, mbaqanga, kwaito, **amapiano** (South Africa, 2010s); Miriam Makeba, Hugh Masekela.
- ☐ **North Africa**: Gnawa (Morocco, UNESCO 2019), raï (Algeria; Khaled), Andalusian music.

### ☐ MUS-07.2 Oceania

- ☐ **Aboriginal Australia**: songlines, the didgeridoo (yidaki) of Arnhem Land, clapsticks; Yothu Yindi, Gurrumul.
- ☐ **Māori music** (New Zealand): waiata (songs), haka (posture dance with chant).
- ☐ **Pacific**: Polynesian choral singing, Hawaiian slack-key guitar and ukulele, Fijian meke.
- ☐ **Pop**: AC/DC, Kylie Minogue, Lorde, Sia.

### ☐ MUS-07.3 How the registers grow

1. **Award lists first** (Grammy, Sangeet Natak Akademi, National Film Awards, Filmfare), because they are official and dated.
2. **Charts and certifications** (Billboard, Official Charts, IMI and IFPI), always with date and type of figure.
3. **Archives and discographies**: Archive of Indian Music, Smithsonian Folkways, British Library Sound Archive, Discogs (checked against labels).
4. **Expert lists with named sources** (*Rolling Stone*, *Gramophone*, Sangeet Natak Akademi documentation).
5. **Readers' suggestions**, checked before they are added.
