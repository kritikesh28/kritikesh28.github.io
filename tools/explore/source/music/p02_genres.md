# PART 2 — GENRES AND STYLES

> **About this Part.** The second great classification is what kind of music it is. Genres are families with shared sounds, instruments and purposes. They overlap and mix constantly, so treat this as a map, not a fence. Each genre has a one- or two-line definition, its roots and examples.

## MUS-08 Art, classical, religious and folk music families

> **Before you start — MUS-08.** "Classical" means different things in different places: Indian classical (MUS-01), Western classical, Arabic and Persian classical, Chinese and Japanese court music. All share long training, theory and a respected canon. **See also:** MUS-14 Timeline.

### ☐ MUS-08.1 Western classical music by period

- ☐ **Medieval** (about 500–1400): Gregorian chant, Hildegard of Bingen, early polyphony (Notre-Dame school).
- ☐ **Renaissance** (about 1400–1600): Josquin des Prez, Palestrina; the madrigal.
- ☐ **Baroque** (about 1600–1750): opera born in Florence (Peri, about 1597–1600) and Monteverdi; Vivaldi (*The Four Seasons*), J. S. Bach, Handel; the concerto and fugue.
- ☐ **Classical** (about 1750–1820): Haydn ("father of the symphony"), Mozart, early Beethoven; sonata form, string quartet.
- ☐ **Romantic** (about 1820–1910): Beethoven's late works, Schubert, Chopin, Liszt, Wagner, Verdi, Brahms, Tchaikovsky, Mahler; nationalism (Dvořák, Grieg, the Russian Five).
- ☐ **Modern and 20th century**: Debussy (impressionism), Stravinsky, Schoenberg (twelve-tone), Bartók, Shostakovich, Gershwin, Britten.
- ☐ **Contemporary**: minimalism (Steve Reich, Philip Glass, Arvo Pärt), film-influenced concert music, John Adams; Indian-Western fusions such as Philip Glass and Ravi Shankar's *Passages* (1990).
- ☐ **Opera, oratorio, symphony, concerto, chamber music, art song (Lied)**: the main forms.

### ☐ MUS-08.2 Religious and spiritual music

- ☐ Vedic chant, bhajan and kirtan, Gurbani, qawwali and Sufi music (MUS-02.2); Gregorian chant; Byzantine chant; Jewish cantillation; Islamic adhan and Qur'anic recitation (tajwid); Buddhist chant; gospel; Christian hymns; Shinto kagura.

### ☐ MUS-08.3 Folk and traditional music

- ☐ Music passed down orally in communities: work songs, ballads, wedding songs, laments, lullabies, dance music; collected by ethnomusicologists (Alan Lomax, Béla Bartók, Komal Kothari in Rajasthan). India's folk music is covered by region in MUS-02.3.

## MUS-09 Popular genres of the world

> **Before you start — MUS-09.** Most popular genres of the 20th century were born in the Americas from African, European and Indigenous roots, then spread worldwide and mixed with local music. Each has many sub-genres. **See also:** MUS-06 Americas, MUS-16 People.

### ☐ MUS-09.1 Blues

Twelve-bar form, "blue notes" and songs of hardship, born among African Americans in the Mississippi Delta around 1900. Robert Johnson, Bessie Smith, Muddy Waters (Chicago electric blues), B. B. King. **Sub-genres:** Delta, Chicago, Texas, electric, blues rock.

### ☐ MUS-09.2 Jazz

Improvisation, swing rhythm and "blue" harmony, born in New Orleans around 1900–1915. **Tags:** #jazz

- ☐ **New Orleans and Dixieland**: Louis Armstrong, Jelly Roll Morton.
- ☐ **Swing and big band** (1930s–40s): Duke Ellington, Count Basie, Benny Goodman, Ella Fitzgerald.
- ☐ **Bebop** (1940s): Charlie Parker, Dizzy Gillespie, Thelonious Monk.
- ☐ **Cool, hard bop and modal**: Miles Davis, John Coltrane (*A Love Supreme*, 1965, influenced by Indian music; he named his son Ravi).
- ☐ **Free jazz, fusion and Latin jazz**: Ornette Coleman, Weather Report, Chick Corea, Tito Puente.
- ☐ **Indo-jazz**: Joe Harriott and John Mayer's *Indo-Jazz Fusions* (1967), Shakti, Louiz Banks ("Godfather of Indian jazz"), Goa's jazz musicians in Bombay's film orchestras.

### ☐ MUS-09.3 Country and folk

Country grew from Appalachian and Southern rural music; folk from older ballad traditions. **Sub-genres:** bluegrass, honky-tonk, Nashville sound, outlaw country, country pop, Americana, folk rock, singer-songwriter.

### ☐ MUS-09.4 Rock

Electric guitars, bass and drums; born from rhythm and blues and country in the 1950s. **Tags:** #rock

- ☐ **Rock and roll** (1950s), **British Invasion** and **psychedelic rock** (1960s; The Beatles' Indian period, George Harrison studying sitar with Ravi Shankar), **hard rock** (Led Zeppelin), **progressive rock** (Pink Floyd, Yes), **glam**, **punk** (Ramones, Sex Pistols, 1970s), **new wave**, **heavy metal** (Black Sabbath, Iron Maiden, Metallica), **grunge** (Nirvana), **alternative and indie**, **Britpop**, **post-rock**.

### ☐ MUS-09.5 Pop

Catchy, song-centred music for wide audiences; the word comes from "popular". Tin Pan Alley, crooners (Frank Sinatra), teen pop, synth-pop, dance-pop, K-pop, J-pop, Bollywood pop, Latin pop, bubblegum, art pop, hyperpop.

### ☐ MUS-09.6 R&B, soul and funk

- ☐ **Rhythm and blues** (1940s): the Black popular music that led to rock and roll.
- ☐ **Soul** (1960s): gospel feeling in secular songs; Ray Charles, Aretha Franklin ("Queen of Soul"), Otis Redding, Motown (Stevie Wonder, Marvin Gaye) and Stax.
- ☐ **Funk** (late 1960s): James Brown, Parliament-Funkadelic; groove on the "one".
- ☐ **Disco** (1970s): Donna Summer, Bee Gees; Bappi Lahiri brought disco to Bollywood.
- ☐ **Contemporary R&B and neo-soul**: Whitney Houston, Mariah Carey, Erykah Badu, Beyoncé, Frank Ocean.

### ☐ MUS-09.7 Hip-hop and rap

Rhythmic speech (rapping) over beats, with DJing, breakdancing and graffiti as the four elements. Born in the Bronx in 1973. **Sub-genres:** old school, gangsta, conscious, trap, drill, grime (UK), boom bap, desi hip-hop and gully rap (India), K-hip-hop.

### ☐ MUS-09.8 Electronic and dance music

Music made mainly with electronic instruments and computers. **Tags:** #electronic

- ☐ **Pioneers**: musique concrète (Pierre Schaeffer, 1948), Karlheinz Stockhausen, the Moog synthesizer (1964), Kraftwerk.
- ☐ **Dance genres**: house (Chicago, 1980s), techno (Detroit), trance, drum and bass, dubstep, EDM, garage, psytrance (born in Goa's beach parties in the 1990s, "Goa trance"), amapiano.
- ☐ **Listening genres**: ambient (Brian Eno, *Music for Airports*, 1978), IDM, synthwave, lo-fi hip-hop.

### ☐ MUS-09.9 Reggae and Caribbean styles

Ska, rocksteady, reggae, dub, dancehall, calypso, soca, zouk, kompa, chutney (MUS-06.3).

### ☐ MUS-09.10 Latin music

Salsa, merengue, bachata, cumbia, tango, bossa nova, samba, mariachi, reggaeton, Latin trap, Latin rock (MUS-06.2).

### ☐ MUS-09.11 Metal and punk families

- ☐ **Metal**: heavy metal, thrash, death, black, doom, power, nu-metal, metalcore, folk metal (Bloodywood from India mixes metal with Punjabi folk).
- ☐ **Punk**: punk rock, hardcore, pop-punk, post-punk, emo.

### ☐ MUS-09.12 World music and fusion

"World music" was a marketing label invented by record companies in London in 1987 for music from outside the Anglo-American mainstream. Fusion examples: Shakti, Ravi Shankar with the Beatles, Nitin Sawhney, Anoushka Shankar, Paul Simon's *Graceland* (1986, South African musicians), Buena Vista Social Club.

### ☐ MUS-09.13 Music for screen, games and stage

- ☐ **Film scores**: Max Steiner, Bernard Herrmann, Ennio Morricone, John Williams (*Star Wars*), Hans Zimmer, A. R. Rahman, Ilaiyaraaja, Satyajit Ray; Indian film songs as a genre of their own (MUS-03).
- ☐ **Video-game music**: Koji Kondo (*Super Mario*), Nobuo Uematsu (*Final Fantasy*).
- ☐ **Musical theatre**: Broadway and West End (Rodgers and Hammerstein, Andrew Lloyd Webber, Lin-Manuel Miranda's *Hamilton*); A. R. Rahman's *Bombay Dreams* (2002).
- ☐ **Jingles and advertising music**: India's "Mile Sur Mera Tumhara" (1988) and ad jingles as public memory.

### ☐ MUS-09.14 Children's music and lullabies

Nursery rhymes, lullabies (lori), school songs, educational songs (Sesame Street, Indian rhymes channels on YouTube, among the most viewed in the world).

# PART 3 — THE BUILDING BLOCKS OF MUSIC

> **About this Part.** What music is made of, explained side by side in Indian and Western terms. This Part is the "grammar" every other Part uses.

## MUS-10 Sound, pitch and the notes

> **Before you start — MUS-10.** Sound is vibration travelling through air. Its **pitch** depends on frequency (vibrations per second, hertz), its **loudness** on amplitude, and its **timbre** (tone colour) on the mix of overtones. **See also:** TEC (acoustics, audio technology), INS-00 How instruments make sound.

### ☐ MUS-10.1 The physics of sound

- ☐ **Frequency and pitch**: concert A is tuned to 440 Hz in most Western music (an international standard since 1955, ISO 16); Indian music has no fixed pitch: the singer chooses the tonic (Sa).
- ☐ **Overtones and harmonics**: a plucked string vibrates at many frequencies at once; the tanpura's buzzing bridge (jawari) brings out its overtones.
- ☐ **Octave**: doubling of frequency; the octave sounds like "the same note, higher" in every culture.
- ☐ **Tuning systems**: just intonation (pure ratios), equal temperament (the piano's 12 equal semitones), the 22 shrutis of Indian theory.

### ☐ MUS-10.2 Notes: swara and solfège

- ☐ **Indian sargam**: Sa Re Ga Ma Pa Dha Ni (shadja, rishabha, gandhara, madhyama, panchama, dhaivata, nishada); Sa and Pa are fixed (achal), the others can be komal (flat) or tivra (sharp), giving 12 notes.
- ☐ **Western notes**: C D E F G A B (do re mi fa sol la ti), with sharps and flats making 12 semitones.
- ☐ **Saptak**: the Indian octave, in three registers: mandra (low), madhya (middle), taar (high).
- ☐ **Shruti**: the 22 micro-intervals described in the *Natyashastra*, used to explain fine pitch differences between ragas.

## MUS-11 Scales, modes, raga and thaat

> **Before you start — MUS-11.** A **scale** is a set of notes in order; a **mode** is a scale with a home note and character; a **raga** is a mode plus rules of movement, ornaments and mood. **See also:** MUS-01 Indian classical, MUS-00.03 Raga and time.

### ☐ MUS-11.1 Western scales and modes

- ☐ Major and minor scales; the church modes (Ionian, Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Aeolian, Locrian); pentatonic scale (five notes; used worldwide from China to Scotland to Indian ragas like Bhupali); blues scale; chromatic scale; whole-tone scale.

### ☐ MUS-11.2 Raga

- ☐ **Aroha and avaroha**: ascending and descending note order.
- ☐ **Vadi and samvadi**: the most important and second most important notes.
- ☐ **Pakad / chalan**: characteristic phrases that identify a raga.
- ☐ **Jati**: number of notes: audav (5), shadav (6), sampurna (7).
- ☐ **Gamaka and meend**: ornaments and glides; Carnatic music uses many gamakas.
- ☐ **Rasa and samay**: mood and time (MUS-00.03).
- ☐ **Ragamala paintings**: ragas personified in Rajput and Mughal miniatures (16th–19th centuries).

### ☐ MUS-11.3 Classifying ragas: thaat and melakarta

- ☐ **Thaat** (Hindustani): V. N. Bhatkhande's ten parent scales: Bilawal, Kalyan, Khamaj, Bhairav, Poorvi, Marwa, Kafi, Asavari, Bhairavi, Todi.
- ☐ **Melakarta** (Carnatic): 72 parent scales, systematised by Venkatamakhin (*Chaturdandi Prakashika*, 17th century) and later Govindacharya; ragas derived from them are janya ragas.
- ☐ **Older systems**: grama and murchhana (*Natyashastra*), raga–ragini families (medieval).

## MUS-12 Rhythm, tempo and tala

> **Before you start — MUS-12.** Rhythm is how music is organised in time. Western music counts beats in bars (measures); Indian music uses **tala**, cycles that return to the first beat, **sam**. **See also:** INS-01.4 Indian percussion.

### ☐ MUS-12.1 Western rhythm

- ☐ Beat, tempo (bpm), metre (time signatures such as 4/4, 3/4 waltz, 6/8), syncopation, polyrhythm, swing, backbeat.
- ☐ **Tempo words**: largo (very slow), adagio, andante, moderato, allegro, presto (very fast).

### ☐ MUS-12.2 Hindustani tala

- ☐ **Elements**: matra (beat), vibhag (section), sam (first beat), khali (empty, "wave" beat), tali (clap), theka (basic drum pattern), laya (tempo: vilambit, madhya, drut), layakari (rhythmic play).
- ☐ **Common talas**: teentaal (16 beats), jhaptaal (10), ektaal (12), rupak (7, starting on khali), dadra (6), keherwa (8), chautaal (12, dhrupad), deepchandi (14).
- ☐ **Bols**: spoken drum syllables (dha, dhin, na, ti, te, ka) used to teach and recite rhythm.

### ☐ MUS-12.3 Carnatic tala

- ☐ **Suladi sapta tala**: seven talas (Dhruva, Matya, Rupaka, Jhampa, Triputa, Ata, Eka), each with five jatis, giving 35 talas.
- ☐ **Adi tala** (8 beats): the most used; **Rupaka**, **Misra Chapu** (7), **Khanda Chapu** (5).
- ☐ **Konnakol**: the vocal art of rhythm syllables (ta ka dhi mi).

### ☐ MUS-12.4 Rhythm around the world

- ☐ African polyrhythm and timeline patterns (the clave and bell patterns), Balkan aksak metres, Arabic iqa'at, gamelan colotomic cycles, Latin clave.

## MUS-13 Melody, harmony, form and notation

> **Before you start — MUS-13.** Melody is notes in a line; harmony is notes together; form is the plan of a piece; notation is how music is written. Indian music is mainly melodic, with harmony coming from the tanpura drone. **See also:** MUS-20 Composition.

### ☐ MUS-13.1 Melody and harmony

- ☐ **Melody**: contour, phrase, motif, range, hook.
- ☐ **Harmony**: intervals, chords (triads, sevenths), consonance and dissonance, chord progressions (the I–V–vi–IV "four chords" of pop), counterpoint (Bach), the drone (tanpura, bagpipes).
- ☐ **Texture**: monophony (one line), heterophony (one tune, varied together, common in Asian music), homophony (melody plus chords), polyphony (many independent lines).

### ☐ MUS-13.2 Form

- ☐ **Western forms**: binary, ternary, rondo, theme and variations, sonata form, fugue, verse–chorus, twelve-bar blues, 32-bar AABA song.
- ☐ **Indian forms**: alap–jor–jhala–gat; khayal's sthayi and antara; Carnatic kriti's pallavi, anupallavi, charanam; film song's mukhda (refrain) and antara (verse).

### ☐ MUS-13.3 Notation

- ☐ **Western staff notation**: developed from medieval neumes; Guido d'Arezzo (about 1025) gave lines and the "ut re mi" syllables.
- ☐ **Indian notation**: sargam letters; **Bhatkhande** and **Paluskar** notation systems (early 20th century); Carnatic notation from Subbarama Dikshitar's *Sangita Sampradaya Pradarshini* (1904).
- ☐ **Other notations**: Chinese gongche, guitar tablature, chord charts, MIDI and the piano roll in software.
- ☐ **Oral teaching (guru–shishya parampara)**: much Indian and world music is still learned by ear from a teacher.
