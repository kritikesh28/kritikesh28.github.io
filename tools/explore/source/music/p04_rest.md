# PART 7 — THE CRAFT AND THE BUSINESS OF MUSIC

> **About this Part.** How music is made, recorded and earned from: composing, arranging, recording, mixing, mastering and live sound; then labels, royalties and copyright.

## MUS-20 Making music: composition to concert

> **Before you start — MUS-20.** A song goes from an idea to a performance or a recording through a chain of crafts. In Indian classical music much of this happens live, in improvisation; in film and pop it happens in the studio. **See also:** INS (instruments), TEC (audio technology).

- ☐ **MUS-20.1 Composition and songwriting**: melody first or lyrics first (Indian film songs often use a "tune first" method); hooks, structure (MUS-13.2); bandish composition in classical music.
- ☐ **MUS-20.2 Lyrics**: rhyme, metre and meaning; Urdu–Hindi film poetry; Carnatic sahitya (lyric) and its languages.
- ☐ **MUS-20.3 Arranging and orchestration**: choosing instruments and parts; the film orchestra of Bombay (strings, mandolin, accordion, santoor) and its arrangers.
- ☐ **MUS-20.4 Improvisation**: alap, taan, swara kalpana, jazz solos, freestyle rap.
- ☐ **MUS-20.5 Recording**: microphones, isolation, multitracking, home studios, the digital audio workstation (DAW: Pro Tools, Logic, Ableton, FL Studio).
- ☐ **MUS-20.6 Mixing and mastering**: balancing levels, EQ, compression, reverb, panning; mastering for loudness and formats; the "loudness war".
- ☐ **MUS-20.7 Live sound and concerts**: PA systems, monitors, acoustics of halls (Shanmukhananda Hall, Mumbai; the Royal Albert Hall), the sabha culture of Chennai.
- ☐ **MUS-20.8 Practice and pedagogy**: riyaz (daily practice), guru–shishya parampara, conservatories (Juilliard, Royal Academy of Music), university music departments (Banaras Hindu University, Kalakshetra, Bhatkhande Sanskriti Vishwavidyalaya, Lucknow), graded exams (Trinity, ABRSM; Gandharva Mahavidyalaya in India).

## MUS-21 The music industry, royalties and copyright

> **Before you start — MUS-21.** Music makes money from recordings, publishing (songwriting rights), live shows, licensing (films, ads, games) and merchandise. In India, film-music labels have long dominated. **See also:** BOK-19.5 Copyright.

- ☐ **MUS-21.1 Record labels**: the three majors (Universal Music Group, Sony Music, Warner Music); independent labels; India's T-Series (founded by Gulshan Kumar; its YouTube channel is among the most subscribed in the world), Saregama (from the Gramophone Company of India, HMV), Tips, Zee Music, Sony Music India, Think Music, Aditya Music, Lahari.
- ☐ **MUS-21.2 How artists earn**: royalties from streams (fractions of a cent per play), performance royalties (radio, TV, venues), mechanical royalties, synchronisation (sync) fees, concerts and merchandise.
- ☐ **MUS-21.3 Collecting societies**: IPRS (Indian Performing Right Society, 1969; reformed after the Copyright Amendment Act, 2012, which gave lyricists and composers a share of royalties), PPL India, ASCAP and BMI (USA), PRS (UK).
- ☐ **MUS-21.4 Copyright and music**: two rights in every recording (the composition and the sound recording); cover versions and remixes; sampling disputes; the 2012 Indian amendment; AI training and voice-cloning cases.
- ☐ **MUS-21.5 Streaming economics**: Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube Music, Amazon Music, JioSaavn, Gaana; playlists as gatekeepers; arguments for fairer payment ("user-centric" models).
- ☐ **MUS-21.6 Live music business**: festivals, tours, ticketing; India's growing concert market (Coldplay's Ahmedabad shows, January 2025, among the largest audiences of the tour).

# PART 8 — IDEAS: MUSIC, MIND, PHILOSOPHY AND SOCIETY

> **About this Part.** Why music moves us, what it does to the brain and body, and what it does in society. Claims come with their evidence; open debates are flagged.

## MUS-22 Music, mind and society

> **Before you start — MUS-22.** Philosophers from Plato and Bharata to modern neuroscientists have asked why music has such power. **See also:** BOK-20.1 Rasa theory, MOV-19.

### ☐ MUS-22.1 Philosophy and aesthetics of music

- ☐ **Nada yoga and nada brahma**: sound as a path to the divine (Indian tradition).
- ☐ **Rasa and bhava in music**: Bharata's rasa theory applied to raga (MUS-00.04).
- ☐ **Pythagoras and the "music of the spheres"**: harmony as cosmic order.
- ☐ **Plato**: some modes build character, others corrupt it; music in education.
- ☐ **Confucius**: music as moral harmony and good government.
- ☐ **Modern views**: Eduard Hanslick (music is "tonally moving forms"), Theodor Adorno (critique of the pop industry), John Cage (silence and chance), Christopher Small's idea of "musicking" (music as an activity, not a thing).

### ☐ MUS-22.2 Music and the brain

- ☐ **Pleasure**: peak moments in music release dopamine (Salimpoor and colleagues, 2011); "chills" or frisson.
- ☐ **Prediction**: the brain enjoys guessing what comes next, and being surprised (David Huron, *Sweet Anticipation*, 2006).
- ☐ **Memory**: music memories survive even in advanced dementia; the documentary *Alive Inside* (2014).
- ☐ **Musical training** and brain changes; the "Mozart effect" claim (1993) was largely not confirmed.
- ☐ **Amusia** (tone deafness) affects a small percentage of people.
- ☐ **Books**: Oliver Sacks, *Musicophilia* (2007); Daniel Levitin, *This Is Your Brain on Music* (2006).

### ☐ MUS-22.3 Music therapy and well-being

- ☐ Clinical music therapy for stroke, autism, depression and pain; raga therapy claims in India (some practised widely, with limited rigorous evidence so far); singing and breathing; lullabies for babies; group singing and loneliness.

### ☐ MUS-22.4 Music and society

- ☐ **Identity**: national anthems ("Jana Gana Mana", adopted 1950), regional pride, diaspora music (bhangra in Britain, chutney in Trinidad).
- ☐ **Protest and freedom songs**: "Vande Mataram", "Sare Jahan Se Achha" (Iqbal), "We Shall Overcome" (sung in Hindi as "Hum Honge Kamyab"), Bob Marley, Fela Kuti, Victor Jara, Faiz's "Hum Dekhenge" (sung by Iqbal Bano, 1986).
- ☐ **Caste, gender and music**: hereditary musician communities (Mirasi, Manganiyar, devadasi and isai vellalar traditions), women performers' struggles, debates on access to Carnatic music (T. M. Krishna, *A Southern Music*, 2013).
- ☐ **Music and religion**: chant, bhajan, qawwali, hymns; debates on music in some religious traditions.
- ☐ **Music and technology**: who controls the algorithms, AI and authorship (MUS-00.08).

## MUS-26 The music lover's practical guide

- ☐ **MUS-26.1 How to start listening to Indian classical music**: begin with instrumental music and short recordings; learn to recognise the alap and the sam; five beginner ragas (Yaman, Bhupali, Bhairavi, Kafi, Desh) and five Carnatic ragas (Mohanam, Kalyani, Hamsadhwani, Shankarabharanam, Kharaharapriya); attend a small baithak.
- ☐ **MUS-26.2 How to start with Western classical, jazz and world music**: starter lists (each linked to registers).
- ☐ **MUS-26.3 How to listen closely**: five questions (what is the pulse, what is the melody doing, who is leading, how does it change, how does it make me feel).
- ☐ **MUS-26.4 Learning an instrument or singing**: choosing a teacher, online lessons, riyaz routines, graded exams (links to INS).
- ☐ **MUS-26.5 Festivals and concert seasons**: Sawai Gandharva Bhimsen Mahotsav (Pune, December, since 1953), the Madras December Season (Music Academy, since 1927–28), Tansen Samaroh (Gwalior), Saptak (Ahmedabad), Dover Lane (Kolkata), Harballabh (Jalandhar, since 1875, among the oldest), Rajasthan International Folk Festival (Jodhpur), Hornbill (Nagaland), NH7 Weekender; Glastonbury, WOMAD (founded by Peter Gabriel, 1982), Montreux Jazz, the BBC Proms, Coachella, Tomorrowland. Check dates every year.
- ☐ **MUS-26.6 Where to listen legally and free**: All India Radio and Prasar Bharati archives, YouTube channels of labels and archives, Archive of Indian Music, Smithsonian Folkways, public radio, library music collections. Always dated.
- ☐ **MUS-26.7 Museums of music**: Sangeet Natak Akademi's museum (Delhi), the Indian Music Experience Museum (Bengaluru, 2019), Musical Instrument Museum (Phoenix), Grammy Museum, Beethoven House (Bonn).
- ☐ **MUS-26.8 Listening well**: protect your hearing (keep volume moderate; the WHO's Make Listening Safe guidance), support artists by buying tickets and music, avoid piracy.

## MUS-E Editor's notes (corrections and decisions on the brief)

1. **"Every song" cannot fit in one booklet**: the booklet gives landmark registers and a plan to grow year-wise lists on the website (MUS-07.3).
2. **Raga time theory** is presented as a living tradition of feeling, not a scientific law, and legends (Tansen's Deepak lighting lamps) are marked as legends.
3. **Attributions to Amir Khusrau** (sitar, tabla, qawwali) are marked as traditional; historians debate several of them. The Instruments index (INS) explains the sitar and tabla origins in detail.
4. **"World music"** is explained as a marketing label, not a genre.
5. **Sales and streaming figures** are given only with source and date.
6. **Latest results** were checked on 3 November 2026; the 2026 Polar Music Prize was not verified, so the 2025 laureates are given. Some reports disagreed on the 2026 Grammy Song of the Year, so only the clearly reported winners are listed.
