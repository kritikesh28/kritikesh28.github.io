# PART 4 — HISTORY AND TIMELINE

> **About this Part.** The story of music in order, with India and the world side by side, then the story of how technology changed listening. Ancient dates are scholarly estimates.

## MUS-14 Timeline of music

> **Before you start — MUS-14.** Music history is not one straight line: many traditions grew at the same time and influenced each other. **See also:** MUS-08.1 Western periods, MUS-01 Indian classical.

### ☐ MUS-14.1 Prehistory and the ancient world (to about 500 CE)

- ☐ **Bone flutes** (about 40,000 years ago, Germany); Bhimbetka rock art of dance (India).
- ☐ **Mesopotamia**: the lyres of Ur (about 2500 BCE); the Hurrian Hymn (about 1400 BCE), the oldest substantially surviving written melody.
- ☐ **India**: **Samaveda** (the Veda of melody); Gandharva music; Bharata's ***Natyashastra*** (about 200 BCE–200 CE) with chapters on music, instruments, shruti and rasa.
- ☐ **China**: bronze bells of Marquis Yi of Zeng (433 BCE), 65 tuned bells; music as part of Confucian ritual.
- ☐ **Greece**: Pythagoras and the mathematics of intervals; Greek modes; the Seikilos epitaph (about 1st century CE), the oldest complete surviving song.
- ☐ **Egypt**: harps, lutes and temple music.

### ☐ MUS-14.2 Medieval period (about 500–1400)

- ☐ **India**: Matanga's *Brihaddeshi* (about 9th century), the first text to use the word "raga" in its technical sense; Jayadeva's *Gita Govinda* (12th century, sung in temples); **Sharngadeva's *Sangita Ratnakara*** (13th century); **Amir Khusrau** (1253–1325) at the Delhi Sultanate court, credited by tradition with qawwali, tarana and other innovations (some attributions are legendary).
- ☐ **Islamic world**: al-Kindi and al-Farabi's *Great Book of Music* (10th century); Ziryab's music school in Córdoba.
- ☐ **Europe**: Gregorian chant, Guido d'Arezzo's notation (about 1025), troubadours, early polyphony.
- ☐ **East Asia**: Tang court music; gagaku comes to Japan.

### ☐ MUS-14.3 Early modern period (about 1400–1750)

- ☐ **India**: the bhakti song movement (Kabir, Meera, Surdas, Purandara Dasa); Raja Man Singh Tomar of Gwalior and dhrupad (15th century); **Tansen** at Akbar's court; the *Ain-i-Akbari* lists musicians of the court; Venkatamakhin's melakarta theory (17th century).
- ☐ **Europe**: printing of music (Ottaviano Petrucci, 1501); Renaissance polyphony; opera (about 1600); Baroque (Bach, Handel, Vivaldi).
- ☐ **Africa and the Americas**: the transatlantic slave trade carries African music to the Americas.

### ☐ MUS-14.4 The long 19th century (about 1750–1914)

- ☐ **India**: the Carnatic Trinity (Tyagaraja, Dikshitar, Syama Sastri); the rise of khayal and gharanas; Wajid Ali Shah's Lucknow and thumri; harmonium arrives (mid-19th century; banned on All India Radio from 1940 to 1971 as "foreign"); the first music schools (Gandharva Mahavidyalaya, Lahore, 1901, by Paluskar).
- ☐ **Europe**: Classical and Romantic eras; public concerts; the piano in middle-class homes.
- ☐ **Americas**: spirituals, ragtime, the birth of blues and jazz (about 1900).
- ☐ **Technology**: phonograph (1877), gramophone (1887), first recordings in India (1902).

### ☐ MUS-14.5 The 20th century (1914–1999)

- ☐ **India**: Bhatkhande's *Hindustani Sangeet Paddhati* and the thaat system; the All-India Music Conferences (from 1916, Baroda); Madras Music Academy (1928) and the December Season; **All India Radio** (1936) and its national programme of music; film songs dominate from 1931; **Ravi Shankar** takes Indian music to Monterey (1967) and Woodstock (1969); cassette boom (T-Series, 1980s); MTV India and Indipop (1990s).
- ☐ **World**: jazz age (1920s), swing (1930s), bebop (1940s), rock and roll (1950s), the Beatles and the 1960s counterculture, reggae and punk (1970s), hip-hop and MTV (1981), grunge and Britpop (1990s), K-pop (1992 onward).

### ☐ MUS-14.6 The 21st century (2000 onward)

- ☐ Napster (1999) and the MP3 crisis; iTunes Store (2003); YouTube (2005); Spotify (2008); India's JioSaavn and Gaana; TikTok and Instagram Reels shape hits; vinyl revival; global K-pop, Latin and Afrobeats; AI music tools (2020s).
- ☐ **Industry today**: global recorded music revenue reached **US$31.7 billion in 2025**, up 6.4%, with streaming about 70% of revenue and 837 million paid subscription users (IFPI *Global Music Report 2026*, March 2026).

## MUS-15 How technology changed listening

> **Before you start — MUS-15.** Each new technology changed what music sounded like, how long songs were, who could make them and who could hear them. **See also:** TEC (audio technology).

- ☐ **MUS-15.1 Notation and printing**: music could travel without the musician.
- ☐ **MUS-15.2 Acoustic recording** (1877–1925): singers shouted into horns; loud voices like Gauhar Jaan's recorded best.
- ☐ **MUS-15.3 Electrical recording and microphones** (from 1925): soft "crooning" became possible.
- ☐ **MUS-15.4 Radio** (1920s): BBC (1922), Indian Broadcasting Company (1927), All India Radio (1936), Radio Ceylon's *Binaca Geetmala* (1952, hosted by Ameen Sayani), Vividh Bharati (1957).
- ☐ **MUS-15.5 Magnetic tape and multitrack** (1940s–60s): editing, overdubbing, the studio as an instrument.
- ☐ **MUS-15.6 The LP and the single** (1948–49): albums and 7-inch hits.
- ☐ **MUS-15.7 The electric guitar and amplification**: Les Paul, Fender (1950s); feedback and distortion as sound.
- ☐ **MUS-15.8 Synthesizers, drum machines and samplers** (1960s–80s): Moog, Roland TR-808 (1980), sampling in hip-hop.
- ☐ **MUS-15.9 The cassette and the Walkman** (Sony, 1979): personal music on the move; cheap local music in India.
- ☐ **MUS-15.10 Digital audio**: the CD (1982), MIDI (1983), digital audio workstations, Auto-Tune (1997).
- ☐ **MUS-15.11 The internet**: MP3, file-sharing, downloads, streaming, playlists and algorithms.
- ☐ **MUS-15.12 AI**: stem separation, generative music, voice cloning; debates on rights (MUS-21.4).

# PART 6 — AWARDS, CHARTS AND RECORDS

> **About this Part.** Each award has the same card: who gives it, since when, for what, the first winner, landmark winners and the latest winner, with the date checked. Latest results were checked on **3 November 2026**. Industry awards (charts, MTV, Billboard) measure popularity, not quality.

## MUS-17 International music awards

### ☐ MUS-17.1 Grammy Awards

Given by the **Recording Academy** (USA) since **1959** (for 1958 recordings), voted by music professionals, in about 90 to 95 categories. **First Album of the Year:** Henry Mancini, *The Music from Peter Gunn*. **Landmarks:** Ravi Shankar (1967, first Indian winner); Beyoncé holds the record for most Grammys won by any artist; Zakir Hussain won three in one night (2024). **Indian winners include** Ravi Shankar, Vishwa Mohan Bhatt (1994, *A Meeting by the River*), Zakir Hussain, A. R. Rahman (two in 2010), Ricky Kej (three), Falguni Shah (2022), Shankar Mahadevan and Shakti (2024); Anoushka Shankar has had many nominations. **Latest (68th Grammys, 1 February 2026):** Album of the Year to **Bad Bunny**, *DeBÍ TiRAR MáS FOToS*, widely reported as the first all-Spanish-language album to win; Best New Artist **Olivia Dean**; Best Global Music Album *Caetano e Bethânia Ao Vivo* (Caetano Veloso and Maria Bethânia). **Tags:** #grammy

### ☐ MUS-17.2 Other international awards

- ☐ **Polar Music Prize** (Sweden, 1992; founded by Stig Anderson of ABBA; first laureates Paul McCartney and the Baltic states): "music's Nobel". Ravi Shankar (1998) is the Indian laureate. **Latest verified (2025):** Queen, Herbie Hancock and Barbara Hannigan.
- ☐ **Latin Grammy Awards** (2000): Spanish- and Portuguese-language music.
- ☐ **Kennedy Center Honors** (USA, 1978) and the **Rock and Roll Hall of Fame** (1983; first inductees 1986).
- ☐ **WOMEX Award** and **Songlines Music Awards**: world music.
- ☐ **Gramophone Awards** (UK, 1977): classical recordings.
- ☐ **Pulitzer Prize for Music** (1943; Kendrick Lamar 2018).
- ☐ **MTV Video Music Awards** (1984), **Billboard Music Awards**, **MAMA** (Asia), **BET Awards**: popularity and industry awards.
- ☐ **UNESCO International Music Council's Music Rights Awards** and the **Aga Khan Music Awards** (2019).

## MUS-18 National music awards, country by country

### ☐ MUS-18.1 India

- ☐ **Sangeet Natak Akademi Awards** (founded 1952): India's national honour for performing artists in music, dance and theatre, given every year in Hindustani and Carnatic vocal and instrumental music, folk and other fields; the **Sangeet Natak Akademi Fellowship (Akademi Ratna)** is the highest. **Ustad Bismillah Khan Yuva Puraskar** (2006) for young artists.
- ☐ **Bharat Ratna to musicians**: M. S. Subbulakshmi (1998), Ravi Shankar (1999), Lata Mangeshkar (2001), Bismillah Khan (2001), Bhimsen Joshi (2009), Bhupen Hazarika (2019).
- ☐ **Padma awards**: many musicians each year.
- ☐ **Sangita Kalanidhi** (Madras Music Academy, 1942): the top title in Carnatic music, given at the December Season; first recipient Mysore Vasudevachar. **Latest:** 2025 to violinist **R. K. Shriramkumar**; vocalist and veena artist **Jayanthi Kumaresh** is named for the Academy's 100th conference (December 2026–January 2027).
- ☐ **Tansen Samman** (Madhya Pradesh government, 1980): Hindustani music, at the Tansen Samaroh in Gwalior.
- ☐ **Kalidas Samman** (Madhya Pradesh), **Sangeet Martand**, state Sangeet Natak Akademi awards.
- ☐ **Film music awards**: National Film Awards (Best Music Direction, Best Playback Singer, Best Lyrics), Filmfare (Best Music Director from 1954, first Naushad for *Baiju Bawra*), IIFA, Mirchi Music Awards.
- ☐ **Dadasaheb Phalke Award** to musicians: Naushad (1981), Lata Mangeshkar (1989), Bhupen Hazarika (1992), Asha Bhosle (2000), Manna Dey (2007).

## MUS-19 Charts, sales and records

> **Before you start — MUS-19.** Charts measure what was bought, streamed or played, by rules that change over time. Sales claims are often estimates. **See also:** BOK-17 (book best-sellers).

- ☐ **MUS-19.1 Major charts**: Billboard Hot 100 (1958) and Billboard 200; UK Official Charts (1952); Billboard Global 200 (2020); Spotify and YouTube charts; India's IMI charts and Radio Mirchi lists; the historic *Binaca Geetmala* countdown (1952–1994).
- ☐ **MUS-19.2 Best-selling artists (estimates)**: The Beatles, Elvis Presley and Michael Jackson are usually listed at the top; Taylor Swift was IFPI's Global Recording Artist of the Year for a record sixth time (2025).
- ☐ **MUS-19.3 Best-selling albums (estimates)**: *Thriller* (often cited as the best-selling album ever), *Back in Black*, *The Dark Side of the Moon*; in India, film soundtracks such as *Aashiqui* (1990) and *Maine Pyar Kiya* sold in tens of millions of cassettes by label claims.
- ☐ **MUS-19.4 Records and curiosities**: Guinness listed Lata Mangeshkar (1974) and later Asha Bhosle (2011) for the most studio recordings; "Happy Birthday to You" is among the most sung songs; John Cage's *4′33″* (1952) is a piece of silence; *As Slow as Possible* (Cage) is being performed in Halberstadt, Germany, until 2640.
