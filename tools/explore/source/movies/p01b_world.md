## MOV-02 Cinema of the Americas

> **Before you start — MOV-02.** From Hollywood, the most powerful film industry in history, to the Golden Age of Mexico, Brazil's Cinema Novo and Argentina's new cinema. **See also:** MOV-10.1 Oscars, MOV-09.4 Studio era, MOV-08 Movements.

### ☐ MOV-02.1 United States: Hollywood

The American film industry, centred in Los Angeles since the 1910s, the world's largest by revenue. Known for the studio and star systems, genre storytelling, blockbusters, franchises and global distribution. **Tags:** #usa #english #hollywood

- ☐ **The Big Five and Little Three (1920s–1948)**: MGM, Paramount, Warner Bros., 20th Century-Fox, RKO; Universal, Columbia, United Artists.
- ☐ **Classical Hollywood (1927–1960)**: invisible editing, three-act stories, the Production Code (1934–1968).
- ☐ **New Hollywood (1967–1980)**: Coppola, Scorsese, Spielberg, Lucas, Altman; the director as author.
- ☐ **Blockbuster era (1975–)**: *Jaws* (1975) and *Star Wars* (1977) created the summer blockbuster.
- ☐ **Franchises and cinematic universes (2008–)**: Marvel Cinematic Universe, DC, Pixar, Disney.
- ☐ **Streaming studios**: Netflix, Amazon MGM, Apple, now major producers.

**Sub-industries and centres of American film**

- ☐ **American independent cinema**: Sundance, Miramax, A24, Neon; John Cassavetes to the Safdie brothers.
- ☐ **New York cinema**: Woody Allen, Spike Lee, Martin Scorsese's city films.
- ☐ **Georgia / Atlanta ("Y'allywood")**: a production hub since tax incentives of 2008.
- ☐ **African-American cinema**: Oscar Micheaux's "race films" (1919–), Blaxploitation (1970s), Spike Lee, Ava DuVernay, Jordan Peele, Ryan Coogler.
- ☐ **Hispanic and Latino American film**, **Asian-American film** (*Everything Everywhere All at Once*, 2022), **Native American film** (*Smoke Signals*, 1998).
- ☐ **Faith-based, Christian films** and the Utah-based Latter-day Saint film scene.
- ☐ **Animation studios**: Disney, Pixar, DreamWorks, Laika, Sony Pictures Animation.

### ☐ MOV-02.2 Canada

English-language production in Toronto and Vancouver ("Hollywood North") and a distinct Québécois French cinema in Montreal. The National Film Board of Canada (1939) pioneered documentary and animation (Norman McLaren). Directors: David Cronenberg, Atom Egoyan, Denis Villeneuve, Xavier Dolan, Sarah Polley. **Tags:** #canada #english #french

### ☐ MOV-02.3 Mexico: the Golden Age and the "Three Amigos"

Mexico's **Época de Oro** (about 1936–1959) made it the leading Spanish-language film industry, with stars María Félix, Pedro Infante and Cantinflas, and director Emilio "El Indio" Fernández. Luis Buñuel made *Los Olvidados* (1950) here. Today: Alfonso Cuarón (*Roma*, 2018), Guillermo del Toro, Alejandro G. Iñárritu. Studios: Estudios Churubusco. Award: the Ariel. **Tags:** #mexico #spanish #latinamerica

| Year | Film | Director | Lead cast | Box office / reception | Awards and why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1936 | Allá en el Rancho Grande | Fernando de Fuentes | Tito Guízar | Hit across Latin America | Launched the ranchera comedy and the Golden Age |
| 1950 | Los Olvidados | Luis Buñuel | Alfonso Mejía | — | Best Director, Cannes 1951 |
| 2000 | Amores perros | Alejandro G. Iñárritu | Gael García Bernal | Hit | Oscar nomination; new Mexican cinema |
| 2001 | Y tu mamá también | Alfonso Cuarón | Gael García Bernal, Diego Luna | Hit | Road film icon |
| 2018 | Roma | Alfonso Cuarón | Yalitza Aparicio | Netflix | Golden Lion; Oscars for Director and Foreign Language Film |

### ☐ MOV-02.4 Brazil: from chanchadas to Cinema Novo

Brazil's popular **chanchada** musical comedies (1930s–1950s), then **Cinema Novo** (1960s), "an idea in the head and a camera in the hand" (Glauber Rocha). Studios Vera Cruz and Atlântida; now Globo Filmes. Landmarks: *O Pagador de Promessas* (1962, Palme d'Or), *Central do Brasil* (1998, Golden Bear), *Cidade de Deus* (2002, Fernando Meirelles), *Ainda Estou Aqui* (*I'm Still Here*, 2024, Walter Salles, Brazil's first Oscar for International Feature). **Tags:** #brazil #portuguese #latinamerica

### ☐ MOV-02.5 Argentina

One of Latin America's oldest industries (tango musicals of the 1930s with Carlos Gardel). The **New Argentine Cinema** of the 1990s: Lucrecia Martel, Pablo Trapero. Oscar winners *La historia oficial* (1985) and *El secreto de sus ojos* (2009). **Tags:** #argentina #spanish

### ☐ MOV-02.6 Chile, Colombia, Peru, Bolivia, Venezuela, Uruguay

- ☐ **Chile**: Patricio Guzmán (*The Battle of Chile*), Pablo Larraín, *Una mujer fantástica* (2017, Oscar).
- ☐ **Colombia**: Ciro Guerra's *Embrace of the Serpent* (2015, Oscar nomination), Víctor Gaviria.
- ☐ **Peru**: Claudia Llosa's *La teta asustada* (2009, Golden Bear).
- ☐ **Bolivia**: Jorge Sanjinés and Grupo Ukamau, cinema in Quechua and Aymara.
- ☐ **Venezuela** and **Uruguay**: *Desde allá* (2015, Golden Lion, Venezuela); *Whisky* (2004, Uruguay).

### ☐ MOV-02.7 Cuba and the Caribbean

- ☐ **Cuba**: ICAIC (founded 1959); Tomás Gutiérrez Alea (*Memories of Underdevelopment*, 1968; *Strawberry and Chocolate*, 1993); the Havana Film Festival (1979).
- ☐ **Jamaica**: *The Harder They Come* (1972), the film that took reggae to the world.
- ☐ **Puerto Rico, Dominican Republic, Haiti, Trinidad and Tobago**: growing industries; Haitian director Raoul Peck (*I Am Not Your Negro*, 2016).

## MOV-03 Cinema of Europe

> **Before you start — MOV-03.** Europe gave the world the first film shows and most of the great film movements. Learn each country's industry here and the movements in Part 3. **See also:** MOV-11 Festivals (Cannes, Venice, Berlin), MOV-08 Movements.

### ☐ MOV-03.1 France

The birthplace of public cinema (Lumière, Méliès, Pathé and Gaumont, the world's oldest film company still active, 1895). Poetic Realism (1930s), the **Nouvelle Vague** (1959–), and a state support system (CNC, 1946) that protects French film. Award: the César; festival: Cannes. **Tags:** #france #french

### ☐ MOV-03.2 United Kingdom

British cinema: Ealing comedies, Hitchcock's British years, the Free Cinema and kitchen-sink realism, Hammer horror, James Bond (1962–), Merchant Ivory, Working Title. Studios: Pinewood, Shepperton, Leavesden (Harry Potter). Institutions: the BFI (1933), BAFTA. Directors: David Lean, Powell and Pressburger, Ken Loach, Mike Leigh, Danny Boyle, Christopher Nolan, Steve McQueen. **Tags:** #uk #english

### ☐ MOV-03.3 Italy

From the silent epic *Cabiria* (1914) to **Neorealism** (*Rome, Open City*, 1945; *Bicycle Thieves*, 1948), the art films of Fellini and Antonioni, **commedia all'italiana**, Sergio Leone's **spaghetti Westerns** and the **giallo** thrillers of Dario Argento. Cinecittà studios (1937). Award: David di Donatello; festival: Venice. **Tags:** #italy #italian

| 1997 | Life Is Beautiful | Roberto Benigni | Roberto Benigni | International hit | Three Oscars including Best Actor |

### ☐ MOV-03.4 Germany and Austria

German Expressionism (*The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari*, 1920; *Metropolis*, 1927) and the giant UFA studio; Leni Riefenstahl and Nazi propaganda; post-war DEFA in East Germany; the **New German Cinema** (Fassbinder, Herzog, Wenders, Schlöndorff); contemporary: *The Lives of Others* (2006), Christian Petzold, Maren Ade. Austria: Michael Haneke, Ulrich Seidl. **Tags:** #germany #austria #german

### ☐ MOV-03.5 Spain and Portugal

- ☐ **Spain**: Luis Buñuel, Luis García Berlanga, Carlos Saura; Pedro Almodóvar and the Movida; Guillermo del Toro's Spanish films; award: the Goya (1987).
- ☐ **Portugal**: Manoel de Oliveira (directed until past 100), Pedro Costa, Miguel Gomes.

### ☐ MOV-03.6 Nordic cinema

- ☐ **Sweden**: Victor Sjöström and the silent golden age; Ingmar Bergman (*The Seventh Seal*, 1957); Roy Andersson; Ruben Östlund (two Palmes d'Or).
- ☐ **Denmark**: Carl Theodor Dreyer; Lars von Trier and **Dogme 95**; Thomas Vinterberg (*Another Round*, Oscar 2021).
- ☐ **Norway**: Joachim Trier (*Sentimental Value*, Oscar for International Feature 2026).
- ☐ **Finland**: Aki Kaurismäki. **Iceland**: Baltasar Kormákur.

### ☐ MOV-03.7 Russia and the Soviet Union

Soviet **montage** cinema of the 1920s (Eisenstein's *Battleship Potemkin*, 1925; Dziga Vertov's *Man with a Movie Camera*, 1929; Pudovkin, Kuleshov), Mosfilm (1923), Socialist Realism, the Thaw (*The Cranes Are Flying*, Palme d'Or 1958), Andrei Tarkovsky (*Andrei Rublev*, *Solaris*, *Stalker*), Sergei Bondarchuk's *War and Peace* (Oscar 1969), Nikita Mikhalkov, Andrey Zvyagintsev (*Leviathan*; Grand Prix Cannes 2026 for *Minotaur*). Soviet audiences adored Raj Kapoor and Mithun Chakraborty. **Tags:** #russia #ussr #russian

### ☐ MOV-03.8 Central and Eastern Europe

- ☐ **Poland**: the Polish School (Andrzej Wajda), Roman Polański, Krzysztof Kieślowski (*Three Colours*), Paweł Pawlikowski (*Ida*, Oscar 2015); Łódź Film School.
- ☐ **Czech Republic and Slovakia**: the Czechoslovak New Wave (Miloš Forman, Věra Chytilová, Jiří Menzel's *Closely Watched Trains*, Oscar 1968); Jan Švankmajer's animation; Karlovy Vary festival.
- ☐ **Hungary**: Miklós Jancsó, Béla Tarr, István Szabó, László Nemes (*Son of Saul*, Oscar 2016).
- ☐ **Romania**: the **Romanian New Wave** (2000s): Cristi Puiu, Cristian Mungiu (Palmes d'Or 2007 and 2026), Corneliu Porumboiu, Radu Jude.
- ☐ **Former Yugoslavia, Bulgaria, Greece (Theo Angelopoulos, Yorgos Lanthimos and the Greek Weird Wave), Georgia (Sergei Parajanov, Otar Iosseliani), Armenia, Ukraine (Alexander Dovzhenko), the Baltic states.**

### ☐ MOV-03.9 Belgium, Netherlands, Ireland, Switzerland

The Dardenne brothers (two Palmes d'Or, Belgium), Chantal Akerman (*Jeanne Dielman*, Sight and Sound's greatest film, 2022), Paul Verhoeven (Netherlands), Irish cinema (*The Banshees of Inisherin*, Cartoon Saloon's animation), Swiss director Jean-Luc Godard's later years.

## MOV-04 Cinema of Asia (beyond India)

> **Before you start — MOV-04.** Asia makes the majority of the world's films. East Asia's industries (China, Japan, Korea) rival Hollywood at home; South and West Asia have proud old traditions; Southeast Asia is the fastest-growing region.

### ☐ MOV-04.1 Japan

One of the oldest and most self-sufficient industries, with studios Shochiku, Nikkatsu, Toho (Godzilla, 1954), Daiei and Toei. Golden age of the 1950s: Akira Kurosawa, Yasujirō Ozu, Kenji Mizoguchi, Mikio Naruse. Also **anime** (Studio Ghibli, Toei Animation), **kaiju** monster films, **J-horror** (*Ringu*, 1998), jidaigeki samurai films. **Tags:** #japan #japanese #anime

| Year | Film | Director | Lead cast | Box office / reception | Awards and why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1950 | Rashomon | Akira Kurosawa | Toshirō Mifune, Machiko Kyō | — | Golden Lion 1951: opened the West to Japanese cinema |
| 1953 | Tokyo Story | Yasujirō Ozu | Chishū Ryū, Setsuko Hara | — | Topped Sight and Sound directors' poll (2012) |
| 1954 | Seven Samurai | Akira Kurosawa | Toshirō Mifune, Takashi Shimura | Hit | Template for the team-on-a-mission film (and Sholay's inspiration) |
| 1954 | Godzilla (Gojira) | Ishirō Honda | Akira Takarada | Hit | Nuclear-age monster; longest-running film franchise |
| 2001 | Spirited Away | Hayao Miyazaki | (voices) Rumi Hiiragi | Japan's top grosser for nearly two decades | Golden Bear 2002; Oscar for Animated Feature |
| 2020 | Demon Slayer: Mugen Train | Haruo Sotozaki | (voices) Natsuki Hanae | Japan's highest-grossing film | Anime's box-office power |
| 2023 | Godzilla Minus One | Takashi Yamazaki | Ryunosuke Kamiki | International hit | Oscar for Visual Effects: first for a Japanese film |

### ☐ MOV-04.2 China, Hong Kong and Taiwan

- ☐ **Mainland China**: Shanghai's golden age (1930s); Communist-era studios; the **Fifth Generation** (Zhang Yimou, Chen Kaige: *Farewell My Concubine*, Palme d'Or 1993); the Sixth Generation (Jia Zhangke); today the world's second-largest box office market; record hits *The Battle at Lake Changjin* (2021) and *Ne Zha 2* (2025, the highest-grossing animated film worldwide).
- ☐ **Hong Kong**: Shaw Brothers and Golden Harvest, **kung fu** and **wuxia** films, Bruce Lee, Jackie Chan, John Woo's "heroic bloodshed", Wong Kar-wai (*In the Mood for Love*, 2000), Hong Kong Film Awards.
- ☐ **Taiwan**: the Taiwan New Cinema (Hou Hsiao-hsien, Edward Yang), Ang Lee (*Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon*, 2000), Tsai Ming-liang; the Golden Horse Awards.
**Tags:** #china #hongkong #taiwan #chinese #mandarin #cantonese

### ☐ MOV-04.3 South Korea (and North Korea)

South Korea's cinema rose after the end of military censorship (1990s) with screen quotas and chaebol investment: Park Chan-wook (*Oldboy*, 2003), Bong Joon-ho (*Parasite*, 2019: Palme d'Or and the first non-English Best Picture Oscar), Lee Chang-dong, Hong Sang-soo, Kim Ki-duk; the record hit *The Admiral: Roaring Currents* (2014). Awards: Blue Dragon, Grand Bell; festival: Busan. North Korea has a state industry, Korean Feature Film Studio. **Tags:** #southkorea #korean #hallyu

### ☐ MOV-04.4 Pakistan (Lollywood)

Urdu and Punjabi films centred in Lahore ("Lollywood"), with a golden age in the 1960s, decline in the 1980s–1990s, and revival since *Khuda Kay Liye* (2007). Pashto films from Peshawar ("Pollywood"), Sindhi films from Karachi. Landmarks: *Jago Hua Savera* (1959), *Bol* (2011), *The Legend of Maula Jatt* (2022, Pakistan's highest-grossing film), *Joyland* (2022, Cannes Un Certain Regard Jury Prize). **Tags:** #pakistan #urdu #punjabi #lollywood

### ☐ MOV-04.5 Bangladesh (Dhallywood)

Bengali-language films from Dhaka ("Dhallywood"). First feature *Mukh O Mukhosh* (1956, Abdul Jabbar Khan); Zahir Raihan's *Jibon Theke Neya* (1970); Tareque Masud's *Matir Moina* (2002, FIPRESCI Prize, Cannes); Mostofa Sarwar Farooki. **Tags:** #bangladesh #bengali

### ☐ MOV-04.6 Nepal, Sri Lanka, Bhutan, Afghanistan, Maldives

- ☐ **Nepal**: Nepali films from Kathmandu ("Kollywood"); *Kalo Pothi* (2015); *Elephants in the Fog* (Jury Prize, Un Certain Regard, Cannes 2026).
- ☐ **Sri Lanka**: Sinhala cinema; Lester James Peries (*Rekava*, 1956), Prasanna Vithanage; Tamil films of Sri Lanka.
- ☐ **Bhutan**: Khyentse Norbu (*The Cup*, 1999), Pawo Choyning Dorji (*Lunana*, Oscar nomination 2022).
- ☐ **Afghanistan**: Siddiq Barmak's *Osama* (2003, Golden Globe); filmmakers in exile.
- ☐ **Maldives**: small Dhivehi-language industry.

### ☐ MOV-04.7 Iran (Persian cinema)

Iranian cinema grew from the popular "Film Farsi" before 1979 and the **Iranian New Wave** (Dariush Mehrjui's *The Cow*, 1969), and after the revolution produced masters despite censorship: Abbas Kiarostami (*Taste of Cherry*, Palme d'Or 1997), Mohsen Makhmalbaf, Majid Majidi (*Children of Heaven*, Oscar nomination), Jafar Panahi (Golden Bear 2015, Palme d'Or 2025 for *It Was Just an Accident*), Asghar Farhadi (two Oscars: *A Separation*, *The Salesman*). Fajr Film Festival. **Tags:** #iran #persian #farsi

### ☐ MOV-04.8 Turkey (Yeşilçam)

Turkish popular cinema of the **Yeşilçam** era (1950s–1980s), named after a street in Istanbul, made up to about 300 films a year. Masters: Yılmaz Güney (*Yol*, Palme d'Or 1982), Nuri Bilge Ceylan (*Winter Sleep*, Palme d'Or 2014), Ferzan Özpetek. Turkish TV dramas (dizi) are now global. Berlin's 2026 Golden Bear went to İlker Çatak's Turkish-language *Yellow Letters*. **Tags:** #turkey #turkish

### ☐ MOV-04.9 The Arab world and Israel

- ☐ **Egypt ("Hollywood on the Nile")**: Studio Misr (1935); the Arab world's biggest industry; Youssef Chahine (Cannes 50th Anniversary Prize, 1997); star Omar Sharif; singer-stars Umm Kulthum and Abdel Halim Hafez; Cairo festival (1976).
- ☐ **Lebanon**: Nadine Labaki (*Capernaum*, 2018, Cannes Jury Prize), Ziad Doueiri.
- ☐ **Palestine**: Elia Suleiman, Hany Abu-Assad (*Paradise Now*, *Omar*).
- ☐ **Saudi Arabia**: Haifaa al-Mansour (*Wadjda*, 2012), cinemas reopened in 2018; Red Sea festival (2021).
- ☐ **Syria, Iraq, Jordan (*Theeb*), Morocco, Tunisia, Algeria (Maghreb cinema, MOV-05.1)**, the Gulf states.
- ☐ **Israel**: Hebrew-language cinema; *Waltz with Bashir* (2008), *Foxtrot* (2017), festivals in Jerusalem and Haifa.

### ☐ MOV-04.10 Central Asia and the Caucasus

Kazakhstan (the Kazakh New Wave, Darezhan Omirbayev), Kyrgyzstan (Aktan Arym Kubat), Uzbekistan (Uzbekfilm), Tajikistan, Mongolia; Georgia and Armenia (in MOV-03.8).

### ☐ MOV-04.11 Southeast Asia

- ☐ **Philippines**: the golden age of the 1950s studios (LVN, Sampaguita), the second golden age of the 1970s (Lino Brocka, Ishmael Bernal), Lav Diaz (Golden Lion 2016), Brillante Mendoza; Metro Manila Film Festival.
- ☐ **Indonesia**: Usmar Ismail, "father of Indonesian film" (*Darah dan Doa*, 1950, whose shooting day is National Film Day); horror boom; Joko Anwar; *The Raid* (2011, made with Welsh director Gareth Evans).
- ☐ **Thailand**: Apichatpong Weerasethakul (*Uncle Boonmee*, Palme d'Or 2010), *Ong-Bak* (Tony Jaa), *How to Make Millions Before Grandma Dies* (2024, a regional hit).
- ☐ **Vietnam**: Trần Anh Hùng (*The Scent of Green Papaya*), a fast-growing box office.
- ☐ **Malaysia, Singapore (Anthony Chen, Eric Khoo), Cambodia (Rithy Panh), Myanmar, Laos.**

## MOV-05 Cinema of Africa and Oceania

> **Before you start — MOV-05.** Africa has the world's fastest-growing film culture, from Nollywood's volume to the art cinema of Senegal, Mali and Burkina Faso, and FESPACO, Africa's biggest festival. Oceania adds Australia, New Zealand and Pacific voices.

### ☐ MOV-05.1 North Africa (the Maghreb and Egypt)

Algeria's *Chronicle of the Years of Fire* (1975, Mohammed Lakhdar-Hamina: Palme d'Or, the first for an African film); Tunisia's Moufida Tlatli and the Carthage Film Festival (1966, Africa's and the Arab world's oldest festival); Morocco's Ouarzazate studios, where many Hollywood epics are shot (Egypt: MOV-04.9).

### ☐ MOV-05.2 West Africa: Nollywood, Ghallywood and Francophone cinema

- ☐ **Nigeria (Nollywood)**: born with the home-video drama *Living in Bondage* (1992, Chris Obi Rapu); now one of the world's largest producers by volume; English, Yoruba, Igbo and Hausa (Kannywood, from Kano) films; landmarks *The Wedding Party* (2016), *Lionheart* (2018), *King of Boys*, *A Tribe Called Judah* (2023, box-office record). Award: AMVCA and AMAA.
- ☐ **Ghana (Ghallywood)** and the Twi-language "Kumawood".
- ☐ **Senegal**: Ousmane Sembène, "father of African cinema" (*Black Girl*, 1966), Djibril Diop Mambéty (*Touki Bouki*, 1973), Mati Diop (*Atlantics*, Cannes Grand Prix 2019; *Dahomey*, Golden Bear 2024).
- ☐ **Mali and Burkina Faso**: Souleymane Cissé (*Yeelen*, 1987, Cannes Jury Prize), Idrissa Ouédraogo; FESPACO in Ouagadougou (1969).
- ☐ **Mauritania**: Abderrahmane Sissako (*Timbuktu*, 2014). **Côte d'Ivoire, Cameroon, Niger.**

### ☐ MOV-05.3 East and Southern Africa

- ☐ **South Africa**: *Tsotsi* (2005, Oscar for Foreign Language Film), *District 9* (2009); Cape Town studios.
- ☐ **Kenya** ("Riverwood"), **Tanzania** ("Bongo" films), **Uganda** (Wakaliwood's micro-budget action, Ramon Film Productions), **Ethiopia** (Haile Gerima), **Rwanda**, **Zimbabwe**, **Somalia**, **Sudan** (*Goodbye Julia*, 2023).

### ☐ MOV-05.4 Australia, New Zealand and the Pacific

- ☐ **Australia**: *The Story of the Kelly Gang* (1906), often called the world's first feature-length narrative film; the **Australian New Wave** of the 1970s (Peter Weir, *Picnic at Hanging Rock*; George Miller, *Mad Max*); Baz Luhrmann; Indigenous filmmakers Warwick Thornton, Rachel Perkins; AACTA Awards.
- ☐ **New Zealand**: Jane Campion (*The Piano*, Palme d'Or 1993, the first by a woman director), Peter Jackson (*The Lord of the Rings*, Weta), Taika Waititi; Māori cinema (*Whale Rider*, 2002).
- ☐ **Pacific Islands**: Fiji, Samoa, Papua New Guinea, Hawaiian films; *Tanna* (2015, Vanuatu, Oscar nomination).

### ☐ MOV-05.5 Diaspora and transnational cinemas

Films made by migrant communities between two homelands: Indian diaspora cinema (Mira Nair, Gurinder Chadha's *Bend It Like Beckham*, Deepa Mehta's *Elements Trilogy*), Black British cinema, Beur cinema in France, Turkish-German cinema (Fatih Akin), Chinese diaspora films (*Crazy Rich Asians*), and global co-productions.

### ☐ MOV-05.6 How the registers grow

The landmark registers above are the trunk. On the website each industry will grow a **complete year-wise register** in this order: (1) every award-winning and nominated film from the official award databases (MOV-10 to MOV-12); (2) every film in national archives and catalogues (NFAI/NFDC for India, Library of Congress for the USA, BFI for the UK, CNC for France, and their equivalents); (3) every film with an entry in Wikidata (which links to IMDb and national IDs), filtered by country of origin and original language, sorted by year of first release. Each row keeps the same fields as above, plus language, runtime, certificate and streaming availability (dated). Box-office data will come only from named trade sources and will always say gross or net, domestic or worldwide, and the date.
