# PART 2 — GENRES, FORMS AND FORMATS

> **About this Part.** The second great classification: what kind of film it is. A genre is a family of films that share a promise to the audience (a thriller promises suspense; a comedy promises laughter). Most films belong to more than one genre. Each entry gives one or two lines and example films from different countries, so you can compare India with the world.

## MOV-06 Film genres and sub-genres

> **Before you start — MOV-06.** Twenty genre families, each with its sub-genres. Use it to find the kind of film you love, then jump to the industries (Part 1) that make it best. **See also:** BOK genre fiction (shared roots), MUS-film scores.

### ☐ MOV-06.1 Drama

Films that take real human problems seriously, aiming at emotion and truth more than thrills. Examples: *Pather Panchali* (India), *Tokyo Story* (Japan), *A Separation* (Iran). **Tags:** #drama

- ☐ **Family drama**: the joys and fights of families; *Hum Aapke Hain Koun..!*, *Kapoor & Sons*, *Shoplifters* (Japan).
- ☐ **Social drama / social problem film**: films that question an injustice; *Do Bigha Zamin*, *Article 15*, *Jai Bhim*.
- ☐ **Melodrama**: heightened emotion, clear good and evil, big music; *Mother India*, Douglas Sirk's *All That Heaven Allows*.
- ☐ **Coming-of-age**: growing up; *The 400 Blows*, *Udaan*, *Boyhood*.
- ☐ **Legal and courtroom drama**: *12 Angry Men*, *Jolly LLB*, *Court*, *Anatomy of a Fall*.
- ☐ **Political drama**: power and its abuse; *Z* (Costa-Gavras), *Raajneeti*, *Garm Hava*.
- ☐ **Medical, sports, workplace and school dramas**: *Chak De! India*, *Dangal*, *Taare Zameen Par*, *Rocky*, *Moneyball*.
- ☐ **Slice-of-life**: ordinary days without a big plot; Ozu, Hrishikesh Mukherjee's middle cinema, *Paterson*.
- ☐ **Tragedy**: a fall that feels inevitable; *Devdas*, *Kaagaz Ke Phool*, *Raging Bull*.

### ☐ MOV-06.2 Comedy

Films built to make us laugh, from slapstick to satire. **Tags:** #comedy

- ☐ **Slapstick and physical comedy**: Chaplin, Keaton, Mehmood, Jagdeep; *Andaz Apna Apna*.
- ☐ **Romantic comedy (rom-com)**: *It Happened One Night*, *Chupke Chupke*, *When Harry Met Sally...*, *Jab We Met*.
- ☐ **Screwball comedy**: fast talk and battles of the sexes (1930s Hollywood).
- ☐ **Satire**: laughter that attacks folly; *Dr. Strangelove*, *Jaane Bhi Do Yaaro*, *Peepli Live*.
- ☐ **Black (dark) comedy**: humour about death and crime; *Fargo*, *Delhi Belly*, *Andhadhun* (thriller-comedy).
- ☐ **Parody and spoof**: *Airplane!*, *Om Shanti Om* (homage-parody).
- ☐ **Comedy of errors and mistaken identity**: *Angoor* (from Shakespeare), *Hera Pheri*.
- ☐ **Situational and middle-class comedy**: Hrishikesh Mukherjee (*Golmaal*, 1979), Basu Chatterjee.
- ☐ **Stand-up and sketch films, mockumentary**: *This Is Spinal Tap*.

### ☐ MOV-06.3 Action

Films driven by physical conflict, stunts and spectacle. **Tags:** #action

- ☐ **Martial arts**: *Enter the Dragon*, *Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon*, *The Raid*.
- ☐ **Mass / masala action (India)**: the hero who defeats fifty men; *Baashha*, *Pokiri*, *K.G.F*, *Pushpa*.
- ☐ **Cop and buddy-cop**: *Lethal Weapon*, *Singham*, *Zanjeer*.
- ☐ **Heist and caper**: *Ocean's Eleven*, *Dhoom*, *Special 26*.
- ☐ **Disaster**: *The Towering Inferno*, *Titanic* (in part), *2018* (Malayalam flood film).
- ☐ **Spy and espionage action**: James Bond, *Mission: Impossible*, *Ek Tha Tiger*, *Baby*.
- ☐ **Superhero**: *Superman* (1978), *The Dark Knight*, Marvel films, *Krrish*, *Minnal Murali*.
- ☐ **Gun-fu and "heroic bloodshed"**: John Woo, *John Wick*.
- ☐ **Car and chase films**: *Bullitt*, *Fast & Furious*, *Mad Max: Fury Road*.

### ☐ MOV-06.4 Adventure

Journeys to unknown places, quests and treasure. *Raiders of the Lost Ark*, *Lawrence of Arabia*, *Life of Pi*, *Kantara* (folk adventure). Sub-genres: **swashbuckler** (pirates and swords), **jungle and survival adventure** (*The Revenant*, *Manjummel Boys*), **lost world**, **treasure hunt**, **sea adventure**. **Tags:** #adventure

### ☐ MOV-06.5 Thriller and suspense

Films that keep us anxious about what happens next. **Tags:** #thriller

- ☐ **Psychological thriller**: *Psycho*, *Black Swan*, *Raat*.
- ☐ **Crime thriller**: *Heat*, *Drishyam*, *Vikram Vedha*.
- ☐ **Political and conspiracy thriller**: *All the President's Men*, *Madras Cafe*.
- ☐ **Erotic thriller**, **legal thriller**, **techno-thriller**, **survival thriller** (*Buried*).
- ☐ **Revenge thriller**: *Oldboy*, *Kill Bill*, *Badlapur*.
- ☐ **Serial-killer and investigative thriller**: *The Silence of the Lambs*, *Memories of Murder*, *Ratsasan*.
- ☐ **Hitchcockian suspense**: the bomb under the table that the audience knows about.

### ☐ MOV-06.6 Crime and gangster

The world of criminals, police and justice. *The Godfather*, *Goodfellas*, *Nayakan*, *Satya*, *Gangs of Wasseypur*, *City of God*, Hong Kong triad films (*Infernal Affairs*). Sub-genres: **film noir** (1940s–1950s shadowy crime films: *Double Indemnity*; neo-noir: *Chinatown*, *Johnny Gaddaar*), **mafia/underworld**, **prison films**, **detective and whodunit** (*Knives Out*, *Byomkesh Bakshy!*), **police procedural**. **Tags:** #crime #noir

### ☐ MOV-06.7 Horror

Films that make us afraid, and let us enjoy it. **Tags:** #horror

- ☐ **Supernatural and ghost**: *The Exorcist*, *Mahal* (1949), *Bhoot*, *Ringu*.
- ☐ **Folk horror**: horror rooted in village belief; *The Wicker Man*, *Tumbbad*, *Bulbbul*.
- ☐ **Slasher**: a masked killer; *Halloween*, *Scream*.
- ☐ **Monster and creature**: *Frankenstein*, *Godzilla*, *Jaws*.
- ☐ **Zombie**: *Night of the Living Dead*, *Train to Busan*, *Go Goa Gone* (zom-com).
- ☐ **Psychological horror**: *The Shining*, *Hereditary*.
- ☐ **Body horror**: David Cronenberg, *The Substance*.
- ☐ **Found footage**: *The Blair Witch Project*, *Paranormal Activity*.
- ☐ **Horror-comedy**: *Stree*, *Bhool Bhulaiyaa*, *Shaun of the Dead*.
- ☐ **Ramsay Brothers horror (India, 1970s–1990s)**: *Purana Mandir*, *Veerana*.
- ☐ **J-horror, K-horror, Thai and Indonesian horror**: regional schools of fear.

### ☐ MOV-06.8 Science fiction

Stories about science, the future, space and "what if". **Tags:** #scifi

- ☐ **Space opera**: *Star Wars*, *Dune*.
- ☐ **Hard science fiction**: *2001: A Space Odyssey*, *Interstellar*, *The Martian*.
- ☐ **Dystopian and post-apocalyptic**: *Metropolis*, *Blade Runner*, *Mad Max*.
- ☐ **Cyberpunk**: *Blade Runner*, *Ghost in the Shell*, *The Matrix*.
- ☐ **Time travel**: *Back to the Future*, *Primer*, *Aditya 369* (Telugu, 1991), *Indru Netru Naalai*.
- ☐ **Alien contact**: *Close Encounters of the Third Kind*, *Arrival*, *Koi... Mil Gaya*, *PK*.
- ☐ **Robots and AI**: *Ex Machina*, *Enthiran*, *Her*.
- ☐ **Mythological science fiction (India)**: *Kalki 2898 AD*.

### ☐ MOV-06.9 Fantasy

Magic, myth and impossible worlds. *The Wizard of Oz*, *The Lord of the Rings*, *Pan's Labyrinth*, *Spirited Away*, *Baahubali*, *Mayabazar*. Sub-genres: **high/epic fantasy**, **fairy tale**, **magical realism** (*Amélie*, *Paheli*), **sword and sorcery**, **urban fantasy**. **Tags:** #fantasy

### ☐ MOV-06.10 Mythological and devotional (India's oldest genre)

Films from the epics, Puranas and saints' lives; the first genre of Indian cinema. *Raja Harishchandra*, *Sant Tukaram*, *Mayabazar*, *Jai Santoshi Maa* (1975, a surprise blockbuster), *Adipurush*, *Hanu-Man*. Also **saint films** (bhakti biopics), **Christian, Islamic and Buddhist religious films** worldwide (*The Ten Commandments*, *The Message*). **Tags:** #mythology #devotional #india

### ☐ MOV-06.11 Romance

Love stories in every shape. *Casablanca*, *Barsaat*, *Bobby*, *DDLJ*, *In the Mood for Love*, *Premam*, *96*. Sub-genres: **tragic romance** (*Ek Duuje Ke Liye*), **romantic drama**, **period romance**, **love triangle**, **star-crossed lovers** (Laila–Majnu, Heer–Ranjha films), **LGBTQ+ romance** (*Brokeback Mountain*, *Call Me by Your Name*, *Badhaai Do*). **Tags:** #romance

### ☐ MOV-06.12 Musical and dance

Films in which songs and dance carry the story. Hollywood musicals (*Singin' in the Rain*, *The Sound of Music*, *La La Land*), Indian films (almost all popular Indian films use songs; full musicals like *Sangam* era dance films, *Disco Dancer*, *ABCD*), **jukebox musicals** (*Mamma Mia!*), **dance films** (*Saturday Night Fever*, *Step Up*), **opera films** and **concert films**. **Tags:** #musical #dance

### ☐ MOV-06.13 Historical and period

Films set in the past. **Tags:** #history #period

- ☐ **Historical epic**: *Ben-Hur*, *Mughal-e-Azam*, *Jodhaa Akbar*, *Ponniyin Selvan*.
- ☐ **Biographical (biopic)**: *Gandhi*, *Bhaag Milkha Bhaag*, *Oppenheimer*, *Sardar Udham*.
- ☐ **Costume drama**: *Pride & Prejudice*, *Umrao Jaan*.
- ☐ **Freedom-struggle films (India)**: *Shaheed* (1965), *Lagaan*, *RRR*.
- ☐ **Partition films**: *Garm Hava*, *Tamas*, *Pinjar*, *Meghe Dhaka Tara*.
- ☐ **Samurai (jidaigeki)** and **wuxia (martial chivalry)** period films.

### ☐ MOV-06.14 War

Films about battles, soldiers and the cost of war. *All Quiet on the Western Front* (1930 and 2022), *Saving Private Ryan*, *Come and See*, *Haqeeqat* (1964), *Border*, *Uri: The Surgical Strike*, *1917*. Sub-genres: **anti-war film**, **combat film**, **war epic**, **home-front drama**, **Holocaust film** (*Schindler's List*), **military biopic** (*Shershaah*). **Tags:** #war

### ☐ MOV-06.15 Western (and its world cousins)

The American frontier: cowboys, outlaws, sheriffs. *Stagecoach*, *The Searchers*, *High Noon*, *Unforgiven*. Cousins: the Italian **spaghetti Western**, India's **curry Western** and dacoit films (*Sholay*, *Mera Gaon Mera Desh*, *Paan Singh Tomar*), the Australian **meat-pie Western**, the **Ostern** (Eastern European and Soviet "Red Westerns"). **Tags:** #western

### ☐ MOV-06.16 Animation (as a genre)

Films made frame by frame with drawings, models or computers (also a form: MOV-07.3). Family films (*Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs*, 1937, the first full-length cel-animated feature in colour; *Toy Story*, 1995, the first fully computer-animated feature), **anime**, **adult animation** (*Persepolis*), **stop-motion** (*Coraline*), Indian animation (*Hanuman*, *Mahavatar Narsimha*). **Tags:** #animation

### ☐ MOV-06.17 Family and children's films

Films made for children or the whole family. *E.T.*, *Paddington*, *Children of Heaven*, *Makdee*, *Chillar Party*, the Children's Film Society India (CFSI, 1955). **Tags:** #family #kids

### ☐ MOV-06.18 Sports films

Films around a game or athlete. *Rocky*, *Raging Bull*, *Chak De! India*, *Lagaan*, *Dangal*, *Iqbal*, *83*, *Ford v Ferrari*. **Tags:** #sports · **See also:** SPT

### ☐ MOV-06.19 Mystery and detective

Puzzle films that ask "who did it?". *Rashomon* (truth itself is the mystery), *Murder on the Orient Express*, *Kahaani*, *Talaash*, Satyajit Ray's Feluda films (*Sonar Kella*). **Tags:** #mystery

### ☐ MOV-06.20 Experimental genres and hybrids

- ☐ **Road movie**: *Easy Rider*, *Y tu mamá también*, *Piku*, *Highway*.
- ☐ **Survival film**: *Cast Away*, *127 Hours*.
- ☐ **Mockumentary and pseudo-documentary**.
- ☐ **Anthology film**: several stories in one; *Bombay Talkies* (2013), *Lust Stories*, *Paris, je t'aime*.
- ☐ **Hyperlink cinema**: crossing storylines; *Pulp Fiction*, *Life in a... Metro*, *Super Deluxe*.
- ☐ **Slow cinema**: long takes, little plot; Béla Tarr, Lav Diaz, Tsai Ming-liang.
- ☐ **Social-media and "screenlife" films**: told on screens; *Searching* (2018), *CTRL* (2024).
- ☐ **Masala film**: India's mix-of-all-genres format, serving every rasa in one film (MOV-00.07).

## MOV-07 Forms, formats and audiences

> **Before you start — MOV-07.** Genre says what a film is about; form says how it is made and shown. **See also:** MOV-16 Technology.

### ☐ MOV-07.1 By length

- ☐ **Feature film**: over 40 minutes by the Academy's rule (many festivals use 60 minutes or more); typically 90 to 180 minutes.
- ☐ **Short film**: under about 40 minutes; festival favourite (Clermont-Ferrand).
- ☐ **Micro-short and vertical short**: under a few minutes, made for phones.

### ☐ MOV-07.2 Documentary and non-fiction

Films about real people and events. Robert Flaherty's *Nanook of the North* (1922), Dziga Vertov, John Grierson (who coined "documentary" in 1926), **cinéma vérité**, **direct cinema**, **observational**, **participatory**, **essay film** (Chris Marker), **nature documentary** (David Attenborough's series), **investigative documentary**, **docudrama**; Indian: Anand Patwardhan, *The Elephant Whisperers*. **Tags:** #documentary

### ☐ MOV-07.3 Animation techniques

Hand-drawn (cel), stop-motion (clay/claymation, puppet, cut-out), computer-generated (3D CGI), rotoscope, motion capture, paint-on-glass, sand animation, pixilation, limited animation (anime), 2.5D. Pioneers: Émile Cohl (*Fantasmagorie*, 1908), Winsor McCay (*Gertie the Dinosaur*, 1914), Lotte Reiniger (silhouette feature *The Adventures of Prince Achmed*, 1926), Ram Mohan ("father of Indian animation").

### ☐ MOV-07.4 Experimental and avant-garde

Films that break story and form: surrealist (*Un Chien Andalou*, 1929), abstract (Oskar Fischinger, Norman McLaren), structural film, video art, Indian experimental cinema (Mani Kaul, Kumar Shahani, Amit Dutta).

### ☐ MOV-07.5 By technology and screen

Silent, sound, black-and-white, colour (Technicolor, 1930s), widescreen (CinemaScope, 1953), 3D (1950s craze; *Avatar* 2009; India's *My Dear Kuttichathan*, 1984, the first Indian 3D film), IMAX (1970), 70 mm, high frame rate, 4DX, VR cinema, interactive film.

### ☐ MOV-07.6 By release channel

Theatrical, festival, direct-to-video (Nollywood's origin), television film, streaming original (OTT), web series (episodic, a neighbour of film), YouTube cinema.

### ☐ MOV-07.7 By audience and certificate

India's CBFC certificates: **U** (all), **U/A 7+, U/A 13+, U/A 16+** (parental guidance by age, from 2024), **A** (adults), **S** (special audiences). USA (MPA): G, PG, PG-13, R, NC-17. UK (BBFC): U, PG, 12A, 15, 18. Each country has its own system (MOV-17.3).

### ☐ MOV-07.8 By budget and production model

Blockbuster/tentpole, mid-budget, low-budget/independent, micro-budget, B-movie, art-house, crowd-funded (*Manthan*, 1976, funded by 500,000 farmers paying ₹2 each), co-production.

# PART 3 — MOVEMENTS, SCHOOLS AND STYLES

> **About this Part.** A movement is a group of filmmakers in a time and place who share ideas about what cinema should be. Movements are how cinema argues with itself. Listed roughly in order of time.

## MOV-08 Film movements of the world

> **Before you start — MOV-08.** Read this with Part 1 (where the movements happened) and Part 8 (the theories behind them). **See also:** MOV-18 Film theory.

- ☐ **MOV-08.1 Early cinema "cinema of attractions" (1895–1906)**: films that showed rather than told: tricks, trains, dances (term by historian Tom Gunning).
- ☐ **MOV-08.2 Swedish silent golden age (1917–1924)**: Victor Sjöström, Mauritz Stiller; nature as emotion.
- ☐ **MOV-08.3 German Expressionism (1919–1930s)**: painted shadows and twisted sets showing inner fear; *Caligari*, *Nosferatu*, *Metropolis*; shaped horror and noir.
- ☐ **MOV-08.4 Soviet Montage (1924–1930)**: meaning made by collision of shots; Eisenstein, Vertov, Pudovkin, Kuleshov.
- ☐ **MOV-08.5 French Impressionism and Surrealism (1918–1930)**: Abel Gance, Germaine Dulac, Luis Buñuel and Salvador Dalí.
- ☐ **MOV-08.6 Classical Hollywood style (1917–1960)**: invisible editing, continuity, star and genre; the "standard" others reacted against.
- ☐ **MOV-08.7 Poetic Realism (France, 1930s)**: doomed lovers in foggy ports; Marcel Carné, Jean Renoir, Jean Vigo.
- ☐ **MOV-08.8 Italian Neorealism (1943–1952)**: real streets, non-actors, poverty after war; Rossellini, De Sica, Visconti; inspired Ray, Bimal Roy and the IPTA films.
- ☐ **MOV-08.9 IPTA and the Indian socialist realist films (1940s–1950s)**: Indian People's Theatre Association artists in cinema; *Dharti Ke Lal* (1946), *Neecha Nagar*, K. A. Abbas, Balraj Sahni.
- ☐ **MOV-08.10 Japanese golden age (1950s)**: Kurosawa, Ozu, Mizoguchi.
- ☐ **MOV-08.11 Free Cinema and British New Wave (1956–1963)**: working-class "kitchen sink" realism; Lindsay Anderson, Tony Richardson.
- ☐ **MOV-08.12 French New Wave / Nouvelle Vague (1958–1968)**: critics of *Cahiers du cinéma* turned directors; jump cuts, location shooting, the director as author; Truffaut, Godard, Rohmer, Rivette, Chabrol, Agnès Varda (Left Bank group).
- ☐ **MOV-08.13 Indian Parallel Cinema / New Indian Cinema (1952–1990s)**: realist, socially engaged, often state-funded; Ray, Ghatak, Sen, then Benegal, Nihalani, Adoor, Aravindan, Mani Kaul, Kumar Shahani, Girish Kasaravalli. Its "middle cinema" cousins: Hrishikesh Mukherjee, Basu Chatterjee, Gulzar.
- ☐ **MOV-08.14 Czechoslovak New Wave (1962–1968)**: absurd humour against censorship; Forman, Chytilová, Menzel.
- ☐ **MOV-08.15 Cinema Novo (Brazil, 1960s)**: "aesthetics of hunger"; Glauber Rocha, Nelson Pereira dos Santos.
- ☐ **MOV-08.16 Third Cinema (Latin America, 1969)**: political, anti-colonial cinema (manifesto by Solanas and Getino, Argentina).
- ☐ **MOV-08.17 New Hollywood (1967–1980)**: young directors, darker stories; *Bonnie and Clyde*, *Taxi Driver*.
- ☐ **MOV-08.18 New German Cinema (1962–1982)**: the Oberhausen Manifesto: "Papa's cinema is dead".
- ☐ **MOV-08.19 Iranian New Wave (1969–)**: poetic realism, children and landscapes; Mehrjui, Kiarostami, Makhmalbaf, Panahi.
- ☐ **MOV-08.20 Australian New Wave (1970s)** and **Taiwan New Cinema (1982–)**.
- ☐ **MOV-08.21 China's Fifth Generation (1984–)**: *Yellow Earth*, *Red Sorghum*.
- ☐ **MOV-08.22 Hong Kong New Wave (1979–)** and **heroic bloodshed**.
- ☐ **MOV-08.23 Cinéma du look (France, 1980s)**: stylish visuals; Luc Besson, Jean-Jacques Beineix.
- ☐ **MOV-08.24 New Queer Cinema (1990s)**: Todd Haynes, Gregg Araki.
- ☐ **MOV-08.25 Dogme 95 (Denmark, 1995)**: ten rules: handheld camera, real locations, no added music.
- ☐ **MOV-08.26 Korean New Wave (late 1990s–)**: genre-bending, bold violence and class satire.
- ☐ **MOV-08.27 Romanian New Wave (2001–)**: long takes, bureaucratic absurdity.
- ☐ **MOV-08.28 Mumblecore (USA, 2000s)**: micro-budget, naturalistic talk.
- ☐ **MOV-08.29 Greek Weird Wave (2009–)**: absurd, cold, disturbing; Yorgos Lanthimos.
- ☐ **MOV-08.30 Malayalam New Generation (2011–)** and the **new Hindi indie wave**: realism, regional texture, strong writing.
- ☐ **MOV-08.31 Pan-Indian cinema (2015–)**: big-budget films released in several languages at once.
- ☐ **MOV-08.32 Slow cinema** and **elevated horror** (contemporary tendencies).

# PART 4 — THE HISTORY OF CINEMA: A TIMELINE

> **About this Part.** The full story in order, with world and Indian events side by side. Every row can become a page on the website.

## MOV-09 Timeline of world and Indian cinema

> **Before you start — MOV-09.** Read the Introduction twig (Part 0) first for the story; use this timeline for the facts. **See also:** TEC (camera and sound technology), SNT-02 history of technology.

### ☐ MOV-09.1 Before cinema (prehistory to 1894)

- ☐ **Prehistory**: cave paintings with multiple legs suggesting movement; storytelling by firelight.
- ☐ **c. 200 BCE–200 CE**: Bharata's Natyashastra; Greek theatre; shadow puppetry spreads in Asia.
- ☐ **c. 1000 CE**: Ibn al-Haytham explains the camera obscura (Book of Optics).
- ☐ **1659**: magic lantern (Christiaan Huygens).
- ☐ **1820s–1830s**: thaumatrope, phenakistiscope (Joseph Plateau, 1832), zoetrope (1834).
- ☐ **1826–1839**: photography: Niépce's first photograph, Daguerre's daguerreotype (1839).
- ☐ **1878**: Muybridge's *Horse in Motion*.
- ☐ **1882**: Étienne-Jules Marey's chronophotographic gun.
- ☐ **1888**: Le Prince's *Roundhay Garden Scene*; Eastman's Kodak camera.
- ☐ **1889**: celluloid roll film (Eastman).
- ☐ **1891–1894**: Edison and Dickson's Kinetograph and Kinetoscope; first Kinetoscope parlour, New York (1894).

### ☐ MOV-09.2 The birth of cinema (1895–1910)

- ☐ **1895**: Skladanowsky brothers' Bioscop show in Berlin (November); Lumières' Cinématographe screening in Paris (28 December).
- ☐ **1896**: Lumière films in Bombay (7 July); cinema reaches every continent.
- ☐ **1899**: H. S. Bhatavdekar films a wrestling match (India).
- ☐ **1902**: Méliès' *A Trip to the Moon*; J. F. Madan begins film shows in Calcutta.
- ☐ **1903**: *The Great Train Robbery*.
- ☐ **1905**: first nickelodeon, Pittsburgh.
- ☐ **1906**: *The Story of the Kelly Gang* (Australia), early feature-length film.
- ☐ **1907**: Elphinstone Bioscope (Madan), early Indian film exhibition chain.
- ☐ **1908**: first animated cartoon (*Fantasmagorie*); Motion Picture Patents Company (USA).

### ☐ MOV-09.3 Silent features and the rise of industries (1910–1927)

- ☐ **1912–1913**: *Shree Pundalik* (1912); *Raja Harishchandra* (3 May 1913).
- ☐ **1914**: *Cabiria*; Chaplin's Tramp appears.
- ☐ **1915**: *The Birth of a Nation*.
- ☐ **1918**: Indian Cinematograph Act; *Keechaka Vadham* (South India).
- ☐ **1919–1920**: *The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari*; United Artists founded (Chaplin, Pickford, Fairbanks, Griffith).
- ☐ **1922**: *Nanook of the North*; *Nosferatu*.
- ☐ **1925**: *Battleship Potemkin*; *The Gold Rush*.
- ☐ **1927**: *Metropolis*; *Napoléon*; Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences founded; Indian Cinematograph Committee report (1927–28).

### ☐ MOV-09.4 The talkies and the studio era (1927–1945)

- ☐ **1927**: *The Jazz Singer*.
- ☐ **1928**: Mickey Mouse in *Steamboat Willie* (synchronised sound cartoon).
- ☐ **1929**: first Academy Awards (16 May).
- ☐ **1931**: *Alam Ara* (14 March); *Kalidas* (Tamil/Telugu).
- ☐ **1932**: first Venice Film Festival, the world's oldest.
- ☐ **1932–1934**: Indian studios: Prabhat (1929), New Theatres (1931), Bombay Talkies (1934).
- ☐ **1934**: Hollywood's Production Code enforced.
- ☐ **1935**: three-strip Technicolor feature *Becky Sharp*; *Devdas* (New Theatres).
- ☐ **1937**: *Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs*; *Kisan Kanya*, India's first indigenous colour film (Ardeshir Irani).
- ☐ **1939**: *Gone with the Wind*, *The Wizard of Oz*; Cannes planned, cancelled by war.
- ☐ **1940–1945**: wartime propaganda films; Gemini Studios rises (1940).

### ☐ MOV-09.5 The post-war golden ages (1945–1959)

- ☐ **1945–1948**: Italian Neorealism; *Bicycle Thieves*.
- ☐ **1946**: first Cannes festival; *Neecha Nagar* shares the Grand Prix.
- ☐ **1948**: Paramount decision ends the studio system in the USA.
- ☐ **1950–1951**: *Rashomon* wins at Venice; Berlin festival founded (1951); Central Board of Film Censors (India, 1951); Cinematograph Act 1952.
- ☐ **1952**: first International Film Festival of India (Bombay); Calcutta Film Society influence.
- ☐ **1953**: CinemaScope (*The Robe*); 3D boom.
- ☐ **1954**: first National Film Awards and first Filmfare Awards (India); *Seven Samurai*; *Godzilla*.
- ☐ **1955**: *Pather Panchali*; Children's Film Society India founded.
- ☐ **1957**: *Mother India*; *Pyaasa*; *The Seventh Seal*.
- ☐ **1959**: French New Wave (*The 400 Blows*, *Breathless* 1960).

### ☐ MOV-09.6 New waves and colour (1960–1975)

- ☐ **1960**: *Mughal-e-Azam*; *Psycho*; *La Dolce Vita*; Film Institute of India, Pune (FTII).
- ☐ **1962**: first James Bond film, *Dr. No*.
- ☐ **1964**: National Film Archive of India founded (Pune).
- ☐ **1968**: MPAA ratings replace the Hays Code; *2001: A Space Odyssey*.
- ☐ **1969**: Film Finance Corporation backs *Bhuvan Shome* and *Uski Roti*; Dadasaheb Phalke Award begins (Devika Rani).
- ☐ **1972**: *The Godfather*.
- ☐ **1973**: *Zanjeer*; *Enter the Dragon*.
- ☐ **1975**: *Sholay*, *Deewaar*, *Jai Santoshi Maa*; *Jaws*; NFDC formed (1975; the FFC merged into it in 1980).

### ☐ MOV-09.7 Blockbusters and video (1975–1995)

- ☐ **1977**: *Star Wars*; Industrial Light & Magic.
- ☐ **1980s**: VHS and the video boom; Doordarshan colour TV in India (1982 Asian Games).
- ☐ **1982**: *Gandhi* (Richard Attenborough), eight Oscars; Bhanu Athaiya becomes the first Indian Oscar winner (1983).
- ☐ **1984**: "Betamax case" ruling (USA); *My Dear Kuttichathan*, India's first 3D film.
- ☐ **1988**: *Salaam Bombay!* wins the Caméra d'Or.
- ☐ **1992**: Satyajit Ray's Honorary Oscar; *Living in Bondage* starts Nollywood; *Roja*.
- ☐ **1993**: *Jurassic Park*, CGI creatures.
- ☐ **1995**: *Toy Story*, first fully CGI feature; *DDLJ*; cinema's centenary.

### ☐ MOV-09.8 The digital turn (1995–2010)

- ☐ **1997**: PVR Anupam, India's first multiplex; *Titanic*.
- ☐ **1998**: Indian government grants film "industry" status (formalised 2000–2001), opening bank finance.
- ☐ **1999**: *The Matrix*; *The Blair Witch Project*; first digital cinema projections.
- ☐ **2001**: *Lagaan* Oscar-nominated; *The Lord of the Rings* begins; *Spirited Away*.
- ☐ **2002**: *Star Wars: Episode II* shot fully on digital video.
- ☐ **2007**: Netflix starts streaming.
- ☐ **2009**: *Avatar*'s 3D; *Slumdog Millionaire* wins eight Oscars, including two for A. R. Rahman and one each for Resul Pookutty and Gulzar (for lyrics, shared with Rahman).

### ☐ MOV-09.9 Streaming, franchises and the global screen (2010–present)

- ☐ **2012**: most US cinemas have converted to digital projection; 35 mm prints are phased out.
- ☐ **2015–2017**: *Baahubali* films; Chinese box office booms.
- ☐ **2016**: Netflix and Amazon Prime Video launch in India (Hotstar began in 2015); Netflix reaches 190 countries.
- ☐ **2019**: *Avengers: Endgame*; *Parasite* (Palme d'Or, then Oscars in 2020).
- ☐ **2020–2021**: COVID-19 closes cinemas; direct-to-streaming releases.
- ☐ **2022**: *RRR*; Films Division and other units merged into NFDC; *Jeanne Dielman* tops Sight and Sound.
- ☐ **2023**: "Naatu Naatu" and *The Elephant Whisperers* win Oscars; WGA and SAG-AFTRA strikes over pay and AI; Cinematograph (Amendment) Act 2023 against piracy.
- ☐ **2024**: *All We Imagine as Light* wins Cannes Grand Prix; CBFC's new age-based U/A categories.
- ☐ **2025**: *Ne Zha 2* becomes the highest-grossing animated film; Jafar Panahi wins the Palme d'Or.
- ☐ **2026**: *One Battle After Another* wins Best Picture (March); Cristian Mungiu's *Fjord* wins the Palme d'Or (May); *Woman Unknown* wins the Golden Lion (September); *Avengers: Endgame* re-takes the all-time box-office record after a re-release (October). (As checked on 3 November 2026.)
- ☐ **The present scenario**: streaming and theatres side by side; AI tools on set; regional-language cinema rising; Asia as the growth centre of world box office.
