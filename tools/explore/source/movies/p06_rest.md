# PART 7 — THE CRAFT, TECHNOLOGY AND BUSINESS OF FILM

## MOV-15 Who does what: the film crew

> **Before you start — MOV-15.** A film credit can list thousands of people. Here is each role in one line. **See also:** MOV-16 Pipeline.

- ☐ **MOV-15.1 Producer**: finds the money, assembles the team and is responsible for the film from start to release.
- ☐ **MOV-15.2 Director**: the creative leader who decides how the story will be told on screen.
- ☐ **MOV-15.3 Screenwriter**: writes the story, scenes and dialogue (in India the story, screenplay and dialogue are often credited separately).
- ☐ **MOV-15.4 Cast**: lead, supporting, character actors, junior artists (extras), stunt performers, body doubles, voice and dubbing artists.
- ☐ **MOV-15.5 Cinematographer / Director of Photography (DOP)**: designs the light and camera; with camera operators, focus pullers and gaffers.
- ☐ **MOV-15.6 Production designer / art director**: creates the world: sets, locations, props.
- ☐ **MOV-15.7 Costume, makeup and hair**: how characters look.
- ☐ **MOV-15.8 Editor**: chooses and joins shots; often called the "final rewrite".
- ☐ **MOV-15.9 Sound team**: location sound recordist, sound designer, Foley artist (recreated everyday sounds), re-recording mixer.
- ☐ **MOV-15.10 Music director / composer, lyricist, playback singer, choreographer**.
- ☐ **MOV-15.11 VFX and animation artists**: compositing, CGI, motion capture, colourist (colour grading, DI).
- ☐ **MOV-15.12 Assistant directors, line producer, casting director, location manager, script supervisor (continuity)**.
- ☐ **MOV-15.13 Distributors, exhibitors, marketing and publicity teams**.

## MOV-16 How a film is made, and the technology behind it

- ☐ **MOV-16.1 Development**: idea, rights, story, screenplay, pitching, financing.
- ☐ **MOV-16.2 Pre-production**: casting, storyboards, location scouting, schedule, budget, rehearsals.
- ☐ **MOV-16.3 Production (the shoot)**: shots, takes, coverage, call sheets; the shot sizes (extreme wide to extreme close-up), angles and camera moves (pan, tilt, dolly, crane, Steadicam, drone).
- ☐ **MOV-16.4 Post-production**: editing, dubbing (ADR), sound design, music, VFX, colour grading, subtitles, censorship certification.
- ☐ **MOV-16.5 Distribution and exhibition**: festivals, theatrical release, streaming, satellite, airlines, re-releases.
- ☐ **MOV-16.6 Film language**: mise-en-scène, montage, continuity editing, the 180-degree rule, match cut, jump cut, long take, deep focus, point-of-view shot, cross-cutting, the MacGuffin, the fourth wall.
- ☐ **MOV-16.7 Camera and film technology**: 35 mm and 16 mm film, 70 mm and IMAX, aspect ratios (1.37:1 to 2.39:1), digital cinema cameras (ARRI, RED, Sony), lenses, anamorphic.
- ☐ **MOV-16.8 Sound technology**: optical sound (1920s), magnetic, Dolby Stereo (1975), digital sound (Dolby Digital, DTS, 1990s), Dolby Atmos (2012).
- ☐ **MOV-16.9 Colour technology**: hand-painting and tinting, two-strip and three-strip Technicolor, Eastmancolor (1950), digital intermediate (2000).
- ☐ **MOV-16.10 Visual effects**: stop-trick (Méliès), matte painting, miniatures, rear projection, stop-motion, blue and green screen, CGI (*Jurassic Park*, 1993), motion capture (*The Lord of the Rings*), de-ageing, virtual production (LED volumes).
- ☐ **MOV-16.11 Animation technology**: cel, rotoscope, multiplane camera (Disney, 1937), CGI render farms.
- ☐ **MOV-16.12 Projection and exhibition**: single screens, multiplexes, digital projection (DCP), 3D, 4DX, laser projection, home theatre.
- ☐ **MOV-16.13 AI in filmmaking**: script tools, dubbing and lip-sync, de-ageing, generative video; rules, consent and credit debates.

# PART 8 — IDEAS: THEORY, PHILOSOPHY, PSYCHOLOGY AND SOCIETY

## MOV-18 Film theory and criticism (the big ideas)

> **Before you start — MOV-18.** Theory asks what cinema is and how it works on us. Each idea gets one line and its thinker. **See also:** BOK literary theory, MOV-19.

- ☐ **MOV-18.1 Rasa and Indian aesthetics**: emotion as a "taste" for the audience (Natyashastra; Abhinavagupta); navarasa in Indian popular film.
- ☐ **MOV-18.2 Montage theory**: meaning from the collision of shots (Eisenstein, Kuleshov, Pudovkin).
- ☐ **MOV-18.3 Realism**: cinema as a window on reality, deep focus and the long take (André Bazin, Siegfried Kracauer).
- ☐ **MOV-18.4 Formalism**: cinema as art through its differences from reality (Rudolf Arnheim).
- ☐ **MOV-18.5 Auteur theory**: the director as author (Truffaut's "politique des auteurs", 1954; Andrew Sarris in the USA).
- ☐ **MOV-18.6 Genre theory**: how genres form, repeat and change (Rick Altman, Thomas Schatz).
- ☐ **MOV-18.7 Semiotics and structuralism**: film as a language of signs (Christian Metz).
- ☐ **MOV-18.8 Psychoanalytic film theory**: the spectator, desire and identification (Laura Mulvey's "male gaze", 1975; Christian Metz; Slavoj Žižek).
- ☐ **MOV-18.9 Feminist film theory**: women in front of and behind the camera; the Bechdel test (1985).
- ☐ **MOV-18.10 Postcolonial and Third Cinema theory**: cinema of the global South (Solanas and Getino; Ashish Rajadhyaksha on Indian cinema).
- ☐ **MOV-18.11 Cognitive film theory**: how perception and the mind process film (David Bordwell, Noël Carroll).
- ☐ **MOV-18.12 Star studies and audience studies**: what stars mean (Richard Dyer); how fans watch (fan clubs of Tamil and Telugu stars).
- ☐ **MOV-18.13 Deleuze's movement-image and time-image** and **Walter Benjamin's "aura" in the age of mechanical reproduction** (1935).

## MOV-19 Cinema, mind and society

- ☐ **MOV-19.1 The psychology of watching**: attention, empathy, suspense (Hitchcock's bomb), immersion, the "willing suspension of disbelief" (Coleridge, 1817).
- ☐ **MOV-19.2 Emotion and the body**: why we cry, jump and laugh together; contagion of laughter in halls.
- ☐ **MOV-19.3 Fandom and celebrity worship**: Indian star temples and fan clubs; parasocial relationships.
- ☐ **MOV-19.4 Cinema and identity**: nation, language, caste, religion, gender, class on screen.
- ☐ **MOV-19.5 Representation**: who is seen, who is missing; stereotypes and their change.
- ☐ **MOV-19.6 Cinema and politics**: propaganda (*Triumph of the Will*), cinema as protest, film stars in politics (India's South).
- ☐ **MOV-19.7 Cinema and religion**: the devotional film, the theatre as temple (*Jai Santoshi Maa* audiences removing shoes).
- ☐ **MOV-19.8 Cinema and children**: media effects research, age ratings, learning from films.
- ☐ **MOV-19.9 Cinema and the economy**: jobs, tourism (film tourism to Switzerland and New Zealand), soft power (Raj Kapoor in the USSR, Korean wave).
- ☐ **MOV-19.10 Philosophy through film**: films as thought experiments on free will (*The Matrix*), memory (*Memento*), truth (*Rashomon*), death (*The Seventh Seal*), goodness (*Ikiru*).

## MOV-20 Critics' Corner: the great film critics

### ☐ MOV-20.1 Critics of the world

- ☐ **André Bazin** (France, 1918–1958): co-founded *Cahiers du cinéma* (1951); champion of realism and the long take; "father" of the New Wave.
- ☐ **François Truffaut** (France): critic before director; his 1954 essay attacked "the tradition of quality" and launched auteurism.
- ☐ **James Agee** (USA): poetic reviews in *Time* and *The Nation* in the 1940s.
- ☐ **Pauline Kael** (USA, *The New Yorker* 1968–1991): passionate, personal, against snobbery; championed *Bonnie and Clyde*.
- ☐ **Andrew Sarris** (USA, *The Village Voice*): brought auteur theory to America (*The American Cinema*, 1968).
- ☐ **Roger Ebert** (USA, *Chicago Sun-Times*, 1967–2013): the first film critic to win a Pulitzer Prize (1975); "thumbs up" TV show with Gene Siskel; his "Great Movies" essays; he wrote that movies are "a machine that generates empathy".
- ☐ **Jonathan Rosenbaum**, **J. Hoberman**, **Manohla Dargis**, **A. O. Scott**, **Richard Brody**, **Kenneth Turan** (USA).
- ☐ **Mark Kermode** and **Peter Bradshaw** (UK); *Sight and Sound* magazine (BFI, 1932).
- ☐ **Tadao Sato** (Japan) and **Donald Richie** (Japan's American interpreter).
- ☐ **Serge Daney** (France), **Jean-Michel Frodon**, **Michel Ciment** (*Positif*).

### ☐ MOV-20.2 Critics and film writers of India

- ☐ **Chidananda Dasgupta**: co-founded the Calcutta Film Society with Satyajit Ray (1947); thoughtful critic and filmmaker.
- ☐ **Satyajit Ray** as a writer: *Our Films, Their Films* (1976).
- ☐ **Iqbal Masud**, **Amita Malik**, **Bikram Singh**, **Khalid Mohamed** (*Times of India*), **Bhawana Somaaya**, **Maithili Rao**.
- ☐ **Contemporary**: Anupama Chopra (Film Companion), Rajeev Masand, Baradwaj Rangan (Tamil and pan-Indian), Shubhra Gupta (*Indian Express*), Saibal Chatterjee, Raja Sen, Sudhir Srinivasan, Namrata Joshi, Mayank Shekhar, Sukanya Verma.

## MOV-21 The critics said, I say (page template)

Each page follows the same order:

1. **The film in two lines**: year, director, cast, what it is about (no spoilers).
2. **What the critics said**: 3 to 5 named critics, each summarised in one or two lines of my own words, with a link to the original review and its date.
3. **Where they agree and disagree**: one paragraph.
4. **What I say**: my view and why, in my own voice (link to MOV-22).
5. **If you liked this, watch next**: three linked films (tags).

## MOV-22 My Review Desk (Kritikesh's review template)

- ☐ **Header**: title, year, language, director, cast, runtime, where to watch (dated), certificate.
- ☐ **My one-line verdict** (the answer first).
- ☐ **What it is about** (no spoilers).
- ☐ **What worked for me**: story, performances, direction, music, visuals.
- ☐ **What did not**.
- ☐ **Rasa meter**: which emotions it served (navarasa: love, laughter, compassion, anger, courage, fear, disgust, wonder, peace).
- ☐ **Who should watch it**: mood, age, patience level.
- ☐ **My rating** on a clear five-point scale, with what each point means.
- ☐ **Spoiler zone** (folded): discussion of the ending.
- ☐ **Tags and watch-next links**.

## MOV-E Editor's notes (corrections and decisions on the brief)

1. **"Tollywood" for all South Indian films** was corrected: Tollywood means Telugu (and also Kolkata's Bengali) cinema; the South has four major industries plus Tulu and others, each with its own section.
2. **"List every film ever made"**: more than a million feature films exist, so the booklet gives a sourced landmark register for each industry plus a clear plan ("How the registers grow", MOV-05.6) to fill the full year-wise lists on the website from official databases.
3. **Box-office figures** are given only where well documented, with the type (worldwide gross, etc.) and date; Indian "net" and "gross" are explained.
4. **Latest winners** were checked on the web on 3 November 2026; where a fact could not be verified (for example the latest Golden Globes), the entry says so instead of guessing.
5. **Reviews**: critic reviews will be summarised in my own words with links; never copied.
6. **Numbering**: the ID system (MOV-01 to MOV-25) matches the Curriculum Index style, so the same IDs can be used in a future Movies sheet and on the website.
