# PART 5 — AWARDS, FESTIVALS AND RECORDS

> **About this Part.** For each award: what it is, who gives it, how and when it began, its first winner, landmark winners, records and the latest winner, with the date the fact was checked. International awards and festivals come first, then national awards country by country, with India in full. **As checked on 3 November 2026.** Sources: the official site of each award (Part 13).

## MOV-10 International film awards

> **Before you start — MOV-10.** "International" here means awards watched by the whole world, even if one country's academy gives them (like the Oscars). **See also:** MOV-11 Festivals, MOV-13 Records, MOV-14 People.

### ☐ MOV-10.1 Academy Awards (the Oscars), USA

Given every year by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS, founded 1927) to honour films released in the previous year; voted by about 10,000 film professionals. The gold statuette was designed by Cedric Gibbons and sculpted by George Stanley. **Tags:** #award #usa #oscars

- ☐ **How it began**: the first ceremony was a 15-minute dinner at the Hollywood Roosevelt Hotel on **16 May 1929**.
- ☐ **First winners**: *Wings* (Outstanding Picture) and *Sunrise* (Unique and Artistic Production); Emil Jannings (Actor) and Janet Gaynor (Actress).
- ☐ **Main categories**: Picture, Director, Actor, Actress, Supporting roles, Original and Adapted Screenplay, International Feature Film (earlier "Foreign Language Film", from 1956 as a competitive category), Animated Feature (from 2001), Documentary, Cinematography, Editing, Score, Song, Sound, Production Design, Costume, Makeup and Hairstyling, Visual Effects, Short films, Casting (first given at the 98th ceremony, 2026), Honorary awards.
- ☐ **Landmark winners**: Hattie McDaniel, first Black winner (1940); *All About Eve* (14 nominations); Sidney Poitier, first Black Best Actor (1964); *Parasite* (2020), first non-English Best Picture; Kathryn Bigelow, first woman Best Director (2010); Michelle Yeoh, first Asian Best Actress (2023).
- ☐ **Records**: most wins for a film: 11 (*Ben-Hur*, *Titanic*, *The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King*); most wins for a person: Walt Disney (22 competitive); most acting wins: Katharine Hepburn (4); most nominations for an actor: Meryl Streep (21).
- ☐ **India at the Oscars**: Bhanu Athaiya (Costume Design, *Gandhi*, 1983), the first Indian winner; Satyajit Ray (Honorary Award, 1992); A. R. Rahman (two), Resul Pookutty and Gulzar for *Slumdog Millionaire* (2009); M. M. Keeravani and Chandrabose for "Naatu Naatu" (*RRR*) and Kartiki Gonsalves and Guneet Monga for *The Elephant Whisperers* (2023); Indian engineers have also won Scientific and Technical Awards. India's nominations for International Feature: *Mother India* (1958), *Salaam Bombay!* (1989), *Lagaan* (2002).
- ☐ **Latest: 98th Academy Awards (15 March 2026)**: Best Picture *One Battle After Another*; Director Paul Thomas Anderson; Actor Michael B. Jordan (*Sinners*); Actress Jessie Buckley (*Hamnet*); Supporting Actor Sean Penn; Supporting Actress Amy Madigan (*Weapons*); International Feature *Sentimental Value* (Norway); Animated Feature *KPop Demon Hunters*.

### ☐ MOV-10.2 BAFTA Film Awards, United Kingdom

The British Academy of Film and Television Arts awards (the academy began in 1947; first film awards in **1949**). The mask trophy was designed by Mitzi Cunliffe. First Best Film: *The Best Years of Our Lives*. Seen as a guide to the Oscars. **Latest (22 February 2026)**: Best Film *One Battle After Another*, which won six BAFTAs. **Tags:** #award #uk

### ☐ MOV-10.3 Golden Globe Awards, USA

Begun in **1944** by the Hollywood Foreign Press Association (dissolved in 2023; the Globes are now run by a private company with an international voting body). Separate Drama and Musical/Comedy categories; also television. First Best Picture: *The Song of Bernadette*. **Latest**: see goldenglobes.com (not verified for this edition). **Tags:** #award #usa

### ☐ MOV-10.4 European Film Awards

Given by the European Film Academy since **1988** (first Best Film: Krzysztof Kieślowski's *A Short Film About Killing*). Celebrates European cinema across borders.

### ☐ MOV-10.5 Asian Film Awards and Asia Pacific Screen Awards

Asian Film Awards (Hong Kong, since **2007**) and the Asia Pacific Screen Awards (Australia, since **2007**) honour films across Asia and the Pacific, including India.

### ☐ MOV-10.6 Other international honours

- ☐ **FIPRESCI Prize**: from the International Federation of Film Critics (founded 1925), given at festivals worldwide.
- ☐ **Annie Awards** (since 1972): the top awards for animation.
- ☐ **Platino Awards** (since 2014): Ibero-American (Spanish- and Portuguese-language) cinema.
- ☐ **Africa Movie Academy Awards (AMAA)** (since 2005) and **Africa Magic Viewers' Choice Awards (AMVCA)** (since 2013).
- ☐ **Lux Audience Award** of the European Parliament.
- ☐ **Saturn Awards** (science fiction, fantasy, horror, since 1972) and the **Golden Raspberry ("Razzies")** for the worst films (since 1981).
- ☐ **Emmy Awards**: for television, not film (included here only to avoid confusion).

## MOV-11 Film festivals of the world and their top prizes

> **Before you start — MOV-11.** Festivals are where art cinema is discovered and sold. The **FIAPF** (International Federation of Film Producers Associations) accredits competitive festivals; the most prestigious are called the "Big Three": Cannes, Venice and Berlin.

### ☐ MOV-11.1 Cannes Film Festival (Festival de Cannes), France

Held every May on the French Riviera. Planned for 1939 as a free alternative to a Venice festival then influenced by fascist Italy, but cancelled by the Second World War; first held in **1946**. **Tags:** #festival #france #cannes

- ☐ **Top prize**: the **Palme d'Or** (from 1955; earlier the Grand Prix). First Palme d'Or: *Marty* (Delbert Mann, 1955).
- ☐ **Other prizes**: Grand Prix, Jury Prize, Best Director, Best Actor, Best Actress, Best Screenplay, Caméra d'Or (best first film), Un Certain Regard, Short Film Palme d'Or; parallel sections Directors' Fortnight and Critics' Week.
- ☐ **Landmarks**: *Neecha Nagar* shared the first Grand Prix (1946); Jane Campion became the first woman to win (*The Piano*, 1993, shared); *Parasite* (2019) was the first Korean winner. Two-time winners include Francis Ford Coppola, Shohei Imamura, Emir Kusturica, Bille August, the Dardenne brothers, Ken Loach, Michael Haneke, Ruben Östlund and Cristian Mungiu.
- ☐ **India at Cannes**: *Neecha Nagar* (1946), *Do Bigha Zamin* (Prix International, 1954), *Pather Panchali* (Best Human Document, 1956), *Kharij* (Jury Prize, 1983), *Salaam Bombay!* (Caméra d'Or, 1988), *Marana Simhasanam* (Caméra d'Or, 1999), *All We Imagine as Light* (Grand Prix, 2024), Anasuya Sengupta (Best Actress, Un Certain Regard, 2024, *The Shameless*).
- ☐ **Latest: 79th festival (May 2026)**: Palme d'Or *Fjord* (Cristian Mungiu, his second); Grand Prix *Minotaur* (Andrey Zvyagintsev); Best Director shared by Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi (*La Bola Negra*) and Paweł Pawlikowski (*Fatherland*).

### ☐ MOV-11.2 Venice International Film Festival (Mostra), Italy

The **oldest film festival in the world**, first held in **1932** as part of the Venice Biennale, every late August–September on the Lido. **Tags:** #festival #italy #venice

- ☐ **Top prize**: the **Golden Lion** (Leone d'Oro), named in 1949; first winner under that name: *Manon* (Henri-Georges Clouzot, 1949).
- ☐ **Other prizes**: Silver Lion (Grand Jury Prize and Best Director), Volpi Cups for acting, Orizzonti section, Golden Lion for Lifetime Achievement.
- ☐ **Landmarks**: *Rashomon* (1951) introduced Japanese cinema to the West; Satyajit Ray's *Aparajito* (1957); Mira Nair's *Monsoon Wedding* (2001); *Joker* (2019) and *Nomadland* (2020).
- ☐ **Latest: 83rd festival (September 2026)**: Golden Lion *Woman Unknown* (*Kvinde Ukendt*, May el-Toukhy, Denmark); Silver Lion Grand Jury Prize *Possible Love* (Lee Chang-dong); Volpi Cups to Mathilde Arcel and John Malkovich. (2025: *Father Mother Sister Brother*, Jim Jarmusch.)

### ☐ MOV-11.3 Berlin International Film Festival (Berlinale), Germany

Founded in **1951** in West Berlin, in February; known for political and diverse cinema and the biggest public audience of the Big Three. **Tags:** #festival #germany #berlin

- ☐ **Top prize**: the **Golden Bear**; Silver Bears for direction, performance (gender-neutral since 2021), screenplay and more; the Teddy Award for LGBTQ+ films (1987).
- ☐ **India at Berlin**: Satyajit Ray won Silver Bears for Best Director (*Mahanagar*, 1964; *Charulata*, 1965) and the Golden Bear for *Ashani Sanket* (1973); Mrinal Sen's *Akaler Sandhane* (Silver Bear, 1981).
- ☐ **Latest: 76th Berlinale (February 2026)**: Golden Bear *Yellow Letters* (İlker Çatak). (2025: *Dreams (Sex Love)*, Dag Johan Haugerud; 2024: *Dahomey*, Mati Diop.)

### ☐ MOV-11.4 Other major competitive festivals (FIAPF-accredited and leading)

- ☐ **San Sebastián** (Spain, 1953): Golden Shell.
- ☐ **Locarno** (Switzerland, 1946): Golden Leopard; open-air Piazza Grande.
- ☐ **Karlovy Vary** (Czech Republic, 1946): Crystal Globe.
- ☐ **Tokyo International** (Japan, 1985): Tokyo Grand Prix.
- ☐ **Shanghai International** (China, 1993): Golden Goblet.
- ☐ **Cairo International** (Egypt, 1976): Golden Pyramid; the Arab world's oldest competitive festival accredited by FIAPF.
- ☐ **Mar del Plata** (Argentina, 1954): Latin America's only FIAPF competitive festival.
- ☐ **Tallinn Black Nights** (Estonia, 1997) and **Warsaw** (Poland, 1985).
- ☐ **International Film Festival of India (IFFI), Goa** (1952): Golden Peacock; FIAPF-accredited competitive festival; Satyajit Ray Lifetime Achievement Award.

### ☐ MOV-11.5 Leading non-competitive and specialist festivals

- ☐ **Toronto International Film Festival (TIFF)** (Canada, 1976): the People's Choice Award, a famous Oscar predictor.
- ☐ **Sundance** (USA, 1978; Sundance name 1991): American independent cinema.
- ☐ **Telluride** (1974) and **New York Film Festival** (1963).
- ☐ **Busan International** (South Korea, 1996): Asia's most important festival.
- ☐ **International Film Festival Rotterdam** (1972): Tiger Awards for new talent.
- ☐ **Annecy** (France, 1960): animation. **Clermont-Ferrand** (France): short films. **IDFA** Amsterdam (1988) and **Hot Docs** Toronto: documentary.
- ☐ **FESPACO**, Ouagadougou (Burkina Faso, 1969): Golden Stallion of Yennenga, Africa's top prize. **Carthage Film Days** (Tunisia, 1966).
- ☐ **Red Sea International** (Saudi Arabia, 2021); **Sydney** (1954); **Melbourne** (1952).
- ☐ **Indian festivals**: Jio MAMI Mumbai Film Festival (1997), International Film Festival of Kerala (1996, the Suvarna Chakoram), Kolkata International (1995), Chennai, Bengaluru, Dharamshala, Pune International, Habitat, Mumbai International Film Festival for documentaries (MIFF, 1990).

## MOV-12 National film awards, country by country

> **Before you start — MOV-12.** Each country honours its own cinema. India comes first and in full; then every region. **See also:** MOV-01 to MOV-05 (each industry's register lists its award winners).

### ☐ MOV-12.1 India

- ☐ **National Film Awards** (since **1954**): India's highest film honours, given by the President of India; managed today by the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting through NFDC (earlier the Directorate of Film Festivals). Golden Lotus (Swarna Kamal) and Silver Lotus (Rajat Kamal) medals. First Best Feature Film: *Shyamchi Aai* (Marathi). Records: Shabana Azmi, five Best Actress awards; Amitabh Bachchan, four Best Actor awards. **Latest verified: 71st National Film Awards** (announced 1 August 2025, presented 23 September 2025, for films of 2023): Best Feature Film *12th Fail*; Best Actor Shah Rukh Khan (*Jawan*) and Vikrant Massey (*12th Fail*); Best Actress Rani Mukerji (*Mrs Chatterjee vs Norway*).
- ☐ **Dadasaheb Phalke Award** (since **1969**): India's highest honour for lifetime contribution to cinema, given with the National Film Awards. First awardee: Devika Rani. Recent: Mithun Chakraborty (2022 award), Mohanlal (2023 award, presented 2025).
- ☐ **Filmfare Awards** (since **21 March 1954**): the oldest major popular film awards, by *Filmfare* magazine (Times Group); voted by audience and jury. First Best Film *Do Bigha Zamin*; first Best Actor Dilip Kumar (*Daag*); first Best Actress Meena Kumari (*Baiju Bawra*). Records: Dilip Kumar and Shah Rukh Khan, eight Best Actor awards each. **Latest: 70th Filmfare Awards (11 October 2025, Ahmedabad)**: Best Film *Laapataa Ladies* (13 awards, equalling *Gully Boy*'s record); Best Actor Abhishek Bachchan and Kartik Aaryan; Best Actress Alia Bhatt (*Jigra*). Also **Filmfare Awards South**, **Marathi**, **Bangla**, **Punjabi** and **OTT** editions.
- ☐ **Bengal Film Journalists' Association Awards** (since **1937**): the oldest film awards association in India.
- ☐ **IIFA Awards** (International Indian Film Academy, since **2000**, held abroad; first Best Film *Hum Dil De Chuke Sanam*), plus IIFA Utsavam for South Indian films.
- ☐ **Screen Awards** (1995), **Zee Cine Awards** (1998), **SIIMA** (South Indian International Movie Awards, 2012).
- ☐ **State film awards**: Maharashtra State (1963), Kerala State (1969), Tamil Nadu State (1967), Karnataka State (1967), Nandi Awards (Andhra Pradesh, 1964), Telangana Gaddar Film Awards (2025), West Bengal, Odisha, Gujarat, Punjab, Assam and others.
- ☐ **Civilian honours to film artists**: Bharat Ratna (Satyajit Ray, 1992; Lata Mangeshkar, 2001; M. G. Ramachandran, 1988, mainly as a political leader), Padma awards.

### ☐ MOV-12.2 The Americas

- ☐ **USA**: Academy Awards (MOV-10.1), Golden Globes, Screen Actors Guild Awards (1995; renamed the Actor Awards), Directors Guild of America Awards (1949), Producers Guild Awards, Independent Spirit Awards (1986), Critics Choice Awards, the National Film Registry (Library of Congress, 1989; an honour of preservation).
- ☐ **Canada**: Canadian Screen Awards (2013; successor of the Genie Awards, 1980, and Canadian Film Awards, 1949); Québec's Prix Iris.
- ☐ **Mexico**: Ariel Awards (1947), by the Mexican Academy of Film.
- ☐ **Brazil**: Grande Prêmio do Cinema Brasileiro (2000); Gramado Festival's Kikito (1973).
- ☐ **Argentina**: Silver Condor (critics, 1943) and Premios Sur (2006).
- ☐ **Colombia, Chile (Premios Caleuche), Cuba (Coral prizes of the Havana Festival), Peru, Venezuela.**

### ☐ MOV-12.3 Europe

- ☐ **France**: César Awards (1976; first Best Film *Le Vieux Fusil*), Louis Delluc Prize (1937), Lumière Awards (foreign press).
- ☐ **Italy**: David di Donatello (1956), Nastro d'Argento (1946, from film journalists, among Europe's oldest).
- ☐ **Spain**: Goya Awards (1987; first Best Film *El viaje a ninguna parte*).
- ☐ **Germany**: German Film Award, the Lola (1951). **Austria**: Austrian Film Award.
- ☐ **United Kingdom**: BAFTA (MOV-10.2), British Independent Film Awards (1998).
- ☐ **Russia**: Nika Award (1988), Golden Eagle (2002).
- ☐ **Sweden**: Guldbagge (1964). **Denmark**: Bodil (1948, one of Europe's oldest) and Robert (1984). **Norway**: Amanda (1985). **Finland**: Jussi (1944). **Iceland**: Edda.
- ☐ **Poland**: Polish Film Awards, the Eagles (1999). **Czech Republic**: Czech Lion (1993). **Hungary, Romania (Gopo Awards), Greece (Hellenic Film Academy), Turkey (Golden Orange, Antalya, 1964), Portugal (Sophia Awards), Netherlands (Golden Calf), Belgium (Magritte), Ireland (IFTA).**

### ☐ MOV-12.4 Asia and the Middle East

- ☐ **Japan**: Japan Academy Film Prize (1978), Kinema Junpo Best Ten (since 1924, the oldest film ranking), Mainichi Film Awards (1946), Blue Ribbon Awards (1950).
- ☐ **China**: Golden Rooster Awards (1981), Hundred Flowers Awards (1962, audience-voted). **Hong Kong**: Hong Kong Film Awards (1982). **Taiwan**: Golden Horse Awards (1962), open to all Chinese-language films.
- ☐ **South Korea**: Grand Bell Awards (1962), Blue Dragon Film Awards (1963), Baeksang Arts Awards (1965).
- ☐ **Pakistan**: Nigar Awards (1957), Lux Style Awards (2002). **Bangladesh**: National Film Awards (1975). **Sri Lanka**: Sarasaviya Awards (1964). **Nepal**: National Film Awards.
- ☐ **Iran**: Crystal Simorgh (Fajr Film Festival, 1982). **Israel**: Ophir Awards. **Egypt**: National Film Festival awards; **Arab world**: Arab Critics' Awards.
- ☐ **Philippines**: FAMAS (1952), Gawad Urian (1977), Metro Manila Film Festival awards. **Indonesia**: Citra Awards (1955). **Thailand**: Suphannahong (1992). **Vietnam**: Golden Kite. **Malaysia**: Malaysia Film Festival awards.

### ☐ MOV-12.5 Africa and Oceania

- ☐ **Nigeria**: AMVCA (2013) and AMAA (pan-African, 2005). **South Africa**: South African Film and Television Awards (2006). **Ghana**: Ghana Movie Awards. **Kenya**: Kalasha Awards. **Pan-Africa**: FESPACO's Golden Stallion (MOV-11.5).
- ☐ **Australia**: AACTA Awards (since 1958 as the AFI Awards). **New Zealand**: New Zealand Film Awards.

## MOV-13 Records, firsts and fun facts

- ☐ **MOV-13.1 Highest-grossing film worldwide**: *Avengers: Endgame* (about US$2.93 billion, as checked 5 Oct 2026, after its 2026 re-release); adjusted for inflation, *Gone with the Wind*.
- ☐ **MOV-13.2 Highest-grossing Indian film**: *Dangal* (about ₹2,000 crore worldwide).
- ☐ **MOV-13.3 Longest-running film in a single cinema (India)**: *Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge* at Maratha Mandir, Mumbai (from 1995; shown for decades).
- ☐ **MOV-13.4 Largest film producer by number**: India (CBFC certification data), followed by Nigeria and the USA or China depending on the year (UNESCO Institute for Statistics).
- ☐ **MOV-13.5 Most screen credits for a living actor**: Brahmanandam (Telugu comedian), recognised by Guinness World Records.
- ☐ **MOV-13.6 Most studio recordings of songs**: Asha Bhosle, recognised by Guinness World Records (2011).
- ☐ **MOV-13.7 Longest film franchise**: *Godzilla* (Japan, from 1954), by continuous production.
- ☐ **MOV-13.8 Firsts in India**: first feature (1913), talkie (1931), indigenous colour film (*Kisan Kanya*, 1937), 3D film (*My Dear Kuttichathan*, 1984), CinemaScope film (*Kaagaz Ke Phool*, 1959), Oscar (1983).
- ☐ **MOV-13.9 Firsts for women**: Alice Guy-Blaché, one of the first film directors (*The Fairy of the Cabbages*, 1896); Fatma Begum, India's first woman director (*Bulbul-e-Paristan*, 1926); Devika Rani, first Dadasaheb Phalke awardee.
- ☐ **MOV-13.10 Greatest films polls**: the BFI *Sight and Sound* poll every ten years since 1952 (2022 critics' winner: *Jeanne Dielman, 23 quai du Commerce, 1080 Bruxelles*; directors' winner: *2001: A Space Odyssey*); in India, CNN-IBN's 2013 poll (*Mayabazar*).
