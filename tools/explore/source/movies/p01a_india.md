# PART 1 — FILM INDUSTRIES OF THE WORLD

> **About this Part.** The first great classification: where films are made. It goes from India to every continent. Each industry has a one- or two-line identity card (language, centre, nickname, first film, signature), its key eras, its legends and a **Film Register** of landmark films, decade by decade. Nicknames ending in "-wood" are informal; the proper name is given first. **The registers are a curated landmark selection, not a complete list**: see "How the registers grow" at the end of this Part.

> **Register fields.** Year · Film · Director · Lead cast · Box office / reception · Major awards and why it matters. A dash (—) means not recorded here, never a guess. Box-office figures are approximate, not inflation-adjusted, and carry their source in Part 13.

## MOV-01 Indian Cinema: one country, more than twenty film languages

> **Before you start — MOV-01.** India is the world's largest film producer by number of films. It is not one industry but a family of language industries that share stars, music and technicians, and increasingly release "pan-Indian" films in many languages at once. Start with Hindi cinema, then the four big southern industries, then the East, West, North-east and the smaller language cinemas. **See also:** MOV-09 History, MOV-12.1 Indian awards, MUS (film music), BOK (adaptations).

### ☐ MOV-01.1 Hindi cinema (Bollywood)

Films in Hindi and Hindustani, centred in Mumbai (Bombay, hence "Bollywood"). Born with *Raja Harishchandra* (1913) and the talkie *Alam Ara* (1931); known worldwide for song-and-dance, melodrama, stars and the masala mix of every emotion. **Tags:** #india #hindi #mumbai #bollywood

- ☐ **Silent era (1913–1931)**: mythologicals and stunts from Phalke's Hindustan Film Company, Kohinoor and Imperial studios.
- ☐ **Studio era (1930s–1940s)**: Bombay Talkies (Himanshu Rai, Devika Rani), Prabhat, Ranjit, Minerva Movietone (Sohrab Modi), Wadia Movietone (Fearless Nadia).
- ☐ **Golden age (1947–1960s)**: social realism and romance of Raj Kapoor, Guru Dutt, Bimal Roy, Mehboob Khan, V. Shantaram; the music of Naushad, S. D. Burman, Shankar–Jaikishan; Lata Mangeshkar and Mohammed Rafi.
- ☐ **Colour, romance and Rajesh Khanna (1960s–early 1970s)**: Kashmir songs, Shammi Kapoor's energy, the first superstar Rajesh Khanna.
- ☐ **Angry young man and masala (1973–1980s)**: Amitabh Bachchan, the writers Salim–Javed, multi-starrers like *Sholay*.
- ☐ **Parallel / New Indian Cinema (1969–1990s)**: state-funded (FFC, later NFDC) realist cinema: Mrinal Sen (Hindi work), Shyam Benegal, Govind Nihalani, Mani Kaul, Kumar Shahani, Saeed Mirza, Sai Paranjpye.
- ☐ **Romance and the NRI era (1989–2000s)**: *Maine Pyar Kiya*, *DDLJ*; Shah Rukh, Salman and Aamir Khan; overseas markets; corporatisation (industry status for film, 2001).
- ☐ **Multiplex and new-wave Hindi films (2000s–2010s)**: Dibakar Banerjee, Anurag Kashyap, Vishal Bhardwaj, Zoya Akhtar, Rajkumar Hirani; small-town stories.
- ☐ **Streaming and pan-India (2020s)**: OTT originals, South-Indian dubbed hits in the Hindi belt, big-event films.
- ☐ **Signature elements**: playback songs, item numbers, dialogue-baazi, family melodrama, interval twist, censorship by CBFC certificate (U, U/A 7+, 13+, 16+, A, S).

### ☐ MOV-01.2 Tamil cinema (Kollywood)

Films in Tamil, centred in Chennai (Kodambakkam, hence "Kollywood"). First talkie *Kalidas* (1931); deeply tied to Dravidian politics, star cults and bold music. **Tags:** #india #tamil #chennai #kollywood

- ☐ **Silent beginnings**: *Keechaka Vadham* (1918, R. Nataraja Mudaliar), the first South Indian silent feature.
- ☐ **Studio era**: Gemini (S. S. Vasan), AVM (A. V. Meiyappan), Modern Theatres (Salem).
- ☐ **Dravidian film politics**: writers C. N. Annadurai and M. Karunanidhi; stars M. G. Ramachandran and J. Jayalalithaa became chief ministers.
- ☐ **The great acting traditions**: Sivaji Ganesan, Gemini Ganesan, Savitri; later Kamal Haasan and Rajinikanth.
- ☐ **Village realism (late 1970s)**: Bharathiraja, Mahendran, Balu Mahendra.
- ☐ **The Mani Ratnam and A. R. Rahman era (1980s–1990s)**.
- ☐ **New-wave Tamil (2000s–)**: Bala, Vetrimaaran, Pa. Ranjith, Karthik Subbaraj, Lokesh Kanagaraj's "Cinematic Universe".

### ☐ MOV-01.3 Telugu cinema (Tollywood)

Films in Telugu, centred in Hyderabad (earlier Madras). Known for mythologicals, larger-than-life heroes and, today, India's biggest pan-Indian spectacles. **Tags:** #india #telugu #hyderabad #tollywood

- ☐ **Beginnings**: *Bhishma Pratigna* (1921, R. S. Prakash) silent; *Bhakta Prahlada* (1932, H. M. Reddy) usually counted as the first Telugu talkie.
- ☐ **Golden age (1950s–1960s)**: Vijaya and Vauhini studios; N. T. Rama Rao (as Krishna and Rama), Akkineni Nageswara Rao, Savitri, S. V. Ranga Rao.
- ☐ **K. Viswanath's music films and the art-house of B. Narsing Rao**.
- ☐ **Mass-hero era**: Chiranjeevi, Balakrishna, Nagarjuna, Venkatesh; then Mahesh Babu, Pawan Kalyan, Allu Arjun, Prabhas, Jr NTR, Ram Charan.
- ☐ **Ramoji Film City** (1996), among the largest film studio complexes in the world (Guinness World Records).
- ☐ **Rajamouli and the pan-Indian wave (2015–)**.

### ☐ MOV-01.4 Malayalam cinema (Mollywood)

Films in Malayalam, from Kerala (Kochi and Thiruvananthapuram). Small market, huge reputation: realism, writing and actors such as Mohanlal and Mammootty. **Tags:** #india #malayalam #kerala #mollywood

- ☐ **Pioneers**: *Vigathakumaran* (1928, J. C. Daniel, "father of Malayalam cinema"; P. K. Rosy, the first heroine, a Dalit woman driven out by upper-caste protests).
- ☐ **First talkie**: *Balan* (1938, S. Nottani).
- ☐ **Literary golden age (1950s–1970s)**: adaptations of Thakazhi, Basheer, M. T. Vasudevan Nair.
- ☐ **Art cinema masters**: Adoor Gopalakrishnan, G. Aravindan, Shaji N. Karun, John Abraham.
- ☐ **Middle cinema (1980s–1990s)**: P. Padmarajan, Bharathan, K. G. George, Sathyan Anthikad, Priyadarshan; writer Sreenivasan.
- ☐ **New Generation (2010s–)**: Lijo Jose Pellissery, Dileesh Pothan, Aashiq Abu; actors Fahadh Faasil, Parvathy Thiruvothu.

### ☐ MOV-01.5 Kannada cinema (Sandalwood)

Films in Kannada, centred in Bengaluru ("Sandalwood" after Karnataka's sandalwood). Home of the beloved Dr Rajkumar, of literary new-wave cinema and of the *K.G.F* and *Kantara* phenomena. **Tags:** #india #kannada #bengaluru #sandalwood

- ☐ **First talkie**: *Sati Sulochana* (1934, Y. V. Rao).
- ☐ **Dr Rajkumar era (1954–2000s)**: from *Bedara Kannappa* (1954) to over 200 films.

- ☐ **Modern stars**: Vishnuvardhan, Ambareesh, Shiva Rajkumar, Puneeth Rajkumar, Sudeep, Yash, Rishab Shetty.
- ☐ **Pan-Indian breakthrough (2018–)**: *K.G.F*, *Kantara*.

### ☐ MOV-01.6 Bengali cinema (Tollywood of Tollygunge)

Films in Bengali, centred in Tollygunge, Kolkata (the original "Tollywood", a name that probably inspired "Bollywood"). Home of Satyajit Ray, Ritwik Ghatak and Mrinal Sen; also a thriving industry in Bangladesh (MOV-04.5). **Tags:** #india #bengali #kolkata #tollywood

- ☐ **Madan Theatres and New Theatres (1917–1950s)**: *Bilwamangal* (1919), *Devdas* (1935, P. C. Barua).
- ☐ **The great trio**: Satyajit Ray (*Apu Trilogy*), Ritwik Ghatak (*Partition trilogy*), Mrinal Sen (*Calcutta trilogy*).
- ☐ **Uttam Kumar and Suchitra Sen**: the romantic pair of the 1950s–1960s.
- ☐ **Later auteurs**: Tapan Sinha, Tarun Majumdar, Aparna Sen, Buddhadeb Dasgupta, Goutam Ghose, Rituparno Ghosh.
- ☐ **Contemporary**: Kaushik Ganguly, Srijit Mukherji, Anik Dutta; detective franchises (Feluda, Byomkesh).

### ☐ MOV-01.8 Bhojpuri cinema

Films in Bhojpuri, made mainly in Mumbai and Patna for audiences in Bihar, eastern Uttar Pradesh, Jharkhand and the Bhojpuri diaspora (Mauritius, Fiji, Suriname, Trinidad). First film *Ganga Maiyya Tohe Piyari Chadhaibo* (1963, Kundan Kumar), encouraged by President Rajendra Prasad. Revived in the 2000s by *Sasura Bada Paisawala* (2004, Manoj Tiwari). Known for folk music, stars such as Ravi Kishan and Dinesh Lal Yadav "Nirahua", and debates over vulgarity in songs. **Tags:** #india #bhojpuri #bihar #up

### ☐ MOV-01.9 Other cinemas of East and North-east India

- ☐ **Odia (Ollywood)**: from Bhubaneswar and Cuttack; first film *Sita Bibaha* (1936, Mohan Sundar Deb Goswami); art-house master Nirad N. Mohapatra (*Maya Miriga*, 1984).
- ☐ **Assamese (Jollywood)**: from Guwahati; first film *Joymoti* (1935, Jyoti Prasad Agarwala, made at his own Chitraban studio); Bhabendra Nath Saikia, Jahnu Barua; Rima Das's *Village Rockstars* (2017) won the National Award and was India's Oscar entry.
- ☐ **Manipuri**: first feature *Matamgi Manipur* (1972, Deb Kumar Bose); Aribam Syam Sharma's *Imagi Ningthem* (1981) won the Grand Prix at the Nantes Three Continents Festival. After armed groups banned Hindi films (2000), Manipur grew its own digital film industry.
- ☐ **Khasi, Garo, Mizo, Naga, Kokborok, Bodo and Nepali-language cinemas**: small but rising industries; *Manik Raitong* (1984) was the first Khasi film.
- ☐ **Maithili, Magahi, Angika and Santali films**: *Kanyadan* (1965) is often cited as the first Maithili film; Santali cinema is growing in Jharkhand and Odisha.
- ☐ **Sikkimese and Darjeeling Nepali-language films** (link MOV-04.6 Nepal).

### ☐ MOV-01.10 Cinemas of West and North India

- ☐ **Gujarati (Dhollywood)**: first talkie *Narsinh Mehta* (1932, Nanubhai Vakil); revival after 2012; *Hellaro* (2019, Abhishek Shah) won the National Award for Best Feature Film; Pan Nalin's *Last Film Show* (*Chhello Show*, 2021) was India's Oscar entry and made the shortlist.
- ☐ **Punjabi (Pollywood)**: from Mohali, Chandigarh and Lahore before Partition; *Sheela* (*Pind di Kudi*, 1935) among the first; *Chann Pardesi* (1980) won a National Award; boom since *Jatt & Juliet* (2012) and *Carry On Jatta* (2012); stars Diljit Dosanjh, Gippy Grewal, Ammy Virk; huge Canadian diaspora market.
- ☐ **Rajasthani**: first film *Nazrana* (1942); hits such as *Bai Chali Sasariye* (1988); small but loved, with Marwari, Mewari and Hadoti dialects.
- ☐ **Haryanvi**: *Chandrawal* (1984) was a landmark rural hit; now growing on YouTube and streaming.
- ☐ **Garhwali and Kumaoni (Uttarakhand)**: *Jagwal* (1983, Parashar Gaur) is regarded as the first Garhwali film.
- ☐ **Kashmiri, Dogri and Ladakhi**: *Mehjoor* (1964) is often cited as the first Kashmiri film.
- ☐ **Chhattisgarhi (Chhollywood)**: *Kahi Debe Sandesh* (1965, Manu Nayak) was the first; revived after 2000 with *Mor Chhainha Bhuinya*.
- ☐ **Konkani**: *Mogacho Anvddo* (1950, Jerry Braganza); *Nachom-ia Kumpasar* (2014) a modern classic.
- ☐ **Sindhi**: *Abana* (1958) in India; Sindhi films also in Pakistan.
- ☐ **Sanskrit**: *Adi Shankaracharya* (1983, G. V. Iyer) was the first Sanskrit feature and won the National Award for Best Feature Film.

### ☐ MOV-01.11 Other South Indian cinemas

- ☐ **Tulu (Coastalwood)**: from Mangaluru and Udupi; first film *Enna Thangadi* (1971); *Kantara*'s world grew from this coast.
- ☐ **Kodava**: from Kodagu (Coorg); *Naada Mann Naada Koota* (1972) is cited as the first.
- ☐ **Beary, Badaga, Irula and other community-language films**: *Byari* (2011) won the National Award for Best Feature Film.

### ☐ MOV-01.12 Indian documentary, animation and independent cinema

- ☐ **Documentary**: Films Division (1948–2022, merged into NFDC), Anand Patwardhan, Deepa Dhanraj, the Oscar-winning short *The Elephant Whisperers* (2022, Kartiki Gonsalves, produced by Guneet Monga; Oscar 2023); *Writing with Fire* (Oscar-nominated, 2022); *All That Breathes* (Shaunak Sen; Cannes L'Œil d'or 2022).
- ☐ **Animation**: *Ek Anek Aur Ekta* (1974, Doordarshan short), *Ramayana: The Legend of Prince Rama* (1992, Indo-Japanese), *Hanuman* (2005), *Mahavatar Narsimha* (2025); Indian studios in global VFX (Prime Focus/DNEG, Red Chillies VFX).
- ☐ **Independent and festival cinema**: Payal Kapadia, Chaitanya Tamhane, Rima Das, Pan Nalin, Amit Masurkar, Kanu Behl; film institutes FTII (Pune, 1960) and SRFTI (Kolkata, 1995).
- ☐ **Short films and YouTube cinema**: a nursery for new talent in every language.
