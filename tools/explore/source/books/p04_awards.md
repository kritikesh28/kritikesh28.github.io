# PART 4 — AWARDS, PRIZES AND RECORDS

> **About this Part.** Each award has the same card: **who gives it, since when, for what, the first winner, landmark winners and the latest winner**, with the date it was checked. Prizes are a guide, not a verdict: many great writers (Tolstoy, Joyce, Premchand) never won the big international prizes. Latest winners were checked on the web on **3 November 2026**. Where a result was due after that date, the card says so.

## BOK-15 International literary prizes

> **Before you start — BOK-15.** Some prizes honour a writer's whole body of work (the Nobel), others a single book (the Booker). Some are open only to English (the Booker), others to translations (the International Booker). **See also:** MOV-10 Film awards (for the same card format).

### ☐ BOK-15.1 Nobel Prize in Literature

Given by the **Swedish Academy** since **1901** under Alfred Nobel's will, to an author for the body of work "in an ideal direction". **First winner:** Sully Prudhomme (France, 1901). **Landmarks:** Rabindranath Tagore (1913, first non-European), Selma Lagerlöf (1909, first woman), Wole Soyinka (1986, first African), Toni Morrison (1993), Bob Dylan (2016, songwriter), Han Kang (2024, first Asian woman). **Latest:** **Anne Carson** (Canada, poet, essayist and classicist), announced 8 October 2026; before her, **László Krasznahorkai** (Hungary, 2025). **Tags:** #nobel #award

- ☐ **Indian-born or Indian-origin laureates**: Rabindranath Tagore (1913); Rudyard Kipling (1907, born in Bombay); V. S. Naipaul (2001, Indo-Trinidadian).
- ☐ **Prizes not awarded**: in some war years and in 2018 (a Swedish Academy scandal; two prizes given in 2019).
- ☐ **Declined**: Boris Pasternak (1958, forced by Soviet authorities) and Jean-Paul Sartre (1964, by choice).

### ☐ BOK-15.2 The Booker Prize

For the best novel written in English and published in the UK or Ireland. Founded **1969** (as the Booker Prize; "Man Booker" 2002–2019). Open to writers of any nationality since 2014. **First winner:** P. H. Newby, *Something to Answer For* (1969). **Indian and Indian-origin winners:** V. S. Naipaul (1971, *In a Free State*), Salman Rushdie (1981), Arundhati Roy (1997), Kiran Desai (2006), Aravind Adiga (2008). **Landmarks:** Hilary Mantel (twice, 2009 and 2012), "Booker of Bookers" to *Midnight's Children* (1993 and 2008). **Latest:** **David Szalay, *Flesh*** (2025). The **2026 winner is due on 9 November 2026**; the shortlist is M. John Harrison (*The End of Everything*), Marlon James (*The Disappearers*), Luke Kennard (*Black Bag*), Rebecca Perry (*May We Feed the King*), Elizabeth Strout (*The Things We Never Say*) and Douglas Stuart (*John of John*). **Tags:** #booker

### ☐ BOK-15.3 The International Booker Prize

For a single book of fiction translated into English, with the £50,000 prize **shared equally by author and translator**. Began **2005** as a biennial award for a body of work (first winner Ismail Kadare, Albania); became an annual prize for one translated book in **2016** (first: Han Kang's *The Vegetarian*, tr. Deborah Smith). **Indian winners:** Geetanjali Shree and Daisy Rockwell (*Tomb of Sand*, Hindi, 2022); Banu Mushtaq and Deepa Bhasthi (*Heart Lamp*, Kannada, 2025). **Latest:** ***Taiwan Travelogue*** by **Yang Shuang-zi**, translated from Mandarin by **Lin King** (announced 19 May 2026; first winners from Taiwan). **Tags:** #internationalbooker #translation

### ☐ BOK-15.4 Other major international prizes

- ☐ **Neustadt International Prize for Literature** (University of Oklahoma, 1970; first winner Giuseppe Ungaretti): called "the American Nobel"; Raja Rao won in 1988. Latest winner: to be checked.
- ☐ **Hans Christian Andersen Award** (IBBY, 1956; first winner Eleanor Farjeon): the highest international honour for children's writers and illustrators, given every two years. **Latest (2026):** Michael Rosen (UK, writing) and Cai Gao (China, illustration).
- ☐ **Astrid Lindgren Memorial Award** (Sweden, 2003): the world's richest children's literature award.
- ☐ **Hugo Award** (World Science Fiction Society, 1953; first Best Novel: Alfred Bester's *The Demolished Man*): voted by fans. **Latest Best Novel (2026):** Alix E. Harrow, *The Everlasting*.
- ☐ **Nebula Award** (Science Fiction and Fantasy Writers Association, 1966; first Best Novel: Frank Herbert's *Dune*): voted by writers.
- ☐ **Women's Prize for Fiction** (UK, 1996; first winner Helen Dunmore, *A Spell of Winter*): for women writing in English. **Latest (2026):** Virginia Evans, a debut novelist.
- ☐ **Dublin Literary Award** (1996): nominated by libraries worldwide.
- ☐ **Prix Goncourt** (France, 1903): see BOK-16.3.
- ☐ **Cervantes Prize** (Spain, 1976): the highest honour in Spanish-language literature.
- ☐ **Camões Prize** (1989): Portuguese-language literature.
- ☐ **Caine Prize / AKO Caine Prize** (2000): African short story in English.
- ☐ **Commonwealth Short Story Prize** (2012): Commonwealth writers, also in translation.
- ☐ **DSC Prize for South Asian Literature** (first awarded 2011 to H. M. Naqvi, *Home Boy*).

## BOK-16 National literary awards, country by country

> **Before you start — BOK-16.** National awards honour a country's own literature. India's are unusual because they cover many languages at once. **See also:** Exam Guide (awards are a favourite topic in competitive exams).

### ☐ BOK-16.1 India

- ☐ **Jnanpith Award** (Bharatiya Jnanpith, first given **1965**): India's highest literary award, for a writer's outstanding contribution in any Eighth Schedule language or in English. **First winner:** G. Sankara Kurup (Malayalam, *Odakkuzhal*, 1965). **Landmarks:** Ashapurna Devi (1976, first woman), Amitav Ghosh (2018, first English writer), Gulzar (Urdu) and Jagadguru Rambhadracharya (Sanskrit) shared the 58th (2023). **Latest:** 59th (2024) **Vinod Kumar Shukla** (Hindi); 60th (2025) **R. Vairamuthu** (Tamil poet and film lyricist), announced 14 March 2026.
- ☐ **Sahitya Akademi Award** (National Academy of Letters, established 1954; awards since **1955**): one book a year in each of 24 languages. **First Hindi winner:** Makhanlal Chaturvedi (1955, *Him Taringini*). **Latest (2025 awards, announced 16 March 2026):** English: Navtej Sarna, *Crimson Spring* (novel); Hindi: Mamta Kalia, *Jeete Jee Allahabad* (memoir); Rajasthani: Jitender Kumar Soni, *Bharkham*; Tamil: Sa. Tamilselvan; Bengali: Prasun Bandyopadhyay; Urdu: Pritpal Singh Betab.
- ☐ **Sahitya Akademi Fellowship**: the Akademi's highest honour, for "immortals of literature"; first fellow S. Radhakrishnan (1968).
- ☐ **Sahitya Akademi Yuva Puraskar** (2011): writers under 35. **Bal Sahitya Puraskar** (2010): children's literature. **Translation Prize** (1989).
- ☐ **Saraswati Samman** (K. K. Birla Foundation, 1991): for a work in any Eighth Schedule language. **First winner:** Harivansh Rai Bachchan (Hindi, autobiography). **Latest:** 2025 to **Ramkumar Mukhopadhyay** for the Bengali novel *Hara Parbati Katha* (announced March 2026); 2024 to Mahamahopadhyay Bhadreshdas Swami (Sanskrit), the first saint-scholar to receive it.
- ☐ **Vyas Samman** (K. K. Birla Foundation, 1991): Hindi literature; first winner Ram Vilas Sharma.
- ☐ **Bihari Puraskar** (K. K. Birla Foundation, 1991): Hindi and Rajasthani writers of Rajasthan.
- ☐ **JCB Prize for Literature** (2018–): fiction by an Indian writer, including translations; first winner Benyamin's *Jasmine Days* (tr. Shahnaz Habib). Later winners include Perumal Murugan's *Fire Bird* (2023, tr. Janani Kannan). Latest edition: to be checked.
- ☐ **Kendriya Hindi Sansthan awards, state academies** (Rajasthan Sahitya Akademi, Kerala Sahitya Akademi, Bangla Academy) and **Ezhuthachan Puraskaram** (Kerala's highest).
- ☐ **Padma awards** to writers, and the **Kabir Samman**, **Kalidas Samman** and **Tulsi Samman** (Madhya Pradesh government).

## BOK-17 Best-sellers, records and numbers

> **Before you start — BOK-17.** Sales figures for books are **estimates**, often from publishers' own claims, and are hard to compare across centuries and languages. Treat every number as approximate and check its source. **See also:** MOV-13 Film records.

### ☐ BOK-17.1 The most printed and translated books

- ☐ **The Bible**: estimated billions of copies; full translations in more than 700 languages (Wycliffe Global Alliance).
- ☐ **The Qur'an**, the **Bhagavad Gita**, the ***Ramcharitmanas***, the **Hanuman Chalisa** (Gita Press, Gorakhpur, founded 1923, has printed hundreds of millions of copies of Hindu texts).
- ☐ **Quotations from Chairman Mao** (the "Little Red Book"): estimates of about a billion copies.
- ☐ **Most translated single literary work besides scripture**: Antoine de Saint-Exupéry's *The Little Prince* (1943), in over 500 languages and dialects; Carlo Collodi's *Pinocchio* is close behind.
- ☐ **Most translated author** (UNESCO *Index Translationum*, data up to the 2010s): Agatha Christie, then Jules Verne and Shakespeare.

### ☐ BOK-17.2 Best-selling single works (estimates)

- ☐ *Don Quixote* (claimed 500 million), *A Tale of Two Cities* (about 200 million), *The Little Prince* (about 200 million), *Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone* (over 120 million), *The Hobbit*, *And Then There Were None* (about 100 million), *The Alchemist* (over 150 million claimed by the author's publishers).
- ☐ **Best-selling series**: *Harry Potter* (over 600 million copies across seven books, according to its publishers).
- ☐ **India**: *Chandrakanta*, *Godaan*, the Gita Press editions, Chetan Bhagat's novels, Amish's *Shiva Trilogy*, *Wings of Fire*, NCERT textbooks and exam guides (the true best-sellers by volume).

### ☐ BOK-17.3 Records and curiosities

- ☐ **Longest epic**: the Kyrgyz *Manas* and the Tibetan *Epic of King Gesar* are often called longer than the *Mahabharata* (about 100,000 shlokas in the full tradition, or about 1.8 million words), depending on how versions are counted.
- ☐ **Largest library**: the Library of Congress (more than 170 million items); India's largest is the National Library, Kolkata.
- ☐ **Largest book fairs**: Frankfurt Book Fair (the biggest trade fair), Kolkata International Book Fair (among the largest by visitors), New Delhi World Book Fair (since 1972, organised by the National Book Trust).
- ☐ **Oldest continuously operating library**: the al-Qarawiyyin library in Fez, Morocco (founded 859).
- ☐ **India's publishing size**: India is one of the largest book markets and English-language publishers in the world; figures vary by report (Federation of Indian Publishers, Nielsen), so the website will cite the latest dated report.
