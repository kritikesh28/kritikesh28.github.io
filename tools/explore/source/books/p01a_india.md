# PART 1 — LITERATURES OF THE WORLD

> **About this Part.** The first great classification is where and in what language a book was written. It begins with India in full depth (classical languages, every Eighth Schedule language, major regional languages, tribal oral literatures and Indian writing in English), then travels continent by continent. Each literature has an identity card (script, oldest works, golden ages, signature forms), its masters, and a **Book Register** of landmark works. **The registers are a curated landmark selection, not a complete list.** See "How the registers grow" (BOK-05.6).

> **Register fields.** Year · Title (original, with English meaning) · Author · Form · Awards and adaptations · Why it matters. Dates of ancient works are scholarly estimates. A dash (—) means not recorded here, never a guess. Film adaptations link to the Movies index (MOV-).

## BOK-01 Indian literatures: one civilisation, many tongues

> **Before you start — BOK-01.** India has 22 languages in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution, six languages declared "classical" by 2014 (Tamil, Sanskrit, Kannada, Telugu, Malayalam, Odia) and five more added in October 2024 (Marathi, Pali, Prakrit, Assamese, Bengali), and hundreds of mother tongues. Sahitya Akademi recognises 24 languages for its awards (the 22 plus English and Rajasthani). Indian literature is therefore not one literature but a family that shares epics, bhakti poets and modern themes. **See also:** BOK-12 History of the book, BOK-16.1 Indian awards, MOV-01 Indian cinema (adaptations).

### ☐ BOK-01.1 Sanskrit literature

The classical language of ancient India, with literature from the Vedas (from about 1500 BCE) to the present day. Written in many scripts, today mostly Devanagari. **Tags:** #sanskrit #classical #india

- ☐ **Vedic literature**: the four Vedas (Rig, Sama, Yajur, Atharva), then Brahmanas, Aranyakas and Upanishads, the philosophical heart of the tradition.
- ☐ **Itihasa (the epics)**: the *Ramayana* (by tradition Valmiki, the "adi kavi", first poet) and the *Mahabharata* (by tradition Vyasa), which contains the *Bhagavad Gita*.
- ☐ **Puranas**: eighteen major Puranas of myth, genealogy and devotion, such as the *Bhagavata Purana*.
- ☐ **Kavya (court poetry)**: Ashvaghosha (*Buddhacharita*), Kalidasa (*Raghuvamsha*, *Kumarasambhava*, *Meghaduta*), Bharavi, Magha, Sriharsha; the five great kavyas (pancha mahakavya).
- ☐ **Drama (nataka)**: Bhasa, Kalidasa (*Abhijnanashakuntalam*), Shudraka (*Mrichchhakatika*), Vishakhadatta (*Mudrarakshasa*), Bhavabhuti (*Uttararamacharita*).
- ☐ **Prose and story**: Banabhatta (*Kadambari*, *Harshacharita*), Dandin (*Dashakumaracharita*), the *Panchatantra*, *Hitopadesha*, *Kathasaritsagara* (Somadeva), *Vetala Panchavimshati*.
- ☐ **Lyric and devotion**: Bhartrihari's *Shatakatraya*, Jayadeva's *Gita Govinda* (12th century), Amaru's *Amarushataka*.
- ☐ **Shastra (knowledge texts)**: Panini's grammar *Ashtadhyayi*, Bharata's *Natyashastra*, Kautilya's *Arthashastra*, Charaka and Sushruta (medicine), Aryabhata (astronomy), Kalhana's *Rajatarangini* (history of Kashmir, 1148–49).
- ☐ **Modern Sanskrit**: writers still publish; Sahitya Akademi gives an annual Sanskrit award.

### ☐ BOK-01.2 Pali and Prakrit literatures

Languages of early Buddhism and Jainism, declared classical in 2024. **Tags:** #pali #prakrit #buddhist #jain

- ☐ **Pali Canon (Tipitaka)**: Vinaya, Sutta and Abhidhamma; the *Dhammapada* and *Jataka* tales (stories of the Buddha's past lives).
- ☐ **Jain Agamas**: in Ardhamagadhi Prakrit; later commentaries.
- ☐ **Prakrit poetry**: Hala's *Gaha Sattasai* (Maharashtri Prakrit, early centuries CE), seven hundred love poems of village life.
- ☐ **Apabhramsha**: the bridge to modern North Indian languages; Svayambhu's Ramayana (*Paumachariu*), Pushpadanta.

### ☐ BOK-01.3 Tamil literature

India's oldest continuous classical literature after Sanskrit, the first language declared classical (2004). **Tags:** #tamil #classical #sangam

- ☐ **Sangam literature** (about 300 BCE–300 CE): the Eight Anthologies (*Ettuthokai*) and Ten Idylls (*Pattuppattu*), sorted into akam (love) and puram (war, public life); *Tolkappiyam*, the oldest grammar.
- ☐ **Ethical works**: Thiruvalluvar's *Thirukkural*, 1,330 couplets on virtue, wealth and love, translated into dozens of languages.
- ☐ **Twin epics**: *Silappatikaram* (Ilango Adigal) and *Manimekalai* (Sattanar).
- ☐ **Bhakti**: the Shaiva Nayanars (*Tevaram*) and Vaishnava Alvars (*Nalayira Divya Prabandham*), including Andal, from the 6th–9th centuries.
- ☐ **Kamban's *Ramavataram*** (12th century): the great Tamil Ramayana.
- ☐ **Modern**: Subramania Bharati (poet of freedom), Pudhumaippithan (short story), Kalki (*Ponniyin Selvan*), Jayakanthan, Sujatha, Ashokamitran, Ambai, Bama (*Karukku*, Dalit memoir), Perumal Murugan, Imayam, Salma, Vairamuthu.

### ☐ BOK-01.4 Kannada literature

Classical language (2008); eight Kannada writers have won the Jnanpith Award. **Tags:** #kannada #classical

- ☐ **Early**: *Kavirajamarga* (9th century, earliest surviving work on poetics); Pampa, Ponna and Ranna, the "three gems" of Jain Kannada poetry.
- ☐ **Vachana movement** (12th century): Basavanna, Akka Mahadevi, Allama Prabhu; short prose poems against caste and ritual.
- ☐ **Haridasa songs**: Purandara Dasa and Kanaka Dasa.
- ☐ **Modern**: Kuvempu (first Kannada Jnanpith, 1967), D. R. Bendre, Shivaram Karanth, Masti Venkatesha Iyengar, V. K. Gokak, U. R. Ananthamurthy (*Samskara*), Girish Karnad (drama), Chandrashekhara Kambara, S. L. Bhyrappa, Devanur Mahadeva, Vivek Shanbhag, Banu Mushtaq (International Booker 2025).

### ☐ BOK-01.5 Telugu literature

Classical language (2008); famous for its musical verse and avadhanam memory feats. **Tags:** #telugu #classical

- ☐ **Kavitrayam**: Nannaya, Tikkana and Errapragada, who rendered the *Mahabharata* in Telugu (11th–14th centuries).
- ☐ **Prabandha age**: Krishnadevaraya's court, the Ashtadiggajas; Allasani Peddana; Tenali Ramakrishna of the folk tales.
- ☐ **Saints and singers**: Vemana (pithy verses), Annamacharya, Tyagaraja (Carnatic songs), Pothana (*Bhagavatam*).
- ☐ **Modern**: Gurajada Apparao (*Kanyasulkam*, 1892 play), Viswanatha Satyanarayana (first Telugu Jnanpith), Sri Sri (*Maha Prasthanam*), C. Narayana Reddy, Ravuri Bharadwaja, Chalam, Volga.

### ☐ BOK-01.6 Malayalam literature

Classical language (2013) with one of India's highest reading cultures. **Tags:** #malayalam #classical #kerala

- ☐ **Early**: *Ramacharitam*, the Manipravalam style (Malayalam mixed with Sanskrit); Cherusseri's *Krishnagatha*.
- ☐ **Thunchaththu Ezhuthachan** (16th century): "father of modern Malayalam", *Adhyatma Ramayanam*.
- ☐ **Modern poets**: the triumvirate Kumaran Asan, Ulloor and Vallathol; G. Sankara Kurup (first Jnanpith ever, 1965, for *Odakkuzhal*).
- ☐ **Novel**: O. Chandu Menon's *Indulekha* (1889), Thakazhi Sivasankara Pillai (*Chemmeen*), Vaikom Muhammad Basheer, S. K. Pottekkatt, M. T. Vasudevan Nair, O. V. Vijayan (*Khasakkinte Itihasam*), Kamala Das (Madhavikutty), M. Mukundan, Benyamin (*Aadujeevitham*), K. R. Meera.

### ☐ BOK-01.7 Hindi literature

The literature of India's most widely spoken language, with roots in Braj, Awadhi and Khari Boli. **Tags:** #hindi

- ☐ **Adikal / Veergatha** (about 1000–1350): heroic ballads such as *Prithviraj Raso* (attributed to Chand Bardai; date debated).
- ☐ **Bhakti kal** (about 1350–1650): Kabir and the nirgun saints; Surdas (Braj, Krishna); Tulsidas (*Ramcharitmanas*, Awadhi, 1574 onward); Malik Muhammad Jayasi (*Padmavat*, 1540); Meera's songs; Raskhan, Rahim.
- ☐ **Riti kal** (about 1650–1850): ornate court poetry: Keshavdas, Bihari (*Satsai*), Bhushan, Ghananand.
- ☐ **Adhunik kal** (modern, from about 1850): Bharatendu Harishchandra, Mahavir Prasad Dwivedi; Chhayavad poets Jaishankar Prasad (*Kamayani*), Suryakant Tripathi "Nirala", Sumitranandan Pant, Mahadevi Verma.
- ☐ **Fiction**: Premchand (*Godaan*, 1936; short stories), Phanishwar Nath Renu (*Maila Anchal*), Yashpal, Jainendra, Agyeya, Bhisham Sahni (*Tamas*), Nirmal Verma, Mohan Rakesh, Mannu Bhandari, Krishna Sobti, Shrilal Shukla (*Raag Darbari*), Manohar Shyam Joshi, Vinod Kumar Shukla, Geetanjali Shree (*Ret Samadhi*, International Booker 2022 as *Tomb of Sand*, tr. Daisy Rockwell).
- ☐ **Poetry after 1947**: Nayi Kavita, Muktibodh, Raghuvir Sahay, Dhumil, Kedarnath Singh, Kunwar Narayan, Harivansh Rai Bachchan (*Madhushala*), Ramdhari Singh "Dinkar", Dushyant Kumar (Hindi ghazal).
- ☐ **Dalit and women's writing**: Omprakash Valmiki (*Joothan*), Mamta Kalia, Maitreyi Pushpa, Chitra Mudgal.

### ☐ BOK-01.8 Urdu literature

Born in the Deccan and Delhi from Persian, Arabic and Indian roots; the great language of the ghazal. **Tags:** #urdu #ghazal

- ☐ **Dakhni beginnings**: Quli Qutb Shah, Wali Dakhni.
- ☐ **Classical ghazal**: Mir Taqi Mir, Sauda, Ghalib (letters and ghazals), Momin, Zauq, Dagh.
- ☐ **Reform and nazm**: Hali, Nazir Akbarabadi (poet of everyday life), Muhammad Iqbal, Josh Malihabadi.
- ☐ **Progressive Writers' Movement** (founded 1936, first conference presided by Premchand): Faiz Ahmad Faiz, Sahir Ludhianvi, Kaifi Azmi, Ali Sardar Jafri, Majrooh Sultanpuri.
- ☐ **Fiction**: Saadat Hasan Manto (*Toba Tek Singh*), Ismat Chughtai (*Lihaaf*), Krishan Chander, Rajinder Singh Bedi, Qurratulain Hyder (*Aag ka Darya*; Jnanpith 1989), Intizar Husain, Shamsur Rahman Faruqi.
- ☐ **Dastangoi**: the art of long oral tales such as the *Dastan-e Amir Hamza*, revived in Delhi from 2005.

### ☐ BOK-01.9 Bengali literature

Classical language (2024); the first Indian literature to win the Nobel Prize (Tagore, 1913). **Tags:** #bengali #bangla

- ☐ **Charyapada** (about 8th–12th centuries): Buddhist songs, the earliest Bengali (and Assamese and Odia) writing.
- ☐ **Medieval**: Chandidas, *Mangal kavya* narratives, Krittivas's Ramayana, Chaitanya biographies.
- ☐ **Bengal Renaissance**: Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar (prose), Michael Madhusudan Dutt (*Meghnad Badh Kavya*, blank verse), Bankim Chandra (*Anandamath* with "Vande Mataram").
- ☐ **Rabindranath Tagore**: *Gitanjali*, *Gora*, *Ghare Baire*, short stories, songs (Rabindrasangeet), two national anthems (India and Bangladesh).
- ☐ **After Tagore**: Sarat Chandra (*Devdas*), Kazi Nazrul Islam (the "rebel poet"), Jibanananda Das, Bibhutibhushan Bandyopadhyay (*Pather Panchali*), Tarashankar, Manik Bandyopadhyay, Mahasweta Devi, Sunil Gangopadhyay, Shirshendu, Satyajit Ray (Feluda), Humayun Ahmed and Taslima Nasrin (Bangladesh).

### ☐ BOK-01.10 Marathi literature

Classical language (2024); strong traditions of saint poetry, theatre and Dalit autobiography. **Tags:** #marathi

- ☐ **Saints (Varkari tradition)**: Dnyaneshwar (*Dnyaneshwari*, 1290), Namdev, Eknath, Tukaram (abhangas), Janabai, Bahinabai.
- ☐ **Powada and lavani**: heroic ballads and folk song.
- ☐ **Modern**: Hari Narayan Apte, V. S. Khandekar (*Yayati*; Jnanpith 1974), Kusumagraj, P. L. Deshpande (humour), Vinda Karandikar, Bhalchandra Nemade (*Kosla*), Durga Bhagwat, G. A. Kulkarni.
- ☐ **Dalit literature**: Namdeo Dhasal (Dalit Panthers, 1972), Daya Pawar (*Baluta*), Laxman Mane, Sharankumar Limbale, Urmila Pawar.
- ☐ **Theatre**: Vijay Tendulkar (*Ghashiram Kotwal*), Mahesh Elkunchwar.

### ☐ BOK-01.11 Gujarati literature

**Tags:** #gujarati

- ☐ **Early**: Hemachandra's grammar (12th century); Narsinh Mehta (15th century, "Vaishnava Jana To"), Akho, Premanand (akhyana storytelling).
- ☐ **Modern**: Narmad, Govardhanram Tripathi (*Saraswatichandra*), Mahatma Gandhi's autobiography (*Satya na Prayogo*, 1925–29), K. M. Munshi, Umashankar Joshi (Jnanpith 1967, shared), Pannalal Patel, Raghuveer Chaudhari.

### ☐ BOK-01.12 Punjabi literature

Written in Gurmukhi (India) and Shahmukhi (Pakistan). **Tags:** #punjabi

- ☐ **Baba Farid** (12th–13th century) and the **Guru Granth Sahib** (compiled 1604, with verses of the Sikh Gurus and saints of many faiths).
- ☐ **Sufi poets**: Bulleh Shah, Shah Hussain, Sultan Bahu; Waris Shah's *Heer* (1766).
- ☐ **Modern**: Bhai Vir Singh, Amrita Pritam (*Pinjar*; "Ajj aakhaan Waris Shah nu" on Partition), Shiv Kumar Batalvi, Gurdial Singh, Surjit Patar, Pash.

### ☐ BOK-01.13 Odia literature

Classical language (2014). **Tags:** #odia

- ☐ **Sarala Das's Mahabharata** (15th century); the **Panchasakha** saint-poets; Upendra Bhanja (ornate kavya).
- ☐ **Modern**: Fakir Mohan Senapati (*Chha Mana Atha Guntha*, 1897, a pioneering realist novel), Gopinath Mohanty (Jnanpith 1973), Sitakant Mahapatra, Pratibha Ray.

### ☐ BOK-01.14 Assamese literature

Classical language (2024). **Tags:** #assamese

- ☐ **Madhava Kandali's Ramayana** (14th century), among the first in a modern Indian language.
- ☐ **Srimanta Sankardev** (15th–16th century): borgeet songs, ankiya naat plays, the Sattriya tradition.
- ☐ **Modern**: Lakshminath Bezbarua, Birendra Kumar Bhattacharya (Jnanpith 1979), Mamoni Raisom Goswami (Indira Goswami), Nilmani Phookan.

### ☐ BOK-01.15 Other Eighth Schedule literatures

- ☐ **Kashmiri**: Lal Ded (Lalleshwari, 14th century vakhs), Habba Khatoon, Mahjoor, Rahman Rahi (Jnanpith 2004).
- ☐ **Konkani**: Shenoy Goembab, Ravindra Kelekar (Jnanpith 2006), Damodar Mauzo (Jnanpith 2022).
- ☐ **Maithili**: Vidyapati (14th–15th century love songs), Harimohan Jha, Nagarjuna (Yatri).
- ☐ **Manipuri (Meiteilon)**: the *Cheitharol Kumbaba* royal chronicle, Hijam Anganghal, Lamabam Kamal.
- ☐ **Nepali**: Bhanubhakta Acharya (Nepali Ramayana), Laxmi Prasad Devkota (*Muna Madan*), Indra Bahadur Rai (Darjeeling).
- ☐ **Sindhi**: Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai (*Shah Jo Risalo*), Sachal Sarmast; writers in India after Partition such as Moti Prakash.
- ☐ **Santali**: written in the Ol Chiki script created by Pandit Raghunath Murmu (1925); oral creation stories.
- ☐ **Dogri**: Padma Sachdev, Dinoo Bhai Pant.
- ☐ **Bodo**: hymns, folk literature and modern poetry in the Devanagari script.

### ☐ BOK-01.16 Rajasthani, Bhojpuri, Awadhi, Braj and other languages

- ☐ **Rajasthani** (recognised by Sahitya Akademi): Dingal and Pingal bardic poetry, *Dhola Maru*, Meera's songs, *Veli Krishan Rukmani ri* (Prithviraj Rathore), Vijaydan Detha ("Bijji", folk tales, *Duvidha*), Kanhaiyalal Sethia, Chandra Prakash Deval.
- ☐ **Bhojpuri**: Bhikhari Thakur (*Bidesiya*, "the Shakespeare of Bhojpuri"), folk songs of migration.
- ☐ **Awadhi and Braj**: the bhakti classics of Tulsidas and Surdas.
- ☐ **Tulu, Kodava, Garhwali, Kumaoni, Chhattisgarhi, Haryanvi** and others: oral epics, songs and modern writing.

### ☐ BOK-01.17 Tribal and oral literatures of India

- ☐ Creation myths, songs and epics of the Gond (*Pandvani*-style performances), Bhil (*Bhilodi Mahabharata*), Santal, Munda, Oraon, Khasi, Mizo, Naga and Toda peoples.
- ☐ Collectors and champions: Verrier Elwin, Ganesh Devy (People's Linguistic Survey of India, Bhasha Research Centre), Ramnika Gupta.
- ☐ Modern Adivasi writers: Hansda Sowvendra Shekhar, Temsula Ao, Mamang Dai, Jacinta Kerketta, Narayan (Malayalam, *Kocharethi*).

### ☐ BOK-01.18 Indian writing in English

- ☐ **Pioneers**: Henry Derozio, Toru Dutt, Bankim's *Rajmohan's Wife* (1864), Sarojini Naidu, Sri Aurobindo (*Savitri*), Tagore's own translations.
- ☐ **The founding trio**: Mulk Raj Anand (*Untouchable*, 1935), R. K. Narayan (Malgudi), Raja Rao (*Kanthapura*, 1938).
- ☐ **Midnight's children**: Salman Rushdie (*Midnight's Children*, Booker 1981), Vikram Seth (*A Suitable Boy*), Amitav Ghosh (Jnanpith 2018, first English-language writer to win it), Arundhati Roy (*The God of Small Things*, Booker 1997), Anita Desai, Kiran Desai (Booker 2006), Rohinton Mistry, Upamanyu Chatterjee, Aravind Adiga (Booker 2008), Jhumpa Lahiri (Pulitzer 2000).
- ☐ **Poets**: Nissim Ezekiel, A. K. Ramanujan, Kamala Das, Dom Moraes, Jayanta Mahapatra, Arun Kolatkar (*Jejuri*), Eunice de Souza, Jeet Thayil.
- ☐ **Popular fiction**: Ruskin Bond, Chetan Bhagat, Amish Tripathi, Anuja Chauhan, Ashwin Sanghi.
- ☐ **Translation as Indian literature**: A. K. Ramanujan, Arshia Sattar, Daisy Rockwell, Deepa Bhasthi, Arunava Sinha and others make the bhasha literatures available across India and the world.
